## The chapter in one minute

Inland from Rotsnout's cottage stands an old fortress. The [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth) built it two centuries ago. Now Shadow Plane invaders called **sklaggans** hold it. Their leader has three faces and is called the **Flesh Stitcher**. It is building the **Skinloom**, a loom of living flesh. The loom is built around the Gullstone Cave [Jewel](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles) fragment. One day it can weave gateways to the Shadow Plane.

Criminals from Moonshadow's abandoned village hold the outer fortress. They wear hollowed-out monster skins. They expect a reward. In truth, they are being fattened for the Skinloom. Behind a black resin dome, an old mithral mine is now an alien lair. There the PCs fight the Flesh Stitcher. Then they pry the fragment from a trapped plate of flesh.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **The Bruised Crystal**, the third Jewel fragment (P21, p. 191).
2. **Defeated the Flesh Stitcher**. Its shadow creatures scatter. Its human lackeys give up.
3. **Up to 2 bundles of purifying tree bark** from the Pristine Clearing (p. 171), if they harvest it. It is a defense special resource.

**Party level:** starts at 6, ends at **7**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** This is the Act 2 dungeon crawl. It has two layers and many ways in. It pays off the Ch 6 investigation. It gives the PCs the third fragment. It also shows how far the cult reached. The P4 throne says so: "The Shivering Behemoth Rises, Under Our Control."

## How the chapter is built

The book's parts (p. 170–191):

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **In the Forest** | The 4-mile walk, past the Pristine Clearing. | First. The clearing can wait for the trip back |
| **Tainted Fortress: outer fortress (P1–P13)** | A wooden fort around a courtyard, held by lackeys in skinsuits. | Any order. Several entry routes |
| **The inner fortress (P14–P21)** | The old mine behind the resin dome, held by sklaggans and beasts. | Opened from P3 or P13 |
| **Skinloom and the Bruised Crystal** | Boss fight, then a complex trap to free the fragment. | Last |

**Ways into the inner fortress:** the P3 resin dome or the dark P13 rear hall. Either way needs a sklaggan tool (a **skagappa**) or a handful of bloody viscera. The only viscera the PCs can easily find is the bowl in **P10**.

> **💡 GM tip (the "how do we assault a fortress" stall):** Book sidebar (p. 173). Players may freeze, unsure how to attack a whole fort. PCs who scout without the swarm assassins seeing them notice sloppy patrols. Tell them the defenders are disorganized and a direct approach can work. Or allow an easy Warfare Lore check, or similar, to learn it. Players who love heists can face organized defenders instead. You can also use the *Gamemastery Guide* **infiltration subsystem**.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** The people here often fight each other, and nobody helps anyone (p. 176). So each room fights alone. Careful PCs can rest between rooms. Alarms break this rule. Watch for the P1 swarm assassins, the P10 butcher, a P8 collapse, and Umbrocious warning his master (P12).

> **⚠️ Content warning (book sidebar, p. 173):** Some sklaggan bio-technology is grotesque. You can soften it. Make the dome-opening viscera a plain shadowy substance. Make the Skinloom blend life force with shadow instead of twisting flesh. Decide before the session.

Covered in earlier chapters:

- [Movement and terrain](gm_chapter_01.md#movement-and-terrain): climbing, falling, Grab an Edge, and cover.
- [Monster Parts](gm_chapter_01.md#monster-parts-treasure-system).
- **Persistent damage:** [Chapter 6](gm_chapter_06.md#persistent-damage-and-bleed). This chapter has four kinds. Bleed comes from Peel Skin. Fire comes from the arsonist spirit. Negative comes from the Soulweaver Trap. Bludgeoning comes from a critical failure against the P8 collapse.
- **Afflictions:** [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#afflictions-and-poison). **Shadow locust poison** (P17) has three 1-round stages. Its maximum duration is **6 rounds**.
- **Haunts:** [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#haunts). Use **Medicine** or **Religion** to disable the **arsonist spirit (P13)**. It stops resetting once the three P20 sklaggans die.
- **Counteracting:** [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting). The P19 glyph and Soulweaver Trap corners list "**dispel magic (4th level; counteract DC 24)**". That means you counteract against **DC 24**.
- **Swarms:** [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#swarms). The suited swarm assassin (P17) is immune to precision damage. Once its skinsuit ruptures, it gains swarm weaknesses, resistances, and immunities ([stats](#p17--skinsuit-prep-room-low-6)).

### Hardness on creatures (skinsuits)

Every humanoid in a **skinsuit** has **Hardness 5**. Subtract 5 from each instance of damage.
- **Example:** a flenser in a skinsuit takes a 12-damage hit. It loses 7 HP.
- **Rupture:** the suit breaks when the wearer drops below **half its maximum HP**. It also breaks **the moment it takes damage from a critical hit**. Then Hardness ends. AC, saves, attack modifiers, and Strike damage each drop by 2.
- **Weapons:** the wearer uses pincers while the suit is intact. After that, it uses its own weapon (kukri, kama).
- **Level bump:** the suit adds 1 to the wearer's level. The stat blocks already include it (p. 175).

> **💡 GM tip:** Breaking the suits is the book's best tactic. Crits rupture them. So fighters, flanking rogues, and flat-footing tricks shine here.

### Regeneration

The **skornatha** (P15, P17) has **regeneration 5 (deactivated by electricity or fire)** (p. 399).
- It gains +5 HP at the start of each of its turns. While regeneration is on, its dying value stops at 3. Damage alone can't kill it.
- **Electricity or fire** switches regeneration off until the end of its next turn. Have the PCs land some the round they drop it.

### Conditions new this chapter

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Enfeebled X** | Status penalty X to Strength-based rolls and DCs: Strength-based melee attack and damage rolls, and Athletics. |
| **Blinded** | Can't see. Normal terrain is difficult terrain. Perception checks that need sight always critically fail. |
| **Fleeing** | Spends its actions moving away from the source. Can't Delay or Ready. |
| **Grabbed** | Flat-footed and immobilized. A manipulate action needs a DC 5 flat check, or the action is lost. Escape ends it. |

Also in this chapter: drained ([Ch 6](gm_chapter_06.md#drained)), slowed ([Ch 4](gm_chapter_04.md#slowed-and-fatigued)), stupefied and stunned ([Ch 5](gm_chapter_05.md#conditions-new-this-chapter)), restrained ([Ch 6](gm_chapter_06.md#restrained-and-escape)).

### Balance and uneven ground

Three edges of the P8 pit are **uneven ground**. Moving onto them takes a **DC 22 Acrobatics** check to **Balance**. The book rules that a failure means falling into the pit.

### Escape, Disarm, and Shove

- **Escape:** [Chapter 6](gm_chapter_06.md#restrained-and-escape). Escape also frees a creature from Weave Together (Escape DC 25).
- **Disarm:** Athletics vs. the target's **Reflex DC**.
  - Success: the effect lasts until the start of the target's next turn. The target takes a **−2 circumstance penalty** to attacks with that item. Further Disarms against it get a **+2 circumstance bonus**.
  - Critical success: the item drops.
  - Snag Weapon (P21) makes the success last longer.
- **Shove:** Athletics vs. the target's **Fortitude DC**. It pushes 5 feet, or 10 feet on a critical success. The P8 flensers use it to push PCs into the pit. The P5 bullies use it with Brutal Bully Push. That knocks the target prone unless the bully Strides after it.

### Darkness and vision

The inner fortress is **completely dark** (p. 183). PCs without darkvision need a light source. Low-light vision doesn't help. Every sklaggan creature has darkvision or greater darkvision. A PC carrying light is easy to spot.

## In the Forest and the Pristine Clearing

**Where:** the 4-mile path from Rotsnout's cottage (p. 170–171). Tarry resin grows thicker on the trees as the PCs go. The sklaggans blight-bomb anything that might be a rival.
- **Off the path:** the book discourages it. Nearly all life here is dead, twisted, or gone. There's nowhere to go but the fortress.

> **🔒 GM-only (what's going on):** The Flesh Stitcher needs living meat for the Skinloom. So it tricked the villagers instead of killing them. It turned them against each other with promises of power. The cruelest got "gifts" like skinsuits. The disloyal or meek were secretly flayed. The rest think they're favored. All of them are bound for the Skinloom (p. 171).

### Pristine Clearing *(defense special resource)*

Close beside the path stands a ring of trees the resin hasn't touched. They hold a purifying magic found only on this island.
- **Spotting the value:** **DC 22 Nature or Survival**, or **DC 17 Forest Lore**. Success shows that the bark concentrates the magic.
- **Limit:** **one character** can try, **once**. The bark weighs **30 Bulk**. It fits in Prince Kulupi's bag of holding.

**Harvest Purifying Tree Bark** *(exploration, manipulate, secret)*: takes a few hours. **Secret DC 22 Arcana, Crafting, or Nature**, or **DC 20 Forest Lore**.
- **Critical success:** **after 1 week**, the PCs gain an **additional** resource. That makes **2 bundles** total.
- **Success:** **1** purifying tree bark resource.
- **Failure:** after 1 week it becomes a **basic wood resource**. It has no special uses, but it trades for Resource Points.
- **Critical failure:** after 1 week, the bark is worthless.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The bark is a Ch 12 **defense special resource**. The PCs can get up to **2 bundles of bark** here. Bark plus an arrow tower builds a **purifying tower**. It deals **15 damage against undead and husks** instead of 5. Any special resource also trades for **10 generic Resource Points** (p. 315). **Record the bundles and any basic wood.**

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The roll is secret, and the result shows a week later. Note it privately. PCs who pass the clearing on the way in can harvest on the way back (p. 191).

## The fortress: features and map

> **🔒 GM-only (history):** The cult built the fortress for two reasons. It guarded the cliffside mithral mines. It was also a base for controlling the island. Digging a tower foundation turned up more mithral. The mines ran dry. The cult left by the end of the War of the Shivering Behemoth. The sklaggans love the twisting mine because it's easy to defend. They expanded it and sealed it with resin that reminds them of home. They leave the fort to their minions (p. 171).

### Outer fortress features (p. 171–173)

- **Walls and doors:** flame-resistant island wood, two centuries old. It's **almost impossible to burn**. Most doors bolt from inside, but **none are bolted** when the PCs arrive.
- **Light:** hooked lanterns. Most are out in the hours before dawn while the people inside rest.
- **Ceilings:** 15 feet, except in the open-air courtyard and access areas (**P1, P2, P3**).
- **Walls:** 20 feet high outside. A low battlement runs around the flat roof. The ladders were burned for firewood. Climbing takes **DC 20 Athletics**.
- **The roof:** nobody goes up there. It's firm except over **P8**.
  - Anyone crossing the middle of P8's roof breaks through. They must **Grab an Edge (DC 20 Reflex)** or fall 15 feet farther into the pit. That's **18 bludgeoning** total. The noise draws nearby people.
  - Other noisy roof travel may draw the room below.

### What happens if the PCs lose (outer fortress)

Book sidebar (p. 176): the winners keep beaten PCs alive. They plan to bargain with the sklaggans. The sklaggans will likely flay the PCs in the end, but not for a couple of days. That gives the PCs plenty of time to escape.

### Secrets of the Sklaggans *(Recall Knowledge)*

Once the PCs know the sklaggans are in charge, they can **Recall Knowledge with Occultism** about them (p. 177). They can do the same for skadboks and skornathas, the creatures the skinsuits come from. They can do this before meeting live ones.
- Success also reveals the sklaggan **hierarchy**. It shows the sklaggans are **duping the convicts**.
- No humanoid here is trained in Occultism. So none of them knows their fate. They likely won't believe the PCs either.

## The outer fortress (P1–P13)

### P1 — Forest Approach *(Low 6)*

> *An old wooden fortress, more than a hundred feet on each side, is built against a low, rocky hill. The walls rise up 20 feet from the ground to form a jagged battlement from sharpened tree trunks that make up the outer walls. A wide wooden gate stands shut in the middle of the wall. Several arrow slits all along the south face of the fortress allow anyone beyond to see out, but the other side is cloaked in deep shadow. Brush grows right up to the fortress's walls, indicating that it hasn't been well-tended for some time, but the path to the gate seems recently used and spattered with globs of black resin.*

Nobody clears the brush. The occupants don't expect an assault.

**Corrupted Swarm Assassins (4):** BB 109, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +10. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp.
- **Corrupted:** filled with resin. They ignore difficult terrain from natural features like swamp and undergrowth. They're **immune to poison damage**.
- **Placement:** roll 1d4 for each one. **1** west, **2** east, **3–4** front. No more than two per spot. Reroll a third.
- **Behavior:** they wander at random. They attack anyone sneaking around and call the others. Underbrush withers as they pass, which gives them away.
- **Walking in:** most humanoids look alike to them. PCs who walk up openly get let in. Talking to one takes **DC 18 Deception**, or the swarms attack.
- **If the PCs get past them:** the swarms hear fighting inside and arrive **after 2d6 rounds**.
- **Morale:** a swarm flees into the forest if it drops **below 25 HP**. It also flees if hit by a **critical hit from a splash weapon**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (swarm count):** The text places **four**. The stat header reads "Corrupted Swarm Assassins (1–2)" (p. 174). Use four. Low 6 needs all of them (4 × 15 = 60 XP).

> **💡 GM tip:** Walking in skips this fight. But the swarms can join P3 in the middle of that fight. Roll the 2d6 when the first fight inside starts.

### P2 — Front Gate

> *The front gate to the fortress consists of two wide doors. Both contain repeating images that look like hills marching off into the distance, but close inspection reveals that these "hills" are titanic crustaceans, like pillbugs.*

- A beam for barring the gate leans inside. Nobody uses it. The gate is shut only to keep out animals. People push it open.
- **If barred:** forcing it open from outside takes **DC 30 Athletics**.

### P3 — Courtyard *(Moderate 6)*

> *The courtyard at the center of the fortress is surrounded by several buildings built into the fort's walls. The grass is heavily trampled and spattered with spots of tarry resin. The rock face at the back side of the fortress has a huge hemisphere of tarry resin bulging from it, like a black balloon. The hemisphere is almost entirely opaque, although there's a faint reflection on its surface of a passage leading back into the rock.*

#### The resin hemisphere (the dome)

The dome is sklaggan bio-technology. It's far tougher than the tree resin.
- **Hardness 30, 60 HP per 5-foot square.** When damaged, it **regenerates 1d4 HP every minute**. This works even with a section destroyed. Only destroying the whole dome stops it.
- It **blocks all light, sound, and incorporeal creatures**. That includes Umbrocious. It runs several feet into the rock. Nobody can burrow or *earth glide* around it.
- **Opening it:** touch it with a sklaggan tool such as a **skagappa**. **A handful of bloody viscera** works too, because the dome can't tell them apart. It opens like an eye's iris, within 6 feet of the touch.
- **Closing:** it closes **after 3 rounds**.
  - Anything in the opening takes **5d8 slashing, DC 22 basic Reflex** ([basic saves](gm_chapter_01.md#basic-saving-throws)). A creature can jump to either side.
  - Non-magical items get cut. Magic items get pushed aside.
  - **A creature reduced to 0 HP by this damage is slain outright, sliced in half.**
- Humanoids may not pass it unless summoned or escorted. They keep viscera for that in the **P10** bowl.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The 0-HP rule skips dying. The creature dies at once. Count the 3 rounds aloud so players see it coming.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** All resin doors in the inner fortress work the same way (p. 183).

#### Creatures

Four lackeys train in **skinsuits** made of skornatha skin. They snap the pincers and enjoy the power.
- They know every villager. So they likely **spot the PCs as intruders** and **attack at once**. The acolyte casts from the back. The flensers fight in melee.
- **None surrenders or flees.** They chase, but they **don't call for help**. They want the credit.
- From midnight to a few hours after dawn, they rest in **P7** with their suits on.

**Evaluating the suits:** each PC who sees them rolls a **secret DC 18 Perception** check. The result holds for later wearers too.
- **Critical success:** a humanoid inside the hollowed-out skin of an alien monster. Enough damage, or a critical hit, ruptures the suit's protection.
- **Success:** a humanoid in some kind of bulky organic armor.
- **Failure:** an aberration that seems wounded and acts strangely.
- **Critical failure:** the PC can't tell them from ordinary skornathas.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Monster Parts):** The parts listed for wearers are **skinsuit shards**. The book never has PCs butcher humanoids.

**G'mayun Acolyte:** Creature 6, female CE Medium g'mayun humanoid, shadow priest (BB 35), p. 175
- **Perception** +14; low-light vision, truecolor sight (sees air, force, negative, and positive effects as easily as fire)
- **Languages** Auran, Common, Orpok
- **Skills** Acrobatics +12, Arcana +12, Intimidation +13, Religion +14, Thievery +12
- **AC** 24 (22 when her skinsuit ruptures); **Fort** +10, **Ref** +14, **Will** +16; **HP** 75; **Hardness** 5
- **Skinsuit** ([how it works](#hardness-on-creatures-skinsuits)). **Speed** 25 ft
- **Melee** pincer +17 (deadly d10), 2d8+7 piercing *(suit intact only)*
- **Melee** kukri +17 (agile, finesse, trip), 1d6+7 slashing *(suit ruptured only)*
- **Divine Spontaneous Spells** DC 24, attack +16. **3rd** (2 slots) *chilling darkness*, *vampiric touch*. **2nd** (3 slots) *darkness*, *death knell*, *faerie fire*. **1st** (3 slots) *bane*, *bless*, *fear*. **Cantrips (3rd)** *chill touch*, *daze*, *forbidding ward*, *guidance*, *sigil*
- **Monster Parts** 30/65/125 gp

**Orpok Flensers (3):** Creature 3, CE Medium orpok humanoid, p. 176
- **Perception** +9, scent (imprecise) 30 feet. **Languages** Common, Orpok
- **Skills** Athletics +10, Intimidation +7, Medicine +9, Survival +9
- **AC** 19 (17 when their skinsuit ruptures); **Fort** +11, **Ref** +8, **Will** +9; **HP** 44; **Hardness** 5
- **Skinsuit** as the acolyte. **Speed** 25 ft
- **Melee** pincer +12 (deadly d10), 1d8+7 piercing *(suit intact only)*
- **Melee** kama +12 (agile, trip), 1d6+5 slashing *(suit ruptured only)*
- **Peel Skin** (2 actions): a melee Strike. On a hit, it deals an extra **1d8 persistent bleed** ([persistent damage](gm_chapter_06.md#persistent-damage-and-bleed)).
- **Monster Parts** 7/18/30 gp

> **📏 Rules refresher (threat math):** The acolyte (level 6, 40 XP) plus three flensers (level 3, 15 each) = 85 XP, **Moderate** at level 6.

### P4 — Great Hall

> *This large hall looks like it was once an audience chamber or receiving room. The walls are carved with images of mountains and towering crustaceans looming over ships and villages. A wooden throne at the east end of the room is carved with similar iconography, but it's been toppled and lies broken on the floor.*

This was the cult's audience hall. The throne's back reads **"The Shivering Behemoth Rises, Under Our Control"** in Common and Terran. The words framed whoever sat there.
- **Treasure:** a PC who examines the toppled throne finds it in the base. It's a **sturdy magic shield made of monster parts (refined shield 7, infused sturdy 7)**. Without Monster Parts, it's a **lesser sturdy shield**.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The cult wanted control of the behemoth. The reborn cult chases it again in Act 3 ([synopsis](gm_story_synopsis.md#the-cult)).

### P5 — Training Room *(Low 6)*

> *This room contains several weapon racks, an archery target, and a training dummy. The training materials here look ready to fall apart from too much use and too little repair. A cell stands in the northwest corner of this room.*

### P6 — Cell

The only door is **latched from the outside**. It has a metal grill to look through.

> *This squalid cell contains only a mat of old, scorched hay and a stained bucket.*

The cult built the cell for prisoners and rebels. It mostly preferred quick public executions. The sklaggans don't use the cell. They turn enemies and lackeys alike into Skinloom material.

**Goaflom** (CN male wildfire leshy 2; BB 120) has been held here nearly a week.
- **Who he is:** a forger. He escaped a prison galley two years ago and hid quietly in the Moonshadow village. He followed the others here and was disgusted. He saw villagers taken into the inner fortress in ones and twos. None came back. When he spoke out, the others locked him up.

### P8 — Pit *(Low 6)*

> *The floor of this chamber drops away into a pit almost 20 feet deep. The narrow edges around the pit look unstable. The wooden walls are carved with images of armored isopods with glittering crystals in their carapaces.*

This is the easternmost room. Unlike the guard room west of it, it has no arrow slits. The cult dug for mithral under the floor before fortifying the room. The digging weakened the structure.

**The pit:**
- The **northern edge is safe**. The other three are **uneven ground**. Moving on them takes **DC 22 Acrobatics** to Balance ([how Balance works](#balance-and-uneven-ground)). On a failure, the creature falls in with crumbling stone.
- **Falling in from the edge:** **10 bludgeoning**, landing **prone**. From the roof: **18 bludgeoning**.
- **Climbing out:** DC 22.

**Collapsing the room:**
- The first failed Balance and fall exposes this corner's **foundation posts**. **DC 20 Crafting** or **DC 15 Engineering Lore** spots that pulling them could topple the room.
- **Setting it up:** another **DC 20 Crafting** or **DC 15 Engineering Lore** check. Or use **DC 25 Athletics** to pull the foundation away.
  - **Success:** the character sets a delay of **0 to 10 rounds** before the collapse.
  - **Critical success:** they can set the collapse for any time **within the next hour**.
- **The collapse:** everyone in the room, pit included, takes **4d12 bludgeoning, DC 22 basic Fortitude**.
  - **Failure:** the creature is **buried**. It's **restrained** and can't free itself.
  - **Critical failure:** buried, plus **1d6 persistent bludgeoning** while trapped.
  - **Digging out:** **DC 20 Athletics**. It takes **4 minutes** (2 on a critical success). Double the time if the creature is in the pit. Halve it with shovels or proper tools.
- **Side effects:** the collapse leaves a new hole in the wall. **Every humanoid in the outer fortress** comes running.

> **💡 GM tip:** The collapse can be a distraction. The PCs set a 10-round collapse and slip to P10 for the viscera. They open the dome while everyone stares at the wreck.

**Orpok Flensers (3):** p. 176, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. Stats as in [P3](#p3--courtyard-moderate-6).

## The inner fortress (P14–P21)

The old mines are now a gallery of alien horrors. Play them more sinister and organic than the outer fortress (p. 183).

| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Ceilings** | 18 feet. Several feet of powdery black foam crust cover them. The crust is half-solid smoke, left behind by sklaggans. The ceiling works as **15 feet** unless the crust is torn out |
| **Ash motes** | The crust sheds bobbing flakes everywhere, all the time. They're too thin for concealment. They smell of scorched rubber |
| **Resin doors** | Like the [P3 dome](#the-resin-hemisphere-the-dome). A skagappa or wet entrails opens them. The closing iris slices the same way |
| **Tarry globs** | Taffy-like strands of resin stretch between globs on the walls. They pulse like tendons |
| **Darkness** | **Completely dark.** The motes sometimes glimmer, but they don't light anything. See [darkness and vision](#darkness-and-vision) |

### P14 — Resinous Entry

> *The chamber on the other side of the black resin hemisphere is carved from the rock. A scattering of broken bones and clumps of hair, both of which seem far more recent than the wall markings, litter the floor.*

- **The tally marks:** the cult's counts of ore and work shifts. **DC 18 Society**, or **training in Mining Lore**, reveals a **mithral mine**. **Prisoners** worked it. The cult drove them to death to raise the tally.
- **Hazards:** **two biting snares** (CRB) hide in the bones and hair. The P15 skadboks made them from their own bodies. See the map.
- **Attracting attention:** opening the dome from **P3** shifts the light and air pressure. The P15 creatures hurry over. Entering through **P13** doesn't alert them.

### P18 — Roiling Shadows

A resin dome seals the only entrance.

> *Eerie shadows roil along the walls of this empty stone room.*

A **pinhole-sized gate** opens to the wildest depths of the Shadow Plane. It feeds the Skinloom through P20's calibration. Even sklaggans fear loose shadow energy, so the room stays sealed.

**If the PCs open the door:**
- Shadows fill the **whole inner fortress over 1 minute**. Every shadow writhes.
- **In areas with writhing shadows, all creatures are flat-footed.** A creature that **Strides** pulls free until the start of its next turn.
- The shadows are bound to sklaggan bioenergy. They **don't spread past the inner fortress**, even with the P3 dome open. They **evaporate 1 minute after** the Flesh Stitcher and all inner-fortress sklaggans fall.
- **Attracting attention:** one **P20** sklaggan comes to look and fights. After 1 round it **runs back to P20 for help**.
- **Payoff:** a better P20 console result ([Recalibrate Shadow Energies](#recalibrate-shadow-energies)).

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The PCs are flat-footed too, unless they Stride every turn. It's a trade. They get easier P21 traps and face a lone sklaggan. In return, the fortress gets messier.

- **Disable:** **DC 26 Thievery** (expert) to drain the glyph harmlessly, or *dispel magic* (4th level; **counteract DC 24**) ([counteracting](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting)).
- **Shadowlight Curse** (reaction; curse, enchantment, mental, occult). **Trigger:** a creature touches the notes without speaking the passphrase in Shadowtongue. **Effect:** shifting light and darkness cling to it like a hood. Others find it unnerving. The creature is targeted by ***outcast's curse*** (**DC 26 Will**). While cursed, it **can't be concealed**. When invisible, it's only **concealed**, not undetected.
- The spray covers the creature until the curse is removed.

**Treasure:** the notes hide a **scroll of *hypercognition*** and a **scroll of *mind reading***. Without Monster Parts, one of the books holds a **wand of *hopeless night*** (*Advanced Player's Guide*). It's being used as an odd bookmark.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The curse ruins hiding in the dark. Read *outcast's curse* (*Core Rulebook*) before the session for its other effects.

### P20 — Calibration Room *(Moderate 6)*

> *A semicircular table of obsidian fills the western part of this room. Several black resin dials and switches are built into the table. Tendrils of shadow curl across and connect them. An array of humanoid skulls are mounted on the wall above the table like gruesome trophies.*

This panel tunes the energy feeding the Skinloom next door. The Skinloom mostly runs itself, but it isn't fully stable. So the sklaggans adjust it on the Flesh Stitcher's orders.

**Creatures:** three sklaggans usually watch the console. Sometimes they visit the outer fortress and push the lackeys toward self-destruction, for their own alien fun. They fear punishment for letting intruders through, so they **fight to the death**.

**Sklaggans (3):** BB 104, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.

> **🔒 GM-only:** These three killed the P13 arsonist. Killing them stops that haunt from resetting.

#### Recalibrate Shadow Energies

Anyone **trained or better in Arcana or Occultism** who studies the table sees it calibrates shadow energy.

**Recalibrate Shadow Energies** *(exploration, manipulate)*: **Requirements:** trained in Arcana or Occultism. It takes **5 minutes**. Roll **DC 22 Arcana or Occultism**.
- **Critical success:** reduce the **Disable DCs of all hazards in P21 by 2**, or **by 3** if the P18 shadow energy has been released.
- **Success:** reduce them **by 1**, or **by 2** if the P18 energy has been released.
- **Failure:** soul-chilling feedback deals **3d6 cold and 3d6 negative**, **DC 20 basic Fortitude**.
- **Critical failure:** as failure, but the save result is one step worse than rolled, and **non-sklaggans can't try again**.

**Limits:** the PC learns the degree of success, not the effect. Reductions **don't stack**. The PCs get **up to three** attempts. Then the controls lock up for good.

> **💡 GM tip:** "Hazards in P21" means the five sinew traps and the Soulweaver Trap. A critical success with P18 released cuts every boss-room Disable DC by 3. Tell the PCs only "it worked well."

### P21 — Skinloom *(Severe 6)*

> *A complicated network of skin, tendons, and exposed muscle fills the eastern end of this room, resembling a grotesque loom. Strands of tissue anchored to the north and south walls pull and twitch irregularly, as though part of some enormous biological machine. The colors around the machine are dull and muted, as though washed of pigment, except for a reddish-purple gemstone growing out of a flat plate of flesh in the loom's center. The gemstone is jagged on two sides, as though broken, and has the color of an angry bruise or swollen lesion.*

The Flesh Stitcher arrived with a small army of aberrations. It has been building the **Skinloom** ever since. The loom draws on three sources: Moonshadow Island's occult nature, the Jewel fragment, and tapped shadow energy. It uses them to weave an opening to the Shadow Plane. The Flesh Stitcher keeps adding fleshy flourishes for art's sake. It never calls the loom finished.

**Hazards:** hard-to-see loops of tendon coil around the room. A careless step tugs one, and a sinew lash whips out. See the map. **The Flesh Stitcher never triggers them.** It's too careful of its masterpiece.

**Whipping Sinew Traps (5):** Hazard 6, magical mechanical trap, p. 188
- **Stealth** DC 23 (trained)
- **Description:** a hair-thin loop of tendon. A long lash of sinew runs through the room. When the loop is disturbed, the lash comes alive and whips through the air.
- **Disable:** **DC 23 Thievery** (expert) to cut the loop safely, or **DC 21 Occultism** (trained) to block the animating magic.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +15, **Ref** +9. **Hardness** 12, **HP** 50 (BT 25). **Immunities** critical hits, object immunities, precision damage
- **Tendon Touch** (reaction). **Trigger:** the loop is pulled or severed, or a creature moves into its space. **Effect:** a sinew Strike against the triggering creature, as long as it's within 10 feet of the trap, and against a random adjacent creature, if any. On a critical hit, the target is **enfeebled 1 for 1 minute**. No multiple attack penalty.
- **Melee** sinew +19 (deadly 1d12), 3d8+3 slashing
- **Reset:** after 1 hour, if the Skinloom hasn't been destroyed, a new loop squirms into the square.

#### The Flesh Stitcher

**Three sklaggans fused together, facing outward.** It's a vain diva of grafted flesh. To it, chitin and bone aren't art. It puts up with the reddish-purple crystal only for its power.

- **Parley:** it hates that intruders "waste" skin on their own organs. **Lavish praise of the Skinloom**, or the **message from P11**, can buy **a round or two of pause**. It **won't let them leave with their skins**. Nothing calms it for long, and it attacks in the end.
- **Tactics:** it has fists, but it prefers disabling spells. It uses **Weave Together** to pin pairs.
- It **never leaves this room**, even to chase.

**Flesh Stitcher:** Creature 8, unique NE Huge aberration shadow, variant sklaggan (BB 104), p. 189
- **Perception** +17; greater darkvision
- **Languages** Aklo, Sklaggan, Shadowtongue (can't speak any language); telepathy 100 feet
- **Skills** Athletics +15, Deception +16, Intimidation +16, Medicine +17, Occultism +20, Thievery +18
- **Str** +3, **Dex** +4, **Con** +4, **Int** +6, **Wis** +3, **Cha** +4
- **AC** 27, all-around vision; **Fort** +18, **Ref** +16, **Will** +13; +1 status to all saves vs. mental
- **HP** 135
- **Extra Reaction:** it gets an additional reaction each turn, usable only for Snag Weapon.
- **Attack of Opportunity** (reaction)
- **Snag Weapon** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature attacks it with a melee weapon and misses. **Effect:** it wraps strands of alien flesh around the weapon and tries to **Disarm** the attacker, using **Thievery** instead of Athletics. On a successful Disarm, the circumstance bonus and penalty **last until the creature or an adjacent creature spends an Interact action to peel off the flesh** ([Disarm](#escape-disarm-and-shove)).
- **Speed** 30 ft
- **Melee** fist +20 (agile, finesse, reach 10 feet), 2d10+7 bludgeoning
- **Occult Innate Spells** DC 26, attack +18. **7th** *plane shift* (self only; to Shadow Plane only). **4th** *confusion*, *nightmare*, *phantasmal killer*, *vampiric touch*. **3rd** *dream message*. **2nd** *blur* (self only), *calm emotions*, *status*. **1st** *mindlink* (×3). **Cantrips (4th)** *daze*, *shield*, *telekinetic projectile*
- **Toxic Shadows** (2 actions; mental, shadow). It Strides up to its Speed. Each creature within 5 feet of its path takes **7d6 mental** and attempts a **DC 25 Fortitude** save. It can't use Toxic Shadows again for 1d4 rounds.
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage.
  - **Failure:** full damage and **sickened 1**.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage and **sickened 2**.
- **Weave Together** (1 action, manipulate; once per round). It targets two **adjacent** creatures within 30 feet and stitches them together with fleshy strings of semisolid shadow. Both attempt a **DC 25 Reflex** save. If **both fail**, or if one critically fails and the other doesn't critically succeed, they're **flat-footed and stuck together (Escape DC 25)**. While stuck, neither can move away from being adjacent to the other. A creature that Escapes is no longer stuck or flat-footed. The other stays flat-footed until it Escapes too.
- **Monster Parts** 64/140/250 gp

> **📏 Rules refresher (threat math):** The Flesh Stitcher (level 8, party +2, 80 XP) plus five simple level-6 traps (8 XP each) = 120, **Severe**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (it fights around its own traps):** PCs trigger loops. It doesn't. Toxic Shadows is a Stride. It can sweep through the PCs and end behind loops. Its fists have 10-foot reach. Mark the trap squares before the fight.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Snag Weapon twice a round):** The extra reaction lets it snag two misses a round. The −2 lasts until someone spends an action peeling the flesh off.

> **💡 GM tip (Weave Together):** Stuck pairs are easy targets for Toxic Shadows and spells. The PCs should spread out.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (pronouns):** The book uses "it" except twice ("his," p. 183, p. 186). This doc uses "it."

#### What happens if the PCs lose (Flesh Stitcher)

Book sidebar (p. 189): it wants the beaten PCs' skin soon. But it may need time to recalibrate the Skinloom for that much flesh. So minions hold the PCs somewhere else. While the Flesh Stitcher is busy, the PCs can escape, turn on their captors, and attack again.

### Recovering the gemstone *(Moderate 6)*

A **1-foot-square** plate of hardened flesh sits in the Skinloom's center. It's stitched in place at **each of its four corners**. The **Bruised Crystal** is fused to it like a crystal bruise. It's the next Jewel piece. It gives the loom much of its eerie power.

- Inside the inner fortress, the gemstone **can't be removed from the flesh plate** by any means. If someone tries, the plate wails through its pores and bleeds.
- The **flesh plate can be carefully removed** from the Skinloom. But **touching it triggers the trap**.
- **Once someone carries it past P14**, the plate turns brittle and flakes away. That frees the crystal.

**Soulweaver Trap:** Hazard 8, unique complex magical trap, p. 190–191
- **Stealth** +18 (expert)
- **Description:** touching the gemstone woven into the Skinloom fills the room with draining pulses. Removing the flesh plate from the Skinloom disables the trap.
- **Disable**, one corner at a time:
  - **DC 28 Athletics** (trained) or **DC 26 Thievery** (expert) **pulls a corner free** and **removes one Feedback action** from the routine.
  - **DC 28 Occultism** (trained) or *dispel magic* (4th level; counteract DC 24) **loosens a corner** and **removes the soul-draining pulse** from the routine.
  - Freeing or loosening **all four corners**, in any combination, disables the trap.
- **Corners:** Hardness 10, HP 64 (BT 32). **Immunities** object immunities. **Resistances** bludgeoning 10, piercing 10. **Weaknesses** slashing 5.
- **Unleash Feedback** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature touches the Bruised Crystal or the flesh plate. **Effect:** the trap uses **Feedback** on the triggering creature, then rolls initiative.
- **Feedback** (1 action; necromancy, occult). The target takes **4d10 mental** and attempts a **DC 26 Will** save.
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage and **slowed 1 for 1 round**.
  - **Failure:** full damage and **slowed 1 for 1 minute**.
  - **Critical failure:** full damage and **slowed 2 for 1 minute**.
- **Routine** (4 actions):
  - **Three Feedback actions.** Each one targets the **closest non-slowed creature to the Skinloom** in P21. If a creature critically succeeds and isn't slowed, it won't be targeted a second time in that routine.
  - **Last action, the soul-draining pulse.** Every creature in P21 attempts a **DC 26 Fortitude** save. **Slowed creatures use a result one step worse than they rolled.**
    - **Critical success:** unaffected.
    - **Success:** **2d8 persistent negative**.
    - **Failure:** **4d8 persistent negative** and **drained 1**.
    - **Critical failure:** **4d8 persistent negative** and **drained 2**.
    - The drained condition stacks across failures, up to **drained 4**.
- **Reset:** 1 minute after no creatures remain in the room, it weaves all four corners back into the Skinloom.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** This is a [complex hazard](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards). It rolls its own initiative. Each freed corner trims its routine. One complex level-8 hazard is 80 XP, **Moderate**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the Feedback actions chase the front line):** Feedback hits the closest non-slowed creature. It slows even on a success. So it works its way down the line of PCs working the corners. Then the pulse punishes everyone who is slowed. Rotate PCs in, strongest Will save first.

> **💡 GM tip (the order of corners):** Using Occultism or *dispel magic* first stops the drain. Using Athletics or Thievery first cuts Feedback. Show the players the choice.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (book wording):** The corner line says "64 (BT 32) to destroy a rune." It was likely copied from another trap. Treat it as corner HP.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (negative damage):** The Remaster renames negative energy to **void**.

**XP Award:** **80 XP** for recovering the Bruised Crystal.

### Bruised Crystal *(Item 7)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** held in 1 hand. **Bulk** negligible. (p. 191, also p. 378)
- A reddish-purple wedge. Its base is broken off from a larger whole. It's near-black at one edge and red at the other.
- **While you hold it in your hand**, your flesh turns spongy. You gain **resistance 3 to bludgeoning damage**. Your appearance doesn't change.

> **🧩 Sets up:** This is the **third fragment**. The [Obsidian Star](gm_glossary.md#obsidian-star) came in Ch 4 and the Aquamarine Wedge in Ch 5. The last is in Ch 8 at [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef). Fragments can't be destroyed one at a time (p. 379).

## Concluding the chapter

Once the Flesh Stitcher falls (p. 191):
- **The remaining shadow creatures** shun the PCs and **flee the fortress**.
- **The remaining humanoids** lose heart and **surrender**. They'd rather go to prison than stay here. The PCs choose:
  - let them go home to the village on Moonshadow,
  - take them aboard as prisoners,
  - or hire them as crew.
- **The bark:** harvest on the way back if skipped. See [the clearing](#pristine-clearing-defense-special-resource).
- **The crew** greets them with admiration and suspicion. Most of the crew bet on whether the PCs would return from the ill-omened island. Those bets get paid.

**Level up:** after leaving Moonshadow Island, the PCs rest and reach **7th level** (milestone: **Defeating the Flesh Stitcher**, p. 11).

> **💡 GM tip:** Short of 1,000 XP? The book suggests an XP award or a few more encounters before Chapter 8.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Chapter 8: Razorback Isle and the cult's sunken temple, for the last fragment.
