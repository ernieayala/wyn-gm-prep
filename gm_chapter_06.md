## The chapter in one minute

The *[Chromatic Queen](gm_glossary.md#chromatic-queen)* sails from [Seaview](gm_glossary.md#seaview) to [Moonshadow Island](gm_glossary.md#moonshadow-island). The island is dark and wooded, a hiding place for criminals. On the way, the scared crew tells ghost stories. They won't go ashore. The PCs row in alone and fight through blighted forest. In Gullstone Cave they solve Poppy's tile-puzzle vault. The fragment is gone. Umbrocious took it and dropped his notes. He is a shadowy fey who serves the Flesh Stitcher. The PCs follow the cult path inland. They pass an empty criminal village and reach the cottage of Rotsnout, a grumpy orpok witch. Invader assassins attack there. The PCs rest, level up, and get ready for the fortress.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Learned the fragment is gone** and who took it (N8 notes).
2. **Reached Rotsnout's cottage**, 5 miles inland and 4 miles short of the fortress.
3. **Found up to two defense special resources:** **fortress metal brackets** (N4) and **pyre combustibles** (O5).

**Party level:** starts at 5, ends at **6**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** The treasure isn't here. The cave still pays off with the puzzle and Poppy's trap. The theft turns a treasure hunt into a rescue with a named villain. The walk inland builds dread for Chapter 7. The PCs find an empty village and a murdered pirate queen. They meet a hermit who watched the villagers march to the fortress.

### Drained

Twigjack and eeko husks cause drained with Draining Impact.

- **Drained X:** status penalty X on Constitution-based checks (such as Fortitude).
- **Lose level × X HP**. Your **maximum HP drops by the same amount**.
- The value drops by 1 per full night's rest.

**Example:** a level-5 PC critically fails a twigjack's DC 20 Fortitude save. The PC is drained 2. They take −10 HP now. Their maximum HP is −10 until they rest. They take −2 on Fortitude.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Drained stacks across fights. If the party does M3, M4, and N1 in one push, their maximum HP can end up well below normal. Draining Impact immunity (24-hour) is per husk.

### Persistent damage and bleed

The N8 gauntlet of cutlasses deals **1d6 persistent bleed damage** on a failed save.

- **Persistent damage** hits at the end of each of the victim's turns. Then the victim rolls a **DC 15 flat check**. A success ends it.
- **Administer First Aid** (2 actions, Medicine) on a bleeding ally: a success gives the ally an extra flat check.
- The same persistent damage twice doesn't stack. Keep the higher one.

### Restrained and Escape

The O1 spiked cages trap **restrains** (Escape DC 25).

- **Restrained:** flat-footed and immobilized. The creature can't use attack or manipulate actions, except **Escape** or Force Open.
- **Escape:** one action, attack trait. Roll an unarmed attack, Acrobatics, or Athletics vs. the DC. It counts toward the multiple attack penalty.

At O1, a successful Escape (DC 25) also destroys the cage.

### Auras and Frightful Presence

An **aura** spreads out to a listed distance. Twigjack husks (30 feet, DC 17) and the eeko husk (30 feet, DC 22) have **Frightful Presence**.

- When a creature first enters the aura, it rolls a **Will save**.
- **Critical success:** unaffected. **Success:** frightened 1. **Failure:** frightened 2. **Critical failure:** frightened 4.
- After that, the creature is immune for 1 minute.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Three twigjacks means three auras and three saves. Frightened values don't add up. Keep the worst one.

### Curses

A **curse** is a lasting magical affliction. Shackled Marni's sword puts a *geas* ritual on anyone who tries to wield it. The *geas* forces them to dominate others and terrorize the seas as a pirate captain. See [O4](#o4--leaders-hut-severe-5).

### Negative healing

The eeko husk has **negative healing**. Negative energy heals it, and positive energy hurts it. So *heal* damages it, and *harm* heals it. Remaster names: **void** (negative), **vitality** (positive).

> **💡 GM tip:** **20 or more fire or positive damage** from one attack or effect in a round ends its swarm. A cleric can cast *heal* on the husk as a real attack option.

### Temporary Hit Points

Rotsnout's stew grants **temporary Hit Points**:
- Damage hits them first.
- They can't be healed.
- They end when the duration ends.
- They don't stack. Keep the higher amount.

### Improvised weapons

An attack with an object that isn't a weapon takes a **−2 item penalty** to the attack roll. At M4, a PC puts the honeycomb heart back with a melee attack. The heart counts as an improvised weapon.

### Rules from earlier chapters

- **Stupefied** ([Chapter 5](gm_chapter_05.md#conditions-new-this-chapter)): Rotsnout's Boorish Quip causes stupefied 1 for 1 round on a failure (2 on a critical failure).
- **Weakness** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#weakness-resistance-and-immunity)): twigjacks have fire 5. The eeko husk has axe 5, cold iron 5, fire 5. If more than one matches, apply only the highest.
- **Concealment** ([Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)): the eeko's bee swarm grants it. In Event 15, the poison clouds conceal creatures on opposite sides.
- **Counteracting** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting)):
  - **O1:** the cage magic is 4th level, counteract DC 25. A 3rd-level *dispel magic* works on a success, because 4 is within one level of 3. A 2nd-level one needs a critical success.
  - **O4:** *dispel magic* can force Marni's sword back to weapon form (counteract DC 27). The book gives no effect level. By the general rule, a level-8 creature counteracts at level 4.
- **Tiny creatures and reach 0** ([Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#tiny-creatures-reach-and-mounts)): twigjacks are Tiny with reach 0 feet claws. They must enter a target's space to claw it (p. 152).
- **Seek and Search** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#seek-search-and-secret-doors)): some tiles and the O5 resource need a PC who is Searching or Investigating. It's **DC 20 Perception** in N3 and O5, and **DC 22 Perception** in N4. Ask each PC's exploration activity before they enter a room.

## Part 1 — The voyage to Moonshadow

### Recall Knowledge about Moonshadow Island

Each PC can try any or all three checks. **Indigo Isles Geography Lore** can replace each skill at a lower DC (p. 148–149). [Secret checks](gm_chapter_01.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge).

**Nature (DC 12, or Indigo Isles Geography Lore DC 10):** the plants and creatures.
- **Critical success:** The success result, plus: plant creatures of several types live on the island. They are safe from civilization in the shadows under the trees.
- **Success:** Moonshadow is known for a thick forest. The trees are so tall the ground is always in shadow. Creatures that lurk in darkness feel at home there. That is one reason people think the island is so dangerous.
- **Failure:** Moonshadow Island has many tall trees.
- **Critical failure:** The island is an untapped source of timber, ready to be harvested.

**Religion (DC 20, or Lore DC 18):** the restless dead.
- **Critical success:** The success result, plus: the island's undead come from tragedy. So many people die there in messy, violent ways that angry spirits aren't rare. To put such a spirit to rest for good, you have to work out how it arose.
- **Success:** Ghostly undead haunt the island. Most are angry spirits like wraiths and specters.
- **Failure:** Nothing about undead on the island.
- **Critical failure:** Anyone who eats the wrong food grown on the island is cursed. They die a painful death at the next moonrise and become a slavering undead creature.

**Society (DC 15, or Lore DC 13):** the people.
- **Critical success:** The success result, plus: small groups of cutthroat criminals or escaped convicts sometimes hide there in secret. There's even a small village of them inland.
- **Success:** Almost nobody lives on the island, except shipwreck survivors or marooned sailors. The trees are too thick and tough to log. Rocky cliffs ring the island, so ships avoid it.
- **Failure:** Nobody lives on Moonshadow Island.
- **Critical failure:** A hidden pirate city is on the island. Its fleet is ready to prey on ships across the Indigo Isles. Someone must find their base and stop them.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The Religion critical success is the lesson of O4. Marni's spirit lingers because of how she died and what she held onto. Moving her sword out of the village lets her rest.

### Sea resources this leg

Any PC can **Seek Marine Resources** once this leg (p. 117). The full rules are in [Chapter 5](gm_chapter_05.md#seek-marine-resources). It is a downtime check that covers the whole voyage:
- **DC 20 Athletics, Crafting, Nature, or Survival**, or **DC 18 Sailing Lore**.
- **Critical success** 2 Kelp Points, **success** 1, **failure** 0, **critical failure** −1. Add up the party's points.
- **2–3 points:** twinekelp. **4–6:** netweed and twinekelp. **7+:** red sargassum, netweed, and twinekelp.
- Points reset each leg. Raise the thresholds for more than four PCs.

Write down the results. They are Chapter 12 defense special resources.

### Tale Telling

On a clear evening, as the ship nears the island, the gloomy crew trades spooky stories (p. 149–150). Each teller has a check a PC can make to impress them.

**Fippin and Mippin:** a ship ran aground on a moonless night. The cooks weren't aboard. A crewmate knew someone who knew someone who was. A dozen ragged people with bloody weapons met the survivors on the beach. Their leader was a huge hardriggan woman with a flaming cutlass and big silver bracelets. She laughed and demanded treasure. When she found none, she said *"Nothing but your lives, so we'll take those."* The sailors swam out into the dark sea. Some were rescued. None returned.
- **DC 20 Society**, or **DC 17 Pirate Lore or Sailing Lore:** the "bracelets" were shackles. She's the infamous pirate captain **Shackled Marni**, who vanished long ago. The cooks are impressed.

**"Slate" Hodda** shows a stone tile about 4 inches square and 1 inch thick: *"This is a key from Moonshadow Island, and it's all I have of my cousin."* Her cousin was marooned on the island. He hauled smugglers' crates into a cave. The cave walls had hundreds of small tiles and a big iron door. A smuggler joked that the wall tiles fit the lock, but nobody knew which ones. The smugglers tried to murder the cousin. He killed them and took their gear and the tile. He rowed home and died of his wounds.
- **DC 20 Diplomacy:** cheers her up. She gives the PCs the tile (a carved **cow**).

**Garnikor** (petting Leafling): she was once sentenced to a prison ship. The ship stopped at Moonshadow for supplies. In the shadows, a branch moved with no wind. It nudged a badly hurt blood mouse toward her. The mouse's proboscis was torn out. She took it in, and the tree was gone. Something on the island had tortured Leafling nearly to death.
- **DC 20 Nature:** arboreals are usually kind plant creatures. That one likely knew Garnikor could help the mouse more than it could. She respects a PC who knows this.

**Leafling:** a PC who can talk to animals (such as with *speak with animals*) learns its story. Cruel people in an island village caught it and tortured it. It escaped into a tall tree, and the tree brought it to Garnikor. Leafling shares only impressions, at animal intelligence.

**XP:** **30 XP** if a successful check earns at least one teller's respect. If a PC tells a silly story that breaks the tension, the crew is openly grateful.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Hodda's tile is a wrong one):** The cow fits the lock but won't open it. The book says "the door in area M2." The door is in **N2**. M2 is the fork.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Every story pays off. Marni's sword is at O4. Hodda's cave is Gullstone Cave. Leafling's "cruel people in a village" are the O1–O5 criminals. They now serve the invaders at the fortress (Ch 7).

### Moonshadow Island

Jagged cliffs ring the island (p. 150). The only clear landing spot is a small rocky beach in a sheltered bay on the south side. Smugglers' tunnels and secret paths are hard to reach. Kulupi won't risk the ship hunting for them. Also, the maps put the fragment in a cave near the beach.

- **Trees:** hundreds of feet tall, packed close. Dim by day, pitch dark at night. Few clearings.
- **The wood:** stringy, nearly as tough as iron, and it won't burn. *Fireball* can't light it. A tree set on fire on purpose doesn't spread the fire. Cutting it takes great effort.
- **Food:** wild root vegetables, herbs, and berries, mostly withered.
- **Sounds:** loud and creepy:
  - Animal cries sometimes almost sound like words. Listeners disagree on the message, but all agree it's a threat.
  - Insect buzzing sometimes drops to a deep hum you feel in your teeth.
  - Wind in the treetops moans like someone far off in pain.
- **Who lives here:** fleeing criminals, escaped convicts, hermits who hate people, and sailors marooned by crews who hated them.

## Part 2 — Moonshadow Shore (M1 to M4)

Map on p. 151. Read aloud as the ship arrives:

> *The shore in this sheltered bay is narrow, giving way almost immediately to dense stands of stout trees that grow hundreds of feet high and cloak the forest in perpetual shadow. The island has several tall hills, and any smaller hills are likely concealed by the dense forest. The island looks dim, inhospitable, and utterly abandoned—except for a dilapidated shack on the beach, just above the tide line.*

**The crew won't come,** not even to row. The PCs take the ship's boat alone.

**Kulupi's bag:** before they go, he hands over a tattered sack as a great treasure. It is one: a ***bag of holding* (type II)**. The top goal is Poppy's fragment. He also asks them to bring back **resources to help Rumplank** in the bag. He hints that high-quality timber might help, but he trusts their judgment.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi is a cult leader (revealed in Ch 11). In Ch 12, these resources defend Rumplank against his own cult. Play him straight: dutiful, a little pompous, and staying aboard.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the bag fills up):** The book says the N4 fortress brackets (**50 Bulk**) fill the bag completely. The O5 pyre combustibles come later. If the PCs fill the bag with timber or loot first, the brackets won't fit.

> **💡 GM tip (the timber hint):** Moonshadow timber isn't a defense special resource (p. 315), and it's hard to cut. If the PCs cut down trees, remind them how tough the wood is. Let them decide if it's worth the time.

### M1 — Old Shack *(Moderate 5)*

The shack has no door, and the roof has fallen in. A **shinglelurk** sits on top, pretending to be the roof.
- **Inside:** under sand and junk are a small anvil, a hammer, and **six pairs of broken manacles**. Someone used these tools to free people from manacles. Those people later moved to the village (O1–O5).
- **The lure:** legs wave down into the shack with a low clicking. **DC 18 Perception** notices them. Only a **critical success** reveals bug-like movement on the ceiling.
- **Attacks** when three creatures come close or when someone discovers it. If the PCs don't come close, it gets hungry and flies at them.

**Shinglelurk:** BB 97, Creature 7. Initiative: Stealth +18. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp.
- **Morale:** retreats below **20 HP**. It drops scales worth its Monster Parts value.
- **Treasure:** a **shark tooth charm** in a corner of the hut.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The first threat is a flying level-7 creature. The fight is only Moderate, but it hits like level 7. A fresh party can handle it.

### M2 — Fork

> *Not far from the beach, the trees loom over the narrow path and cloak the ground in deep shadow. Amid the undergrowth, the narrow path splits. One path leads deeper into the island, while the right-hand path leads in the direction indicated on Poppy's map.*

- **Right:** to the cave (M3, M4, N1).
- **Inland:** no tracks for several weeks. The abandoned village (**O1**) is about 2 miles along. See [Part 4](#part-4--heading-inland).

### M3 — Blighted Grove *(Low 5)*

> *The vegetation in this open clearing is plainly unhealthy. Black spots of tarry goop cling to the underbrush, and portions of the trees are badly scarred, as though from acid. The woodland infection is worst around a narrow path leading south.*

**The resin:** black and tarry. It won't burn and smells a little like scorched rubber.
- **DC 25 Arcana, Nature, or Occultism:** it comes from another plane. **Critical success:** it comes from dangerous regions of the Shadow Plane.
- The drips show one blast here and another to the south.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Sklaggans "blight bombed" here on the Flesh Stitcher's orders. They targeted places where the island's natural defenders gather.

**Twigjack Husks (3):** p. 152 (BB 44, *Pathfinder Bestiary 2* 270), Creature 3. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. They look like tiny scowling dolls made of branches and brambles. Their skin is thorny. They are husked and drip resin.
- They attack at once and fight to the death. They chase foes who flee.
- Up close, they prefer claws and Splinter Spray.
- **Perception** +9; darkvision. **AC** 18; **Fort** +9, **Ref** +11, **Will** +7; **HP** 45. **Immunities** emotion. **Weaknesses** fire 5 ([weakness](gm_chapter_03.md#weakness-resistance-and-immunity)).
- **Frightful Presence** (aura) 30 feet, DC 17 ([auras](#auras-and-frightful-presence)). **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** blighted claw +11 (agile, finesse, reach 0 feet), 1d10+4 slashing plus draining impact ([reach 0](gm_chapter_02.md#tiny-creatures-reach-and-mounts)).
- **Ranged** blighted splinter +11 (deadly 1d6, range increment 30 feet), 1d6+4 piercing plus draining impact.
- **Draining Impact:** **DC 20 Fortitude**. Failure: drained 1. Critical failure: drained 2 ([drained](#drained)). Either way, the target is immune to that husk's draining impact for 24 hours.
- **Splinter Spray** (2 actions): 15-foot cone, **4d6 piercing**, **DC 20 basic Reflex**. Recharges in 1d4 rounds.
- **Bramble Jump:** from undergrowth, it teleports to undergrowth within 60 feet. This doesn't trigger reactions.

### M4 — Warped Beehives *(Low 5)*

> *This clearing is also covered in tarry droplets and is even more sickly than the one to the northwest. Three stumps each bear a beehive the size of a rain barrel. The beehives have all been splattered with the same black resin and large chunks of them have decayed away. A glimmer of bright gold emanates from within each destroyed hive. A heap of earth and stone bearing a low cave entrance is just visible in another clearing to the east.*

East is **N1**, Gullstone Cave, where Poppy hid the fragment called the **Bruised Crystal**.

**What happened:** an eeko like Briargrove's cared for these hives. When the blight came, it tore out its honeycomb heart and broke it up to save the hives. That left it open to corruption. It became a husk of anger and emptiness. A healthy piece of honeycomb is still in each hive. That makes the gold glow.

**Eeko Husk:** p. 154 (BB 44, 27), Creature 6. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp. It attacks when the PCs come near any hive. Its open chest is empty except for a few rotting undead bees. It never retreats or surrenders. It won't follow foes out of the clearing.
- **Perception** +17; low-light vision. It talks only with sign language.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +14, **Will** +15; **HP** 110, [negative healing](#negative-healing).
- **Immunities** emotion, swarm mind. **Weaknesses** axe vulnerability 5, cold iron 5, fire 5. **Resistances** bludgeoning 5, piercing 5.
- **Frightful Presence** 30 feet, DC 22. **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** blighted branch +16 (agile, finesse), 2d6+6 slashing plus draining impact (**DC 24 Fortitude**, same results as the twigjacks').
- **Primal Innate Spells** DC 25: 3rd *earthbind*, *fear*, *vomit swarm*; 2nd *darkness*, *entangle*, *tree shape*; cantrips *dancing lights*, *detect magic*, *stabilize*, *tanglefoot*; constant *speak with plants*. It casts without sound.
- **Unleash Hive** (2 actions, once per minute): a Huge undead bee swarm forms, centered on the eeko and moving with it. It lasts 1 minute, or until the eeko takes **20+ fire or positive damage from one attack or effect in a round**. While the swarm is active, the eeko gets all-around vision, [concealment](gm_chapter_02.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected), fast healing 3, and Charnel Stings.
- **Charnel Stings** (1 action, swarm active): each enemy in the swarm takes **2d8 piercing**, **DC 25 basic Reflex**, plus draining impact. On a failure, the enemy is also sickened 1 (sickened 2 on a critical failure).

**Saving the eeko:**
1. **Pull out each hive's honeycomb:** Interact, **DC 18 Nature, Survival, or Thievery**. Undead bees sting anyone who tries: **2d4 piercing plus 2d4 negative**, **DC 20 basic Reflex**. The bees blend into the hive, so the first PC to reach in likely finds them.
2. **Rebuild the heart:** once all three pieces are out, Interact, **DC 20 Medicine or Nature**. The result is a fist-sized, heart-shaped honeycomb.
3. **Put it back:** a melee hit with the heart as an [improvised weapon](#improvised-weapons) **paralyzes** the husk. It roots in place and can't act. Over several hours, the heart cleans out the blight.

**Restored:** the eeko is deeply grateful. It helps however it can without leaving its hives. It gives **magical honey worth its full Monster Parts value**.

**XP:** **80 XP** for restoring it, instead of 60 XP for defeating it.

> **💡 GM tip:** Players who remember Ch 5's eeko know eekos have honeycomb hearts. Point out the empty chest and the heart-shaped glow. If they ask, a Nature check confirms it.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the book's slip):** The text puts the friendly eeko "on Seaview Island." Seaview is Bluebell Island's town, and the eeko was in Briargrove. The book means the Ch 5 eeko.

## Part 3 — Gullstone Cave (N1 to N8)

Map on p. 156.

> **🔒 GM-only (the cave's history (p. 155)):** Long before Poppy, a pirate captain hid treasure here behind a strong iron door. Three tiles lock the door. He carved them to match the thousands of others. Poppy's crew suggested the cave as a hiding place. She solved the puzzle and took his treasure. It was part of the haul that founded Rumplank. She sealed the fragment in the vault with more traps and guardians. She stored lesser loot around the cave.
>
> The sklaggans bombed the area outside but ignored the cave. The cave bothered the Flesh Stitcher's advisor, the skotogelia **Umbrocious**. His research found a powerful, unique magic item inside. He trusted his incorporeal body to keep him safe and went in. He got the gem, set off Poppy's trap, and left notes behind. **The fragment isn't here anymore.**

### Cave features

- **Slope:** high in the north, low in the south. Seawater seeps into the southern rooms, so they're clammy and dripping.
- **Light:** only what the PCs bring. **Ceilings:** 10 feet unless noted.
- **The tiles:** thousands cover the walls and ceilings. They're thickest in N2 and N4.
  - Each is about **4 inches square and 1 inch thick**. Each is carved with one natural thing (bird, shrub, river, fish).
  - Every tile is unique and magically tough.
  - A tool pries one out in several minutes. Dozens of empty sockets show that visitors did this.
  - The **gull** tile by the entrance gives the cave its name.
- **The three key tiles** show **man-made** things: the pirate's **tricorn hat**, **cutlass**, and **pirate ship**. None of them is mounted on a wall. Treasure hunters left many loose decoy tiles around.

### N1 — Cave Entrance *(Moderate 5)*

A few twisted tropical trees, their limbs dripping with the tarry substance, grow from the rock above the cave.*

The opening is only **5 feet high**, with crude steps going down.

**Weak Arboreal Husk:** BB 9, 46, Creature 7. Initiative: Stealth +21. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp. A tropical arboreal with long roots. The blight made it a husk before it understood what was happening. It's too big for the cave. It dimly remembers that the cave matters, so it guards it.

- It climbs down only if no foe is within reach of its long branches.
- It attacks anyone going in or out, until it's destroyed.

> **💡 GM tip:** This may be Garnikor's arboreal (the book doesn't say). A PC who made the Tale Telling DC 20 Nature check knows arboreals are usually kind. That makes this fight sad.

### N2 — Puzzle Chamber

> *This low cave is wet with moisture. Two natural passages lead up, and one leads down. The floor of each has been chipped into rough stairs. At the far end of the cave, furthest from the entrance, stands a massive iron door with an enormous lock. Some shattered chips of stone lie on the floor in front of the door.*

**The lock:** no handle, only **three indentations stacked vertically**.
- **Pick the Lock:** six successful **DC 35 Thievery** checks.
- **Force Open:** **DC 45 Athletics**.
- **The door:** Hardness 50, 250 HP.

**Floor clue:** the chips are a broken **mouse** tile. **"WRONG ONE"** is scratched on the back. An explorer wrote it after learning that some tiles fit but don't work.

**Opening the door:** put in the pirate tiles **top to bottom: tricorn hat, cutlass, pirate ship**. Wrong tile or wrong order: a low click, and the door stays shut. Right tiles and order: several clicks, and the door swings wide.

**XP:** **30 XP** for solving the puzzle and opening the door.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** DC 35 and DC 45 are out of reach at level 5. They tell the players force won't work. The puzzle is the way in.

> **💡 GM tip:** Show every loose tile and keep a list. The theme is the answer: three man-made pirate objects among hundreds of nature carvings. If they're stuck on the order, think of a pirate standing up. Hat on the head, cutlass at the hip, ship under the feet.

### N3 — Flooded Cave *(Low 5)*

> *The passage descending into this room is low and slippery, although the steps carved into the floor provide a modicum of traction. The passage opens into a cave with a narrow beach to the north. Clumps of seaweed drift gently in the water.*

The sea squirts came in from the sea through narrow cracks in the south wall.

**Sea Squirt Patches (3):** BB 87, Hazard 4. **Stealth DC 24.** Locations are on the map.

**Giant Sea Squirt:** BB 87, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- It rushes out from underwater when anyone **triggers or disables** a patch.
- It can't trigger patches. It's mindless, but it pushes or lures others into them.
- It never surrenders or leaves the water. Its reach covers almost the whole room.

**Tiles:** tree, bat, and **tricorn hat** are in the **patch farthest from the entrance**. Triggering or disabling that patch reveals them. Otherwise, a Searching PC needs **DC 20 Perception**.

### N4 — Bound Boxes *(Moderate 5)* — defense resource

> *Several cargo boxes are stacked in this room, each sagging and coated with moss. Coils of thick rope are looped around the boxes, as though keeping them together and in place along one of the room's walls. A corpse is sprawled on the floor in front of the boxes.*

Seven boxes hold Poppy's spare loot. The food and drink (including straw-packed fine wine) have spoiled.

**The corpse:** a heavily tattooed orpok convict and treasure hunter. Clear marks show strangling by a rope as thick as the ropes on the boxes.

**Knotsman:** BB 54, Creature 7. Initiative: Perception +15. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp. The knotsman is the rope around the boxes.
- It attacks anyone who **tries to move the ropes or open the boxes**. It uncoils and pulls itself into a humanoid shape.
- It fights until destroyed but **won't leave the room**. If the PCs leave it alone, it coils back around the boxes.

**Resource (fortress metal brackets):** the heaviest box holds excellent brackets for anchoring lumber. They're still in good shape. Poppy meant them for a dock or fortress she never built.
- **Weight:** 50 Bulk. They fill Kulupi's *bag of holding*.
- **Use:** Chapter 12 defense special resource (p. 315).

**Tiles:** bee, **cutlass**, and berry bush are under one crate. Moving the crates reveals them. A PC Investigating the boxes can roll **DC 22 Perception** to notice that one crate sits unevenly.

> **💡 GM tip:** The corpse is the warning. Reward players who notice the strangling. They can fight on their terms or back out, because the knotsman won't follow.

### N5 — Twisting Tunnels

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The N5 text calls Gullet "the nobbler in area M6." There is no M6. Gullet's lair is in **N6**.

### N6 — Upper Pit *(Severe 5)*

> *This cave slopes steeply down to the south, where it narrows like a funnel to a small chamber below. A few ridges in the stone provide the only stable footing. A few corpses are scattered around the chamber, all wearing ragged clothing.*

- **Terrain:** **difficult terrain**. A creature that falls **prone** slides **5 feet** toward N7.
- **The bodies:** pirates and convicts, long dead, covered in tiny rat and snake bites.

**Mogadbs (4):** BB 63, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. They are ship's burlap sacks stuffed with sea snakes, rats, and other vermin. Two lurk in the top northwest and northeast corners. Two lurk near the bottom.

**Gullet:** Creature 5, unique NE Medium construct, variant nobbler (BB 67), p. 159. Initiative: Deception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp. Gullet is a nobbler made from a smuggler's magic bag. It crawls on all fours with the mogadbs, and they accept it.
- **Bag of Smarts:** it's dim (Int −2). Unlike most nobblers, it isn't mindless, so it's **not immune to mental effects**.
- **Tells:** it picks its way carefully over the slope. The mogadbs ignore the slope. Sharp-eyed players can spot the difference.
- **Tactics:** when the fight starts, it backs toward the pit. Once it's out of sight in N7, it plays dead among the empty sacks there.
- **Tiles:** it ate the **pirate ship** and a pig. Like all nobblers, it expels everything when destroyed.

### N7 — Lower Pit

> *This is a low, muddy pit. Several scraps of old sacks and other garbage litter the floor around a small, murky pool.*

This is an old dump for smugglers and pirates. Primal power sometimes surges here. Sacks that closed over vermin became mogadbs, and more could form. The pool is tainted seawater.

**Gullet's trick:**
- It pretends to be a thrown-away bag. When PCs poke around, it leaps on them and tries to **Gobble** one. Then it runs.
- After it swallows a PC, or when it's **below 40 HP**, it flees north through tiny passages to N5. Swallowed creatures sit in an extradimensional space, so it still fits.
- It may lead a chase and try the bag trick again. It's too dim to know the trick only works once.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (a swallowed PC goes with it):** The party can't enter foot-wide tunnels. Track where Gullet comes out and keep the chase moving. The book says the PCs should be able to corner it in the end. They need the ship tile.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (ability names):** N6 calls Gullet's blend-in ability "Go Limp." N7 calls it "Lie Still." Check BB 67.

### N8 — Gemstone Chamber *(Moderate 5)*

The only way in is through the N2 door.

> *Old ropes and rusty cutlasses dangle from the ceiling all throughout this wedge-shaped room. A low pedestal stands at the south end of the room, amid a few coils of frayed rope on the dusty floor.*

Poppy trapped this room like Briargrove's gemstone chamber. The cutlasses are in plain sight, and there are too many to take. **Six pressure plates** trigger the trap. So does trying to remove a cutlass or cut a rope.

**Gauntlet of Cutlasses** *(complex mechanical trap, Hazard 7)*: p. 160–161
- **Stealth** +15 (expert). The ropes and cutlasses are **DC 0** to notice. A complex hazard's Stealth is its initiative. Its hidden parts (the pressure plates) are DC 10 higher to spot.
- **Disable:** **DC 25 Thievery (expert)** changes the swing so the cutlasses cut each other's ropes. Each success disables one of three sections and lowers routine damage by 2d6. The third success disables the trap.
- **AC** 25; **Fort** +18, **Ref** +12. **Section** Hardness 15, HP 60 (BT 30). Immune to critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Cutlass Swing** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature steps on a pressure plate, or someone pulls or cuts the ropes. **Effect:** falling weights **slam the door shut**, and the cutlasses swing wildly. The trap Strikes the triggering creature with a cutlass, then rolls initiative.
- **Melee** cutlass +20 (forceful), 3d6+6 slashing.
- **Routine** (1 action): the cutlasses sweep every N8 square **except the 4 squares around the pedestal**. Each creature takes **6d6 slashing**, **DC 25 basic Reflex**. On a failure, it also takes **1d6 persistent bleed** ([persistent damage](#persistent-damage-and-bleed)).
- **Reset:** after **3 minutes**, the weights settle and the trap goes inactive. Counterweights reset it an hour later.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The door slams, and the trap runs for 3 minutes (30 rounds). To survive, the PCs stand on the safe pedestal squares or disable it with Thievery. The book doesn't say if the door opens from inside. Decide before the session.

**The fallen note:** Umbrocious thought the gem was unguarded. He turned corporeal and brushed a rope. The cutlasses cut him up. He grabbed the gem and fled in incorporeal form. His notes lie in the dust. They're sliced and spattered with a shadowy fluid, something between ink and ichor.
- **DC 28 Nature or Occultism:** it's **skotogelia** blood (a shadowy fey).
- The notes hold a crude sketch of a web or net (the **Skinloom**) and this handout:

> *The Master says the Skinloom needs more power; a potent item south of the fortress near the island's southern shore might provide this boost. This item appears to be a gem of some kind. A self-styled hero once bore it, but it's now just lying around for the taking. If I retrieve it, the Master can put it into the heart of the Skinloom. More favors are certain to be mine!*

**Treasure:**
- **Without Monster Parts:** Umbrocious also dropped a **runestone with a shadow rune**.
- **An open crate** in a corner: **three gold chalices** (40 gp each), **ten gold plates** (10 gp each), and **silverware** worth 100 gp in all.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book lists the crate right after the runestone and starts it with "Additionally." So the crate may also be for games without Monster Parts only. Pick one reading and stay consistent.

**XP:** **80 XP** for finding the clue about the stolen fragment.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The Skinloom is area P21 of the Flesh Stitcher's fortress (**Chapter 7**). "The Master" is the Flesh Stitcher. The gem is the Bruised Crystal.

### What happens if the characters lose?

The p. 161 sidebar says the PCs must leave knowing **the fragment isn't here and went inland**. Umbrocious's note shows this best. If they can't beat the cutlass trap but want to go inland, encourage it. That puts them back on track.

## Part 5 — The abandoned village (O1 to O5)

Map on p. 162.

> **🔒 GM-only (the village's history (p. 162–163)):**
> - A village has stood at this spring for centuries. The spring has good water and good drainage.
>   - The cult used it as a **base camp** while building the fortress.
>   - Later it was an outpost. Cult scouts led visitors and prisoners from the shore to the fortress.
> - The village was empty after the cult fell. Soon after, mutinous convicts wrecked here and took it over. They built the beach shack (M1) first.
> - They took in fugitives. They murdered the rare innocent who wandered in. Fighting among themselves kept their numbers to one or two dozen. They never settled on the latest name, **"Lawbreaker's Rest."**
> - **The sklaggans** are inhuman Shadow Plane monsters. They reached the fortress not long ago and spread out. They blighted the forest and built strange resin structures. They found a village as wicked as they were. They lured everyone to the fortress with promises of power and aberrant technology.
> - The leader, **Shackled Marni**, was a pirate. She warned that the sklaggans couldn't be trusted. They killed her in front of everyone, messily. The rest obeyed.
> - The village has been empty for a few months.

### O1 — Village Gate *(Moderate 5)*

> *The path leading deeper into the island runs past a wooden palisade that's in poor repair. A large wooden gate stands in the wall, slightly ajar, and reveals a small village of ramshackle huts within. There's no sign of activity in the village. Next to this gate is a crude sign reading, "Lawbreaker's Rest," but the word "Rest" has been crossed out and replaced with "Roost," which has been crossed out and replaced with "Rest" again.*

**Other ways in:** **DC 15 Athletics** to Climb the palisade, or **DC 20 Athletics** to Force Open a hole.

**Spiked Cages Trap** *(complex magical mechanical trap, Hazard 7)*: p. 163. This is a pile of lumber and metal by the gate. The sklaggans enchanted it. The criminals meant to build with it but never did.
- **Stealth:** the book prints "DC +17 (expert)." The pile itself is **DC 0** to notice.
- **Disable:**
  - **DC 25 Thievery (expert)** frees someone else and destroys the cage.
  - **DC 27 Thievery (trained)** or **Escape DC 25** frees yourself and destroys the cage ([restrained and Escape](#restrained-and-escape)).
  - **DC 27 Occultism**, or ***dispel magic*** (4th level; counteract DC 25), dispels the pile's shadow magic. Then no more cages form ([counteracting](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting)).
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +20, **Ref** +12. **Cage** Hardness 15, HP 60 (BT 30). Immune to critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Encage** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature comes within **10 feet** of the pile by the gate. **Effect:** the trap makes a cage Strike against it, then rolls initiative.
- **Ranged** cage +20 (range 50 feet). **Effect:** the target is **restrained** (Escape DC 25).
- **Routine** (2 actions):
  1. Shadowy cords pull a spiked wood-and-iron cage around an uncaged creature within 50 feet (a cage Strike).
  2. Every cage with someone inside squeezes: **2d10+9 piercing**, **DC 27 basic Fortitude**. A critical success Escapes automatically and destroys the cage.
- **Reset:** the trap shuts off at the end of any turn when nobody is caged. Before it does, it makes one last cage Strike, because it always takes its turn. If nobody dispels it, it resets in **10 minutes**.
- **Treasure:** a **traveler's any-tool** in the pile.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (two different triggers):** The description says the trap triggers when someone passes through the gate. Encage says within 10 feet of the pile. Use 10 feet. PCs who climb in or break in somewhere else may never trigger it.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the constrict damage):** 2d10+9 a round at DC 27 is heavy at level 5, and new cages keep coming. Free allies fast. An uncaged PC with *dispel magic* can stop new cages.

### O2 — Empty Huts

The huts are shoddy and neglected. The villagers left them in a hurry, days or weeks ago. Nothing of interest or value.

### O3 — Bunkhouse *(Moderate 5)*

A villager with no art training painted snarling hardriggan faces all over the outside.

> *This irregularly shaped room is haphazardly stacked with bunk beds and footlockers. A battered table stands at the north end of the room, covered in old food stains. The odor of bad booze fills the room.*

The rowdiest, most bloodthirsty villagers lived here. Old bloodstains and loose teeth show they fought often.

**Two objects out of place:** a **skagappa** (BB 163) is under the northernmost bed, and another is under the southernmost. Both are used up and wrapped in resin, so sklaggans can collect and recharge them.

**Sklaggans (2):** BB 104, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- **Arrival:** they arrive when the PCs find the first skagappa or 5 minutes after the PCs enter, whichever is first. They likely come from the gate side and go around the trapped pile. Any direction works.
- **Tactics:** Toxic Shadows at once, then *blur*, then fists. They grab the skagappas only if it's easy. A wounded sklaggan casts *vampiric touch*. Below **20 HP**, a sklaggan flees for good.
- **Captured:** they know the basics of what happened and the way to the fortress. They trade this only for a believable offer of freedom. They won't talk until freed. Then they run off without answering, unless a PC succeeds at **DC 20 Diplomacy** (Request), **DC 22 Deception** (Lie), or **DC 25 Intimidation** (Coerce).

**Treasure:** **DC 18 Crafting or Occultism** restores a skagappa. A failure destroys it. On a success, the resin flakes off, so each skagappa can be restored only once.

### O4 — Leader's Hut *(Severe 5)*

> *This large hut is draped with pirate flags, open sea chests filled with a variety of fine clothes, and a frame supporting a hammock that would be more suitable on a ship than in a forest village. A rough map is tacked to one wall. Strange globs of black resin cover another wall, forming a humanoid silhouette like a blast shadow. Tiny chunks of bone, skin, hair, and viscera are embedded in the wall, as though someone exploded against it with incredible force. A gleaming cutlass is jammed point-first into the hut's wooden floor.*

This is the best hut. The strongest villager held it. The last one was **Shackled Marni** (she/her), a hardriggan pirate captain. Her crew marooned her because she was too bloodthirsty. She ran the village with leadership and her blade. She held meetings here.

**The wall map:** a rough sketch of Moonshadow, not to scale. It shows what's up the path.
- The beach, the winding path, the village (huts around a spring inside a palisade), and the fortress (wooden, with a big central gate).
- A resin glob near the fortress. It marks where a sklaggan pointed the villagers.
- Between the village and the fortress: tufted reeds and a small house with a cauldron. That's Rotsnout's cottage.

**How Marni died:** she saw through the sklaggans' promises and spoke out. They wanted to silence her and show their strength. So they blasted her against her own wall, and into it. Her spirit stays bound to the blade she killed so many with.

**Creature:** when anyone pokes through her things, the sword rises. It uses Summon Ghost Pirate and attacks until its pirate form is destroyed.

**Shackled Marni's Sword:** Creature 8, unique NE Medium construct, soulbound. Variant living blade (BB 55), p. 164–165. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- **Perception** +18; darkvision. **Languages** telepathy 100 feet, *tongues*.
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +18, Athletics +17, Deception +17, Intimidation +17, Sailing Lore +14.
- **AC** 27; **Fort** +12, **Ref** +18, **Will** +16; **HP** 100 (as ghost pirate).
- **Immunities:** bleed, death effects, disease, doomed, drained, fatigued, healing, necromancy, nonlethal attacks, paralyzed, poison, sickened, unconscious.
- **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** scimitar +19 (forceful, magical, sweep), 2d6+9 slashing plus 1d6 fire.
- **Occult Innate Spells** DC 27, attack +17: 3rd *heroism*, *water breathing*; 2nd *water walk*; 1st *true strike*; constant (5th) *tongues*.
- **Rituals** DC 27: *geas* (see the curse).
- **Weapon Form:** while the ghost pirate is inactive, it's a ***+1 striking flaming might (6) scimitar (7)***. It can't attack or move, but it can curse a wielder. On its turn, it can cast spells that target only its cursed wielder.
- **Summon Ghost Pirate** (3 actions, once per hour). **Requirement:** no intelligent creature is wielding or carrying the sword. **Effect:** it forms a phantom of Shackled Marni out of plain soul-stuff from the Ethereal Plane. The phantom has only fragments of her personality. It lets the sword move and act. **DC 27 Perception** identifies the ghost pirate for what it is. The sword can dismiss it as a free action.
- **Back to weapon form:** at 0 HP, or when counteracted by *dispel magic* (counteract DC 27). It stays a weapon until the next Summon Ghost Pirate.
- **Curse of the Pirate's Blade** (curse, enchantment, mental, occult). Anyone who tries to wield the sword rolls a **DC 27 Will save**. The sword casts *geas* on them, and the spell always works. The *geas* forces them to dominate all other creatures and terrorize the seas as captain of a pirate ship.
  - **Critical success:** immune to the curse for 24 hours.
  - **Failure:** falls under the *geas*. The victim can't willingly drop or give away the sword. The *geas* lasts **1 week**. Then the wielder gets a new Will save.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the save has gaps):** The book lists only critical success and failure. Suggested ruling for success: the PC resists this time but can be targeted the next time they wield it. The book doesn't cover critical failure.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (cutlass or scimitar):** The read-aloud and one backstory line say cutlass. Another line, the stat block, and the treasure say scimitar. Use scimitar stats.

**Spotting the curse before someone grabs it:**
- **Expert in Arcana, or DC 20 Arcana:** the sword feels oddly solid when the spirit carries it. That signals a dire effect on a wielder.
- **Expert in Religion, or DC 20 Religion:** the ghost pirate comes from a curse in the scimitar that binds it here.
- **Critical success on either, or master in either:** describe the Curse of the Pirate's Blade in more detail.
- **DC 22 Arcana, Occultism, or Religion:** safely taking the scimitar out of the village breaks its hold on her spirit and frees her.

**Laying her to rest:** if the sword is outside the village and nobody is cursed, **Shackled Marni's spirit departs forever**.

**Treasure:** once the curse is lifted, it's a normal ***+1 striking flaming might (6) scimitar (7)***. Without Monster Parts, it's a normal ***+1 striking flaming scimitar***.

> **💡 GM tip (carrying it out):** Summon Ghost Pirate needs the sword to be unwielded and uncarried. The curse hits only a creature trying to **wield** it. A PC who stores it without drawing it (in a pack or Kulupi's bag) keeps the ghost away and avoids the curse. This is one reading of the stat block wording. The book has no such rule and means the sword to be dangerous. So offer the knowledge checks before anyone grabs it.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It pays off Fippin and Mippin's story and the Religion Recall Knowledge. It shows how ruthless the sklaggans are, so the Ch 7 fortress feels earned.

### O5 — Storage Shed — defense resource

> *This large storage shed of rough logs and overlapping leaves contains a variety of casks, barrels, and boxes. Nearest the entrance is a large anvil set on a flat stone. A few tools, planks of lumber, and sawed-off pieces of wood are scattered around it.*

This is a messy workroom for everything. Marni never had it cleaned. It's full of stolen, mostly useless goods: old nails, scrap lumber, casks of water tainted with oil.

**Resource (pyre combustibles):** a Searching PC with **DC 20 Perception** finds a **crate of alchemical supplies**. Alone, it's worth little. But it's a set of **pyre combustibles** for a Chapter 12 inferno tower (p. 315). The book also calls this an inferno cannon tower: an upgraded flame cannon tower (p. 316).

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** This is easy to miss. With no Searching PC and no DC 20 Perception success, the shed looks like junk.

### Where to go next

Nothing in the village brings the PCs closer to the fragment. The O4 map shows the path to the fortress, past the witch's cottage.

## Part 6 — The Witch's Cottage

> **🔒 GM-only (Rotsnout (p. 166)):** The orpok witch **Rotsnout** (he/him) was an outcast even among criminals. He's a grumpy loner who hates people. His hygiene is so poor that nobody wanted him nearby. The villagers ran him out. He went up the path, but the fortress was too creepy, so he stopped short. He built a cottage with a swamp dock. He goes moonlight fishing there in a boat that badly needs repair. He lives so far off that he missed the sklaggans' arrival. He only noticed when the villagers trooped past behind strange creatures. He hid, and the sklaggans never found him.

The cottage is **3 miles** past the village and **4 miles** short of the fortress. Map on p. 167.

### The Swamp Witch *(Trivial 5)*

Trees and shrubs screen the cottage, but anyone on the path notices it. From the trail, it looks like a heap of lumber or a ruin. Up close:

> *This cottage at the edge of a misty swamp looks haphazard at best, built of wood scraps and held together with salvaged metal. A wooden dock, perhaps the most solid-looking construction of the entire affair, juts into the swamp. A busted rowboat is partially concealed beneath the dock.*

- **Terrain:** vines and mold everywhere. Only the **swamp water** is difficult terrain.
- **Rotsnout's entrance:** unless the PCs are unusually quiet, he storms out to scold the "unruly and unwelcome trespassers." He's a middle-aged orpok in never-washed black leather.
- **Grug:** his familiar, a living lump of junk that rarely leaves the cottage.
- **Won't fight armed strangers.** If attacked, he hides inside and casts defensive spells. Once the PCs stop fighting, or if they never start, he talks, grudgingly. He's a coward, so he **surrenders at 30 HP or fewer**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Rotsnout at 0 HP):** He's ornery but never hostile. If the PCs drop him anyway, he's unconscious and dying under the normal PC dying rules. He holds no grudge, because fighting is normal on Moonshadow (p. 168).

**Rotsnout:** Creature 4, unique NE Medium humanoid orpok, male orpok recluse (p. 167)
- **Perception** +11; scent (imprecise) 30 feet. **Languages** Common, Orpok.
- **Skills:** Arcana +10, Crafting +10, Medicine +9, Nature +11, Survival +11, Swamp Lore +10.
- **Str** +0, **Dex** +3, **Con** +1, **Int** +4, **Wis** +3, **Cha** −1. **Items:** 3 daggers, leather armor.
- **AC** 21; **Fort** +9, **Ref** +11, **Will** +13; **HP** 55; **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** dagger +13 (agile, finesse, versatile S), 1d4+3 piercing.
- **Ranged** dagger +13 (agile, thrown 10 feet, versatile S), 1d4+3 piercing.
- **Primal Prepared Spells** DC 21, attack +13: 2nd *barkskin*, *faerie fire*, *vomit swarm*; 1st *heal*, *mage armor*, *summon plant or fungus*; cantrips (2nd) *acid splash*, *dancing lights*, *detect magic*, *produce flame*, *tanglefoot*.
- **Witch Hexes** DC 21, 3 Focus Points: 2nd *blood ward*, *cackle*, *phase familiar*; cantrip (2nd) *wilding word*.
- **Boorish Quip** (free action, once per round). **Trigger:** his last action was a successful weapon Strike. **Effect:** he makes a rude but insightful comment about the foe he hit. The foe takes **2d8 mental**, **DC 20 basic Will**. On a failure, it's also [stupefied](gm_chapter_05.md#conditions-new-this-chapter) 1 for 1 round (stupefied 2 on a critical failure). Either way, the target is immune for 1 hour.

### What Rotsnout knows

He's rude but secretly glad to talk. He mocks the PCs' manners, clothes, and smell (his are worse). He hopes they'll deal with the invaders, but he won't ask. Read or paraphrase (p. 168):

> *"You probably saw that village down the trail, closer to the shore. There were some real nasty folks living there: escaped criminals, mutineers, bloodthirsty killers, and all kinds. I'm not particularly choosy about who I associate with because I like my privacy and I give others the same, so when I say folks are nasty, you can believe it.*
>
> *"I lived there for a little while, but I couldn't stand the neighbors, so I moved out. This path goes all the way to some old wooden fortress that was built ages ago and abandoned some time back as well. That was too much space for the likes of me, so I instead built this old cottage right here, and it suits me just fine. No one from the village comes this far unless they're willing to pay for my services, and no one comes from the old fortress, because there isn't anybody there.*
>
> *"At least, there wasn't anybody there until recently. It wasn't long ago that I caught wind of some new group that'd come to the island. Not just new to the island, like they sailed from anywhere, but new to this world. They came from some other plane, one that's all horror and shadow and evil. Spindly, alien things. They started raiding out from that fortress in small groups—not to kill, but to ruin. They've blighted parts of the forest with this gross, tarry resin. It turns the plants and animals wicked. Fortunately, they've left my swamp and my cottage alone; they might not even know I'm out here.*
>
> *"But they sure found the village, they did. And they didn't blight it or attack it. They...converted, I suppose, the people there. Must have promised them a lot of work or treasure or... improvements by coming to the fortress, because everyone in the village just up and relocated, tromping up that path like they were in a parade led by the most sinister creatures I ever saw. At least, it sounded like everyone; if they left anyone behind in that village, I'm sure they didn't leave them in any state I care to set eyes on.*
>
> *"Since then, none of the villagers have been back, and the raids across the island have stopped. Whatever those monsters are doing, they're doing it right there at the fortress. You won't catch me going within a mile of that place, as I value my hairy hide too much. But someone ought to check it out, even if it's a fortress filled with reprobates and alien monsters. Good luck with that."*

- **DC 20 Occultism:** the invaders come from the Shadow Plane.
- If the PCs describe sklaggans, he says he's seen them. He's also seen similar **bulkier, tentacle-faced monstrosities**.
- He knows nothing about the Jewel and little about Gullstone Cave. He avoids the kind plant creatures he thinks live near the cave. The PCs know that's out of date.

**Gift:** if the PCs commit to the fortress, he decides they're not smart enough to succeed without him. He fetches a ***ring of the ram***, a ***salve of slipperiness***, and a ***moderate healing potion***.

**XP:** **30 XP** for hearing his tale.

> **🎯 Milestone:** Meeting Rotsnout is the story milestone (p. 11). The PCs reach **level 6** by resting here.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The "bulkier, tentacle-faced monstrosities" hint at what waits in the **Chapter 7** fortress.

### Event 15 — Swamp Raiders *(Severe 5)*

**When:** in the middle of the talk. For example, as it winds down, or while Rotsnout fetches the gift. The PCs only need to be able to see the mist past the end of the dock.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Villagers with loose lips told the sklaggans about Rotsnout not long ago. The sklaggans sent a veenlijk and two loyal swarm assassins to kill him before he becomes a problem. They're to kill anyone with him too.

> *The swamp mist thickens and curls into strange shapes. A few bells tinkle from within the mist.*

If Rotsnout is there: *"Those are my warning bells, from out in the swamp. Something's coming, and I've already had enough trespassers for one day."*

**Hazard: poisonous mist** raised by the veenlijk:
- **Three 10-foot bursts** roll north toward the shore. Place them as shown on the map.
- A breathing creature that **starts its turn** in one takes **2d8 poison**, **DC 20 basic Fortitude**.
- Each cloud moves **1d4 squares north** at the end of each round.
- The clouds are greenish and easy to see from far off. They give no concealment inside. But a creature on one side is [concealed](gm_chapter_02.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected) from a creature on the other side. That lets the attackers sneak close.

**Creatures:**
- **Veenlijk:** BB 117, Creature 7. Initiative: Stealth +18. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp. It climbs up the end of the dock.
- **Corrupted Swarm Assassins (2):** BB 109, Creature 3. Initiative: Stealth +11. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. They attack from the sides of the dock.
  - **Corrupted:** sklaggan resin lets them ignore natural difficult terrain (swamp, undergrowth). It also makes them immune to poison damage.
- All of them fight to the death.

> **💡 GM tip:** The book calls this fight difficult. If the PCs struggle, it allows Rotsnout to help more directly. He has *heal*, *barkskin*, and *faerie fire* prepared. Use them before the fight turns deadly.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The swarm assassins are immune to poison, so the mist can't hurt them. That line doesn't mention the veenlijk. Check BB 117 before you treat it as safe in the clouds.

### After the attack

Rotsnout urges the PCs to rest in his cottage before the fortress, since the ship is a long walk back. He offers healing.

**Swamp Slurp Surprise:** he dumps laundry out of the cauldron and starts cooking without wiping it. The last filthy sock is still in there. He adds mushrooms, swamp grasses, herbs, moss, and a few live frogs. Grug stirs with a flat paddle. Sometimes Grug falls in and climbs back out. The result is chunky green sludge that smells good and tastes better. PCs who refuse hear: *"Your loss, friend, all the more for me!"*
- **A full bowl:** **10 temporary Hit Points** on waking, for **8 hours** ([temporary HP](#temporary-hit-points)).
- **A taste only:** maybe **1 temporary Hit Point**, so they know what they missed.
