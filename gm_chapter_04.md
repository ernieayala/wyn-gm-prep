## The chapter in one minute

Poppy's elevator takes the PCs down into her proving grounds. She built them to train someone to take her place.

- The "pirate treasure" was a cover story.
- Her journal shows she wanted a successor to fight the [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth).
- Her real prize is the [Obsidian Star](gm_glossary.md#obsidian-star). It is one of four pieces of the [Jewel of the Indigo Isles](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles).

The PCs survive the Testing Hall and the Stoneflower Arena. Then they solve Poppy's last riddle by destroying statues of her friends. Lifting the Star brings the mountain down. They escape on her ship, the [Chromatic Queen](gm_glossary.md#chromatic-queen). Only the Star unlocks its helm.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Poppy's journal** (G9): the truth about the cult, plus hints to the other three Jewel pieces.
2. **The Obsidian Star** (G26), an item 7 artifact.
3. **The Chromatic Queen** (G28), unlocked with the Star.
4. **Escaped Shivering Mountain** (Event 13).

**Party level:** starts at **3**, ends at **4**. End of Act 1 (Part One, *Search for the Missing Map*).

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** This is where the campaign turns. The players came for gold. They find an empty vault and a mission instead. The Hall of Heroes says: don't worship legends. The journal says the legend was a disguise. The Star and the ship start Act 2.

## How the chapter is built

The chapter is one dungeon, **Poppy's proving grounds**. It has areas **G1–G28** plus **Event 13** (p. 86–109; map p. 88). The book says the PCs can go in any order. In play, some rooms block others until the PCs clear them:

| Part | Areas | What it is | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front halls | G1–G4 | Arrival, the broken teleport circle, the Gilded Hall, the Chamber of Decisions | Open |
| Training rooms | G5–G9 | Four rooms granting Poppy's Blessings, plus her secret reading room with the journal and key | Open. G9 is behind a secret door |
| Testing Hall | G10 | Three complex traps at once | Doors lock behind the PCs |
| Student wing | G11–G15 | Well, shrine, strategy room, dorm, stairs | G11 is locked (key in G14) |
| Private wing | G16–G21 | Poppy's balcony, quarters, vault, and two balconies | G16, G17, G18, G20 need Poppy's key |
| Final test | G22–G26 | The arena, the Victory Walk, the Hall of Heroes, the Altar | Reached through G10, or around it through G20 |
| The cove | G27–G28, Event 13 | The *Chromatic Queen* and the escape | Cove tunnels connect back to the complex. G26's door to it is barred from the G26 side |

**Long rests** (the PCs will need a few; p. 87):
- **G12 Shrine of Aoz**, once its two monsters are dead. 8 hours here restores twice the normal HP.
- **G14 Dormitory**, once Zakatari (G11) is dead. Before that, sleepers get *nightmare*.
- **G22 Arena Threshold** is a trap. Sleepers wake fatigued, and the shale charger comes looking.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** Are the PCs resting after every fight? The book suggests wandering monsters (p. 87). Use them to push the party along, not to punish it.

## PF2 rules new in this chapter

This section skips the Chapter 1 rules. Find them here: [degrees of success](gm_chapter_01.md#degrees-of-success), [encounter difficulty and XP](gm_chapter_01.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp), [basic saving throws](gm_chapter_01.md#basic-saving-throws), [secret checks and Recall Knowledge](gm_chapter_01.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge), [conditions](gm_chapter_01.md#conditions-youll-see-this-chapter), [movement, falling, and Grab an Edge](gm_chapter_01.md#movement-and-terrain), [hazards](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards), [Monster Parts](gm_chapter_01.md#monster-parts-treasure-system), and [NPC companions](gm_chapter_01.md#npc-companions).

### Bonus types and stacking

There are three types: **circumstance**, **item**, and **status**.
- Within one type, only the highest bonus counts. Penalties work the same way.
- Bonuses of different types add together.

**This chapter:** these are all **status** bonuses:
- Poppy's Blessings (G5–G8).
- Rally the Crowd (G23).
- Spells like *bless* and *heroism*.

Example: Pillar of Grace (+1 status to Reflex) plus the crowd's +2 status to saves = +2 to Reflex, not +3.

### Elite and weak creatures

See [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#elite-and-weak-creatures). Adjustments: BB 9.

**This chapter:**
- **Elite:** the G5 training constructs (springjaw dolls, Creature 2).
- **Weak:** the G12 chamber ooze and ruin bruiser, and the G23 shale charger.

Use the level on the stat line for XP.

### Slowed and fatigued

- **Slowed X:** lose X actions at the start of each turn (slowed 1 = 2 actions instead of 3).
- **Fatigued:** see [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#conditions-new-this-chapter).

**This chapter:**
- G12's two monsters are slowed 1 from centuries of fighting each other.
- Any PC in the G23 stands is slowed 1 and also frightened 1.
- Sleeping in G22 leaves a PC fatigued.

### Searching for secret doors

See [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#seek-search-and-secret-doors). Most secret doors in this dungeon are easy to find from one side and hard from the other:

| Secret door | Easy side | Hard side |
|---|---|---|
| G3 stage door | Far side: automatic | From the stage: DC 15 Perception while searching |
| G8 ↔ G9 | G9 side: book doesn't say | From G8: pull the "Rum" plank, or Search for hours and make DC 30 Perception |
| Hall south of G7 ↔ G9 | G9 side: no check | From the hall: DC 35 Perception, then pull a loose brick and flick the switch |
| G18 ↔ G19 | G18 side: turn the wheel | From G19: Seek and DC 20 Perception, then press a ceiling button (use the iron rod) |
| G20 ↔ hall west of G10 | G20 side: obvious | From the hall: DC 20 Perception while Searching |
| G26 ↔ G27 | G26 side: plain, but barred | From G27: DC 35 Perception, and it's barred from the other side |

### Locks and checks that need several successes

**Pick a Lock:** 2 actions, Thievery, thieves' tools, trained rank. Each lock quality has a DC and a success count:
- **Poor lock:** DC 15, 2 successes.
- **Average lock:** DC 25, 4 successes.

- A success counts once.
- A critical success counts twice.
- A critical failure breaks the tools.

Some traps work the same way. Each ice and fire vent takes **two** DC 19 Thievery successes. The book says: "Two DC 19 Thievery (trained) checks."

**This chapter:**
- **Poor locks:** G11 and the G10 crystal latches.
- **Average locks:** G16, G17, G18, G20, and the G10 balcony door.

DC 25 is hard for a level-3 rogue. The book means for the PCs to use Poppy's key.

### Hardness and breaking objects

See [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#breaking-and-forcing-objects) for Hardness, HP, Broken Threshold, and object immunities.

**This chapter:**
- Undayo's statue (G25) has **Hardness 19**. A hit needs at least 20 bludgeoning to break it. Undayo's warhammer (G2) is the exception: it ignores the Hardness.
- The G10 electric orb has only Hardness 3 and weakness sonic 5. That's why the book suggests *shatter*.

### Resting and healing

8 hours of rest restores Constitution modifier (minimum 1) × level HP. It also removes fatigued. Daily preparations come next (spells, investing items).

**This chapter:** the G12 Shrine of Aoz doubles this healing. It stops once the Obsidian Star leaves its pedestal.

### Counteracting

See [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting).

**This chapter:** the *Chromatic Queen*'s helm ward is **6th level, counteract DC 35** (p. 109).
- Level-3 and level-4 casters can cast *dispel magic* at 2nd level at most.
- A critical success at counteract level 2 reaches only level 5. That is still short.
- The Obsidian Star is the answer.

### Artifacts and invested items

- **Invested** items work only after the wearer invests them, usually at daily preparations. A wearer can have **10** at most.
- An **artifact** is beyond normal crafting. Normal means can't destroy it.
- An **innate spell** comes from a creature's or item's own magic. "Once per day" = once between daily preparations.

**This chapter:** the [Obsidian Star](#the-obsidian-star-item-7) is an invested artifact. It grants an innate occult spell once per day.

### Identify Magic

See [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#identify-magic-and-learn-a-spell).

**This chapter:** the PCs can identify Poppy's Blessings with **DC 20 Religion or Occultism** (G4).

## Arrival — the proving grounds (G1)

### Background (p. 86–87)

> **🔒 GM-only:** Soon after landing on Goldcrop, Poppy and her crew swore to take the secrets of the war and the Jewel to their graves.
> - Decades later she still sensed the cult. It had followed her to the island, so she blamed traitors in her crew.
> - She trained recruits from Rumplank and other Goldcrop settlements. The story was that one would inherit the title of Gilded Pirate.
> - The truth: she wanted a successor who could carry the legend of the War of the Shivering Behemoth and destroy the returning cult.
> - Every pupil died in her deadly tests.
> - In old age she paid for the magic map and left a journal of clues.

Her crew found these limestone caves when they docked the *Chromatic Queen* in the cove (G27). Over many years she shaped them into a "dungeon" like the ruins of her youth. It holds a port, a vault, crew and student quarters, a hideaway, and a training ground.

**Features:** cut flagstone floors, finished stonework, and **10-foot ceilings** unless noted. Unlit torch sconces hang on nearly every wall.

### Recall Knowledge (secret check)

During the ride down, roll a **secret DC 20 Society or DC 15 Poppy Lore** check for each PC. A **Poppy fanatic** gets an automatic critical success (p. 87).

- **Critical success:** as success, plus:
  - Beloved Poppy was a legendary trickster. Her humor was wicked and sometimes heartless.
  - She showed her disciples no mercy. She pitted them against monsters and harsh training. She reminded them they were always free to leave.
  - People said her proving grounds were full of traps, hazards, and creatures.
- **Success:** Poppy's hideout, her "proving grounds," lay in Goldcrop's jungles.
  - People said she hoarded vast wealth there and trained would-be Gilded Pirates.
  - She meant to leave her treasure, her title, and perhaps other secrets to the first worthy student. None proved worthy.
- **Failure:** nothing specific.
- **Critical failure:** a confident lie. The PC "knows" that only poppies or poppy seeds get you safely to Poppy's treasure. Scattering them takes one manipulate action and beats the few dangers on the way.

> **💡 GM tip:** The lie is a trap for the players. Poppies do nothing in the Testing Hall. Let them laugh about it later.

### G1. Floating Platform

> *In the center of this chamber, a circular, gilded platform floats just inches above the flagstone floor. A long hallway leads to the east, with burnt-out torches in iron sconces on the stonework walls.*

It works like F29. Stand on the disc and imagine it rising to return to Shiverskull Cave.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** G1 is a working exit. The PCs can go up for supplies or a rest in the caves above.

## The front halls (G2–G4)

### G2. Teleportation Circle

> *A large circular seal is embedded in the floor at the center of this intersection. The seal is made from hundreds of pieces of stone, all artfully fused together around an arcane geometric symbol made of obsidian. The circle has been smashed in multiple spots, significantly marring the design and even reducing it to rubble in parts. The obsidian warhammer lying on the ground next to the smashed circle seems the likely culprit of the vandalism; the purpose of the small disk-shaped medallion lying next to the warhammer is less certain.*

[Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red)'s talismans once let Poppy and her students teleport here from anywhere in the Isles. Her last possible successor died in the Testing Hall. Then Poppy smashed the circle with her old friend Undayo's warhammer so no one could misuse it (p. 87–89).
- **DC 20 Arcana or Occultism** (Recall Knowledge): it's a teleportation circle for anyone holding a keyed medallion like the one on the floor. It's broken for good.

**Treasure:**
- **Undayo's obsidian warhammer:** a ***+1 striking warhammer (4)*** with a solid obsidian head. **DC 20 Arcana or Nature** (Recall Knowledge): it's tuned to destroy certain obsidian objects. It **ignores the Hardness** of the G25 obsidian statue.
- **Gilded medallion:** useless, but worth **5 gp** as a historical artifact.

**Background special effect:** a **Poppy fanatic** knows the warhammer on sight. So does anyone who makes **DC 25 Poppy Lore or DC 30 Society**. It was forged from the remains of lava beasts. The book suggests making it a relic with earth and fire gifts, using the *Gamemastery Guide* relic rules.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the barbarian's name):** The book prints **Undoyo** (G2 background, all of G25) and **Undayo** (G2 treasure). Chapter 1 and the glossary use [Undayo of Stonegulch](gm_glossary.md#undayo-of-stonegulch). They are the same person.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The warhammer is the easy answer to a G25 statue. Make sure someone carries it there.

### G3. Gilded Hall

> *Large flags adorn the stone walls of this broad hall. Each flag is a different color and adorned with an ornate symbol denoting one of the major islands of the Indigo Isles: a yellow flag with a jolly roger in the shape of a g'mayun's skull for Goldcrop Island, a cerulean flag with a bluebell flower for Bluebell Island, a black flag with a silver crescent moon for Moonshadow Isle, and so forth.*
>
> *Evenly spaced pillars throughout the hall are crafted in the likenesses of legendary figures both fictional and real, heroic and villainous—the Gray-Eyed Fencer, Mirakano the Underhanded, Queen Rosameses, Yrabold Poxfoot, and others. The columns support friezes depicting scenes of flying g'mayuns, towering orpoks, rainbow-hued chochoris, and other gods, all in the midst of grandiose acts: slaying giant horned beetles, snatching sacred gemstones, pouring vessels of infinite water to fill the ocean floor around the Indigo Isles. In the middle of the room, a faded yellow carpet stretches from west to east, terminating at a raised platform accessible by a short staircase. Though the entire hall enraptures the senses, the cloth-of-gold tapestry hanging on the eastern wall over the platform commands the most attention; embroidered on this magnificent work of art is a tremendous bird's-eye map of the entire Indigo Isles archipelago.*

Poppy held ceremonies, meals, inductions, memorials, and speeches here. She modeled the hall on wonders from her travels. She wanted it to inspire students to explore (p. 89).

- **The extra flag:** **DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** spots one flag too many. It is dark red with a serrated curved sword. It's [Razorback Isle](gm_glossary.md#razorback-isle), which sank in the war with the cult. The PCs can name it **only after reading Poppy's journal**.
- **Secret door:** Poppy's "secret" stage entrance is in the eastern corner of the southern wall. Most students knew about it. Finding it takes **DC 15 Perception** while searching the stage. From the other side it's automatic.

**Treasure:**
- **The flags:** 100 gp total (20 gp each), 1 Bulk folded. King Okeki would be honored to hang them in Castle Rumplank.
- **The tapestry map:** 50 gp, 1 Bulk.

**Background special effect:** Poppy drew the tapestry map in detail herself. An **indigo trailblazer** who brings it back earns extreme notoriety as a trailblazer.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The **Bluebell Island flag** is blue cloth. It's one answer for Arahosko's statue (G25). The Razorback flag foreshadows [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef), the isle's sunken remains, in Chapter 8.

### G4. Chamber of Decisions

> *Four sturdy wooden doors, each emblazoned with a single word in thick, bold letters, punctuate the southern wall of this circular stone chamber. A corridor to the northeast offers a fifth exit.*

From west to east, the red-painted doors read **Strength, Wisdom, Grace, Genius**. They open to training rooms G5–G8. Poppy built these rooms to prepare students for the Testing Hall and Stoneflower Arena. No student ever passed the final test (p. 90).

#### Poppy's Blessings

[Pinomeri Elemarte](gm_glossary.md#pinomeri-elemarte), Poppy's cleric friend, laid divine magic on the four training rooms.
- **How to get one:** spend at least **1 hour** in the room.
- **Duration:** 24 hours, or until the creature leaves the proving grounds.
- **Limits:**
  - A creature can get each room's blessing **once ever**.
  - A creature can hold **only one blessing at a time**. Trying for a second while holding one fails automatically.
  - Only Poppy could reapply one.
- **Can't be dispelled**, disabled, or moved.
- **Identifying it:** **DC 20 Religion or Occultism** (Identify Magic). Anyone can feel the rooms' kind aura. A blessed PC knows how the blessing works.

| Room | Blessing (+1 status bonus to...) |
|---|---|
| **G5** Dojo of Strength | Strength-based checks and damage rolls: Strength-based melee attack rolls and damage rolls, Athletics |
| **G6** Space of Wisdom | Wisdom-based skill checks, Perception checks, and Wisdom-based spell attack rolls |
| **G7** Pillar of Grace | Dexterity-based checks: Reflex saves, ranged weapon and ranged unarmed attacks, Dexterity-based finesse attacks, Acrobatics, Stealth, Thievery. Also +1 status to AC if the PC isn't at their armor's Dex cap |
| **G8** Library of Genius | Intelligence-based checks and DCs: spell attack rolls, spell DCs, Arcana, Crafting, Lore, Occultism, Society |

> **📏 Rules refresher:** The blessings are status bonuses. They don't stack with *bless*, *heroism*, or the G23 crowd bonus. See [bonus types and stacking](#bonus-types-and-stacking).

> **💡 GM tip:** Each PC gets one blessing, for 24 hours at most. Dexterity helps in G10 (Reflex, Thievery). Strength helps in G23. Let the players plan.

## The four training rooms (G5–G9)

### G5. Dojo of Strength *(Low 3)*

> *Though the walls of this oddly shaped chamber appear to have once been padded with thick floor-to-ceiling cushions filled with straw, the padding has long since rotted. The straw filling has all spilled to the floor along the room's edges and deteriorated into dark gray mush. The room's southern half is shaped like an oblong octagon. Filling the floor in the center of the octagon is a simple square cloth mat with a series of drawn circles radiating from its center. Worn and decrepit, this combat training hall is mired with dents, scars, and bygone struggles.*

Students sparred with each other here, sometimes to the death. They also fought constructs that only knocked them out. Poppy took on birthday challengers and never lost (p. 90). A south door leads to G6.

**Training Constructs (3):** Creature 2, elite springjaw dolls (BB 9, 108). Initiative: Perception +7. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp.
- They look like wooden dummies. They deal **only nonlethal** damage. They have no Wind-up Scream or gnaw.
- **Trigger:** they step out of the walls when a creature steps onto the square mat.
- They stay in the room. They go back to their alcoves once every creature in the room is unconscious.
- **Below half HP:** a construct lights up, plays a victory fanfare, and retreats to the wall for repair.
- **Attacked while retreating** (say, for Monster Parts), it fights back.

**Blessing:** Strength ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the threat label):** Three level-2 creatures = 30 + 30 + 30 = 90 XP. On paper that's past Moderate. The book says **Low 3** because the damage is nonlethal and each one quits at half HP. Use the book's label.

> **💡 GM tip:** Describe the first fanfare. The players learn it's sparring. Then they can decide if Monster Parts are worth a real fight.

### G6. Space of Wisdom

> *A five-foot-wide archway bisects this area into two halves: a ten-foot-wide hallway in the northern half and a fifteen-foot-wide area to the south with an oval sandpit set into the floor. The flagstone floor of the hallway bears countless scuffs and scratches from boots or talons that have treaded the space in an oval pattern; a sturdy boar-bristle broom with a five-foot-long iron handle leans against the northeastern corner of this hall. The glittering, gold-colored sand in the pit to the south has been combed into a remarkable pattern, likely using the rake leaning against the southeast corner, which has the same sturdy five-foot-long iron handle as the broom.*

Students did walking meditation in the hallway. Many of the scuffs are from Poppy's talons. They did silent meditation at the sandpit (p. 91).
- **The sand:** looks like gold or topaz.
  - **DC 15 Crafting or DC 13 Mining Lore:** it's beautiful, ordinary beach sand.
  - **DC 20 Nature or DC 15 Indigo Isles Geography Lore:** it comes from Goldcrop's northern shores.
- **Useful tools:** the **iron handles** of the broom and rake can wedge the G10 crushing pit (Trap A).

**Blessing:** Wisdom ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

> **⚠️ Gotcha (a room-number error in the book):** The Testing Hall text puts the iron rods in "the Space of Wisdom (area G7)." It means **G6**. G7 is the Pillar of Grace.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Nudge the players to take the broom and rake. Without an iron bar, the G10 pit is far deadlier.

### G7. Pillar of Grace

> *The ceiling of this circular chamber soars nearly forty feet above the floor. Embedded into the center of the stonework floor is a wooden pillar the diameter of a ship's mast. The pillar, itself nearly thirty feet long, stretches toward the ceiling. Though it bears more than a few gouges, scrapes, and scars, the wooden pillar is still a beautiful thing to behold; it's made of striking yellow and tan hardwood, and it shines as though it were freshly lacquered.*

This room trains agility, balance, and climbing (p. 91–92).
- **Climbing the pillar:** **DC 35 Athletics** to Climb. Magic like *fly* would help. The book says that's likely beyond the party at this level.
- **Balancing on top:** no check if the PC stands still on both feet.
- **Treasure (without Monster Parts only):** a marble-sized **diamond worth 100 gp** sits on the pillar's flat top.
- **Secret door:** the hallway south of here seems to dead-end at its east end.
  - Searching toward the wall with **DC 35 Perception** finds a loose brick in the upper right.
  - Pull the brick and flick the switch behind it. That opens the way to **G9**.

**Blessing:** Dexterity ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

#### Defense special resource: wo'a wood

The pillar is a mast Poppy took from a destroyed Cult of the Shivering Behemoth warship. It was cut from the heart of a **wo'a**. That's a rare Goldcrop tree with strong, gold-colored hardwood.
- **DC 25 Nature or DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** (Recall Knowledge) identifies the wood.
- It's far too large and heavy to carry off on a whim. With some planning, it's a valuable crafting material.
- **If the PCs retrieve it**, it helps defend Rumplank (p. 315). In Chapter 12 it upgrades a **Wall** (Tier 1, Guard 25) to a **Fortress Wall** (Tier 2, Guard 40, anti-burrow). Or the PCs can trade it for **10 Resource Points**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (getting it out):** The book never says how to remove it. It also never says whether Event 13 buries it. Decide before the session. One judgment call: the PCs come back later by sea with laborers. The book allows that for the cove (G27).

> **🧩 Sets up:** Chapter 12's defense of Rumplank. Write it down if the PCs take it.

### G8. Library of Genius *(Moderate 3)*

> *Dozens of bookshelves made of wooden planks line the walls of this library. The planks and books are in varying states of decay, with a heavy layer of mold blanketing much of the place. On each plank-shelf is carved a word or two indicating the subject of the books, pamphlets, and volumes lined up on that plank. The subjects are all classic pirate interests: broad categories such as treasure, sailing, sea monsters; specific interests like gold, rum, cannons; people and places like g'mayuns, chochori, the Indigo Isles; and so forth. Only one section of wall isn't covered floor to ceiling in bookshelves; hung on the stone wall in this gap is a conspicuously framed piece of parchment paper.*

This room trains the mind. It holds books from across the Isles, and a few by Poppy (p. 92–94).

#### Hazard — Toxic Mildew *(Hazard 1, simple)*

Pulling books or bumping the crumbling shelves can release spores.
- **Stealth DC 20 (expert).**
- **Disable:** **DC 17 Survival (trained) or Thievery (trained)** to wipe the mildew off one 5-foot section of shelving.
- **AC** 17; **Fort** +10, **Ref** +4. **Bookshelf** HP 20 (BT 10); object immunities; weakness fire 5.
- **Waft Spores (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature interacts with any book or bookshelf. Or, a creature next to a bookshelf Strikes or is hit by a Strike. **Effect:** a 5-foot emanation centered on the triggering creature, **2d4 poison**, **DC 17 basic Fortitude**.
- **Reset:** each 5-foot section triggers once. The mold takes months to regrow.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Strikes beside a shelf trigger it too. Track which sections have gone off.

#### Wave 1

**Scroll Molds (4):** Creature 0, BB 86. Initiative: Perception +6. Monster Parts 2.25/5/9 gp each.

**Living Proof:** Creature 1, BB 58. Initiative: Perception +7. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp. A narcissist the molds treat as their king.

- The living proof orders the molds to attack anyone who enters. They stay in the room.
- **Scroll mold companion:** maybe the Chapter 1 [scroll mold](gm_glossary.md#scroll-mold) came along. If so, it can talk the other molds into surrendering once the living proof falls. This works only if the PCs spare them.

#### Wave 2

Once wave 1 falls, an animated pile of feather dusters, bookmarks, ink pots, and quills rises. It is Poppy's dead bookish disciples. They want to destroy anyone promising enough to have impressed her. It fights until destroyed.

**Liminal Librarian:** Creature 4, dishrag dervish (BB 101). Initiative: Perception +10. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.

> **📏 Rules refresher (the threat math):**
> - Wave 1: four level-0 molds at 10 XP each, plus a level-1 living proof at 20 XP = 60, Low.
> - Wave 2: one level-4 at 60 XP, also Low.
> - Back to back, 120 XP is Severe on paper. Use the book's **Moderate 3**.
>
> Expect the party to be worn down when the librarian arrives. See [encounter math](gm_chapter_01.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp).

#### After the fights

Once both waves are down and each section's mildew is disabled, the PCs can search safely.
- **Treasure:** hauling out the whole library takes days. That's beyond this adventure. **DC 18 Library Lore or Rumplank Lore** picks the most valuable books: **20 gp**, 2 Bulk total.
- **Research:** long biographies of Poppy's companions. They are a backup for G25.

#### Poppy's Puzzle

The framed parchment is the only hint. It slips from its frame. The back is blank. The front has a poem in Poppy's pirate scrawl. When a PC picks it up or examines it, hand out **Handout 2: Poppy's Poem**:

> *Only the most special venue*
> *would ever do*
> *to unveil my past, that bloody shawl*
> *a place where everyone knows my face—*
> *my home of homes,*
> *my final port of call.*

**The answer is Rumplank**, as a pun.
- Books on Rumplank (History, Geography, and so on) are red herrings.
- The PCs pull the plank labeled **Rum** (liquor and ale).
- A sliding door in the **southern wall** opens to G9.
- **Without solving it:** a few hours of Searching and **DC 30 Perception**. No student managed it.

**Blessing:** Intelligence ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

> **💡 GM tip:** Are the players stuck? Ruff (G17) can show Poppy pulling a shelf here. A tired table may not want a long riddle after two waves.

### G9. Poppy's Reading Room

> *A low table and an accompanying wooden chair are the only pieces of furniture in this cozy nook. Stacked on the floor next to the chair is a pile of books, all heavy with rot. Lying on the center of the table is a single leatherbound journal that's somehow been left untouched by the passage of time.*

This was Poppy's late-night retreat, out of the students' sight. A few students, like Akirin (G14), suspected a secret room in the library (p. 94–95).
- **Secret door, west wall:** obvious from this side. From the hall south of G7, it takes DC 35 Perception (see G7).

#### Poppy's Journal

This is the dungeon's most valuable treasure. It holds dozens of entries from Poppy's final years, which take days to read. The last few entries reveal:
- She trained her best pupils here. She wanted a successor worthy of her title and of the tragic secret behind her wealth.
- That secret: a war that threatened the world, against a cabal of necromancers called the **Cult of the Shivering Behemoth**.
- Her grandest treasure is a magic pendant hidden in the dungeon, the **Obsidian Star**. It is one of four parts of a powerful relic, the **Jewel of the Indigo Isles**.
- The other pieces are scattered across the Isles. Someone should reclaim and rejoin them *"when the time is right."*
- The journal never says when or why. Its hints are cryptic. It would take days or weeks in Rumplank's libraries to decode them.

**Poppy's Key:** it sits in a cut-out in the journal's bookplate. It opens **G16, G17, G18, and G20**, and the G10 balcony door.

**Background special effect:** the journal fascinates an **indigo historian** and distracts a **Poppy fanatic**. It overturns long-held beliefs about Poppy and the Isles. That earns the historian induction into the **Indigo Isles Historical Society** as an influential new member.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (two versions of the journal):**
> - G9 says "when the time is right" and "days, if not weeks."
> - The p. 109 sidebar says "when the time and place are right" and "weeks, if not months." It adds that the pieces seem drawn to each other. It also says the journal holds maps and rough sketches of where they are.
>
> Use p. 109 for the Chapter 5 hand-off. It's the fuller version.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The twist starts here: Poppy was hiding a war. Let the players read the "cabal of necromancers" line themselves. Then move on. The Star is still in the dungeon.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The journal starts the Act 2 search ([Synopsis, Act 2](gm_story_synopsis.md#act-2-voyage-to-runaway-reef-chapters-58)). If the PCs never find it, the Star replays its key points as visions ([Concluding the chapter](#concluding-the-chapter)).

## The Testing Hall (G10)

### G10. Testing Hall *(Severe 3)*

- **Where:** a 15-foot-wide stone double door from the north hallway.
- **Goal:** survive and disable three traps, or leave the room empty of living creatures for a minute.

The doors are carved **"TESTING HALL"** and, below that, **"NO TURNING BACK."** Handholds swing the doors outward.
- When a character enters, ticking echoes.
- **Two rounds (12 seconds) later**, the doors swing shut. They knock anyone in the doorway inside.
- Then every trap activates (p. 95).

> *The floor, thirty-foot-high ceiling, and walls of this wide corridor are all made of flat, solid stone and painted in faded but still quite stark colors. The northern third of the room is painted yellow, the middle third is painted red, and the final third is painted bright blue. The yellow floor is made of intricate ceramic tilework that invites closer inspection. The red walls are riddled with evenly spaced holes. From the blue ceiling hangs a metal rod; around the tip of the rod is suspended a cannonball-sized glass orb. The southern end of the hall sports a ten-foot-tall, stone-railed viewing platform with a closed door in an alcove along the southern wall. Just west of the viewing platform is another closed door.*

**Start encounter mode when anyone enters.** The call for initiative is the warning.
- An invisible sensor detects the slightest movement.
- The doors lock with a "ka-chunk."
- The room erupts in grinding gears, fire, frost, and lightning.

**Doors:**
- **North double door and SW-corner door:** iron rods hold them shut. They **can't be picked or Forced Open**. They unlock when every trap is disabled. They also unlock 1 minute after the sensor detects no living creatures.
- **Balcony door (south wall, to G20):** average lock. It takes **four DC 25 Thievery** successes, or Poppy's key (G9).

**The traps stay active** until the PCs disable them or the sensor detects no living creatures. Dodging doesn't end the encounter.

> **📏 Rules refresher (the threat math):** Three level-3 complex hazards, 40 XP each = 120 = **Severe**. Each trap rolls its own initiative. See [hazards](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards).

#### Trap A — Crushing Pit *(Hazard 3, complex mechanical trap)*

A spiked pit hides under the yellow tiles. Then its walls close in (p. 96).
- **Stealth** +13 (expert) to notice the tilework is a trap door.
- **Description:** a 25-foot-wide by 20-foot-long section of floor drops into a 20-foot-deep spiked pit of old bones and gear. Its west and east walls push toward the center.
- **Disable:** **DC 18 Athletics or Survival** to wedge a sturdy iron bar between the walls. Anything similar with **Hardness 10 or more** also works. The G6 broom or rake works. A weapon works too, but the PC will likely lose it.
- **AC** 17; **Fort** +10, **Ref** +10. **Trap door** Hardness 6, HP 28 (BT 14). **Wall** Hardness 10, HP 50 (BT 25). Object immunities.
- **The Test Begins (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature enters and stays in the Testing Hall for at least 2 rounds. **Effect:**
  - The doors slam and lock, and the trap door swings open.
  - Creatures on it fall 20 feet onto spikes. They can Grab an Edge with **DC 23 Reflex**.
  - A faller takes **10 bludgeoning** from the fall and **2d6 piercing** from the spikes (**DC 20 basic Reflex** against the spikes).
  - The trap rolls initiative.
- **Routine (1 action):** the walls move **2½ feet per round** toward each other.
  - After **5 rounds**, any creature still in the pit takes **20d6 bludgeoning** (**DC 25 basic Reflex**). Then the walls slide back in one round.
  - Treat a creature on top of a wall as if The Test Begins hit it. It may fall in, and it can Grab an Edge.
  - This repeats until someone disables or resets the trap.
- **Reset:** 1 minute after the sensor detects no creatures in the hall.
- **The keys:** Seeking at the pit's bottom with **DC 20 Perception** finds two keys on an iron ring among the spikes. They open Trap B's crystal latches.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (20d6):** It averages 70. That's enough to drop a level-3 PC who fails the save. Count the five rounds out loud so the players feel the walls coming.

#### Trap B — Ice and Fire Trap *(Hazard 3, complex mechanical trap)*

Eight vents on the west and east walls shoot frost and flame (p. 96–97).
- **Stealth** +10 (expert) to spot the western dial and the eastern slot. Noticing the vents is DC 0.
- **Description:** one wall shoots frost while the other shoots flame. They swap every round.
- **The dial:** it sits in the middle of the west wall, near the floor. It flicks between "I" and "II" at the start of the trap's turn. **Interacting** with it each round holds it and stops next round's swap. Breaking it sticks it for good.
- **The crystal:** it sits in the middle of the east wall, near the floor.
  - It's six inches back, behind a slot 4 inches tall and 12 inches wide.
  - Two latches with **poor locks** hold it. The pit keys open them.
  - Removing or breaking the crystal shuts down the whole trap.
- **Disable:** pick one:
  - **Two DC 19 Thievery (trained)** per vent.
  - **Three DC 21 Thievery (expert)** to lock the dial.
  - **Four DC 15 Thievery (trained)** to Pick the latch Locks and pull the crystal. This disables the entire trap.
- **AC** 19; **Fort** +11, **Ref** +9. **Vent** Hardness 5, HP 20 (BT 10). **Dial** Hardness 10, HP 30 (BT 15). **Crystal** Hardness 12, HP 40 (BT 20). Object immunities.
- **The Test Begins (reaction).** Same trigger. **Effect:** the doors slam and lock. Frost blasts from the west wall and flame from the east. Creatures in a 10-foot line take **2d6 cold or 2d6 fire**. The trap rolls initiative.
- **Routine (8 actions; cold, fire):** each vent is a 5-foot wall section. Each spends 1 action on a 10-foot line toward the room's middle: **2d6 cold or fire**, **DC 22 basic Reflex**. All vents on one wall shoot the same element each round.
- **Protection works:** magic, items, and abilities that protect against harsh environments can block the jets.
  - Immunity to damage from **severe cold** cancels this trap's cold damage.
  - Immunity to damage from **severe heat** cancels its fire damage.
- **Reset:** same as Trap A.

**Safe spaces:** a few squares between the jets in the middle of the hall. None holds more than three Medium creatures (map, p. 96).

> **💡 GM tip:** The book says the G13 elixirs (lesser winter wolf, lesser salamander) make this trap far easier. Did the PCs skip G13? Then the pit's keys are the next best help.

#### Trap C — Electric Orb Trap *(Hazard 3, complex mechanical trap)*

This is the simplest trap, built to trip up players who overthink it. The fix: shatter the glass orb (p. 97).
- **Stealth** +11 (expert) to see the edges of the clear glass orb.
- **Description:** a 1-foot glass sphere hangs on a 3-foot rod from the 30-foot ceiling. Its bottom is 25 feet up. Area: **10-foot emanation** centered on the floor below.
- **Disable:** **two DC 23 Thievery (trained)** to remove the orb, or **one DC 23 Athletics** to Force Open and shatter it. Either way, the PC must reach it.
- **Or break it at range:** arrows, bolts, bullets, or *shatter* (the scroll is in the G11 well).
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +6, **Ref** +14. Hardness 3, HP 40 (BT 20). Object immunities. **Weakness sonic 5.**
- **The Test Begins (reaction).** Same trigger. **Effect:** the doors slam and lock. The orb lets out stored energy: **1d12 electricity** to each creature in the emanation (**DC 23 basic Reflex**). The trap rolls initiative.
- **Routine (1 action):** **1d12 electricity** in the emanation, **DC 23 Reflex**:
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage.
  - **Failure:** full damage and flat-footed for 1 round.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage, flat-footed for 1 round, and knocked prone.
- **Reset:** same as Trap A.

#### Aftermath

The north and southwest doors unlock with a loud sound (p. 98). This happens when all three traps are disabled, or 1 minute after the sensor detects no living creatures.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the bypass):** A secret door in the hallway **west** of the Testing Hall leads up to G20 and on to G22. It skips G10. Finding it takes DC 20 Perception while Searching. The book allows this route, so don't block it if the PCs find it.

> **🎯 Why this room exists:** It's an exam on the earlier rooms: rods (G6), elixirs (G13), scroll (G11), keys (the pit). Players who explored walk through. Players who rushed learn why Poppy's students died.

## The student wing (G11–G15)

### G11. Abandoned Well *(Trivial 3)*

The hallway doors have **poor locks**. The key is on the dormitory floor across the hall (G14).

> *A low stone well is the only feature of note in this unworked cavern. A steel bucket tied to a rope lies discarded in a far corner.*

The well gave the students clean water, until a tragedy (p. 98).

**Zakatari:** Creature 3, parasite husk (BB 71). Initiative: Perception +7. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp.
- He was a promising pupil. He let loose a box of nasty magic worms to jam a Testing Hall trap. The worms ate him, and he became an undying monster.
- Poppy and the students lured him here and bricked him into a corner. His moans warned other would-be cheaters.
- Years after Poppy's death, he broke out of the bricks. He never got past the flimsy lock. He lives on foul water and wall fungus, and he attacks on sight.

**Treasure:** a ***scroll of shatter*** in a steel case. It lies at the bottom of the **30-foot-deep** well, under murky water.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The scroll breaks the G10 orb. Killing Zakatari makes G14 safe to sleep in.

### G12. Shrine of Aoz *(Severe 3)*

> *Wide, stone double doors are the only exit from this broad, cross-shaped chamber. The chamber appears to have once been a place of peaceful refuge or sanctuary, but nearly everything inside has been ruined or eroded. The red velvet carpet on the floor has been worn down to tatters, as have the now-indiscernible banners on the walls and shattered pews reduced to mere splinters on the floor. Even the flagstone tiles on the floor and the brickwork on the walls are heavily chipped, gouged, and, in some spots, nearly obliterated. In the southern alcove, a statue of a tall, masked figure stares down toward the floor, ominously.*

Many students followed [the Balance](gm_glossary.md#the-balance), though Poppy didn't. Pinomeri ran this shrine to [Aoz](gm_glossary.md#aoz), god of life, as a place to honor the Parents (p. 98–99).

**The standoff:** Poppy built the ruin bruiser to guard against mountain monsters. It has fought a chamber ooze for centuries.
- The shrine heals the ooze.
- The ooze's acid can't harm stone.
- So neither one can win. The PCs are new targets.

**Weak Chamber Ooze:** Creature 4 (BB 9, 16). Initiative: Perception +7 (slowed 1). Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- **Regeneration 2** while it stays in this room.

**Weak Ruin Bruiser:** Creature 5 (BB 9, 78). Initiative: Perception +13 (slowed 1). Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- Its stone plates carry Poppy's orders in **Terran**. A **DC 19 Crafting** check also gives the meaning and the maker. The orders: *"In the event of my death, protect this sanctum. —Poppy"* She expected her successor to beat it.

**How the fight runs:**
- Both are **weak** and **slowed 1** ([slowed](#slowed-and-fatigued)). They get 2 actions each.
- They **take turns** attacking each other and the party. This lasts until the PCs excel and prove they're the bigger threat.
- A level-4 and a level-5 would be 140 XP (60 + 80). Because of these handicaps, the book prices it at **120 XP, Severe**.

**Healing magic:** the shrine draws its healing from the Obsidian Star (G26). An **8-hour rest here restores twice the normal HP** ([resting](#resting-and-healing)). This lasts until the Star leaves its pedestal.

> **💡 GM tip:** You decide what "excelling" means. One option: once a PC lands a critical hit or drops one monster below half HP, the survivor turns on the party.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Taking the Star ends the double healing. Rest here **before** the final test.

### G13. Strategy Room

> *A broad wooden table fills the center of this square chamber, a rough map of the Indigo Isles sprawled atop it, its corners pinned to the table's surface. The periphery of the stone chamber is lined with crates, barrels, chests, and other storage containers. Hundreds of candles of various shapes, sizes, and colors fill the open crates. The other containers are half-filled with building supplies and nautical tools such as hempen rope, iron nails, saws, and hammers, though one small box contains a few wineskins, some still half-full. A wooden door in the western wall exits the room.*

This room was for planning and storage (p. 99). It holds:
- Thousands of candles. Most students were g'mayuns who weren't used to dark tunnels.
- Digging tools.
- Spare *Chromatic Queen* parts.

**Treasure:**
- If you like, any **common items worth 5 sp or less** from *Core Rulebook* Chapter 6.
- A blue vial and a red vial: **lesser winter wolf elixir** and **lesser salamander elixir**. They're useful anywhere. They're also the answer to G10's ice and fire trap.
- **Wineskins**, some half-full. That's alcohol for Pinomeri's statue (G25).

### G14. Dormitory

> *Dusty old bunks line the walls of these adjoining dormitories. The hay-filled mattresses are slightly sunken, though the wooden bed frames appear to be in fine shape. Tally marks in faded chalk have been scratched upon a few sections of the walls.*

Only Poppy had privacy. Students could sleep in the jungle or on the *Chromatic Queen*. Many used these bunks and kept a tally of their days on the walls (p. 100–101).
- **G11 key:** on the floor. Its worn tip scratched the tally marks.
- **Treasure:** **two lesser healing potions** in a gap in one bunk's boards.

**Akirin's Diary:** under a rotten pillow. Give the players **Handout 3: Akirin's Diary** (p. 100). Entries from Days 14, 22, and "25?" show:
- Akirin was proud to train under the Gilded Pirate. He was sure he'd take her place. Some of his peers had already died.
- He followed Poppy into the library, and she vanished. So he suspected secret passages.
- Zakatari cheated and became "that... thing." Poppy had the students brick him in. His moaning kept everyone awake.
- On his last night, Akirin gave Poppy his pendant. She threw it in her hearth, *"just like she'd done with so many before me."* Then they drank rum from her cask. The next day he and his friends were to face the Testing Hall and the Stoneflower Arena.

**Haunted room:** the beds are safe. But anyone who sleeps here **before** Zakatari is destroyed faces ***nightmare* (DC 23)**: a stone well bursting with worms. Once Zakatari is dead, the room is clean.

**Background special effect:** a **Poppy fanatic** learns they descend from one of Poppy's disciples. Their family never knew. Use a name from the handout, or add an entry. Poppy's harsh trials likely killed that ancestor. Now the PC is about to finish what the ancestor couldn't.

> **💡 GM tip:** The diary is the emotional center of the chapter. Akirin, or the fanatic's ancestor, is on G24's chalk list. If the players read the diary first, that list hits hard.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The pendant in the fire ties to G17's hearth. That hearth is Ruff's home now.

## Poppy's private wing (G16–G21)

**G16, G17, G18, and G20** have **average locks**: four **DC 25 Thievery** successes per door, or Poppy's key (G9 or G18).

### G16. Poppy's Balcony

> *This massive stone balcony overlooks an underground sea cove to the south. Droplets of condensation gather and fall from the tips of stalactites dangling from the tall, cavernous ceiling above. Below, luminescent sea creatures in hazy neon shades of cyan blue, lime green, and poppy pink flit beneath the surface of the black water. A beautiful ship is anchored just off the wooden dock that spans most of the cavern's northwestern wall.*

This was Poppy's thinking spot. She showed it to each student, over rum in her quarters, the night before their final test. Students could reach the *Chromatic Queen* from the long hallway east of the well and dorms. But the view from here impressed them (p. 101).

> **🧩 Sets up:** This is likely the PCs' first look at the *Queen*. Let them want it.

### G17. Poppy's Quarters

> *These lavish living quarters are outfitted in a variety of finery in varying states of decay. Moth-eaten tapestries embroidered with various crests and flags line the walls. A fine bedframe of dark wood supports a hay-filled mattress covered in white linens. Along the eastern wall, a low fire crackles in a stone hearth. Though the fire emits heat, and stray embers sizzle as they pop from the hearth, the flames don't emit any smoke.*

Poppy thought comfort made new pirates soft. But she felt a war hero had earned some luxury late in life (p. 101–102).

**Ruff:** Creature 4, hearth hound (BB 40). Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- He was Poppy's beloved hunting companion. He died of old age here. His spirit filled the hearth, and he stayed faithful until she died too.
- **Not hostile.** He's thrilled by company. He's sure anyone who gets this far is his master's friend. He leaps from the fire and circles the PCs, barking. He defends himself if attacked.
- He is bound to this room.
- **Befriended, with Monster Parts:** he gives magic hearth embers worth his Monster Parts value.

**What Ruff knows:** Poppy's name and commands (sit, fetch, roll over). He shapes his flames into simple scenes.
- **DC 18 Nature** to communicate. He shows Poppy's favorite spot, **the library**. In the scene, Poppy pulls out a shelf to reveal a secret door. Then she writes in a journal at a low table. This is the clue to G8–G9.
- Optional memories: Rumplank's beach, fighting a **shale charger** beside Poppy (the one now in G23), and resting with her here.

> **💡 GM tip:** This is the softest moment in a hard dungeon. Let the players play fetch. Killing him gains nothing that friendship doesn't.

### G18. Poppy's Vault

> *Clods of dusty cobwebs are the only treasures that line the stone shelves of this structurally impressive vault. On the floor are a few oak barrels, though only one of the vessels seems to have survived the ravages of time; the others' sides have ruptured in places, and their contents have long since spilled.*

Legends promised piles of riches. Poppy put nearly all of it into Rumplank before she died. Dust outlines mark the bare shelves (p. 102–103).
- **Spare key:** on a peg by the door. It opens **G16, G17, G18, and G20**, like the G9 key.
- **Treasure:** a large cask of ancient rum that's still drinkable. It's worth **30 gp** if the PCs cart it out. It also solves Pinomeri's statue (G25).
- **Secret door (south wall):** plain from this side. A chunky wheel turns the wall on a central vertical bar. That opens the way to G19.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The empty vault is the point. Play the letdown straight. The real treasure is the journal's mission.

### G19. Western Balcony

> *Short, wide steps carved into the rock itself lead up a tunnel to this sparsely appointed chamber, the southeastern portion of which is a railed balcony that overlooks an awesome underground cove. In the northern alcove of the room, a slender rod of rusty iron about one foot long lies discarded on the floor.*

This is a student balcony. The false wall in the northern alcove was a test of cunning. No student found it. Only Poppy used the iron rod on its ceiling switch (p. 103).
- **Secret door to G18:** an old, minor earthquake cracked the false wall. Seeking in the alcove with **DC 20 Perception** spots a vertical iron bar through the crack.
- **The trigger:** a button in a hole in the ceiling near the wall. The hole is 1 foot deep and nearly invisible. Pressing the button turns the wall 90 degrees on the bar. That opens the vault.
- **Backup route:** did the PCs miss the G9 key? They can get in here and take G18's spare.

### G20. Unfinished Room *(Moderate 3)*

Average locks (four DC 25 Thievery, or Poppy's key). This is an empty antechamber. Poppy never decided what it was for. It sits between her quarters and the viewing platforms over the Testing Hall and arena (p. 103).

**Chamber Ooze:** Creature 5, BB 16. Initiative: Stealth DC 28. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- Unlike G12's ooze, it never had to defend its territory. It lies spread across the whole 30-by-35-foot room, waiting for the party to enter.

> **📏 Rules refresher (the threat math):** One level-5 creature against a level-3 party is 80 XP, **Moderate**. It's at full strength and two levels up, so it hits hard.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (its initiative):** The book prints "Initiative Stealth DC 28," but initiative is a roll. Judgment call:
> - Roll its Stealth modifier (BB 16) for initiative.
> - Use DC 28 as the Perception DC to spot it before it attacks.

**Secret door (staircase, NW corner):** obvious from this side. From the hallway west of the Testing Hall, it takes **DC 20 Perception** while Searching. **This is how a party skips G10** and reaches G22.

### G21. Arena Balcony

This is Poppy's arena box, 25 feet up. She never used it (p. 103).
- Crafty PCs may climb onto the seats to open the south portcullis and skip the fight.
- That fails. Machinery and magic hold the portcullis. **The only way to open it** is to step onto the sand as a group (G23) and defeat the shale charger.

## The final test (G22–G26)

### G22. Arena Threshold

> *Bloodstains coat much of the roughly hewn, uneven stone floor in this hall. Deep scrapes, gouges, and gashes mar the walls as if from thick, sharp claws. Here and there, bits of shiny bug carapace sparkle in the hall's cobwebbed corners. A faint, hot breeze emanates from the open archway to the southwest.*

This is the last chance for Testing Hall survivors to prepare. Poppy kept the floor rough so they couldn't rest long (p. 103–104).
- **No sleeping:**
  - Sleepers wake **fatigued** ([fatigued](#slowed-and-fatigued)).
  - Before anyone gets a full night's sleep, the shale charger comes to see what's keeping its prey. That explains the blood.
- **Treasure:** a ***+1 striking morningstar (4)*** in the anteroom's northwest corner. It didn't save one of the charger's last victims.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book doesn't say how a charger fight here runs. Judgment call: run it as G23 without the crowd and portcullis.

### G23. Stoneflower Arena *(Severe 3)*

> *A thin layer of gritty sand covers the stone floor of this massive oval fighting arena. The cave around the fighting pit is empty except for ghostly figures of spectators—each shrouded in an ethereal golden cloak obscuring their faces—who stand in silence and stare down from the stone benches. Flags of Rumplank hang from the rails of a rounded balcony that looms twenty-five feet over the eastern portion of the stadium seating. Wide, steep stairs descend fifteen feet from the portcullis in the northern wall to the fighting pit below. The ceiling is unfinished natural stone, sixty feet above the floor of the fighting pit. To the south, another portcullis, this one flush with the floor of the fighting pit, separates the arena from a stone hall that stretches east.*

When **every creature from G22 is on the sand**, the north portcullis slams down. The crystal creature in the center wakes from centuries of magical sleep, screeching (p. 104).

**Weak Shale Charger:** Creature 5 (BB 9, 94). Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- Poppy killed many of these in the war with the cult. She thought her successor must know this enemy.
- She caught this one in the Shiverskull Caves. She put it in stasis like Dugari's (F15).
- **In stasis:** it can't act and is immune to all effects.
- It's furious about its captivity. It attacks whoever frees it. It wants to hurt anyone who looks like its pirate captors.

#### Rally the Crowd

The ghostly spectators love showmanship.

**Rally the Crowd** ◆ (concentrate), once per round. The PC makes a grand gesture, a boast, or a display of skill. Roll **DC 18 Acrobatics, Athletics, Diplomacy, or Intimidation**.
- **Critical success:** the crowd goes wild. The PC and each ally in the arena gain a **+2 status bonus** to attack rolls, saving throws, and skill checks for 1 round.
- **Success:** as above, but +1.
- **Failure:** the crowd watches stoically.
- **Critical hit:** a PC's critical hit on the shale charger counts as an automatic success, no action spent.

**Entering the stands:** the PC feels a chill. While there, the PC is **slowed 1** and **frightened 1**. The frightened value can't drop while the PC stays there. Slowed ends when the PC leaves.

**Aftermath:** the charger's death opens both portcullises. The arena, Testing Hall, G24, and G25 stay open. The PCs don't have to repeat either trial.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the creature's name):** The G23 text says "a monstrous shale spitter called a shale charger." Use **shale charger**. The stat line and the rest of the book do.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the threat label):** One level-5 creature against a level-3 party is 80 XP. On paper that's Moderate. Use the book's **Severe 3**, for three reasons:
> - The PCs are locked in.
> - The stands punish retreat.
> - They may arrive hurt from G10.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Rally the Crowd is a status bonus. It doesn't stack with a Poppy's Blessing on the same roll. See [bonus types and stacking](#bonus-types-and-stacking).

> **💡 GM tip:** Show the ghost crowd reacting to every big hit. Then the players will try Rally the Crowd without a prompt.

### G24. Victory Walk

> *Large, gilded metal frames line the walls of this curving hall. The frames are the right size and shape for portraits, though none bears so much as a single sketch. At the northern end of the corridor, on a bare stone wall opposite a sturdy iron door, is a list of what appear to be names scrawled in chalk. The names become less legible the farther down the list one goes.*

Poppy meant the frames for portraits of students who beat the arena. None did. Instead, one section of wall lists those who died in the Testing Hall or arena. Poppy stayed stoic in front of her pupils, but she grieved anyway. Her handwriting gets worse down the list (p. 104–105).

**Background special effect:** the **Poppy fanatic** PC's ancestor from G14 is on the list.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It's a breath before the puzzle. It's also the players' first look at Poppy's guilt. Read the read-aloud slowly.

### G25. Hall of Heroes

- **Goal:** destroy four statues of Poppy's friends, assemble the sword hidden inside them, and plunge it into Poppy's statue.

> *Gilded filigreed friezes line this lavish chamber, the walls of which sport murals painted in shocking reds, blues, and yellows that have refused to fade with time. Four statues of different heroes stand in the chamber's corners, all facing the center of the room. Each statue is made of a different material—obsidian, bloodstone, jade, and moonstone—and each bears a plaque with an inscription at its foot.*
>
> *At the head of this lavish chamber is a statue of Poppy von Barnacle herself, cast entirely in gold. In the center of Poppy's statue, directly in the middle of her chest between her ribs, is a peculiar vertical slot no more than an inch wide, a few inches from top to bottom, and of indiscernible depth. At the base of the gilded statue is a gilded plaque engraved with these words: "Lay Low Your Heroes." The expression on each statue's face is a dour, serious one, and the poses are similarly humble, giving little clue as to what the represented heroes might be thinking.*

PCs who don't know about the Star may think this room is the reward. It's Poppy's last challenge: a puzzle with no fight (p. 105–106).

**The final lesson:**
- Poppy saw her part in the war as grim duty.
- She wanted a successor just as humble, someone who serves instead of seeking glory.
- The riddle is easy. Destroying statues of Rumplank's heroes is the hard part.
- Under her cocky front, she'd have found a gold idol of herself distasteful.

#### The four statues

Each statue holds one piece of a ceremonial replica of Poppy's legendary sword, the **Gilded Blade**.

| Hero | Statue | Plaque | How to break it | U'oko's Ch 1 fact | Piece inside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Jesea Swiftarrow](gm_glossary.md#jesea-swiftarrow) | Jade | "X Marks the Spot" | Hit the **X-shaped scar on her jaw** with a projectile, such as an arrow or bolt. The pose hides the scar. It shows from one angle only: the opposite corner, 35 feet away. The statue shatters | X-shaped scar below her left ear | Jade pommel |
| [Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red) | Bloodstone | "Never Not Red" | **Drape it fully in blue cloth**, then pull the cloth away. The statue is gone. The G3 Bluebell flag or any blue cloak or blanket works | The only way to beat her was to dress her in blue | Bloodstone guard, joining hilt to blade |
| [Pinomeri Elemarte](gm_glossary.md#pinomeri-elemarte) | Moonstone | "Always Thirsty" | **Douse it in alcohol**, enough to coat the whole statue. It melts into goo. The G18 rum cask, G13 wineskins, or any jug of ale works | He loved rum, wine, and ale equally | Moonstone pommel, in the residue |
| [Undayo of Stonegulch](gm_glossary.md#undayo-of-stonegulch) | Obsidian | "Only Strength Will Do" | **One melee Strike dealing at least 20 bludgeoning damage.** Hardness 19; immune to piercing, slashing, and critical hits. It cracks in half. **Undayo's warhammer (G2) breaks it in one hit** by ignoring the Hardness | You had to beat him one-on-one | A curved blade of sharpened obsidian |

> **⚠️ Gotcha (two pommels):** The book lists a **jade pommel** and a **moonstone pommel**, plus a guard and a blade. A sword needs one pommel and a grip. The book doesn't fix this. Call one of them the grip. Keep both materials so the sword mixes all four statues.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Undayo's statue at level 3):** 20 bludgeoning in one Strike is a lot at level 3. The warhammer is the intended answer. Did the PCs leave it in G2? They walk back for it.

> **💡 GM tip (rewarding creativity):** The book says a solution the text doesn't cover still works if the whole table finds it brilliant.

> **💡 GM tip: no notes from Ch 1?** The biographies in the G8 library and the G9 journal hold the four facts.

#### Assembling the blade

The pieces slot together easily into a sturdy sword. Only the mismatched materials show it was ever four pieces.

#### Laying Poppy low

The Gilded Blade fits the slot in Poppy's chest. It locks with a satisfying "ka-chunk." Ancient gears turn. The statue and much of the wall behind it sink into a hidden hole in the floor. That opens the way to the **Altar of the Obsidian Star**.

**Treasure:** afterward the PCs can pull out the replica Gilded Blade, a ***+1 striking scimitar (4)***.

**Background special effect:** this may be hard on a **Poppy fanatic**. Honoring Poppy's legacy means dropping blind devotion. It means seeing past heroes as they were.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It pays off U'oko's Chapter 1 trivia. It also states the chapter's main idea: the players destroy the legend with their own hands, right before they learn what it hid.

### G26. Altar of the Obsidian Star

> *A slender pedestal of gray stone rises from a dais at the southern end of this simple chamber. No grand tapestries adorn the walls; no patterns decorate the tiles on the floor. The only item of note is a gemstone the size of a walnut made of the purest, darkest obsidian. The gem is immaculately cut into the shape of a star and delicately placed in a specifically made groove atop the pedestal so that it faces the northern doorway. To the east is a sturdy wooden door built into a natural cave wall, barred from this side by a thick horizontal beam of fine hardwood.*

This is Poppy's true inheritance. Lifting the Star off the pedestal claims it. And Poppy always has one more trick (p. 106–107).

- **East door to G27:** plain from this side. The hardwood beam takes **two creatures** to lift. From the cove side, the door can't open past it.

**Chaos breaks out:** taking the Star shakes the complex apart. Cracks and fissures open, and the mountain is about to split. The PCs need no check to sense the danger. **Go straight to [Event 13](#event-13--escape-from-shivering-mountain).**

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the shrine):** Taking the Star ends G12's double healing.

#### The Obsidian Star *(Item 7)*

- **Traits:** unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** worn. **Bulk** —.
- A plain silver pendant with a broken crystal in the rough shape of a star.
  - The crystal is black, with ghostly white shapes swimming deep inside.
  - It fades to **violet** at one broken edge and **deep blue** at the other.
- **Innate spell:** while invested, the wearer can cast ***earthbind*** or ***ghostly weapon*** as an innate occult spell **once per day**. The wearer picks one each day. *Ghostly weapon* targets only a metal or stone weapon.
- **One of four:** touch it to the other pieces and it joins them without a seam. That restores the **Jewel of the Indigo Isles**.
- **Durability:** it's far weaker than almost any other artifact, but just as hard to destroy. The four fragments **can't be destroyed one at a time**. They can be destroyed only once combined.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** See [artifacts and invested items](#artifacts-and-invested-items). It counts toward the wearer's 10 invested items.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The Star is a piece of the cult's relic. Poppy shattered the relic and hid the pieces. Every piece the PCs gather in Act 2 brings the cult closer. The cult steals the assembled Jewel in Chapter 9 ([Synopsis](gm_story_synopsis.md#act-3-battle-at-barnacle-bay-chapters-912)).

> **🧩 Sets up:** The broken edges point to the other pieces. The rule "can only be destroyed once combined" matters later.

## The cove and the escape (G27, G28, Event 13)

### G27. Secret Cove

> *Inky blue water stretches from wall to wall in this massive underground cove. Bobbing gently in the center of the cove is a gorgeous brigantine, its finely lacquered planks somehow untouched by time or the elements. High above, stalactites hang from a cavernous ceiling. A rickety boardwalk along the western perimeter of the cove connects to several tunnels that double back on the rest of the complex. To the south, the cove stretches on, out of sight, with only a pinprick of light in the far distance to mark the exit out to sea.*

The *Chromatic Queen* is still anchored here. The Star unlocks its helm, so with the Star the PCs can claim the ship and escape (p. 107–108).
- **Size:** 200 feet west to east, 150 north to south. The *Queen* barely fits through the south tunnel out to sea. The ceiling clears its tallest mast by at least 100 feet.
- **Sturdier than the rest:** **DC 15 Mountain or Engineering Lore** (Recall Knowledge): the cove could survive nine hurricanes and nine earthquakes. The cove and the *Queen* survive the collapse intact.
- **Secret door to G26:** **DC 35 Perception** from this side. It's barred from the other side.
- **Coming back later:** the PCs can return even without the *Queen*. They sail a ship to the tunnel mouth south of the cove (not on the map). Then they clear the collapse with magic or laborers.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (they may find the ship first):** The book allows the PCs to reach the *Queen* through the cove tunnels before they have the Star. The ship won't move until the Star is in its wheel. That gives them a reason to finish the dungeon.

### G28. The Chromatic Queen

> *The richly polished boards of this large sailing ship are all made from an incredible golden-hued hardwood, unmarred by even a single knot. A single thick stripe of crimson paint runs the length of the ship's fat, sturdy hull. In a shining mother-of-pearl white, the words "Chromatic Queen" are emblazoned across the hull's bow just below the forecastle. Staircases, trap doors, and gun ports hint at numerous secrets below deck. The shrouds, masts, and sails are not only seaworthy but pristine. Secured to the ship's bow is a gilded figurehead in the shape of an impossibly tall, divinely handsome g'mayun goddess, her gaze impassive and stalwart. Above her, the bowsprit points true, ready to lead the way to a great new adventure on the high seas.*

Unmooring is easy: untie the knots and scrape off the barnacles. But magic anchors the ship. The wheel stays locked until its key is in place (p. 108–109).
- **The helm:** a star-shaped dent in the wheel's center, where a keystone might fit.
- **DC 15 Arcana or Sailing Lore** (Recall Knowledge): the keystone is powerful, perhaps an artifact. People in the Isles know this kind of magic, but this ward is very strong.
- **The ward:** **counteract DC 35** with *dispel magic*, **6th level** ([counteracting](#counteracting); full rule in [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting)). That's beyond the party for now.
- **Unlocking it:** set the **Obsidian Star in the center of the wheel**.
  - The ward crumbles, the wheel unlocks, and the anchor rises.
  - The PCs **don't need the Star to sail it again**.

**Background special effect:** an **indigo pirate** easily recruits sailors. They're eager to serve Poppy's successor aboard her own ship. Did that PC win the Chapter 1 duel against [Melar Kan](gm_glossary.md#melar-kan)? Then more crew may be ready.

> **🧩 Sets up:** This is the party's Act 2 ship. Chapter 5 covers its first voyage and who commands it ([Synopsis, Act 2](gm_story_synopsis.md#act-2-voyage-to-runaway-reef-chapters-58)).

### Event 13 — Escape from Shivering Mountain

- **Trigger:** the PCs take the Obsidian Star from its pedestal in G26.
- **Goal:** get out before the mountain falls on them.

The mountain shakes hard. The PCs must flee before the sanctum crumbles (p. 109).

**The escape route:** east of the altar.
1. Lift the beam and cross into G27.
2. Board the *Chromatic Queen*.
3. Set the Star in the helm. That raises the anchor and unlocks the wheel.
4. Anyone with a little sailing knowledge can steer south. The tunnel grows brighter and leads out of the still-shaking mountain.

**Once in G27, the PCs are safe.** From the *Queen* they can watch the proving grounds crumble as long as they like.

**Two ways to run it (book options):**
1. **Montage:** describe the voyage from the cove, down the Tentacle River, and back to [Barnacle Bay](gm_glossary.md#barnacle-bay). Skim the details. It makes a dramatic final scene.
2. **Climactic:** don't tell the players the cove is safe. They'll scramble to unmoor the *Queen* and reach open sea.

> **💡 GM tip:** Option 2 works best if the players already know the ship needs the Star. Then the race is to the wheel. Use no checks. Build tension with falling stone, groaning timbers, and the light at the tunnel's end.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (anything left behind):** The book says the sanctum comes down. Anything the PCs didn't take may be lost: the tapestry map, flags, rum cask, and wo'a pillar. Give the players a moment before they lift the Star. Or decide ahead of time what survives.

## Concluding the chapter

With the Star in hand, the PCs can report to [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather). He's most interested in the Star and any sibling gems it may have. The journal's riddles may lead to the other pieces' hiding places. Finding the other three drives the next chapters (p. 109).

**If the PCs never found the journal:** the Star's holder can get its key points as **magical flashbacks**. The book notes this lets you control what they learn and when.

**Level up:** the PCs rest and advance to **level 4**. This is the book's end-of-Act-1 milestone, *Escaping from Shivering Mountain* ([Synopsis, levels](gm_story_synopsis.md#levels-and-milestones)).
- **Short on XP?** The book suggests a **story award** for recovering the Obsidian Star and escaping Shivering Mountain.

**Back in Rumplank:** the PCs can take downtime and tie up loose ends. When they're ready, they sail into the next adventure, *Voyage to Runaway Reef*.

> **💡 GM tip:** [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi) may attend the report. If he does, remember what the PCs hold: the first piece of the cult's relic, and the journal that tracks the rest. Play the royals as delighted. Kulupi has his own reasons.

**Next chapter:** [Chapter 5](gm_chapter_05.md), *Ghosts of Seaview*. The Star and the journal point to the next fragment on [Bluebell Island](gm_glossary.md#bluebell-island).
