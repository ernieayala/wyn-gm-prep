## The chapter in one minute

Poppy's elevator takes the PCs into her proving grounds, built to train successors. The "pirate treasure" was a cover: her journal reveals she sought a successor to fight the [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth), and her real prize, the [Obsidian Star](gm_glossary.md#obsidian-star), is one of four pieces of the [Jewel of the Indigo Isles](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles). The PCs survive the Testing Hall and the Stoneflower Arena, then solve Poppy's last riddle by destroying statues of her friends. Lifting the Star collapses the mountain; they escape on her ship, the [Chromatic Queen](gm_glossary.md#chromatic-queen), whose helm only the Star unlocks.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Poppy's journal** (G9): the truth about the cult, hints to the other three Jewel pieces.
2. **The Obsidian Star** (G26), an item 7 artifact.
3. **The Chromatic Queen** (G28), unlocked with the Star.
4. **Escaped Shivering Mountain** (Event 13).

**Party level:** starts at **3**, ends at **4**. End of Act 1 (Part One, *Search for the Missing Map*).

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** The campaign's turn. The players came for gold and find an empty vault and a mission. The Hall of Heroes says don't worship legends; the journal says the legend was a disguise. The Star and ship launch Act 2.

## How the chapter is built

One dungeon, **Poppy's proving grounds**: areas **G1–G28** plus **Event 13** (p. 86–109; map p. 88). The book says any order works. In practice, rooms gate each other:

| Part | Areas | What it is | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front halls | G1–G4 | Arrival, the broken teleport circle, the Gilded Hall, the Chamber of Decisions | Open |
| Training rooms | G5–G9 | Four rooms granting Poppy's Blessings, plus her secret reading room with the journal and key | Open. G9 is behind a secret door |
| Testing Hall | G10 | Three complex traps at once | Doors lock behind the PCs |
| Student wing | G11–G15 | Well, shrine, strategy room, dorm, stairs | G11 is locked (key in G14) |
| Private wing | G16–G21 | Poppy's balcony, quarters, vault, and two balconies | G16, G17, G18, G20 need Poppy's key |
| Final test | G22–G26 | The arena, the Victory Walk, the Hall of Heroes, the Altar | Reached through G10, or around it through G20 |
| The cove | G27–G28, Event 13 | The *Chromatic Queen* and the escape | Cove tunnels connect back to the complex. G26's door to it is barred from the G26 side |

**Long rests** (the PCs will need a few; p. 87):
- **G12 Shrine of Aoz**, once its two monsters are dead: 8 hours restores twice the normal HP.
- **G14 Dormitory**, once Zakatari (G11) is dead; before that, *nightmare*.
- **G22 Arena Threshold** is a trap: sleepers wake fatigued, and the shale charger comes looking.

> **💡 GM tip — pacing:** Resting after every fight? The book suggests wandering monsters (p. 87). Push, don't punish.

## PF2 rules new in this chapter

Chapter 1 rules aren't repeated. Links: [degrees of success](gm_chapter_1.md#degrees-of-success), [encounter difficulty and XP](gm_chapter_1.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp), [basic saving throws](gm_chapter_1.md#basic-saving-throws), [secret checks and Recall Knowledge](gm_chapter_1.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge), [conditions](gm_chapter_1.md#conditions-youll-see-this-chapter), [movement, falling, and Grab an Edge](gm_chapter_1.md#movement-and-terrain), [hazards](gm_chapter_1.md#hazards), [Monster Parts](gm_chapter_1.md#monster-parts-treasure-system), and [NPC companions](gm_chapter_1.md#npc-companions).

### Bonus types and stacking

Types: **circumstance**, **item**, **status**. Within a type, only the highest applies (penalties too). Different types add.

**This chapter:** Poppy's Blessings (G5–G8), Rally the Crowd (G23), and spells like *bless* and *heroism* are all **status** bonuses. Pillar of Grace (+1 status to Reflex) plus the crowd's +2 status to saves = +2 to Reflex, not +3.

### Elite and weak creatures

See [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#elite-and-weak-creatures). Adjustments: BB 9.

**This chapter:** **elite** G5 training constructs (springjaw dolls, Creature 2); **weak** G12 chamber ooze and ruin bruiser and G23 shale charger. Use the stat-line level for XP.

### Slowed and fatigued

- **Slowed X:** lose X actions at the start of each turn (slowed 1 = 2 actions instead of 3).
- **Fatigued:** see [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#conditions-new-this-chapter).

**This chapter:** slowed 1 hits G12's two monsters (centuries of fighting each other) and any PC in the G23 stands (also frightened 1). Sleeping in G22 leaves a PC fatigued.

### Searching for secret doors

See [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#seek-search-and-secret-doors). Most of this dungeon's secret doors are easy from one side, hard from the other:

| Secret door | Easy side | Hard side |
|---|---|---|
| G3 stage door | Far side: automatic | From the stage: DC 15 Perception while searching |
| G8 ↔ G9 | G9 side: book doesn't say | From G8: pull the "Rum" plank, or Search for hours and make DC 30 Perception |
| Hall south of G7 ↔ G9 | G9 side: no check | From the hall: DC 35 Perception, then pull a loose brick and flick the switch |
| G18 ↔ G19 | G18 side: turn the wheel | From G19: Seek and DC 20 Perception, then press a ceiling button (use the iron rod) |
| G20 ↔ hall west of G10 | G20 side: obvious | From the hall: DC 20 Perception while Searching |
| G26 ↔ G27 | G26 side: plain, but barred | From G27: DC 35 Perception, and it's barred from the other side |

### Locks and checks that need several successes

**Pick a Lock:** 2 actions, Thievery, thieves' tools, trained rank. Each lock quality has a DC and a success count:
- **Poor lock:** DC 15, 2 successes.
- **Average lock:** DC 25, 4 successes.

A success counts once, a critical success twice; a critical failure breaks the tools. Some traps work the same way: each ice and fire vent takes **two** DC 19 Thievery successes (the book: "Two DC 19 Thievery (trained) checks").

**This chapter:** poor locks on G11 and the G10 crystal latches; average locks on G16, G17, G18, G20, and the G10 balcony door. DC 25 is hard for a level-3 rogue, so Poppy's key is the intended route.

### Hardness and breaking objects

See [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#breaking-and-forcing-objects) for Hardness, HP, Broken Threshold, and object immunities.

**This chapter:** Undayo's statue (G25) has **Hardness 19**, so a hit needs at least 20 bludgeoning, unless it's Undayo's warhammer (G2), which ignores it. The G10 electric orb has only Hardness 3 and weakness sonic 5, so the book suggests *shatter*.

### Resting and healing

8 hours restores Constitution modifier (minimum 1) × level HP and removes fatigued; then daily preparations (spells, investing items).

**This chapter:** double at the G12 Shrine of Aoz, until the Obsidian Star leaves its pedestal.

### Counteracting

See [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting).

**This chapter:** the *Chromatic Queen*'s helm ward is **6th level, counteract DC 35** (p. 109). Level-3 and level-4 casters top out at 2nd-level *dispel magic*; even a critical success at counteract level 2 reaches only level 5. The Obsidian Star is the answer.

### Artifacts and invested items

- **Invested** items work only once invested, usually at daily preparations. Maximum **10** at once.
- An **artifact** is beyond normal crafting; normal means can't destroy it.
- An **innate spell** comes from a creature's or item's own magic. "Once per day" = once between daily preparations.

**This chapter:** the [Obsidian Star](#the-obsidian-star-item-7) is an invested artifact that grants an innate occult spell once per day.

### Identify Magic

See [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#identify-magic-and-learn-a-spell).

**This chapter:** the PCs can identify Poppy's Blessings with **DC 20 Religion or Occultism** (G4).

## Arrival — the proving grounds (G1)

### Background (p. 86–87)

> **🔒 GM-only:** Soon after landing on Goldcrop, Poppy and her crew swore to take the secrets of the war and the Jewel to their graves. Decades later she still sensed the cult; since it had followed her to the island, she blamed traitors in her crew. She trained recruits from Rumplank and other Goldcrop settlements, supposedly to inherit the title of Gilded Pirate; in truth, to find a successor who could carry the legend of the War of the Shivering Behemoth and destroy the returning cult. Every pupil died in her lethal tests. In old age she commissioned the magic map and left a journal of clues.

Her crew found these limestone caves when they docked the *Chromatic Queen* in the cove (G27), and over many years she finished them into a "dungeon" like the ruins of her youth: port, vault, crew and student quarters, hideaway, training ground.

**Features:** cut flagstone floors, finished masonry, **10-foot ceilings** unless noted. Unlit torch sconces on nearly every wall.

### Recall Knowledge (secret check)

During the ride down, roll a **secret DC 20 Society or DC 15 Poppy Lore** check for each PC. A **Poppy fanatic** gets an automatic critical success (p. 87).

- **Critical success:** success, plus: beloved Poppy was a legendary trickster with a wicked, sometimes heartless humor, merciless to disciples (whom she reminded were always free to leave), whom she pitted against monsters and harrowing training. Her proving grounds were said to be full of traps, hazards, and creatures.
- **Success:** Poppy's hideout, her "proving grounds," lay in Goldcrop's jungles. There she hoarded supposedly vast wealth, trained aspiring Gilded Pirates, and reportedly meant to leave treasure, title, and perhaps other secrets to the first worthy student. None proved worthy.
- **Failure:** nothing specific.
- **Critical failure:** a confident lie: Poppy's treasure cache can be reached safely only with poppies or poppy seeds; scattering them, one manipulate action, overcomes the few dangers on the way.

> **💡 GM tip:** The lie is a trap for the players: poppies do nothing in the Testing Hall. Let them laugh later.

### G1. Floating Platform

> *In the center of this chamber, a circular, gilded platform floats just inches above the flagstone floor. A long hallway leads to the east, with burnt-out torches in iron sconces on the stonework walls.*

Works like F29: stand on the disc and imagine it rising to return to Shiverskull Cave.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** G1 is a working exit, for supplies or a rest in the caves above.

## The front halls (G2–G4)

### G2. Teleportation Circle

> *A large circular seal is embedded in the floor at the center of this intersection. The seal is made from hundreds of pieces of stone, all artfully fused together around an arcane geometric symbol made of obsidian. The circle has been smashed in multiple spots, significantly marring the design and even reducing it to rubble in parts. The obsidian warhammer lying on the ground next to the smashed circle seems the likely culprit of the vandalism; the purpose of the small disk-shaped medallion lying next to the warhammer is less certain.*

[Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red)'s talismans once let Poppy and her students teleport here from anywhere in the Isles. When her last potential successor died in the Testing Hall, Poppy smashed the circle with her old friend Undayo's warhammer to prevent misuse (p. 87–89).
- **DC 20 Arcana or Occultism** (Recall Knowledge): a teleportation circle for anyone holding a keyed medallion like the one on the floor. Broken for good.

**Treasure:**
- **Undayo's obsidian warhammer:** a ***+1 striking warhammer (4)***, solid obsidian head. **DC 20 Arcana or Nature** (Recall Knowledge): attuned to destroy certain obsidian objects. It **ignores the Hardness** of the G25 obsidian statue.
- **Gilded medallion:** useless; **5 gp** as a historical artifact.

**Background special effect:** a **Poppy fanatic**, or **DC 25 Poppy Lore or DC 30 Society**, recognizes the warhammer on sight, forged from the remains of lava beasts. Under the *Gamemastery Guide* relic rules, the book suggests a relic with earth and fire gifts.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the barbarian's name:** The book prints **Undoyo** (G2 background, all of G25) and **Undayo** (G2 treasure). Chapter 1 and the glossary use [Undayo of Stonegulch](gm_glossary.md#undayo-of-stonegulch). Same person.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Make sure someone carries the warhammer to G25.

### G3. Gilded Hall

> *Large flags adorn the stone walls of this broad hall. Each flag is a different color and adorned with an ornate symbol denoting one of the major islands of the Indigo Isles: a yellow flag with a jolly roger in the shape of a g'mayun's skull for Goldcrop Island, a cerulean flag with a bluebell flower for Bluebell Island, a black flag with a silver crescent moon for Moonshadow Isle, and so forth.*
>
> *Evenly spaced pillars throughout the hall are crafted in the likenesses of legendary figures both fictional and real, heroic and villainous—the Gray-Eyed Fencer, Mirakano the Underhanded, Queen Rosameses, Yrabold Poxfoot, and others. The columns support friezes depicting scenes of flying g'mayuns, towering orpoks, rainbow-hued chochoris, and other gods, all in the midst of grandiose acts: slaying giant horned beetles, snatching sacred gemstones, pouring vessels of infinite water to fill the ocean floor around the Indigo Isles. In the middle of the room, a faded yellow carpet stretches from west to east, terminating at a raised platform accessible by a short staircase. Though the entire hall enraptures the senses, the cloth-of-gold tapestry hanging on the eastern wall over the platform commands the most attention; embroidered on this magnificent work of art is a tremendous bird's-eye map of the entire Indigo Isles archipelago.*

Poppy's ceremonies, meals, inductions, memorials, and speeches, in a hall modeled on wonders from her travels to inspire students to explore (p. 89).

- **The extra flag:** **DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** spots one flag too many: dark red, serrated curved sword. It's [Razorback Isle](gm_glossary.md#razorback-isle), sunk in the war with the cult, but the PCs can name it **only after reading Poppy's journal**.
- **Secret door:** Poppy's "secret" stage entrance (eastern corner, southern wall); most students knew it. **DC 15 Perception** while searching the stage; automatic from the other side.

**Treasure:**
- **The flags:** 100 gp total (20 gp each), 1 Bulk folded. King Okeki would be honored to hang them in Castle Rumplank.
- **The tapestry map:** 50 gp, 1 Bulk.

**Background special effect:** an **indigo trailblazer** who brings back the tapestry map, detailed by Poppy herself, earns extreme notoriety as a trailblazer.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The **Bluebell Island flag** is blue cloth, one answer for Arahosko's statue (G25). The Razorback flag foreshadows [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef), the isle's sunken remains, in Chapter 8.

### G4. Chamber of Decisions

> *Four sturdy wooden doors, each emblazoned with a single word in thick, bold letters, punctuate the southern wall of this circular stone chamber. A corridor to the northeast offers a fifth exit.*

West to east, the red-painted doors read **Strength, Wisdom, Grace, Genius**, opening to training rooms G5–G8 that Poppy built to prepare students for the Testing Hall and Stoneflower Arena. No student ever passed the final test (p. 90).

#### Poppy's Blessings

Divine magic laid on the four training rooms by [Pinomeri Elemarte](gm_glossary.md#pinomeri-elemarte), Poppy's cleric friend.
- **How to get one:** spend at least **1 hour** in the room.
- **Duration:** 24 hours, or until the creature leaves the proving grounds.
- **Limits:** each room's blessing **once ever** per creature, and **only one blessing at a time** (trying for a second while holding one automatically fails). Only Poppy could reapply one.
- **Can't be dispelled**, disabled, or moved.
- **Identifying it:** **DC 20 Religion or Occultism** (Identify Magic). Anyone can feel the rooms' benevolent aura; a blessed PC knows how it works.

| Room | Blessing (+1 status bonus to...) |
|---|---|
| **G5** Dojo of Strength | Strength-based checks and damage rolls: Strength-based melee attack rolls and damage rolls, Athletics |
| **G6** Space of Wisdom | Wisdom-based skill checks, Perception checks, and Wisdom-based spell attack rolls |
| **G7** Pillar of Grace | Dexterity-based checks: Reflex saves, ranged weapon and ranged unarmed attacks, Dexterity-based finesse attacks, Acrobatics, Stealth, Thievery. Also +1 status to AC if the PC isn't at their armor's Dex cap |
| **G8** Library of Genius | Intelligence-based checks and DCs: spell attack rolls, spell DCs, Arcana, Crafting, Lore, Occultism, Society |

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Status bonuses. They don't stack with *bless*, *heroism*, or the G23 crowd bonus. See [bonus types and stacking](#bonus-types-and-stacking).

> **💡 GM tip:** One blessing, 24 hours max: Dexterity suits G10 (Reflex, Thievery), Strength suits G23. Let the players plan.

## The four training rooms (G5–G9)

### G5. Dojo of Strength *(Low 3)*

> *Though the walls of this oddly shaped chamber appear to have once been padded with thick floor-to-ceiling cushions filled with straw, the padding has long since rotted. The straw filling has all spilled to the floor along the room's edges and deteriorated into dark gray mush. The room's southern half is shaped like an oblong octagon. Filling the floor in the center of the octagon is a simple square cloth mat with a series of drawn circles radiating from its center. Worn and decrepit, this combat training hall is mired with dents, scars, and bygone struggles.*

Students sparred with each other (sometimes fatally) and with constructs that only knocked them out. Poppy took birthday challengers and never lost (p. 90). A south door leads to G6.

**Training Constructs (3)** — Creature 2, elite springjaw dolls (BB 9, 108). Initiative: Perception +7. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp.
- Wooden-dummy look, **only nonlethal** damage, no Wind-up Scream or gnaw.
- **Trigger:** they step out of the walls when a creature steps onto the square mat.
- They stay in the room and return to their alcoves once every creature in it is unconscious.
- **Below half HP:** lights up, plays a victory fanfare, and retreats to the wall for repair.
- **Attacked while retreating** (say, for Monster Parts), it fights back.

**Blessing:** Strength ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the threat label:** Three level-2 creatures = 30 + 30 + 30 = 90 XP, past Moderate on paper. The book says **Low 3** (nonlethal, quits at half HP), and its label wins.

> **💡 GM tip:** Describe the first fanfare. The players learn it's sparring and can weigh parts against a real fight.

### G6. Space of Wisdom

> *A five-foot-wide archway bisects this area into two halves: a ten-foot-wide hallway in the northern half and a fifteen-foot-wide area to the south with an oval sandpit set into the floor. The flagstone floor of the hallway bears countless scuffs and scratches from boots or talons that have treaded the space in an oval pattern; a sturdy boar-bristle broom with a five-foot-long iron handle leans against the northeastern corner of this hall. The glittering, gold-colored sand in the pit to the south has been combed into a remarkable pattern, likely using the rake leaning against the southeast corner, which has the same sturdy five-foot-long iron handle as the broom.*

Walking meditation in the hallway (many scuffs are Poppy's talons) and silent meditation at the sandpit (p. 91).
- **The sand:** looks like gold or topaz. **DC 15 Crafting or DC 13 Mining Lore**: beautiful ordinary beach sand. **DC 20 Nature or DC 15 Indigo Isles Geography Lore**: from Goldcrop's northern shores.
- **Useful tools:** the broom's and rake's **iron handles** can wedge the G10 crushing pit (Trap A).

**Blessing:** Wisdom ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — a room-number error in the book:** The Testing Hall text puts the iron rods in "the Space of Wisdom (area G7)." It means **G6**; G7 is the Pillar of Grace.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Nudge the players to take the broom and rake; without an iron bar, the G10 pit is far deadlier.

### G7. Pillar of Grace

> *The ceiling of this circular chamber soars nearly forty feet above the floor. Embedded into the center of the stonework floor is a wooden pillar the diameter of a ship's mast. The pillar, itself nearly thirty feet long, stretches toward the ceiling. Though it bears more than a few gouges, scrapes, and scars, the wooden pillar is still a beautiful thing to behold; it's made of striking yellow and tan hardwood, and it shines as though it were freshly lacquered.*

Trains agility, balance, and climbing (p. 91–92).
- **Climbing the pillar:** **DC 35 Athletics** to Climb. Magic like *fly* would help, but the book says it's likely beyond the party at this level.
- **Balancing on top:** no check if the PC stands still on both feet.
- **Treasure (without Monster Parts only):** a marble-sized **diamond worth 100 gp** on the pillar's flat top.
- **Secret door:** the hallway south of here seems to dead-end at its east end. Searching toward the wall with **DC 35 Perception** finds a loose upper-right brick; pull it and flick the switch behind to open the way to **G9**.

**Blessing:** Dexterity ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

#### Defense special resource: wo'a wood

A mast Poppy took from a destroyed Cult of the Shivering Behemoth warship, cut from the heart of a **wo'a**, a rare Goldcrop tree with strong gold-colored hardwood.
- **DC 25 Nature or DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** (Recall Knowledge) identifies the wood.
- Far too large and heavy to remove casually; with preparation, a valuable crafting material.
- **Retrieved**, it defends Rumplank (p. 315): in Chapter 12 it upgrades a **Wall** (Tier 1, Guard 25) to a **Fortress Wall** (Tier 2, Guard 40, anti-burrow), or trades for **10 Resource Points**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — getting it out:** The book never says how to remove it or whether Event 13 buries it. Decide before the session. One judgment call: a later return by sea with laborers, as the book allows for the cove (G27).

> **🧩 Sets up:** Chapter 12's defense of Rumplank. Note it if taken.

### G8. Library of Genius *(Moderate 3)*

> *Dozens of bookshelves made of wooden planks line the walls of this library. The planks and books are in varying states of decay, with a heavy layer of mold blanketing much of the place. On each plank-shelf is carved a word or two indicating the subject of the books, pamphlets, and volumes lined up on that plank. The subjects are all classic pirate interests: broad categories such as treasure, sailing, sea monsters; specific interests like gold, rum, cannons; people and places like g'mayuns, chochori, the Indigo Isles; and so forth. Only one section of wall isn't covered floor to ceiling in bookshelves; hung on the stone wall in this gap is a conspicuously framed piece of parchment paper.*

The mind: books from across the Isles, a few by Poppy (p. 92–94).

#### Hazard — Toxic Mildew *(Hazard 1, simple)*

Pulling books or bumping the crumbling shelves risks spores.
- **Stealth DC 20 (expert).**
- **Disable:** **DC 17 Survival (trained) or Thievery (trained)** to wipe the mildew off one 5-foot section of shelving.
- **AC** 17; **Fort** +10, **Ref** +4. **Bookshelf** HP 20 (BT 10); object immunities; weakness fire 5.
- **Waft Spores (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature interacts with any book or bookshelf, or a creature adjacent to a bookshelf Strikes or is hit by a Strike. **Effect:** a 5-foot emanation centered on the triggering creature, **2d4 poison**, **DC 17 basic Fortitude**.
- **Reset:** each 5-foot section triggers once; the mold takes months to regrow.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Strikes beside a shelf trigger it too. Track which sections have fired.

#### Wave 1

**Scroll Molds (4)** — Creature 0, BB 86. Initiative: Perception +6. Monster Parts 2.25/5/9 gp each.

**Living Proof** — Creature 1, BB 58. Initiative: Perception +7. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp. A narcissist the molds treat as their king.

- The living proof orders the molds to attack anyone who enters; they stay in the room.
- **Scroll mold companion:** if the Chapter 1 [scroll mold](gm_glossary.md#scroll-mold) came along, once the living proof falls it can talk the other molds into surrendering, if the PCs spare them.

#### Wave 2

Once wave 1 falls, an animated pile of feather dusters, bookmarks, ink pots, and quills rises: Poppy's dead bookish disciples, bent on destroying anyone promising enough to have impressed her. It fights until destroyed.

**Liminal Librarian** — Creature 4, dishrag dervish (BB 101). Initiative: Perception +10. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.

> **📏 Rules refresher — the threat math:** Wave 1 (four level-0 molds at 10 XP, a level-1 living proof at 20 XP) = 60, Low. Wave 2 (one level-4, 60 XP) is also Low. Back to back, 120 XP is Severe on paper; the book's **Moderate 3** wins. Expect a worn-down party for the librarian. See [encounter math](gm_chapter_1.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp).

#### After the fights

With both waves down and each section's mildew disabled, the PCs can search safely.
- **Treasure:** hauling out the whole library is a days-long job beyond this adventure. **DC 18 Library Lore or Rumplank Lore** picks the most valuable tomes: **20 gp**, 2 Bulk total.
- **Research:** long biographies of Poppy's companions, a backup for G25.

#### Poppy's Puzzle

The framed parchment, the only hint, slips from its frame: blank back, a poem in Poppy's pirate scrawl on the front. When a PC picks it up or examines it, hand out **Handout 2: Poppy's Poem**:

> *Only the most special venue*
> *would ever do*
> *to unveil my past, that bloody shawl*
> *a place where everyone knows my face—*
> *my home of homes,*
> *my final port of call.*

**The answer is Rumplank**, as a pun. Books on Rumplank (History, Geography, and so on) are red herrings; pull the plank labeled **Rum** (liquor and ale). A sliding door in the **southern wall** opens to G9.
- **Without solving it:** a few hours of Searching and **DC 30 Perception**. No student managed it.

**Blessing:** Intelligence ([table](#poppys-blessings)).

> **💡 GM tip:** Stuck players? Ruff (G17) can show Poppy pulling a shelf here. A tired table may not want a long riddle after two waves.

### G9. Poppy's Reading Room

> *A low table and an accompanying wooden chair are the only pieces of furniture in this cozy nook. Stacked on the floor next to the chair is a pile of books, all heavy with rot. Lying on the center of the table is a single leatherbound journal that's somehow been left untouched by the passage of time.*

Poppy's late-night retreat, out of students' sight. A few, like Akirin (G14), suspected a secret room in the library (p. 94–95).
- **Secret door, west wall:** obvious from here. From the hall south of G7, DC 35 Perception (see G7).

#### Poppy's Journal

The dungeon's most valuable treasure: dozens of entries from Poppy's final years, days of reading. The last few reveal:
- She trained her best pupils here, seeking a successor worthy of her title and of the tragic secret behind her wealth: a world-threatening war with a cabal of necromancers, the **Cult of the Shivering Behemoth**.
- A magic pendant hidden in the dungeon, the **Obsidian Star**, is her grandest treasure and one of four parts of a powerful relic, the **Jewel of the Indigo Isles**.
- The other pieces, scattered across the Isles, should be reclaimed and rejoined *"when the time is right."* Never when or why; its cryptic allusions would take days or weeks in Rumplank's libraries to decipher.

**Poppy's Key:** in a cut-out in the journal's bookplate. Opens **G16, G17, G18, and G20**, and the G10 balcony door.

**Background special effect:** it fascinates an **indigo historian** and distracts a **Poppy fanatic**. Overturning long-held beliefs about Poppy and the Isles earns the historian induction into the **Indigo Isles Historical Society** as an influential new member.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — two versions of the journal:** G9 says "when the time is right" and "days, if not weeks." The p. 109 sidebar says "when the time and place are right" and "weeks, if not months," and adds that the pieces seem attracted to each other and that the journal holds maps and rough sketches of their locations. Use p. 109 for the Chapter 5 hand-off; the difference is cosmetic.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The twist starts here: Poppy was hiding a war. Let the players read the "cabal of necromancers" line themselves, then move on; the Star is still in the dungeon.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The journal launches the Act 2 search ([Synopsis, Act 2](gm_story_synopsis.md#act-2-voyage-to-runaway-reef-chapters-58)). If the PCs never find it, the Star replays its key points as visions ([Concluding the chapter](#concluding-the-chapter)).

## The Testing Hall (G10)

### G10. Testing Hall *(Severe 3)*

- **Where:** a 15-foot-wide stone double door from the north hallway.
- **Goal:** survive and disable three traps, or leave the room empty of living creatures for a minute.

Carved **"TESTING HALL"** and, below, **"NO TURNING BACK."** Handholds swing the doors outward. When a character enters, ticking echoes; **two rounds (12 seconds) later** the doors swing shut, knocking anyone in the doorway inside, and every trap activates (p. 95).

> *The floor, thirty-foot-high ceiling, and walls of this wide corridor are all made of flat, solid stone and painted in faded but still quite stark colors. The northern third of the room is painted yellow, the middle third is painted red, and the final third is painted bright blue. The yellow floor is made of intricate ceramic tilework that invites closer inspection. The red walls are riddled with evenly spaced holes. From the blue ceiling hangs a metal rod; around the tip of the rod is suspended a cannonball-sized glass orb. The southern end of the hall sports a ten-foot-tall, stone-railed viewing platform with a closed door in an alcove along the southern wall. Just west of the viewing platform is another closed door.*

**Start encounter mode when anyone enters**; the initiative call is the warning. An invisible sensor detects the slightest movement. The doors lock with a "ka-chunk," and the room erupts in grinding gears, fire, frost, and lightning.

**Doors:**
- **North double door and SW-corner door:** held by iron rods; **can't be picked or Forced Open**. They unlock when every trap is disabled, or 1 minute after the sensor detects no living creatures.
- **Balcony door (south wall, to G20):** average lock, **four DC 25 Thievery** successes, or Poppy's key (G9).

**The traps stay active** until disabled or no living creatures are detected; dodging doesn't end the encounter.

> **📏 Rules refresher — the threat math:** Three level-3 complex hazards, 40 XP each = 120 = **Severe**. Each rolls its own initiative. See [hazards](gm_chapter_1.md#hazards).

#### Trap A — Crushing Pit *(Hazard 3, complex mechanical trap)*

A spiked pit under the yellow tiles; then its walls close in (p. 96).
- **Stealth** +13 (expert) to notice the tilework is a trap door.
- **Description:** a 25-foot-wide by 20-foot-long section of floor drops into a 20-foot-deep spiked pit of old bones and gear. Its west and east walls push toward the center.
- **Disable:** **DC 18 Athletics or Survival** to wedge a sturdy iron bar, or similar with **Hardness 10 or more**, between the walls. The G6 broom or rake works; a weapon works but is likely lost.
- **AC** 17; **Fort** +10, **Ref** +10. **Trap door** Hardness 6, HP 28 (BT 14). **Wall** Hardness 10, HP 50 (BT 25). Object immunities.
- **The Test Begins (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature enters and stays in the Testing Hall for at least 2 rounds. **Effect:** the doors slam and lock and the trap door swings open. Creatures on it fall 20 feet onto spikes (**DC 23 Reflex** to Grab an Edge), taking **10 bludgeoning** from the fall and **2d6 piercing** from the spikes (**DC 20 basic Reflex** against the spikes). The trap rolls initiative.
- **Routine (1 action):** the walls move **2½ feet per round** toward each other. After **5 rounds**, any creature still in the pit takes **20d6 bludgeoning** (**DC 25 basic Reflex**), and the walls slide back in one round. A creature on top of a wall is treated as in The Test Begins and may fall in (it can Grab an Edge). Repeats until disabled or reset.
- **Reset:** 1 minute after the sensor detects no creatures in the hall.
- **The keys:** Seeking at the pit's bottom with **DC 20 Perception** finds two keys on an iron ring among the spikes. They open Trap B's crystal latches.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — 20d6:** Averages 70, enough to drop a level-3 PC who fails. Count the five rounds out loud so the players feel the walls coming.

#### Trap B — Ice and Fire Trap *(Hazard 3, complex mechanical trap)*

Eight vents on the west and east walls shoot frost and flame (p. 96–97).
- **Stealth** +10 (expert) to spot the western dial and the eastern slot. Noticing the vents is DC 0.
- **Description:** one wall shoots frost while the other shoots flame. They swap every round.
- **The dial:** mid-west wall, near the floor; flicks between "I" and "II" at the start of the trap's turn. **Interacting** with it each round holds it and stops next round's swap. Breaking it sticks it permanently.
- **The crystal:** behind a 4-inch-tall, 12-inch-wide slot mid-east wall, near the floor, six inches back, held by two latches with **poor locks** (the pit keys open them). Removing or breaking it shuts down the whole trap.
- **Disable:** **two DC 19 Thievery (trained)** per vent; **three DC 21 Thievery (expert)** to lock the dial; or **four DC 15 Thievery (trained)** to Pick the latch Locks and pull the crystal, disabling the entire trap.
- **AC** 19; **Fort** +11, **Ref** +9. **Vent** Hardness 5, HP 20 (BT 10). **Dial** Hardness 10, HP 30 (BT 15). **Crystal** Hardness 12, HP 40 (BT 20). Object immunities.
- **The Test Begins (reaction).** Same trigger. **Effect:** the doors slam and lock. Frost blasts from the west wall and flame from the east: **2d6 cold or 2d6 fire** to creatures in a 10-foot line. The trap rolls initiative.
- **Routine (8 actions; cold, fire):** each vent (a 5-foot wall section) spends 1 action on a 10-foot line toward the room's middle: **2d6 cold or fire**, **DC 22 basic Reflex**. All vents on a wall shoot the same element each round.
- **Protection works:** magic, items, and abilities that protect against harsh environments can block the jets. Immunity to damage from **severe cold** or **severe heat** negates this trap's cold or fire damage, respectively.
- **Reset:** same as Trap A.

**Safe spaces:** a few squares between the jets mid-hall, none holding more than three Medium creatures (map, p. 96).

> **💡 GM tip:** The G13 elixirs (lesser winter wolf, lesser salamander) make this trap far easier, per the book. Skipped G13? The pit's keys are next best.

#### Trap C — Electric Orb Trap *(Hazard 3, complex mechanical trap)*

The simplest, built to trip up overthinkers: shatter the glass orb (p. 97).
- **Stealth** +11 (expert) to see the edges of the clear glass orb.
- **Description:** a 1-foot glass sphere on a 3-foot rod from the 30-foot ceiling, its bottom 25 feet up. Area: **10-foot emanation** centered on the floor below.
- **Disable:** **two DC 23 Thievery (trained)** to remove the orb, or **one DC 23 Athletics** to Force Open and shatter it. Either way, the PC must reach it.
- **Or break it at range:** arrows, bolts, bullets, or *shatter* (scroll in the G11 well).
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +6, **Ref** +14. Hardness 3, HP 40 (BT 20). Object immunities. **Weakness sonic 5.**
- **The Test Begins (reaction).** Same trigger. **Effect:** doors slam and lock; the orb discharges stored energy: **1d12 electricity** to each creature in the emanation (**DC 23 basic Reflex**). The trap rolls initiative.
- **Routine (1 action):** **1d12 electricity** in the emanation, **DC 23 Reflex**:
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage.
  - **Failure:** full damage and flat-footed for 1 round.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage, flat-footed for 1 round, and knocked prone.
- **Reset:** same as Trap A.

#### Aftermath

When all three traps are disabled, or 1 minute after the sensor detects no living creatures, the north and southwest doors unlock with a loud sound (p. 98).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the bypass:** A secret door in the hallway **west** of the Testing Hall (DC 20 Perception while Searching) leads up to G20 and on to G22, skipping G10. The book allows it; don't block it if they find it.

> **🎯 Why this room exists:** An exam on the earlier rooms: rods (G6), elixirs (G13), scroll (G11), keys (the pit). Explorers walk through; rushers learn why Poppy's students died.

## The student wing (G11–G15)

### G11. Abandoned Well *(Trivial 3)*

The hallway doors have **poor locks**. The key is on the dormitory floor across the hall (G14).

> *A low stone well is the only feature of note in this unworked cavern. A steel bucket tied to a rope lies discarded in a far corner.*

Students' clean water, until a tragedy (p. 98).

**Zakatari** — Creature 3, parasite husk (BB 71). Initiative: Perception +7. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp.
- A promising pupil who loosed a box of nasty magic worms to gum up a Testing Hall trap. They ate him, and he became an undying monster.
- Poppy and the students lured him here and bricked him into a corner; his moans warned other would-be cheaters.
- Years after Poppy's death he broke out of the bricks but never beat the flimsy lock. He lives on fetid water and wall fungus, and attacks on sight.

**Treasure:** at the bottom of the **30-foot-deep** well, under murky water, a ***scroll of shatter*** in a steel case.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The scroll breaks the G10 orb. Killing Zakatari makes G14 safe to sleep in.

### G12. Shrine of Aoz *(Severe 3)*

> *Wide, stone double doors are the only exit from this broad, cross-shaped chamber. The chamber appears to have once been a place of peaceful refuge or sanctuary, but nearly everything inside has been ruined or eroded. The red velvet carpet on the floor has been worn down to tatters, as have the now-indiscernible banners on the walls and shattered pews reduced to mere splinters on the floor. Even the flagstone tiles on the floor and the brickwork on the walls are heavily chipped, gouged, and, in some spots, nearly obliterated. In the southern alcove, a statue of a tall, masked figure stares down toward the floor, ominously.*

Many students followed [the Balance](gm_glossary.md#the-balance), though Poppy didn't. Pinomeri oversaw this shrine to [Aoz](gm_glossary.md#aoz), god of life, for honoring the Parents (p. 98–99).

**The standoff:** Poppy's ruin bruiser, built to guard against mountain monsters, has fought a chamber ooze for centuries; the shrine heals the ooze, and its acid can't harm stone, so neither can win. The PCs are new targets.

**Weak Chamber Ooze** — Creature 4 (BB 9, 16). Initiative: Perception +7 (slowed 1). Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- **Regeneration 2** while it stays in this room.

**Weak Ruin Bruiser** — Creature 5 (BB 9, 78). Initiative: Perception +13 (slowed 1). Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- Its stone plates carry Poppy's orders in **Terran** (or **DC 19 Crafting** for meaning and maker): *"In the event of my death, protect this sanctum. —Poppy"* She expected her successor to beat it.

**How the fight runs:**
- Both **weak** and **slowed 1** ([slowed](#slowed-and-fatigued)): 2 actions each.
- They **alternate** attacks between each other and the party, until the PCs prove the greater threat by excelling.
- The book prices it at **120 XP, Severe**, down from 140 XP (60 + 80) for a level-4 and a level-5, because of these handicaps.

**Healing magic:** drawn from the Obsidian Star (G26). An **8-hour rest here restores twice the normal HP** ([resting](#resting-and-healing)) until the Star leaves its pedestal.

> **💡 GM tip:** "Excelling" is your call. Say, once a PC lands a critical hit or drops one monster below half HP, the survivor turns on the party.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Taking the Star ends the double healing. Rest here **before** the final test.

### G13. Strategy Room

> *A broad wooden table fills the center of this square chamber, a rough map of the Indigo Isles sprawled atop it, its corners pinned to the table's surface. The periphery of the stone chamber is lined with crates, barrels, chests, and other storage containers. Hundreds of candles of various shapes, sizes, and colors fill the open crates. The other containers are half-filled with building supplies and nautical tools such as hempen rope, iron nails, saws, and hammers, though one small box contains a few wineskins, some still half-full. A wooden door in the western wall exits the room.*

Planning and storage: thousands of candles for students, mostly g'mayuns unused to dark tunnels, excavation tools, spare *Chromatic Queen* parts (p. 99).

**Treasure:**
- At your discretion, any **common items worth 5 sp or less** from *Core Rulebook* Chapter 6.
- A blue and a red vial: **lesser winter wolf elixir** and **lesser salamander elixir**. Useful anywhere; the answer to G10's ice and fire trap.
- **Wineskins**, some half-full: alcohol for Pinomeri's statue (G25).

### G14. Dormitory

> *Dusty old bunks line the walls of these adjoining dormitories. The hay-filled mattresses are slightly sunken, though the wooden bed frames appear to be in fine shape. Tally marks in faded chalk have been scratched upon a few sections of the walls.*

Only Poppy had privacy. Students could sleep in the jungle or on the *Chromatic Queen*; many used these bunks and tallied their days on the walls (p. 100–101).
- **G11 key:** on the floor; its worn tip scratched the tallies.
- **Treasure:** **two lesser healing potions** in a gap in one bunk's boards.

**Akirin's Diary:** under a rotten pillow. Give the players **Handout 3: Akirin's Diary** (p. 100). Entries from Days 14, 22, and "25?" show:
- Akirin was proud to train under the Gilded Pirate and sure he'd succeed her; some peers had already died.
- Poppy vanished after he followed her into the library, so he suspected secret passages.
- Zakatari cheated, became "that... thing," and Poppy had the students brick him in. His moaning kept everyone awake.
- His last night, Akirin gave Poppy his pendant; she threw it in her hearth, *"just like she'd done with so many before me,"* and they drank rum from her cask. Next day he and his friends were to face the Testing Hall and the Stoneflower Arena.

**Haunted room:** safe beds, but sleepers **before** Zakatari is destroyed face ***nightmare* (DC 23)**: a stone well bursting with worms. Once Zakatari is dead, the room is clean.

**Background special effect:** a **Poppy fanatic** learns they descend from one of Poppy's disciples, unknown to their family (use a handout name or add an entry). Her harsh trials likely killed that ancestor, and the PC is about to finish what the ancestor couldn't.

> **💡 GM tip:** The diary is the chapter's emotional center. Akirin, or the fanatic's ancestor, is on G24's chalk list; if the players read the diary first, that list hits hard.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The pendant in the fire ties to G17's hearth, Ruff's home now.

## Poppy's private wing (G16–G21)

### G16. Poppy's Balcony

> *This massive stone balcony overlooks an underground sea cove to the south. Droplets of condensation gather and fall from the tips of stalactites dangling from the tall, cavernous ceiling above. Below, luminescent sea creatures in hazy neon shades of cyan blue, lime green, and poppy pink flit beneath the surface of the black water. ### G17. Poppy's Quarters

> *These lavish living quarters are outfitted in a variety of finery in varying states of decay. Moth-eaten tapestries embroidered with various crests and flags line the walls. A fine bedframe of dark wood supports a hay-filled mattress covered in white linens. Along the eastern wall, a low fire crackles in a stone hearth. Though the fire emits heat, and stray embers sizzle as they pop from the hearth, the flames don't emit any smoke.*

Poppy thought comfort softened new pirates, but felt a war hero had earned some late-life luxury (p. 101–102).

**Ruff** — Creature 4, hearth hound (BB 40). Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- Poppy's beloved hunting companion; he died of old age here, and his spirit filled the hearth, faithful until she died too.
- **Not hostile.** Thrilled by company, sure anyone this far is his master's friend, he leaps from the fire and circles the PCs, barking. Defends himself if attacked.
- Bound to this room.
- **Befriended, with Monster Parts:** gives magic hearth embers worth his Monster Parts value.

**What Ruff knows:** Poppy's name and commands (sit, fetch, roll over). He shapes his flames into simple scenes.
- **DC 18 Nature** to communicate: he shows Poppy's favorite haunt, **the library**: Poppy pulling out a shelf to reveal a secret door, then writing in a journal at a low table. The clue to G8–G9.

> **💡 GM tip:** The softest moment in a hard dungeon. Let the players play fetch; killing him gains nothing friendship doesn't.

> *Clods of dusty cobwebs are the only treasures that line the stone shelves of this structurally impressive vault. Legends promised riches galore; Poppy invested nearly all of it in Rumplank before she died. Dust outlines mark the bare shelves (p. 102–103).
- **Spare key:** on a peg by the door; opens **G16, G17, G18, and G20**, like the G9 key.
- **Treasure:** a large cask of ancient, still-drinkable rum, **30 gp** if carted out; it also solves Pinomeri's statue (G25).
- **Secret door (south wall):** plain from here. A chunky wheel rotates the wall on a central vertical bar, opening to G19.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The empty vault is the point. Play the letdown straight; the real treasure is the journal's mission.

### G19. Western Balcony

> *Short, wide steps carved into the rock itself lead up a tunnel to this sparsely appointed chamber, the southeastern portion of which is a railed balcony that overlooks an awesome underground cove. In the northern alcove of the room, a slender rod of rusty iron about one foot long lies discarded on the floor.*

A student balcony. The northern alcove's false wall tested for cunning; no student found it, and only Poppy used the iron rod on its ceiling switch (p. 103).
- **Secret door to G18:** an old minor earthquake cracked the false wall. Seeking in the alcove with **DC 20 Perception** spots a vertical iron bar through the crack.
- **The trigger:** a button in a nearly invisible 1-foot-deep hole in the ceiling near the wall. Pressing it rotates the wall 90 degrees on the bar, opening the vault.
- **Backup route:** missed the G9 key? Get in here and take G18's spare.

### G20. Unfinished Room *(Moderate 3)*

Average locks (four DC 25 Thievery, or Poppy's key). An empty antechamber, purpose never decided, between Poppy's quarters and the viewing platforms over the Testing Hall and arena (p. 103).

**Chamber Ooze** — Creature 5, BB 16. Initiative: Stealth DC 28. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.

**Secret door (staircase, NW corner):** obvious from here; **DC 20 Perception** while Searching from the hallway west of the Testing Hall. **This is how a party skips G10** and reaches G22.

## The final test (G22–G26)

### G22. Arena Threshold

> *Bloodstains coat much of the roughly hewn, uneven stone floor in this hall. Deep scrapes, gouges, and gashes mar the walls as if from thick, sharp claws. Here and there, bits of shiny bug carapace sparkle in the hall's cobwebbed corners. A faint, hot breeze emanates from the open archway to the southwest.*

Testing Hall survivors' last chance to prepare; Poppy kept the floor rough so they couldn't rest long (p. 103–104).
- **No sleeping:** sleepers wake **fatigued** ([fatigued](#slowed-and-fatigued)), and before anyone gets a full night's sleep the shale charger comes to see what's keeping its prey, hence the blood.
- **Treasure:** in the anteroom's northwest corner, a ***+1 striking morningstar (4)*** that didn't save one of the charger's last victims.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book doesn't say how a charger fight here runs. Judgment call: run it as G23 without the crowd and portcullis.

### G23. Stoneflower Arena *(Severe 3)*

> *A thin layer of gritty sand covers the stone floor of this massive oval fighting arena. The cave around the fighting pit is empty except for ghostly figures of spectators—each shrouded in an ethereal golden cloak obscuring their faces—who stand in silence and stare down from the stone benches. Flags of Rumplank hang from the rails of a rounded balcony that looms twenty-five feet over the eastern portion of the stadium seating. Wide, steep stairs descend fifteen feet from the portcullis in the northern wall to the fighting pit below. The ceiling is unfinished natural stone, sixty feet above the floor of the fighting pit. To the south, another portcullis, this one flush with the floor of the fighting pit, separates the arena from a stone hall that stretches east.*

When **every creature from G22 is on the sand**, the north portcullis slams down and the crystalline creature in the center wakes from centuries of magical slumber, screeching (p. 104).

**Weak Shale Charger** — Creature 5 (BB 9, 94). Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- Poppy killed many in the war with the cult and thought her successor must know this enemy. She caught this one in the Shiverskull Caves and put it in stasis like Dugari's (F15).
- **In stasis:** can't act, immune to all effects.
- Raging at its captivity, it attacks whoever frees it, hungry to hurt anyone resembling its pirate captors.

#### Rally the Crowd

The ghostly spectators love showmanship.

**Rally the Crowd** ◆ (concentrate), once per round: a grand gesture, boast, or display of skill, **DC 18 Acrobatics, Athletics, Diplomacy, or Intimidation**.
- **Critical success:** the crowd goes wild. The PC and each ally in the arena gain a **+2 status bonus** to attack rolls, saving throws, and skill checks for 1 round.
- **Success:** as above, but +1.
- **Failure:** the crowd watches stoically.
- **Critical hit:** a PC's critical hit on the shale charger counts as an automatic success, no action spent.

**Entering the stands:** a chill; the PC is **slowed 1** and **frightened 1** there. Frightened can't drop while there; slowed ends on leaving.

**Aftermath:** the charger's death opens both portcullises. The arena, Testing Hall, G24, and G25 stay open without repeating either trial.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the creature's name:** The G23 text says "a monstrous shale spitter called a shale charger." Use **shale charger**, as the stat line and the rest of the book do.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the threat label:** One level-5 creature against a level-3 party is 80 XP, Moderate on paper. The book's **Severe 3** wins: the PCs are locked in, the stands punish retreat, and they may arrive hurt from G10.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Rally the Crowd is a status bonus; it doesn't stack with a Poppy's Blessing on the same roll. See [bonus types and stacking](#bonus-types-and-stacking).

> **💡 GM tip:** Show the ghost crowd reacting to every big hit; players will try Rally the Crowd unprompted.

### G24. Victory Walk

> *Large, gilded metal frames line the walls of this curving hall. The frames are the right size and shape for portraits, though none bears so much as a single sketch. At the northern end of the corridor, on a bare stone wall opposite a sturdy iron door, is a list of what appear to be names scrawled in chalk. The names become less legible the farther down the list one goes.*

Poppy meant the frames for portraits of students who beat the arena; none did. Instead, one section of wall lists those who died in the Testing Hall or arena. Stoic for her pupils, Poppy grieved anyway: her handwriting worsens down the list (p. 104–105).

**Background special effect:** the **Poppy fanatic** PC's ancestor from G14 is on the list.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** A breath before the puzzle, and the players' first look at Poppy's guilt. Read the read-aloud slowly.

### G25. Hall of Heroes

- **Goal:** destroy four statues of Poppy's friends, assemble the sword hidden inside them, and plunge it into Poppy's statue.

> *Gilded filigreed friezes line this lavish chamber, the walls of which sport murals painted in shocking reds, blues, and yellows that have refused to fade with time. Four statues of different heroes stand in the chamber's corners, all facing the center of the room. Each statue is made of a different material—obsidian, bloodstone, jade, and moonstone—and each bears a plaque with an inscription at its foot.*
>
> *At the head of this lavish chamber is a statue of Poppy von Barnacle herself, cast entirely in gold. In the center of Poppy's statue, directly in the middle of her chest between her ribs, is a peculiar vertical slot no more than an inch wide, a few inches from top to bottom, and of indiscernible depth. At the base of the gilded statue is a gilded plaque engraved with these words: "Lay Low Your Heroes." The expression on each statue's face is a dour, serious one, and the poses are similarly humble, giving little clue as to what the represented heroes might be thinking.*

PCs who don't know about the Star may think this room is the reward. It's Poppy's last challenge, a puzzle with no fight (p. 105–106).

**The final lesson:** Poppy saw her war role as grim duty and wanted an equally humble successor, serving over seeking glory. The riddle is easy; destroying statues of Rumplank's heroes is hard. Under the cocky front, she'd have found a gold idol of herself distasteful.

#### The four statues

Each statue holds one piece of a ceremonial replica of Poppy's legendary sword, the **Gilded Blade**.

| Hero | Statue | Plaque | How to break it | U'oko's Ch 1 fact | Piece inside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Jesea Swiftarrow](gm_glossary.md#jesea-swiftarrow) | Jade | "X Marks the Spot" | Hit the **X-shaped scar on her jaw** with a projectile, such as an arrow or bolt. The pose hides the scar; it's visible from one angle only, the opposite corner, 35 feet away. The statue shatters | X-shaped scar below her left ear | Jade pommel |
| [Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red) | Bloodstone | "Never Not Red" | **Drape it fully in blue cloth**, then pull the cloth away; the statue is gone. The G3 Bluebell flag or any blue cloak or blanket works | The only way to beat her was to dress her in blue | Bloodstone guard, joining hilt to blade |
| [Pinomeri Elemarte](gm_glossary.md#pinomeri-elemarte) | Moonstone | "Always Thirsty" | **Douse it in alcohol**, enough to coat the whole statue. It melts into goo. The G18 rum cask, G13 wineskins, or any jug of ale works | He loved rum, wine, and ale equally | Moonstone pommel, in the residue |
| [Undayo of Stonegulch](gm_glossary.md#undayo-of-stonegulch) | Obsidian | "Only Strength Will Do" | **One melee Strike dealing at least 20 bludgeoning damage.** Hardness 19; immune to piercing, slashing, and critical hits. It cracks in half. **Undayo's warhammer (G2) breaks it in one hit** by ignoring the Hardness | You had to beat him one-on-one | A curved blade of sharpened obsidian |

> **⚠️ Gotcha — two pommels:** The book lists a **jade pommel** and a **moonstone pommel** plus a guard and blade, but a sword needs one pommel and a grip. The book doesn't fix this. Call one the grip; keep both materials so the sword mixes all four statues.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Undayo's statue at level 3:** 20 bludgeoning in one Strike is a lot at level 3; the warhammer is the intended answer. Left it in G2? They walk back.

> **💡 GM tip — rewarding creativity:** Per the book, a solution the text doesn't cover works if the whole table finds it brilliant.

> **💡 GM tip — no notes from Ch 1?** The G8 library and G9 journal biographies hold the four facts.

#### Assembling the blade

The pieces slot together easily into a sturdy sword; only the mismatched materials show it was ever four.

#### Laying Poppy low

The Gilded Blade fits the slot in Poppy's chest and locks with a satisfying "ka-chunk." Ancient gears turn; the statue and much of the wall behind it sink into a hidden floor cavity, opening the way to the **Altar of the Obsidian Star**.

**Treasure:** afterward the PCs can pull out the replica Gilded Blade, a ***+1 striking scimitar (4)***.

**Background special effect:** may be hard on a **Poppy fanatic**: honoring Poppy's legacy means dropping blind devotion and seeing past heroes realistically.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It pays off U'oko's Chapter 1 trivia and states the chapter's thesis: the players destroy the legend by hand, right before learning what it hid.

### G26. Altar of the Obsidian Star

> *A slender pedestal of gray stone rises from a dais at the southern end of this simple chamber. No grand tapestries adorn the walls; no patterns decorate the tiles on the floor. The only item of note is a gemstone the size of a walnut made of the purest, darkest obsidian. The gem is immaculately cut into the shape of a star and delicately placed in a specifically made groove atop the pedestal so that it faces the northern doorway. To the east is a sturdy wooden door built into a natural cave wall, barred from this side by a thick horizontal beam of fine hardwood.*

Poppy's true inheritance. Lifting it off the pedestal claims it, and Poppy always has one more trick (p. 106–107).

- **East door to G27:** plain from here. The hardwood beam takes **two creatures** to lift; from the cove side, the door can't open past it.

**Chaos ensues:** taking the Star shakes the complex apart (cracks, fissures, a mountain about to split). No check needed to sense the danger. **Go straight to [Event 13](#event-13--escape-from-shivering-mountain).**

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the shrine:** Taking the Star ends G12's double healing.

#### The Obsidian Star *(Item 7)*

- **Traits:** unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** worn. **Bulk** —.
- A plain silver pendant with a broken crystal in the rough shape of a star: black, ghostly white shapes swimming deep inside, fading to **violet** at one broken edge and **deep blue** at the other.
- **Innate spell:** while invested, cast ***earthbind*** or ***ghostly weapon*** as an innate occult spell **once per day**, choosing each day. *Ghostly weapon* targets only a metal or stone weapon.
- **One of four:** touched to the other pieces, it joins them seamlessly to restore the **Jewel of the Indigo Isles**.
- **Durability:** far weaker than almost any other artifact but as hard to destroy. The four fragments **can't be destroyed one at a time**, only once combined.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** See [artifacts and invested items](#artifacts-and-invested-items). It counts toward the wearer's 10 invested items.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The Star is a piece of the cult's relic, which Poppy shattered and hid. Every piece the PCs gather in Act 2 brings the cult closer; it steals the assembled Jewel in Chapter 9 ([Synopsis](gm_story_synopsis.md#act-3-battle-at-barnacle-bay-chapters-912)).

> **🧩 Sets up:** The broken edges point to the rest; "can only be destroyed once combined" matters later.

## The cove and the escape (G27, G28, Event 13)

### G27. Secret Cove

> *Inky blue water stretches from wall to wall in this massive underground cove. Bobbing gently in the center of the cove is a gorgeous brigantine, its finely lacquered planks somehow untouched by time or the elements. High above, stalactites hang from a cavernous ceiling. A rickety boardwalk along the western perimeter of the cove connects to several tunnels that double back on the rest of the complex. To the south, the cove stretches on, out of sight, with only a pinprick of light in the far distance to mark the exit out to sea.*

The *Chromatic Queen* is still anchored here; with the Star to unlock its helm, the PCs can claim it and escape (p. 107–108).
- **Size:** 200 feet west to east, 150 north to south. The south tunnel barely fits the *Queen* out to sea; the ceiling clears its tallest mast by at least 100 feet.
- **Sturdier than the rest:** **DC 15 Mountain or Engineering Lore** (Recall Knowledge): it could survive nine hurricanes and nine earthquakes. Cove and *Queen* survive the collapse intact.
- **Secret door to G26:** **DC 35 Perception** from here, but barred from the other side.
- **Coming back later:** even without the *Queen*, the PCs can return by ship to the tunnel mouth south of the cove (not on the map) and clear the collapse with magic or laborers.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — they may find the ship first:** The book allows the PCs to reach the *Queen* through the cove tunnels before the Star. It won't move until the Star is in its wheel, which gives them a reason to finish the dungeon.

### G28. The Chromatic Queen

> *The richly polished boards of this large sailing ship are all made from an incredible golden-hued hardwood, unmarred by even a single knot. A single thick stripe of crimson paint runs the length of the ship's fat, sturdy hull. In a shining mother-of-pearl white, the words "Chromatic Queen" are emblazoned across the hull's bow just below the forecastle. Staircases, trap doors, and gun ports hint at numerous secrets below deck. The shrouds, masts, and sails are not only seaworthy but pristine. Secured to the ship's bow is a gilded figurehead in the shape of an impossibly tall, divinely handsome g'mayun goddess, her gaze impassive and stalwart. Above her, the bowsprit points true, ready to lead the way to a great new adventure on the high seas.*

Unmooring is easy (untie knots, scrape barnacles), but it's magically anchored and the wheel stays locked until its key is in place (p. 108–109).
- **The helm:** a star-shaped indentation in the wheel's center, where a keystone might fit.
- **DC 15 Arcana or Sailing Lore** (Recall Knowledge): the keystone is powerful, perhaps an artifact; such magic is known in the Isles, but this ward is formidable.
- **The ward:** **counteract DC 35** with *dispel magic*, **6th level** ([counteracting](#counteracting); full rule in [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting)). Beyond the party now.
- **Unlocking it:** set the **Obsidian Star in the center of the wheel**. The ward crumbles, the wheel unlocks, the anchor rises, and the PCs **don't need the Star to sail it again**.

**Background special effect:** an **indigo pirate** easily recruits sailors eager to serve Poppy's successor aboard her own ship. If that PC won the Chapter 1 duel against [Melar Kan](gm_glossary.md#melar-kan), more crew may be ready.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The party's Act 2 ship. Its first voyage, and who commands it, is Chapter 5's business ([Synopsis, Act 2](gm_story_synopsis.md#act-2-voyage-to-runaway-reef-chapters-58)).

### Event 13 — Escape from Shivering Mountain

- **Trigger:** the PCs take the Obsidian Star from its pedestal in G26.
- **Goal:** get out before the mountain falls on them.

The mountain shakes violently; flee before the sanctum crumbles (p. 109).

**The escape route:** east of the altar. Lift the beam, cross into G27, board the *Chromatic Queen*, and set the Star in the helm to raise the anchor and unlock the wheel. Anyone with a little sailing knowledge can steer south through a brightening tunnel out of the still-quivering mountain.

**Once in G27, the PCs are safe.** From the *Queen* they can watch the proving grounds crumble as long as they like.

**Two ways to run it (book options):**
1. **Montage:** describe the voyage from the cove, down the Tentacle River, and back to [Barnacle Bay](gm_glossary.md#barnacle-bay), skimming details. A dramatic final scene.
2. **Climactic:** don't tell the players the cove is safe. They'll scramble to unmoor the *Queen* and reach open sea.

> **💡 GM tip:** Option 2 works best if the players already know the ship needs the Star, so the race is to the wheel. No checks; build tension with falling stone, groaning timbers, and the light at the tunnel's end.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — anything left behind:** The book says the sanctum comes down. Whatever the PCs didn't take (tapestry map, flags, rum cask, wo'a pillar) may be lost. Give the players a moment before they lift the Star, or decide in advance what survives.

## Concluding the chapter

With the Star in hand, the PCs can report to [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather), most interested in the Star and its possible sibling gems. The journal's riddles may lead to the other pieces' hiding places; finding the remaining three drives the next chapters (p. 109).

**If the PCs never found the journal:** the Star's holder can get its key points as **magical flashbacks**, which, the book notes, lets you control what they learn and when.

**Level up:** rest and advance to **level 4**, the book's end-of-Act-1 milestone, *Escaping from Shivering Mountain* ([Synopsis, levels](gm_story_synopsis.md#levels-and-milestones)).
- **Short on XP?** The book suggests a **story award** for recovering the Obsidian Star and escaping Shivering Mountain.

**Back in Rumplank:** the PCs can take downtime and tie up loose ends; when they're ready, they sail into the next adventure, *Voyage to Runaway Reef*.

> **💡 GM tip:** If [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi) attends the report, remember the PCs hold the first piece of the cult's relic and the journal tracking the rest. Play the royals delighted; Kulupi has his own reasons.

**Next chapter:** [Chapter 5](gm_chapter_5.md), *Ghosts of Seaview*. The Star and the journal point to the next fragment on [Bluebell Island](gm_glossary.md#bluebell-island).
