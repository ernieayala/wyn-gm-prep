## The chapter in one minute

The royals send the PCs after the cult and its victims.
- From the Crawling Caves and Okapi Hills, the PCs cross the [Crackbeak Salt Desert](gm_glossary.md#crackbeak-salt-desert) in three days. They face glare, a saltstorm, burrowers, and salt stalkers.
- In a cliffside ruin they find four cultists behind a barricade, and two captives.
- They may take a side trip for the Salt Stone.
- In the Drowned Jungle, galtzagorri scouts lead them to Pliticori and the vermilion dragon [Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby). She keeps the captives safe while the PCs push on.

**By the end the PCs have:**
1. **Crossed the salt desert** (80 XP, p. 247).
2. **Rescued Re'tkalis and Shalzan** at the Cliffside Ruin (area W).
3. **Learned the cult's destination** (cultists or map), and that a "Lady of Bone and Bounty" leads the ritual.
4. **Met Peerless Ruby** and left the captives in Pliticori.
5. Optionally: the **Salt Stone** (area X), the living blade **Aranelle** (W14), Ruby's coquecigrue reward (Event 25).

**Party level:** **8** to **9**. The milestone is **meeting Peerless Ruby** (p. 11).

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** The festival heroes are left with only mounts, rations, and skill. The desert tests survival. The ruin shows cultists who don't know what they serve. Pliticori gives a safe harbor, an ally, and Chapter 11's ride home.

## How the chapter is built

This chapter is a **pursuit on a schedule**. Here is the book's **Pursuit Timeframe** (p. 245):

| Event or area | Day |
|---|---|
| Event 17: Scrubland Gathering | 1 |
| Event 18: A Source of Water | 1 |
| Area U: Desert Oasis | 1 |
| Event 19: It Came From Below | 1 (night) |
| Event 20: Fallen and Abandoned | 2 |
| Event 21: Saltstorm | 2 |
| Event 22: Fatal Foliage | 2 |
| Area V: Saline Canyon | 2 |
| Area W: Cliffside Ruin | 3 |
| Area X: Salt Den (optional) | 4 |
| Event 23: On a Hundred Skittering Legs | 4 |
| Event 24: Fairy Lights | 4 |
| Pliticori Village | 4 (night) |
| Event 25: Rainbow Nest | 5 |
| Event 26: Rainstorm | 5 |
| Event 27: Patchwork Predator | 5 |
| Area Y: Embattled Caverns | 6 (Chapter 11) |

**The two halves:**

| Part | Pages | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crackbeak Salt Desert** | 245–268 | 48 miles. Morning Survival checks, environmental dangers, four wilderness fights, the Cliffside Ruin, optional Salt Den | Fixed, by day |
| **The Drowned Jungle** | 268–281 | 18 miles. Two fights, galtzagorri scouts, Pliticori, an optional quest, the vitavore | Fixed, by day. Event 25 optional |

**Travel times (p. 246, 268):**
- **Desert:** 4 days on foot at Speed 20 or 25. **3 days** mounted. The Salt Den adds 2 hours into a 4th day. The PCs still reach Pliticori by the 4th night.
- **Jungle:** the rest of the day they enter, plus one more day, mounted.
- **Flight:** at this level, flight doesn't last long enough to beat the mounts. It helps with scouting.
  - Tracking from the air is harder.
  - Spotting landmarks like the stormspire (U3) from the air takes **DC 25 Perception** in the glare.
  - In the canyons after Event 22, flight helps only if the PC stays up long enough to see the maze (p. 246).

> **💡 GM tip (describe the transitions):** The book wants the players to feel each change of land: scrubland, dunes, salt pan, slot canyons, jungle (p. 245). A sentence or two at each change sells the journey better than a map.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** Losing steam? Run Track checks, glare saves, and Event 18 in a minute each. Spend the time on the oasis, the saltstorm, the ruin, and Pliticori.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XP | 10 | 15 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 120 | 160 |

**Worked examples (level 8 in the desert, 9 in the jungle after Ruby):**
- **U2:** four aurochs (level 4, 10 each) + stormbringer (level 8, 40) = 80, **Moderate 8**.
- **W2:** four scallywags (level 5, 15 each) = 60, **Low 8**.
- **X3:** four salt stalkers (10 each) + salt mother (level 10, 80) = 120, **Severe 8**.
- **Event 25:** coquecigrue (level 8, 30) + two prismatic ghosts (level 6, 15 each) = 60, **Low 9**.

### Accomplishment XP

**Minor** 10, **moderate** 30, **major** 80. This chapter gives:
- Desert crossing: **80 XP** (major, when they reach the jungle).
- Saltstorm: **30 XP**.
- Ruby's respect: **30 XP**.
- Jungle crossing: **30 XP** (when they reach the mountain).

### Track, Sense Direction, and Subsist

- **Track** (trained only): normally half speed with rechecks. Here it is **one check per day** ([Charting a course](#charting-a-course-the-chases-track-check)).
- **Sense Direction:** in the jungle, PCs who know their destination roll at **DC 20**. This replaces Track at DC 30.
- **Subsist:** **−5 penalty** while exploring, unless a PC has a way around it (p. 248). **Forager** matters in Event 17.

### Charting a course (the chase's Track check)

At the start of **Day 1** and **Day 2**, one PC **trained in Survival** rolls to Track (p. 246). The book says this isn't a normal Track check. Abilities based on Tracking may not work as usual. Your call.

- **DC:** **20**, **+2 per day that passes**. Days lost to critical failures count. Day 1 is DC 20, Day 2 is DC 22.
- **No check on Day 3** (Day 4 with area X). Event 21 wipes out the trail, but area W is close.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | Track the cultists. Learn **two clues** (below) and reveal them when you choose. Gain a **+1 circumstance bonus to initiative** that day |
| **Success** | A quick path and **one clue** |
| **Failure** | Wander an hour, then may retry. All PCs are **fatigued** until a full night's rest. If already fatigued, treat it as a critical failure |
| **Critical failure** | Lost. Pick a **trouble** (below). The stretch takes **two days**. No new check for the extra day |

**What a critical failure shifts:** Day 1 splits in two: Events 17–18, then area U and Event 19. Day 2 splits in two: Events 20–21, then Event 22 and area V.

**Clues** (p. 247), or invent your own:
- **Unusual Footprints:** slower walkers fell behind in smaller groups. There are pacing marks around camps and signs of a scuffle.
- **Campsite:** an old cult camp. The cultists gain a little ground each day. The layout shows organized kidnappers who far outnumber their victims.
- **Ribbon:** from Atriella's festival outfit. It is tied to a stone column and can be seen only from the PCs' side. She's leaving clues.
- **Religious Symbol:** a lost [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth) symbol. It is a modern take on the Razorback symbols.

**Troubles** (p. 247):
- **Severe Sunburn:** the PCs are **enfeebled 1** and **clumsy 1** for 2 days.
- **Salt Ticks:** for 2 days, each **manipulate** action (somatic spells too) needs a **DC 5 flat check**. On a failure, scratching disrupts the action.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the Religious Symbol clue):** The book points to Razorback (Chapter 8) "and the cult rooms in the caves in Chapter 10." But this is Chapter 10. Mention Razorback and any cult rooms your PCs have seen.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The ribbon shows Atriella is alive and fighting back. That makes Chapter 11's reveal hit harder: Kulupi is free and in charge.

### Glare and salt storms

Desert dangers (p. 247):

**Glare.** Before each **daylight** encounter, each PC rolls **DC 20 Fortitude**. On a failure, they are **dazzled** for that encounter. *Penumbral shroud* and similar magic prevent it.

**Detecting a salt storm.** **DC 20 Survival**, trained only.

**Minor salt storm.** A creature out in it for 10 minutes without shelter rolls **DC 20 Fortitude**. On a failure, it is **fatigued**.

**Severe salt storm.** Creatures without shelter:
- take **2d6 slashing** each round.
- roll **DC 25 Fortitude** each minute. On a failure, they are **blinded** for 1 minute.
- swallow salt unless they hold their breath. They stay **fatigued** until they drink **2 days' worth of water**.

**Building a shelter (Shelter Points).**
- If the PCs spot the storm early, each PC gets **two checks**. If not, each gets one before it hits.
- Each check takes **5 rounds**. Only PCs can roll.
- The check is **DC 22 Athletics, Crafting, or Survival**. A fitting Lore is DC 20. A far-fetched method is DC 25.
- **Critical success** +2. **Success** +1. **Failure** +0. **Critical failure** −1.
- A blinded PC takes a **−2 circumstance penalty**.

| Shelter Points earned | Result |
|---|---|
| Fewer than half the number of PCs | Shelter **blows away**. Reset to 0 and retry, 5 rounds per set of attempts, until at least half. Then mild-storm effects inside |
| Between half the number of PCs and the number of PCs | **Compromised.** Mild-storm effects inside |
| More than the number of PCs | Storm effects **negated** |

**Four PCs:** 0–1 points, blown away. 2–4 points, mild-storm rules. 5 or more points, safe.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Shelter Point threshold):** The book first says "half of the number of PC successes," then "half the number of PCs." The table uses "half the number of PCs," which fits the passage. Decide before the session.

> **💡 GM tip:** If nobody spots a severe storm, it deals 2d6 a round while they build. Prompt a PC trained in Survival to roll the DC 20 check while you describe the Event 21 cloud.

### Create a Diversion

W2 scallywags can use it for Diverting Finisher. Roll Deception against the Perception DCs of the creatures. On a success, you are hidden from those creatures. This lasts until your turn ends or you do anything but Step, Hide, or Sneak.

### Improved Grab and Constrict

W12 astringent armors.
- **Improved Grab:** Grab as a **free action** after hitting with the listed Strike.
- **Constrict:** one action. Every grabbed creature takes damage, with a **basic Fortitude** save.

Attach is a *Battlezoo Bestiary* ability. Read BB 13 before the fight.

### Burrowing foes

The drillworm (Event 19) and pistolweeds (Event 22) attack from below and escape underground. Most PCs can't follow, so expect these fights to end with the foe running away. The book gives full Monster Parts either way.

### Aranelle's curse

Mental. A wielder rolls **DC 27 Will**. On a failure, she imposes a *geas* ([W14](#w14--warriors-lament-moderate-8) has the terms).

### Familiars

A PC whose class or feats grant a familiar may bond a **baby salt stalker** in X2 after combat. Use the normal rules. It **always has the climber ability** (p. 267).

### Dubious Knowledge

Pliticori's Tall Tales give every Gather Information check there the **Dubious Knowledge** effect. On a failure (not a critical failure), the PC learns one true fact and one false fact. You don't say which is which.

### Peerless Points

This is the subsystem for Ruby's audience (p. 273–275).

- Each PC makes **two checks**, answering "why are you worthy to enter my domain?"
- **Options:**
  - DC 26 Deception (make up a story).
  - DC 24 Diplomacy (honest appeal).
  - DC 22 Performance (tell of their deeds).
  - DC 24 Intimidation (the stakes, the monsters slain).
  - DC 28 **any other skill** the player can justify (rare knowledge, a physical feat, a crafted item).
- **Aeternal dragon** and **galtzagorri** PCs get a **+2 circumstance bonus**.
- **Critical success** +2. **Success** +1. **Critical failure** −1. The book gives no failure result, so a failure adds nothing.
- **Sovala and Wisp's trust** (Event 24) starts the party at **2 Peerless Points**.

| Peerless Points | Result |
|---|---|
| Fewer than the number of PCs | Hospitality, but **unimpressed**. No one shares cult news. Skip to Tales and Refreshments |
| At least the number of PCs | **Respect.** Puppet tale, and they may question her captive. **30 XP** |
| More than 1.5 times the number of PCs | **Delighted.** As respect, plus a ***phoenix flask*** (*Secrets of Magic*) from her condensed magic |

**Four PCs:** 8 checks (+2 for the scouts' trust). Respect at 4 points. Flask at 7.

## Scene 1 — Royal Request

- **Where:** Castle Rumplank, or a messenger on the road.
- **Who:** [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather) and [Queen Maxelle](gm_glossary.md#queen-maxelle-redfeather).
- **Goal:** the PCs accept and get mounts and supplies (p. 244).

**Two ways in:**
- **Pushed on through the caves:** a messenger brings mounts, supplies, and good wishes. The day is nearly over. Run a first night with no combat, then the desert events.
- **Went back to the capital** (more likely after the festival): the royals call an audience.

**Read aloud (audience):**

> *The king and queen of Rumplank have eyes rimmed with red. Though they've since cleared their eyes, they've both been crying recently. Nonetheless, they look at you with resolved gazes.*
>
> *"Thank you for finding the trail," the queen speaks, "We hope you will be willing to follow that trail and continue to search for our missing children. They aren't the only ones who appear to have gone missing, either. The Chromatic Queen is already yours, and it's certainly an option for you to give up here, but we dearly hope you'll choose to continue onward. If you're willing to search, we'll provide you with mounts and a magic bag filled with as many nonperishable provisions as we can fit, enough for all of you and for any of the missing people you can find and recover. With the city in recovery from the fires, it'll take some time to liquidate any more funds from our royal assets and holdings, but we assure you, you will be richly rewarded for your bravery on your return. Just follow the trail onward and see where it leads. If you find some of the kidnapping victims and they aren't all together, use your best judgment as to whether it's safe to send them back unescorted or have them continue with you until you find the rest. Our priority is our children, but, of course, we hope you can save everyone."*

**What they provide:**
- **Mounts:** twice the number of PCs plus NPCs who tag along. The PCs can swap mounts or lend them to victims.
- **A *bag of holding* type II** with **250 weeks of rations** and **250 filled waterskins**. That is more than enough for every victim.
- Little information. Family messages give descriptions of some missing people.

> **💡 GM tip (the victims' names):** The book leaves out a full list on purpose. Most captives will be sacrificed, and knowing them first is depressing. If players care about certain victims, swap those victims in for the area W captives (p. 245).

**Into the desert:** about **five hours** through the rocky Crawling Caves with horses, NPCs, and gear. Read:

> *The winding tunnel finally ends, depositing you on a rocky hillside covered in shrubs and herbs, some of which seem unusual and possibly rare, even at a glance. This scrubland lies at the margin of Goldcrop Island's infamous desert, and it is into that very desert that the abductors' trail leads. Crossing the desert will be a true test of your endurance, combat ability, and survival skills!*

### What the PCs can know about the desert

The [Crackbeak Salt Desert](gm_glossary.md#crackbeak-salt-desert) lies west of Rumplank. It has scrubland, dunes, a huge cracked salt pan, and slot canyons.
- The pan was a lake fed by rivers. Then the ground shifted and sent the rivers elsewhere.
- The water dried up and left a salt crust. Saltstorms spread the salt every year.
- Now, in the dry season, a few salty pockets of water remain. Burrowing **drillworms** (p. 387) opened some of them.

**Recall Knowledge:** **DC 25 Nature** or **DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** (p. 246).
- **Success:** the facts above.
- **Critical success:** the facts above, plus the drillworms' role. The PC also gets a separate Recall Knowledge check on drillworms.
- **Critical failure:** the PC believes the desert is **sunsand**. This is a rare magical sand that soaks up any healing spell cast within 10 feet.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Sunsand is false, and healing works. A PC who believes it may hold back healing. Let the critical failure do its job.

## Scene 2 — Desert, Day 1

Open with the [Track check](#charting-a-course-the-chases-track-check) (DC 20).

### Event 17 — Scrubland Gathering *(resources)*

This rocky scrubland on the desert's east edge is full of plants that can live with salt. Few Rumplankers come this far, so some plants are valuable. A PC trained in Survival notices this early (p. 248).

**Gathering on the move:**
- One check, early in the day. A PC **trained in Survival** rolls **DC 17 Survival** to Subsist (**−5 penalty** while exploring).
- **Success:** a **bag of rythrian root**. It is restorative and ideal for elixirs like the *elixir of life*.
- **Critical success:** the root, plus a safely stored **sack of witchclaw branches**. Witchclaw is a thorny bush. Its toxin can be refined into a paralytic poison.
- The party gets one of each, no matter how many PCs succeed. **Forager** doubles a PC's haul.

> **🧩 Sets up (Defense special resources (Ch 12, p. 315–316)):** Write both down.
> - **Rythrian root:** **Medic** → **Physicker**. The district gains 50 extra maximum Hit Points instead of 25.
> - **Witchclaw:** **Arrow Tower** → **Poison Tower**. Damaged armies take 1 extra damage at the end of each round, even resistant ones. Undead and construct armies are not affected.
> - Or spend either for **10 Resource Points**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Forager vs. the resource table):** The Chapter 12 table allows only one bag of root and one stack of branches. Decide whether Forager's extra counts.

### Event 18 — A Source of Water

This happens an hour past the scrubland, shortly before midday. Read aloud:

> *About an hour after you leave the scrublands, you notice a strange collection of plants in the distance. The plants have long stems tipped with colorful, round pods.*

**Waterweed:** its deep roots store groundwater in bulbs underground. It pushes salt into hard pods at the tips of its stems.

**Recall Knowledge, DC 15 Nature** (p. 248):
- **Critical success:** the PC knows waterweed. They also know its meat-eating lookalike, **pistolweed**, which never passes up a meal. They know the signs that tell the two apart. None of these plants is pistolweed.
- **Success:** waterweed only.
- **Failure:** neither.
- **Critical failure:** the PC thinks waterweed is poisonous to drink from, and that its pods are valuable to artists.

**Digging a well:** both plants mean groundwater is below. A well takes **2 hours**. The PCs don't need one yet.

> **💡 GM tip (the map trick):** The book says to lay out a battle map like Event 22's (p. 253), even though nothing attacks. When the scene repeats in Event 22, the map won't warn of danger.

### Area U — Desert Oasis

Three-quarters of the way through the day, salty dunes hide an oasis. Its fresh water is clear and full of fish. An ancient **water purification device** keeps it pure. The device sits in a small stone **stormspire** (p. 249). Map: p. 249. The PCs enter from the northeast.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (where the spire stands):** The area text puts it near the **edge**. The U1 read-aloud puts it at the **center**. Go with the map.

**Recall Knowledge about the architecture:** **DC 25 Crafting**, **DC 20 Society**, or **DC 15 Indigo Isles History Lore**.
- **Critical success:** the builders were the **Jirigati**. They were magical inventors of many ancestries who often moved from place to place. They lived here until a few centuries ago. Their devices still turn up nearby. The PC also gets a **+1 circumstance bonus** to Investigate the U3 device or Disable the U3 hazard.
- **Success:** as above, no bonus.
- **Failure:** nothing.
- **Critical failure:** the PC thinks it matches the Moonshadow Island and Razorback Island cult architecture.

#### U1 — Dune's Crest

The dunes are **difficult terrain**. They hide the oasis until the PCs reach the ridge. Read aloud:

> *A series of sand dunes break up the desert's otherwise flat expanse. From the top of a ridge amidst the dunes, you see a refreshing-looking oasis with a variety of different fish swimming through the water. Numerous plants sprout from the oasis's tranquil shore, and large aurochs roam through the area, eyeing you warily from the distance. An ornate stone spire, still standing despite the signs of significant age, stands at the oasis's center.*

- Going **down** the ridge is difficult terrain. Going back **up** is **greater difficult terrain**.
- If the PCs attack from the ridge, the herd takes cover behind trees or scatters along the oasis.

#### U2 — Water's Shore *(Moderate 8)*

> *Numerous plants sprout from the tranquil shores of the oasis.*

A herd of **runebranded aurochs** lives here. A **stormbringer** leads them. The tower's magic changed it. The herd eats shore plants, plus electricity the stormbringer channels from the tower (Herd Conductivity). Salt stalkers made them protective of their land (p. 250).

**Creatures:**
- **Runebranded Aurochs (4):** BB 79, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- **Runebranded Stormbringer (1):** variant of BB 79, Creature 8. The stat block is below.

**Tactics:**
- If nobody befriends the herd (with Wild Empathy, for example), it attacks.
- The stormbringer uses **Herd Conductivity**, **Electrocute**, then **Trample**. The rest Trample with **Storm Step**.
- The stormbringer **retreats at 30 HP** and gives up its land. The rest back down only after it falls or retreats.
- They **don't chase** PCs who flee.

**Peaceful option:** the PCs might befriend the herd, or make it back down without a kill. If so, the stormbringer gives them an **electric mote**. It is worth the **full Monster Parts value of every aurochs** in the encounter. The book gives no DC, so it's your call.

  - **Failure:** under the geas. The wielder must advance her agenda: **slay powerful monsters and protect the people of Goldcrop Island from peril, whatever the cost.** They can't willingly drop or give away the blade.

## Scene 5 — Area X: Salt Den (optional, Day 4)

- **Where:** a few hours off the trail, in the heart of salt stalker territory. It adds **2 hours** into a 4th day. The PCs still reach Pliticori by the 4th night, a few hours later (p. 265).
- **Goal:** the **Salt Stone** and a sack of magical salt.

**What it is:** a man-made den.
- A Jirigati device took the salt out of water and refined it into magical salt. The Jirigati harvested it about once a month.
- After they left, it kept running for centuries. It grew huge crystals and the **Salt Stone**.
- Then salt stalkers nested here and wrecked it.

**Fluidsense is the whole problem:**
- Dozens of salt stalkers live here. Most are out hunting.
- **With precautions:** the PCs face only one hunting group (X1).
- **Without precautions:** dozens of salt stalkers, scorchers, and gliders keep arriving from up to a mile away. The PCs will likely retreat.
- **Invisibility** and other magic that only fools sight **doesn't** fool fluidsense.
- **Valiant's warning:** if the PCs don't think of precautions, each PC who read Valiant's notes rolls a **secret DC 20 Nature** check. On a success, they recall his fix: surround yourself with a decoy body of water, or hide behind solid cover thick enough to block the sense.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book warns that extra salt stalker fights can give out too much XP and Monster Parts. Run a few waves if the group wants, but watch the totals.

Map: p. 265.

### X1 — Canyon Approach *(Low 8)*

A winding slot canyon, **40-foot**-deep, on the old river. It is like the Saline Canyon. The den is further on, **20 feet** up the wall. The PCs enter from the southwest.

**Creatures** (the PCs always fight them before the den):
- **Salt Stalkers (2):** BB 80, Creature 4. Initiative: Stealth +13. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- **Salt Scorchers (2):** BB 81, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.

**Tactics:**
- The stalkers are careful. They ambush using their **climb Speed** and ranged attacks. They try to lure a few PCs up the cliff. There, the scorchers surround those PCs, so all four foes get **ambush attackers** damage.
- If the scorchers are losing at range, they reluctantly go into melee in pairs to get their own ambush attackers damage. The stalkers hang back.
- Water is scarce. They use **Spew Fiery Grease** only on PCs who are already wet or fighting desiccation venom. They use it only when several PCs line up.
- The stalkers **retreat to the den** once the scorchers fall.

**After the fight:** spilled PC blood alerts the den and every hunter within a mile. If the PCs stay longer than a few minutes, waves of foes arrive one after another. There is **no time to Treat Wounds or Refocus**.

> **💡 GM tip:** Tell the players about the clock. Entering X3 worn down means a Severe fight.

### X2 — Hatchling Chamber

This room holds eggs and new hatchlings. Its entrance is hidden among hundreds of huge salt crystals.
- A PC who Searches with **DC 25 Perception** finds the secret door. By then, the salt mother can see and sense the PCs.
- So X3 almost always comes first. To reach X2 first, the PCs must slip past fluidsense and the mother, then use the door unseen.
- **If the PCs sneak in:** any X3 stalkers here are surprised (**−2 circumstance penalty to initiative**). They fight to the death for their young.
- **The four stalkers move here** after they sense X1 blood. They plan to hit the PCs from behind in X3.
- **Babies:** they don't fight. They cower, and any area effect in the room kills them.
- **Familiar:** after combat, a PC may bond a baby as a [familiar](#familiars). Use the normal rules. It always has **climber**.

### X3 — Salt Mother's Roost *(Severe 8)*

> *Giant salt crystals grow from every surface of this strange cavern, piercing the bone-dry air like spears. In the chamber's southeast corner, a glowing stone is surrounded by a salty haze and glows faintly, surrounded by sundered metal fragments.*

The Salt Stone is the centerpiece of the nest. Torn-up pieces of the device decorate it. The crystals are **difficult terrain** for creatures without a climb Speed.

**Creatures:**
- **Salt Mother:** BB 83, Creature 10. Initiative: Perception +21. Monster Parts 125/275/500 gp. She rests in her nest in the **southwest corner**.
- **Salt Stalkers (4):** BB 80, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +12, Stealth +13. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.

**Tactics:**
- **If they sensed blood in X1:** the four stalkers wait in X2 to hit the back line from behind. If not, they rest at the back with the mother.
- **If the PCs didn't hide again after X1:** the mother tracks them. She uses a **frenzied hiss** right before they arrive. All the stalkers rush into melee and try to trap the PCs from both sides, coming from X2.
- **If that fails:** two stalkers go after the back-line PC who looks weakest. The rest shoot.
- Only the mother prefers melee (**jaws**).
- They all keep fighting for their home and young.

**Resource:** besides the Salt Stone, the PCs find a **sackful of high-quality magical salt**.

> **🧩 Sets up (Defense special resource (Ch 12, p. 315–316)):** **High-quality magical salt:** **Arrow Tower** → **Salt Tower**. It deals 12 damage instead of 5 against aquatic creatures, even amphibious ones on land. It doesn't count ships, aquatic constructs, and the like. Or spend it for **10 Resource Points**.

**Treasure without Monster Parts:** a ***+1 striking flaming warhammer*** in her nest. It is a trophy of her strongest kill, and it keeps her warm on cold nights.

**Salt Stone** *(Item 10)*. Unique evocation magical. **Price** 1,000 gp. **Usage** held in 1 hand. **Bulk** 1. A palm-sized crystal of super-condensed magical salt. It formed naturally and is a curiosity for researchers. It has two powers that dry out creatures (p. 268, also p. 379).
- **Activate** (2 actions; envision, Interact), **once per 10 minutes.** The target makes a **Fortitude** save.
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** −1 status penalty to all checks and DCs for 1 round.
  - **Failure:** −1 status penalty to all checks and DCs for 1 minute.
  - **Critical failure:** −2 status penalty to all checks and DCs and **dazzled**, both for 1 minute.
  - Water-trait or mostly liquid creatures, like most oozes, get a result one degree worse.
  - Creatures with little or no water, like many constructs, are immune.
  - Drinking any liquid, including an elixir or potion, ends the effect. A water-trait creature, or one that can't drink, ends it with a two-action concentrate activity.
- **Activate** (2 actions; envision, Interact), **once per hour.** It makes a cutting salt storm that blocks vision. It works as *obscuring mist*. Creatures that enter it or start their turn in it take **2d6 slashing**. Water-trait or mostly liquid creatures take double.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the Salt Stone has no DC):** Neither version (p. 268, p. 379) gives a save DC for the first activation. Pick one before anyone uses it, and write it on the item card.

## Scene 6 — The Drowned Jungle (Day 4)

On Day 4, the PCs reach the **Drowned Jungle**. They arrive early, or shortly before midday if they went to area X. The jungle is friendlier to life, but just as dangerous. Most Goldcrop travelers avoid it (p. 268). Read aloud:

> *It seems like you've been stuck in the salt desert forever, but finally, the desert landscape gives way to a lush jungle. It's so sudden as to be startling to see the two biomes switch so quickly as you trudge through an endless sea of white salt one minute and set foot into a deep ocean of trees a few minutes later.*

**Award 80 XP** each for crossing the desert.

**Soggy, Foggy, and Froggy:**
- Hazy drizzle and downpours, almost all the time.
- Wild toogtoogs, amphibians, birds, and insects.
- Puddles and marsh that slow travel.
- **DC 15 Nature** Recall Knowledge: a damp jungle beside a desert isn't fully natural. Height and wind explain part of it. Another force is at work deep inside.

**Finding the way:**
- Tracking the trail, now days old: **DC 30 Survival**.
- If the PCs know the destination, **DC 20 Survival** to **Sense Direction** finds a reliable path.
- **Failure:** they lose hours. They reach Event 23 **fatigued** (no exploration activities).

**Resting:** the first night is in Pliticori. The second night is near the area Y cave mouth, a full day on. So the PCs are unlikely to backtrack.

**Crossing the jungle:** **30 XP** each (moderate) when they reach the mountain.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (XP vs. the milestone):** With XP, the book expects the PCs to reach **9th level by the time they enter the jungle**.
> - Slightly short? You may make the jungle reward **major (80 XP)**.
> - Further behind? Event 23's two fights can finish it. Each is Low at 9 and Moderate at 8. Give a few rounds to heal while the mantises gather.
> - **Under milestone leveling**, Event 23 runs at level 8.

### Event 23 — On a Hundred Skittering Legs & Buzzing Wings *(labeled "Medium 9")*

This happens shortly after midday, or near day's end if the PCs went to area X. The swamp smell hides dried corpses. The grotto, **Sopping Snarl**, belongs to a **thorn crawler**. Some branches show clear signs of having been cut by a previous traveler, though their efforts provide little assistance. The air is thick with moisture and the pungent aroma of swamp water. **Terrain:**

**Creatures:**

- **Jungle Mantis Swarms (2):** BB 50, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +14, Stealth +20. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.

**Tactics:**
- The thorn crawler ambushes the PCs while they are stuck in the mud. It uses **thorn burst**, then focuses on anyone cut off in the muck.
- **Below 50 HP:** it goes back to its nest and attacks at range.
- The mantises wait. **Once the thorn crawler dies**, they follow the blood to the weakened PCs.

**Treasure without Monster Parts:** ***bracers of armor I***. They grew into its body from a meal years ago.

**Development:** the PCs find a mostly digested cultist. The thorn crawler caught this cultist while the others escaped. The body is fresh enough to show the PCs are close behind.

**If the PCs lose:** the Event 24 galtzagorris may save them.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the threat label):** "Medium 9" isn't a PF2 threat level. The text says it is **a chain of two Low-threat encounters for level 9**.
> - Thorn crawler: 60 XP (Low 9).
> - Two swarms at 30 each: 60 XP (Low 9).
> - At level 8, each half is 80 XP, Moderate.
> - If both overlap at level 9, it's 120 XP, Severe. Run them one after the other.

## Scene 7 — Pliticori Village

At first the PCs see a few buildings around a campfire. Then fluttering and gossip overhead reveal dozens of tiny treetop homes, hidden by leaves (p. 273).

**Pliticori Village:** Settlement 6, CG village
- Galtzagorri village hidden in the jungle and ruled by a dragon.
- **Government** pseudo-chiefdom
- **Population** 50 (98% galtzagorri, 2% vermilion dragon)
- **Languages** Common, Draconic, Sylvan
- **Religions** [The Balance](gm_glossary.md#the-balance)
- **Threats** dangerous jungle creatures, apocalyptic evil cult with nearby headquarters
- **Tall Tales:** villagers know a lot and love to share, but they exaggerate. Gather Information gets a **+2 circumstance bonus** and always works as [Dubious Knowledge](#dubious-knowledge).
- **Peerless Ruby** (CG vermilion dragon 13): dragon hero, lover of tall tales, and village chief in theory.

**The village:**
- [Galtzagorris](gm_glossary.md#galtzagorri) live in tiny treetop homes of woven leaves. They are built too fragile for larger folk on purpose. Most galtzagorris fly. **Seats and pulleys** carry Tiny PCs and galtzagorris without wings.
- **Ground-level buildings** with woven-leaf roofs fit Medium creatures. These are Ruby's home, a shared storeroom, and gathering shelters.
- **DC 25 Perception** or **DC 20 Crafting** on Ruby's home: the roof has been ripped off and repaired many times. Her size-changing, which she can't control, causes this (the PCs see it later).

### Audience with Peerless Ruby

**Peerless Ruby** (CG female vermilion dragon storyteller, she/her) is about the size of an [orpok](gm_glossary.md#orpok) or [hardriggan](gm_glossary.md#hardriggan). She greets the PCs first with a big show. She swoops down to ask why they're worthy to enter her domain.

Run [Peerless Points](#peerless-points): two checks per PC.

**If she's impressed (respect or better):** the puppets come out. She tells her epic tale of dozens of invaders charging through the jungle. Her dragon power and Pliticori's bravery beat them. Read aloud:

> *A dozen galtzagorri gather around as Peerless Ruby withdraws several puppets, leans forward, and begins her tale. Her low, booming voice easily commands their attention. "It began as a day like any other, a day where the chorus of birds mingled with the pitter-patter of rain upon the canopy and the flitter-flap of dozens of ladybug wings. But the day's peace was not to last, for among those wings that made merry in the skies was a terrified pair that did its best to carry a dying soldier to the last bastion of safety. A valiant scout who came to bear witness and a utter dire prophecy through trembling lips: 'invaders were coming!'" Although the crowd obviously knows the outcome of this tale, they still gasp as one.*
>
> *Peerless Ruby continues her tale, "Though strained lips and rasping breath, the scout finishes his warning. 'They approach in numbers far greater than our own, filled with contempt for all that drew breath and a desire to conquer any who would dare to stand in their way.' As that crucial warning reached the ears of all in Pliticori, the scout, having done his duty, collapsed upon the ground. And every single one of us, from child to elder, knew that we were about to face a desperate battle, one that would determine our very survival. The herbalists rushed to the scout's side, staunching his wounds with masterful hands, while the rest of us prepared for battle." A murmur spreads through the audience as they appear genuinely concerned for the outcome of this tale, even though they just lived through it.*
>
> *"It was not long before our dread foes reached us, marching through the jungle like an implacable and relentless tide, bearing torches in their hands and oblivion in their eyes—echoes of the demonic sin corrupting the g'mayun's great song given tangible form. They sang of fire and destruction and burning, but they did not know the true meaning of fire. I called upon the mighty wellspring of draconic power that is my birthright, and I grew to tower over my home, I grew to tower over the trees, and yet taller still, until I looked down upon them all! The trees trembled when I sucked in my breath to fill my massive chest! And when I breathed out, an inferno as mighty as a volcanic eruption engulfed the monsters in flames! The weak among their number crumbled to ash in an instant, but the four strongest continued to march ever onward. Each survivor carried a pick, honed to a point so sharp and strong it could crack a diamond, and were the pick to connect, whichever poor soul faced its wrath would surely perish in an instant. But the warriors of Pliticori were undaunted. Onward they flew, their ladybug wings beating with unparalleled grace. One after another, their bows, their words, and their spells struck true. With each blow, the swinging of the dread picks slowed until, at long last, they swung no more. But as the last invader fell, they spoke words that chilled us to the bone: this was only the beginning. The invader warned that more and yet still more of their companions would come. They served a dragon under the mountain that could rival even me, and they would not be deterred." The crowd, which was cheering wildly, goes deadly silent.*

After the tale, she says one invader survived and is held in one of the buildings. Read aloud:

> *Peerless Ruby puts away her puppets and wraps up her tale. "The invader remains here now, locked securely away. We can only hope that she will choose to share the secrets she knows before we must all rise to fight once more."*

- **DC 20 Perception** to Sense Motive: the tale is wildly exaggerated.
- Her captive claims a dragon patron. Ruby suspects a **demon**, since she'd know of a dragon god under the mountain. Getting the plain truth from her is as tall an order as her tales.
- **Reward:** **30 XP** each for her respect.

**Wisp's stick:** if any PC believed the tale, Wisp waits until the PCs stop talking to the dragon. Then Wisp grabs a random stick and tries to sell it as a sacred artifact for 100 gp. Read aloud:

> *Wisp's eyes widen as they catch sight of a fallen branch, "Now, what's this? I'm sensing quite the aura from that branch... Could it be? The sacred seed of Gaia's might sprouted into a tree whose roots tunnel to the center of the world and whose branches kiss the stars, and from that tree, a thousand branches fell to bless all who walk Alacar's face. Humble though they may appear to an untrained eye, each of these branches holds within it the power to sprout a new forest in its wake. Pliticori has already enjoyed so many of Than Dat's blessings, but perhaps you could make use of them? Still, it would not do to simply part with such a treasure without anything in return. Were you to donate a mere 100 gold coins to the prosperity of our humble village, I am sure that Than Dat herself would bless your endeavors."*

- If the PCs balk, Wisp laughs: *"Aww, but you believed Peerless Ruby's story, why not mine too?"*
- If a PC tries to pay, Wisp is shocked. Wisp refuses, throws up their hands, and flies off.

> **🔒 GM-only (what happened):** **Four g'mayun cultists** came through. Village scouts beat them after Spotty's whistle. There was no giant dragon, no inferno, and no army. Ruby blew up a small skirmish into an epic.

### Questioning the cultist

The survivor, **L'shuri** (CE female g'mayun cultist), is in a locked, guarded building. Ruby allows the PCs to question her (p. 275).
- She graduated from **Garardi's**. She knows what the W2 cultists know ([Questioning the cultists](#questioning-the-cultists)). She also knows the captives' role, though her group had no captives.
- She's told Ruby all this, and she shares it freely.
- **Her secret:** the "dragon" patron is a **demon**. First, **DC 20 Perception** to Sense Motive shows she's hiding something. Then a fitting **DC 25** check gets it out, like Deception to seem an ally, or Intimidation.
- She thinks Ruby spares her out of curiosity about the dragon. She fears Ruby will kill her once Ruby learns it's a demon.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Ruby keeps L'shuri alive as insurance. If the cult succeeds, L'shuri can explain what happened and help Ruby protect the village.

### Tales and Refreshments

Impressed or not, Ruby hosts the PCs. She offers food, hammocks, and stories told with handmade puppets.

**Why is there a jungle next to a desert?** These are her favorite answers. She isn't sure which is true:
- An ancient deity blessed it before the Balance came to Alacar.
- A powerful water [eldamon](gm_glossary.md#eldamon) here brings extra rain.
- A cerulean dragon caught her wife sneaking off to adventure once too often. Their brawl released enough water to keep the jungle wet ever since.

**Peerless Rumors** (warnings about jungle beasts):
- An aberrant fungus with a shared mind eats roots. It leaves **circles of dead foliage**. Its sensing tendrils look like **tiny blood-red mushrooms**. It makes copies of people that cry for help, to lure travelers to their deaths.
- A color-changing beast with rainbow feathers stomps and warbles through the jungle. It has a rooster's head and legs, a serpent's tongue and neck, and a glowing green heart. Its spit holds every element. Maybe someone could talk to it and learn why it haunts these lands.
- An innocent-looking animal on a bush may be a small part of a far bigger predator. That predator swallows 100 times its weight and holds an ocean of water in its stomach.

**DC 31 Occultism** Recall Knowledge: the second rumor is a real **coquecigrue** (Event 25). The others mix fantasy with real sightings of the **vitavore**'s work (Event 27).

### Peerless Quest

Something to the **northwest**, near the PCs' route, makes fliers vanish. Ruby asks PCs headed that way to investigate for a reward, even if she's unimpressed. She advises them to retreat at once if they're outmatched.

**Why doesn't she go herself?** She fears she's no match and hates admitting it. But the village matters more than her pride. If pressed, she sends the villagers away and confesses. She **shrinks to housecat size** as she does. Praise or encouragement quickly restores her to Medium.

**Peerless Mystery:** if asked about the size-changing, she blames a fickle genie of the winds.

> **🔒 GM-only:** It comes from a failed ritual decades ago, when she tried to become an [aeternal dragon](gm_glossary.md#aeternal-dragon). She embraces it. She won't retry *lux aeterna* or remove it. The PCs see her grow huge at the end of Chapter 11 (p. 304).

**Leaving:**
- If the PCs accepted the quest, run **Event 25** as they leave. If not, skip to **Event 27** (it saves only half an hour).
- As they pack, **Re'tkalis and Shalzan ask to stay**. Ruby promises to protect them until it's time to return to Rumplank.

**Quest reward:** the PCs defeat or calm the coquecigrue and report back. Ruby gives them a **bag of vermilion scales** from her latest molt (**275 gp** without Monster Parts). If they don't return right away, she most likely hands it over at the end of Chapter 11 (p. 304).

**Peerless Ruby's Gift:** Monster Parts 125/275/500 gp
- **Eligible Refinements:** weapons (bludgeoning, piercing), armor (medium or heavy), shield, perception item, skill items (Acrobatics, Athletics, Cooking Lore, Deception, Diplomacy, Nature, Performance, Stealth).
- **Eligible Imbued Properties:** bane, charisma, chaotic, good, energy resistant (fire), fire, sensory (darkvision, scent), strength, wild, winged.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It gives the captives a safe place. It gives the PCs a friendly face after days of danger. It confirms who they're chasing. It also gives them their ride home and an ally for Act 3. The puppet show mirrors Rumplank's Poppy legends: a real event blown up into myth.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Ruby flies the PCs to Rumplank at the end of Chapter 11 (p. 304). In Chapter 12, she greets X'na'zed as an old acquaintance and helps defend the city. How the PCs treated her colors both scenes.

### Concluding milestone — level 9

**Meeting Peerless Ruby:** the PCs **level up to 9** (p. 11). Under milestone leveling, do it after the audience, before they leave Pliticori.

## Scene 8 — Jungle, Day 5

### Event 25 — Rainbow Nest *(Low 9)*

*Optional: only if the PCs took Peerless Ruby's quest.*

About an hour from Pliticori, the PCs find a crunchy pile of bones at the river's edge. Across the water is a riot of color. It's a nest of torn tents, clothing, and linens, with an odd creature on top (p. 277–279). Map: p. 278. The PCs enter from the east.

**Terrain:** the bone pile is **difficult terrain**. The stream is **greater difficult terrain**.

**Coquecigrue:** BB 21, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- It has animal intelligence. It isn't evil, and it isn't hungry enough to take on a well-armed party. It wants to build its nest.
- The PCs' approach startles it. It lets out a **mind-bending warble of alarm**, even if nobody provoked it. It doesn't realize the warble is aggressive.

**Peaceful option:** the PCs look past the warble, stay calm, and offer **colorful objects as tribute**.
- **DC 30 Diplomacy** gets them passage. Normally it can't be a target for Requests, since it has no language.
- With **Wild Empathy** or something similar, it's **DC 20**, even though it isn't an animal.
- If befriended and paid, it energizes **prismatic feathers**. They are worth the full Monster Parts of the coquecigrue and the ghosts.

**If the PCs attack:** two **prismatic ghosts** rise from the bones to defend it. They were bound to it in death.
- **Prismatic Ghosts (2):** p. 392, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.

**Tactics:**
- Coquecigrue: it keeps its distance and uses **prismatic spittle** rather than its weaker beak.
- Ghosts: melee with **red anger**. They use **green flexibility** against PCs out of reach.

**Treasure:**
- Mostly muddy clothing and feathers.
- **DC 25 Perception** while Searching, or *detect magic*, finds a ***stormfeather*** soaked in magic (*Secrets of Magic*). A friendly coquecigrue doesn't know about it and lets them take it.
- A ***clandestine cloak***. It looks like a plain gray scrap, and the coquecigrue ignores it.
- **Without Monster Parts:** a hidden cloak pocket holds gems worth **270 gp**.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Reporting back earns Ruby's vermilion scales, now or at the end of Chapter 11.

### Event 26 — Rainstorm

Two hours after Event 25, a sudden downpour hits. If Event 25 was skipped, it's two and a half hours after Pliticori. The rain isn't deadly, but it cuts visibility and movement (p. 279).
- **One PC** rolls **DC 22 Survival** to build a quick shelter.
- **Failure:** all PCs are **fatigued** going into Event 27.
- The rain lasts **one hour**. It leaves the ground soaked and full of puddles.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It shows nature is still a force outside the desert. It also sets up Event 27's mud and water.

### Event 27 — Patchwork Predator *(Moderate 9)*

Two hours after the rain, in thick forest (**difficult terrain**), the PCs hear a sad cry for help. It is the lure of a **vitavore**, one of the jungle's deadliest predators. The PCs can't see far, so they must get close. That's what the vitavore wants (p. 279–281). Map: p. 280, the **Patchwork Nest**. The PCs enter from the southwest.

Read aloud:

> *As you make your way into a particularly dense and waterlogged portion of the jungle, filled with all sorts of trees, bushes, and shrubs, you suddenly hear a soft and plaintive cry in the distance. "Is someone there? Please... help me!" An unsettling patch of jungle lies just beyond the source of the cries—a perfectly circular area of dead foliage.*

**Terrain:**
- **The stream** (south edge): 15 feet wide, deep, and fast. **Swim DC 20**. It flows west to east at **40 feet per round**. The waterlogged banks, 10 feet on each side, are **greater difficult terrain**.
- **The log** across the stream: slippery and loose. **DC 20 Acrobatics** to Balance. **Critical failure:** the log floats off into the current with the PC on it. Nobody else can use it.
- **Everywhere else:** thick foliage, **difficult terrain**.
- **Northeast corner:** a circle of dead foliage from Energy Siphon. It is **not** difficult terrain.

**Vitavore:** p. 405, Creature 11. Initiative: Perception +22. Monster Parts 175/390/720 gp.
- It waits for some PCs to approach the cry. It ambushes once they're slowed down, like in the river or stuck in a bog.
- It knows every mud bog. It avoids them and baits PCs into them.
- It uses **Energy Siphon** first. Then it uses **tendril** or **carrion toss**, based on distance, on the tastiest and easiest PCs.
- **Energy Siphon kills all ordinary plants** in its area. It doesn't harm plant PCs or companions. This clears difficult terrain and **reveals any mud bogs**.
- If it faces death next round, it reluctantly uses **Expend Reservoir**.
- It is proud of its twisted "art" made from its victims' corpses. **Criticizing the craftsmanship** might bait it into a tactical mistake.

**Hazard: Mud Bogs (8)** *(Hazard 3, complex environmental)*. They are at the **B** squares. Alone they are harmless at this level. Together with the river, they cramp movement. These features give **20 XP** total.
- **Stealth** +12 (trained). If the surface is disturbed, it's −10, and no proficiency is needed. Each is a 15-foot-wide patch of water and sand.
- **Disable:** **DC 18 Survival (trained)** to disturb the surface.
- **Submerge (free action).** **Trigger:** a Huge or smaller creature walks onto it. **Effect:** the creature sinks to its **waist**. The bog rolls initiative if it hasn't yet.
- **Routine (1 action):** each creature in it sinks one step. Waist goes to **neck**. Neck goes to **pulled under**, where it must [hold its breath](gm_chapter_08.md#holding-your-breath-and-suffocation).
- **Getting out:** **DC 20 Athletics** to Swim. At neck or worse, a success raises the creature one step. At waist, a success moves it 5 feet. A **critical failure** sinks it a step. Swimming out frees it, and it is **prone** beside the patch. Allies can Aid (usually with a rope). They can also pull with their own DC 20 Athletics, with the same results.
- **Reset:** it still swallows walkers. The surface hides again only after it settles for 24 hours.

**Treasure:** a ***ring of wizardry* (type I)** on one of the fingers sprouting from its body. **Without Monster Parts:** more rings on more of those fingers, **390 gp** total.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the hardest fight of the chapter):** A level 11 foe vs. party level 9 is 80 XP, Moderate. But the terrain splits the party up and can drown them. Fatigue from Event 26 makes it worse. The book lets the PCs rest after this fight, before Chapter 11. So let them spend resources freely.
