## The chapter in one minute

The royals send the PCs after the cult and its victims. From the Crawling Caves and Okapi Hills they cross the [Crackbeak Salt Desert](gm_glossary.md#crackbeak-salt-desert) in three days (glare, saltstorm, burrowers, salt stalkers), find four barricaded cultists and two captives in a cliffside ruin, and may detour for the Salt Stone. In the Drowned Jungle, galtzagorri scouts lead them to Pliticori and the vermilion dragon [Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby), who keeps the captives while the PCs push on.

**By the end the PCs have:**
1. **Crossed the salt desert** (80 XP, p. 247).
2. **Rescued Re'tkalis and Shalzan** at the Cliffside Ruin (area W).
3. **Learned the cult's destination** (cultists or map), and that a "Lady of Bone and Bounty" leads the ritual.
4. **Met Peerless Ruby** and left the captives in Pliticori.
5. Optionally: the **Salt Stone** (area X), the living blade **Aranelle** (W14), Ruby's coquecigrue reward (Event 25).

**Party level:** **8** to **9**; milestone is **meeting Peerless Ruby** (p. 11).

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** It strips the festival heroes to mounts, rations, and skill. The desert tests survival, the ruin shows cultists who don't know what they serve, and Pliticori gives a harbor, an ally, and Chapter 11's ride home.

A **pursuit on a schedule**. The book's **Pursuit Timeframe** (p. 245):

| Event 18: A Source of Water | 1 |
| Area U: Desert Oasis | 1 |
| Event 19: It Came From Below | 1 (night) |
| Event 20: Fallen and Abandoned | 2 |
| Event 21: Saltstorm | 2 |
| Event 22: Fatal Foliage | 2 |
| Area V: Saline Canyon | 2 |
| Area W: Cliffside Ruin | 3 |
| Area X: Salt Den (optional) | 4 |
| Event 23: On a Hundred Skittering Legs | 4 |
| Event 24: Fairy Lights | 4 |
| Pliticori Village | 4 (night) |
| Event 25: Rainbow Nest | 5 |
| Event 26: Rainstorm | 5 |
| Event 27: Patchwork Predator | 5 |
| Area Y: Embattled Caverns | 6 (Chapter 11) |

**The two halves:**

| Part | Pages | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crackbeak Salt Desert** | 245–268 | 48 miles. Morning Survival checks, environmental dangers, four wilderness fights, the Cliffside Ruin, optional Salt Den | Fixed, by day |
| **The Drowned Jungle** | 268–281 | 18 miles. Two fights, galtzagorri scouts, Pliticori, an optional quest, the vitavore | Fixed, by day. Event 25 optional |

**Travel times (p. 246, 268):**
- **Desert:** 4 days on foot at Speed 20 or 25; **3 days** mounted. The Salt Den adds 2 hours into a 4th day; Pliticori is still reached by the 4th night.
- **Jungle:** the rest of the entry day plus one more, mounted.
- **Flight:** at this level, too short-lived to beat the mounts. It aids scouting, but aerial tracking is harder, and spotting landmarks like the stormspire (U3) from the air takes **DC 25 Perception** in the glare. In the post-Event 22 canyons it helps only if the PC stays up long enough to see the maze (p. 246).

> **💡 GM tip — describe the transitions:** The book asks for each biome change to be felt (scrubland, dunes, salt pan, slot canyons, jungle; p. 245). A sentence or two at each sells the journey better than a map.

> **💡 GM tip — pacing:** Losing steam? Run Track checks, glare saves, and Event 18 in a minute each. Spend the time on the oasis, the saltstorm, the ruin, and Pliticori.

### Encounter math at levels 8 and 9

Chapter 1's table stops at ±2 levels. This chapter goes to −4:

| Creature level vs. party | −4 | −3 | −2 | −1 | same | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XP | 10 | 15 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 | 80 | 120 | 160 |

**Worked examples (level 8 in the desert, 9 in the jungle after Ruby):**
- **U2:** four aurochs (level 4, 10 each) + stormbringer (level 8, 40) = 80, **Moderate 8**.
- **W2:** four scallywags (level 5, 15 each) = 60, **Low 8**.
- **X3:** four salt stalkers (10 each) + salt mother (level 10, 80) = 120, **Severe 8**.
- **Event 25:** coquecigrue (level 8, 30) + two prismatic ghosts (level 6, 15 each) = 60, **Low 9**.

### Accomplishment XP

**Minor** 10, **moderate** 30, **major** 80. This chapter: desert crossing **80 XP** (major, on reaching the jungle); saltstorm **30 XP**; Ruby's respect **30 XP**; jungle crossing **30 XP** (on reaching the mountain).

### Track, Sense Direction, and Subsist

- **Track** (trained only): normally half speed with rechecks; here **one check per day** ([Charting a course](#charting-a-course-the-chases-track-check)).
- **Sense Direction:** in the jungle, **DC 20** for PCs who know their destination, instead of Track at DC 30.
- **Subsist:** **−5 penalty** while exploring unless a PC has a workaround (p. 248). **Forager** matters in Event 17.

### Charting a course (the chase's Track check)

Start of **Day 1** and **Day 2**: one PC **trained in Survival** rolls to Track (p. 246). The book says it isn't a normal Track check, so Tracking-based abilities may not work as usual; your call.

- **DC:** **20**, **+2 per day that passes** (critical-failure days count). Day 1 DC 20, Day 2 DC 22.
- **No check on Day 3** (Day 4 with area X): Event 21 erases the trail, but area W is close.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | Track the cultists, learn **two clues** (below), revealed when you choose, and gain **+1 circumstance bonus to initiative** that day |
| **Success** | Efficient path, **one clue** |
| **Failure** | Wander an hour, then may retry. All PCs **fatigued** until a full night's rest. Already fatigued: treat as critical failure |
| **Critical failure** | Lost. Pick a **trouble** (below). The stretch takes **two days**, with no new check for the extra day |

**What a critical failure shifts:** Day 1 splits into Events 17–18, then area U and Event 19. Day 2 splits into Events 20–21, then Event 22 and area V.

**Clues** (p. 247), or invent your own:
- **Unusual Footprints:** slower walkers fell behind into smaller groups; pacing marks around camps, signs of a scuffle.
- **Campsite:** an old cult camp. The cultists gain a little daily; the layout shows organized kidnappers far outnumbering their victims.
- **Ribbon:** from Atriella's festival outfit, tied to a stone column, visible only from the PCs' side. She's leaving clues.
- **Religious Symbol:** a lost [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth) symbol, a modern take on the Razorback iconography.

**Troubles** (p. 247):
- **Severe Sunburn:** the PCs are **enfeebled 1** and **clumsy 1**, 2 days.
- **Salt Ticks:** 2 days of a **DC 5 flat check** per **manipulate** action (somatic spells too), or scratching disrupts it.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the Religious Symbol clue:** The book cites Razorback (Chapter 8) "and the cult rooms in the caves in Chapter 10," but this is Chapter 10. Mention Razorback and any cult rooms your PCs have seen.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The ribbon shows Atriella alive and resisting, so Chapter 11's reveal that Kulupi is free and in charge hits harder.

### Glare and salt storms

Desert dangers (p. 247):

**Glare.** Before each **daylight** encounter, each PC: **DC 20 Fortitude** or **dazzled** for it. *Penumbral shroud* and the like prevent it.

**Detecting a salt storm.** **DC 20 Survival**, trained only.

**Minor salt storm.** Over 10 minutes unsheltered: **DC 20 Fortitude** or **fatigued**.

**Severe salt storm.** Unsheltered creatures:
- take **2d6 slashing** each round;
- roll **DC 25 Fortitude** each minute or are **blinded** for 1 minute;
- unless holding their breath, swallow salt: **fatigued** until they drink **2 days' worth of water**.

**Building a shelter (Shelter Points).**
- Storm spotted early: **two checks per PC**; otherwise one before it hits.
- **5 rounds** each, PCs only: **DC 22 Athletics, Crafting, or Survival**; DC 20 fitting Lore, DC 25 far-fetched method.
- **Critical success** +2. **Success** +1. **Failure** +0. **Critical failure** −1.
- Blinded: **−2 circumstance penalty**.

| Shelter Points earned | Result |
|---|---|
| Fewer than half the number of PCs | Shelter **blows away**. Reset to 0 and retry, 5 rounds per set of attempts, until at least half. Then mild-storm effects inside |
| Between half the number of PCs and the number of PCs | **Compromised.** Mild-storm effects inside |
| More than the number of PCs | Storm effects **negated** |

**Four PCs:** 0–1 blown away; 2–4 mild-storm rules; 5 or more safe.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Shelter Point threshold:** The book says "half of the number of PC successes," then "half the number of PCs." The table uses the latter, which fits the passage. Decide before the session.

> **💡 GM tip:** An unseen severe storm costs 2d6 a round during building. Prompt a Survival-trained PC for the DC 20 check as you describe the Event 21 cloud.

### Create a Diversion

W2 scallywags can use it for Diverting Finisher. Deception vs. Perception DCs; success hides you from those creatures until your turn ends or you do anything but Step, Hide, or Sneak.

### Improved Grab and Constrict

W12 astringent armors.
- **Improved Grab:** Grab as a **free action** after hitting with the listed Strike.
- **Constrict:** one action; every grabbed creature takes damage, **basic Fortitude** save.

Attach is a *Battlezoo Bestiary* ability; read BB 13 before the fight.

### Burrowing foes

The drillworm (Event 19) and pistolweeds (Event 22) attack from below and escape underground. Most PCs can't follow, so expect fights to end in retreats; the book gives full Monster Parts either way.

### Aranelle's curse

Mental. A wielder rolls **DC 27 Will**; on a failure she imposes a *geas* ([W14](#w14--warriors-lament-moderate-8) has the terms).

### Familiars

A PC whose class or feats grant a familiar may bond a **baby salt stalker** in X2 after combat: normal rules, **always the climber ability** (p. 267).

### Dubious Knowledge

Pliticori's Tall Tales give every Gather Information check there the **Dubious Knowledge** effect: a failure (not critical) yields one true and one false fact, unlabeled.

### Peerless Points

Ruby's audience subsystem (p. 273–275).

- **Two checks per PC** answering "why are you worthy to enter my domain?"
- **Options:** DC 26 Deception (fabricate), DC 24 Diplomacy (earnest appeal), DC 22 Performance (exploits), DC 24 Intimidation (stakes, monsters slain), or DC 28 **any other skill** the player justifies (esoteric knowledge, a physical feat, a crafted item).
- **Aeternal dragon** and **galtzagorri** PCs: **+2 circumstance bonus**.
- **Critical success** +2. **Success** +1. **Critical failure** −1. No failure line in the book, so failure adds nothing.
- **Sovala and Wisp's trust** (Event 24) starts the party at **2 Peerless Points**.

| Peerless Points | Result |
|---|---|
| Fewer than the number of PCs | Hospitality, but **unimpressed**. No one shares cult news. Skip to Tales and Refreshments |
| At least the number of PCs | **Respect.** Puppet tale, and they may question her captive. **30 XP** |
| More than 1.5 times the number of PCs | **Delighted.** As respect, plus a ***phoenix flask*** (*Secrets of Magic*) from her condensed magic |

**Four PCs:** 8 checks (+2 for scouts' trust); respect at 4, flask at 7.

## Scene 1 — Royal Request

- **Where:** Castle Rumplank, or a messenger on the road.
- **Who:** [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather) and [Queen Maxelle](gm_glossary.md#queen-maxelle-redfeather).
- **Goal:** the PCs accept and get mounts and supplies (p. 244).

**Two ways in:**
- **Pushed on through the caves:** a messenger brings mounts, provisions, and good wishes. The day is nearly over, so a combat-free first night, then the desert events.
- **Returned to the capital** (likelier after the festival): the royals call an audience.

**Read aloud (audience):**

> *The king and queen of Rumplank have eyes rimmed with red. Though they've since cleared their eyes, they've both been crying recently. Nonetheless, they look at you with resolved gazes.*
>
> *"Thank you for finding the trail," the queen speaks, "We hope you will be willing to follow that trail and continue to search for our missing children. They aren't the only ones who appear to have gone missing, either. The Chromatic Queen is already yours, and it's certainly an option for you to give up here, but we dearly hope you'll choose to continue onward. If you're willing to search, we'll provide you with mounts and a magic bag filled with as many nonperishable provisions as we can fit, enough for all of you and for any of the missing people you can find and recover. With the city in recovery from the fires, it'll take some time to liquidate any more funds from our royal assets and holdings, but we assure you, you will be richly rewarded for your bravery on your return. Just follow the trail onward and see where it leads. If you find some of the kidnapping victims and they aren't all together, use your best judgment as to whether it's safe to send them back unescorted or have them continue with you until you find the rest. Our priority is our children, but, of course, we hope you can save everyone."*

**What they provide:**
- **Mounts:** twice the PCs plus tag-along NPCs, to rotate or lend to victims.
- **A *bag of holding* type II** with **250 weeks of rations** and **250 filled waterskins**, more than enough for every victim.
- Little information beyond descriptions of some missing people from family messages.

> **💡 GM tip — the victims' names:** The book gives no full list on purpose: most captives are destined for sacrifice, and knowing them first is depressing. If players attach to certain victims, swap them in for the area W captives (p. 245).

**Into the desert:** about **five hours** through the rocky Crawling Caves with horses, NPCs, gear. Read:

> *The winding tunnel finally ends, depositing you on a rocky hillside covered in shrubs and herbs, some of which seem unusual and possibly rare, even at a glance. This scrubland lies at the margin of Goldcrop Island's infamous desert, and it is into that very desert that the abductors' trail leads. Crossing the desert will be a true test of your endurance, combat ability, and survival skills!*

### What the PCs can know about the desert

The [Crackbeak Salt Desert](gm_glossary.md#crackbeak-salt-desert), west of Rumplank: scrubland, dunes, a huge cracked salt pan, slot canyons. The pan was a river-fed lake until seismic shifts diverted the rivers; evaporation built the crust and saltstorms spread it yearly. Now, in the dry season, a few salty pockets remain, some opened by burrowing **drillworms** (p. 387).

**Recall Knowledge:** **DC 25 Nature** or **DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** (p. 246).
- **Success:** the facts above.
- **Critical success:** plus the drillworms' role, and a separate Recall Knowledge check on drillworms.
- **Critical failure:** the PC believes the desert is **sunsand**, a rare magical sand that soaks up any healing spell cast within 10 feet.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Sunsand is false and healing works. A believer may hold back healing; let the critical failure do its job.

## Scene 2 — Desert, Day 1

Open with the [Track check](#charting-a-course-the-chases-track-check) (DC 20).

### Event 17 — Scrubland Gathering *(resources)*

Rocky east-edge scrubland of salt-tolerant plants. Few Rumplankers come this far, so some are valuable, as a Survival-trained PC notices early (p. 248).

**Gathering on the move:**
- One check, early in the day: a PC **trained in Survival** rolls **DC 17 Survival** to Subsist (**−5 penalty** while exploring).
- **Success:** a **bag of rythrian root**, restorative, ideal for elixirs like the *elixir of life*.
- **Critical success:** plus a safely stored **sack of witchclaw branches**, a thorny bush whose toxin refines into a paralytic poison.
- One of each per party, however many succeed. **Forager** doubles a PC's haul.

> **🧩 Sets up — Defense special resources (Ch 12, p. 315–316):** Write both down.
> - **Rythrian root:** **Medic** → **Physicker**; the district gains 50 extra maximum Hit Points instead of 25.
> - **Witchclaw:** **Arrow Tower** → **Poison Tower**; damaged armies take 1 extra damage at the end of each round, ignoring resistant, but not undead or construct armies.
> - Or spend either for **10 Resource Points**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Forager vs. the resource table:** The Chapter 12 table caps it at one bag of root and one stack of branches. Decide whether Forager's extra counts.

### Event 18 — A Source of Water

An hour past the scrubland, shortly before midday. Read aloud:

> *About an hour after you leave the scrublands, you notice a strange collection of plants in the distance. The plants have long stems tipped with colorful, round pods.*

**Waterweed:** deep roots store groundwater in underground bulbs and push salt into hard stem-tip pods.

**Recall Knowledge, DC 15 Nature** (p. 248):
- **Critical success:** waterweed, plus its carnivorous lookalike **pistolweed** (never passes up a meal) and the cues that tell them apart. None here is pistolweed.
- **Success:** waterweed only.
- **Failure:** neither.
- **Critical failure:** they think waterweed is poisonous to drink from, and its pods are valuable to artists.

**Digging a well:** both plants mean groundwater; **2 hours** for a well. Not needed yet.

> **💡 GM tip — the map trick:** The book says to lay out a battle map like Event 22's (p. 253) though nothing attacks, so the map won't signal danger when the scene repeats.

### Area U — Desert Oasis

Three-quarters through the day, salty dunes hide an oasis of clear, fish-filled fresh water, kept pure by an ancient **water purification device** in a small stone **stormspire** (p. 249). Map: p. 249. The PCs enter from the northeast.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — where the spire stands:** Area text: near the **edge**. U1 read-aloud: the **center**. Go with the map.

**Recall Knowledge about the architecture:** **DC 25 Crafting**, **DC 20 Society**, or **DC 15 Indigo Isles History Lore**.
- **Critical success:** **Jirigati**, multi-ancestry, semi-nomadic magical inventors who lived here until a few centuries ago; their devices still turn up locally. Plus **+1 circumstance bonus** to Investigate the U3 device or Disable the U3 hazard.
- **Success:** as above, no bonus.
- **Failure:** nothing.
- **Critical failure:** the PC thinks it matches the Moonshadow Island and Razorback Island cult architecture.

#### U1 — Dune's Crest

**Difficult terrain** dunes hide the oasis until the ridge. Read aloud:

> *A series of sand dunes break up the desert's otherwise flat expanse. From the top of a ridge amidst the dunes, you see a refreshing-looking oasis with a variety of different fish swimming through the water. Numerous plants sprout from the oasis's tranquil shore, and large aurochs roam through the area, eyeing you warily from the distance. An ornate stone spire, still standing despite the signs of significant age, stands at the oasis's center.*

- **Down** the ridge: difficult terrain. Back **up**: **greater difficult terrain**.
- Attacked from the ridge, the herd takes cover behind trees or scatters along the oasis.

#### U2 — Water's Shore *(Moderate 8)*

> *Numerous plants sprout from the tranquil shores of the oasis.*

**Runebranded aurochs** led by a **stormbringer** the tower's magic changed. They eat shore plants plus electricity it channels from the tower (Herd Conductivity). Salt stalkers made them territorial (p. 250).

**Creatures:**
- **Runebranded Aurochs (4):** BB 79, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.
- **Runebranded Stormbringer (1):** variant of BB 79, Creature 8, stat block below.

**Tactics:**
- Unbefriended (Wild Empathy, say), the herd attacks.
- Stormbringer: **Herd Conductivity**, **Electrocute**, then **Trample**. The rest Trample with **Storm Step**.
- Stormbringer **retreats at 30 HP**, ceding territory; the rest back down only after it falls or retreats.
- They **don't chase** fleeing PCs.

**Peaceful option:** befriended or backed down without a kill, the stormbringer gives an **electric mote** worth the **full Monster Parts value of every aurochs** in the encounter. No DC given; your call.

**Runebranded Stormbringer** — Creature 8, rare N Huge animal, p. 251
- **Perception** +18; low-light vision, scent (imprecise) 30 feet
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +16, Athletics +18
- **Str** +6, **Dex** +4, **Con** +5, **Int** −4, **Wis** +2, **Cha** +2
- **AC** 26; **Fort** +19, **Ref** +16, **Will** +14; **HP** 165; **Resistances** electricity 10
- **Buck** (reaction) DC 26. **Speed** 35 feet
- **Melee** hoof +20 (reach 10 feet), 2d8+8 bludgeoning plus 1d6 electricity
- **Melee** horns +20 (reach 15 feet, deadly 1d8), 2d6+8 piercing plus 1d6 electricity
- **Electrocute** (2 actions; primal, electricity, evocation): **80-foot line**, **9d6 electricity**, **DC 26 basic Reflex**. Can't use it again for 1d4 rounds.
- **Herd Conductivity** (1 action): charges its horns and every runebranded aurochs' in a **60-foot emanation**. Until the end of its next turn, all deal **+1d6 electricity** on Strikes and gain Forking Critical.
- **Forking Critical:** a critical Strike arcs electricity to up to **2 more creatures** of its choice within 10 feet of the target.
- **Trample** (3 actions): Medium or smaller, hoof, **Reflex DC 23**.
- **Monster Parts** 64/140/250 gp.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Electrocute:** An 80-foot line at 9d6 can catch several PCs. Watch how they line up.

### Peerless Quest

**Why doesn't she go herself?** She fears she's no match and hates admitting it, but the village outranks her pride. Pressed, she sends the villagers away and confesses, **shrinking to housecat size**; praise or encouragement quickly restores her to Medium.

**Peerless Mystery:** asked about the size-changing, she credits a capricious genie of the winds.

> **🔒 GM-only:** It's from a failed ritual to become an [aeternal dragon](gm_glossary.md#aeternal-dragon) decades ago. She embraces it and won't retry *lux aeterna* or remove it. The PCs see her grow huge at the end of Chapter 11 (p. 304).

**Leaving:**
- Quest accepted: **Event 25** on departure. Otherwise skip to **Event 27** (saves only half an hour).
- As they pack, **Re'tkalis and Shalzan ask to stay**; Ruby promises to protect them until it's time to return to Rumplank.

**Quest reward:** defeat or calm the coquecigrue and report back for a **bag of vermilion scales** from her latest molt (**275 gp** without Monster Parts). If they don't return right away, she most likely hands it over at the end of Chapter 11 (p. 304).

**Peerless Ruby's Gift** — Monster Parts 125/275/500 gp
- **Eligible Refinements:** weapons (bludgeoning, piercing), armor (medium or heavy), shield, perception item, skill items (Acrobatics, Athletics, Cooking Lore, Deception, Diplomacy, Nature, Performance, Stealth).
- **Eligible Imbued Properties:** bane, charisma, chaotic, good, energy resistant (fire), fire, sensory (darkvision, scent), strength, wild, winged.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** A safe place for the captives, a friendly face after days of hostility, and confirmation of who they're chasing, plus their ride home and Act 3 ally. The puppet show mirrors Rumplank's Poppy legends: a real event inflated into myth.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Ruby flies the PCs to Rumplank at the end of Chapter 11 (p. 304); in Chapter 12 she greets X'na'zed as an old acquaintance and helps defend the city. How they treated her colors both.

### Concluding milestone — level 9

**Meeting Peerless Ruby:** the PCs **level up to 9** (p. 11). Under milestone leveling, do it after the audience, before leaving Pliticori.

## Scene 8 — Jungle, Day 5

### Event 25 — Rainbow Nest *(Low 9)*

*Optional: only if the PCs took Peerless Ruby's quest.*

About an hour from Pliticori, a crunchy bone pile at the river's edge; across the water, a riot of color: a nest of torn tents, clothing, and linens with an odd creature on top (p. 277–279). Map: p. 278. The PCs enter from the east.

**Terrain:** bone pile **difficult terrain**; stream **greater difficult terrain**.

**Coquecigrue** — BB 21, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- Animal intelligence; not evil, not hungry enough for a well-armed party. It wants to build its nest.
- Startled by the PCs' approach, it lets out a **mind-bending warble of alarm** even unprovoked, not realizing that's aggressive.

**Peaceful option:** look past the warble, stay non-aggressive, offer **colorful objects as tribute**:
- **DC 30 Diplomacy** gets passage, though without language it normally can't be a target for Requests.
- **DC 20** with **Wild Empathy** or similar, though it isn't an animal.
- Befriended and paid, it energizes **prismatic feathers** worth the full Monster Parts of the coquecigrue and the ghosts.

**If the PCs attack:** two **prismatic ghosts** bound to it in death rise from the bones to defend it.
- **Prismatic Ghosts (2):** p. 392, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.

**Tactics:**
- Coquecigrue: distance and **prismatic spittle** over its weaker beak.
- Ghosts: melee with **red anger**; **green flexibility** against PCs out of reach.

**Treasure:**
- Mostly muddy clothing and feathers.
- **DC 25 Perception** while Searching, or *detect magic*: a magic-soaked ***stormfeather*** (*Secrets of Magic*). A friendly coquecigrue, unaware, lets them take it.
- A ***clandestine cloak***, a plain gray scrap it ignores.
- **Without Monster Parts:** a hidden cloak pocket holds gems worth **270 gp**.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Reporting back earns Ruby's vermilion scales, now or at the end of Chapter 11.

### Event 26 — Rainstorm

Two hours after Event 25 (two and a half after Pliticori if skipped): a sudden, non-deadly downpour that cuts visibility and movement (p. 279).
- **One PC**, **DC 22 Survival**, for a quick shelter.
- **Failure:** all PCs **fatigued** into Event 27.
- **One hour**, leaving soaked, puddled ground.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** Nature stays a force outside the desert too, and it sets up Event 27's mud and water.

### Event 27 — Patchwork Predator *(Moderate 9)*

Two hours after the rain, in thick forest (**difficult terrain**), a plaintive cry for help: the lure of a **vitavore**, one of the jungle's deadliest predators. Visibility forces the PCs close, as the vitavore wants (p. 279–281). Map: p. 280, the **Patchwork Nest**. The PCs enter from the southwest.

Read aloud:

> *As you make your way into a particularly dense and waterlogged portion of the jungle, filled with all sorts of trees, bushes, and shrubs, you suddenly hear a soft and plaintive cry in the distance. "Is someone there? Please... help me!" An unsettling patch of jungle lies just beyond the source of the cries—a perfectly circular area of dead foliage.*

**Terrain:**
- **The stream** (south edge): 15 feet wide, deep, fast, **Swim DC 20**, flowing west to east at **40 feet per round**. Waterlogged banks 10 feet each side: **greater difficult terrain**.
- **The log** across: slippery, loose, **DC 20 Acrobatics** to Balance. **Critical failure:** it floats off with the PC into the current, gone for everyone.
- **Everywhere else:** thick foliage, **difficult terrain**.
- **Northeast corner:** an Energy Siphon circle of dead foliage, **not** difficult terrain.

**Vitavore** — p. 405, Creature 11. Initiative: Perception +22. Monster Parts 175/390/720 gp.
- Waits for some PCs to approach the cry, then ambushes once they're hampered (in the river, stuck in a bog).
- Knows every mud bog, avoids them, baits PCs in.
- **Energy Siphon** first, then **tendril** and **carrion toss** by distance to the tastiest, easiest PCs.
- **Energy Siphon kills all mundane plants** in its area (not plant PCs or companions), clearing difficult terrain and **revealing any mud bogs**.
- Facing death next round, it reluctantly uses **Expend Reservoir**.
- Proud of its twisted "art" made from victims' corpses: **criticizing the craftsmanship** might bait a tactical mistake.

**Hazard — Mud Bogs (8)** *(Hazard 3, complex environmental)*. At the **B** squares. Harmless alone at this level, but with the river they cramp movement. **20 XP** total for these features.
- **Stealth** +12 (trained), or −10 with no minimum proficiency if the surface is disturbed. A 15-foot-wide patch of water and sand.
- **Disable:** **DC 18 Survival (trained)** to disturb the surface.
- **Submerge (free action).** **Trigger:** a Huge or smaller creature walks onto it. **Effect:** it sinks to its **waist**, and the bog rolls initiative if it hasn't.
- **Routine (1 action):** each creature in it sinks a step: waist to **neck**, neck to **pulled under**, where it must [hold its breath](gm_chapter_8.md#holding-your-breath-and-suffocation).
- **Getting out:** **DC 20 Athletics** to Swim raises a creature a step from neck or worse, or moves it 5 feet from waist. **Critical failure** sinks it a step. Swimming out frees it, **prone** beside the patch. Allies can Aid (usually with a rope) or pull with their own DC 20 Athletics, same results.
- **Reset:** still swallows walkers, but the surface hides again only after settling 24 hours.

**Treasure:** a ***ring of wizardry* (type I)** on one of the fingers sprouting from its body. **Without Monster Parts:** more rings on more body-fingers, **390 gp** total.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the hardest fight of the chapter:** Level 11 vs. party level 9 is 80 XP, Moderate, but the terrain splits and drowns, and Event 26 fatigue makes it worse. The book lets them rest after this fight before Chapter 11, so let them spend freely.
