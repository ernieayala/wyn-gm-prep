## The chapter in one minute

The PCs sail home to Rumplank's delayed [Founder's Day](gm_glossary.md#founders-day) festival. On the way, the [Jewel of the Indigo Isles](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles) fuses into one piece, and it is cursed. [Prismatic Colvi](gm_glossary.md#prismatic-colvi) offers to suppress the curse. The gods [Amon](gm_glossary.md#amon) and [Castilli](gm_glossary.md#castilli) appear. Castilli breaks a cult ritual that was working on the city guard. The PCs fight saboteurs and arsonists. Meanwhile, [Princess Atriella](gm_glossary.md#princess-atriella), [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi), Colvi, and the Jewel vanish. The trail leads into the Crawling Caves.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Brought the Jewel home**, likely handing it to Colvi (Scene 1).
2. **Competed in the festival** for prizes and fame (Scene 2).
3. **Met Amon and Castilli** and learned of the ritual (Scene 3).
4. **Beaten the saboteurs and arsonists**, maybe saving the historian Monbak (Scenes 4–5).
5. **Accepted the king's plea** and **found the cult's trail** (Scenes 6–7).

**Party level:** starts at **8**, ends at **8**. The next level comes in Chapter 10, when they meet [Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby).

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** The PCs get one day of glory. Then they lose the Jewel, the prince and princess, and the feeling of winning. The festival shows the city they will defend in Chapter 12. The cult wins because the PCs acted like heroes.

## Part Three setup

Part Three, *Battle at Barnacle Bay*, has no introduction of its own. Its first pages (p. 216–219) open Chapter 9 and set up Chapters 9–12. Read this section first.

### The Jewel comes together (p. 216)

- **Aboard ship:** "good" grog flows. Kulupi gives each PC a bottle of fine rum. He sends word ahead on a magical seabird made with his signet ring.
- **No more marine resources:** the last check was at the end of Part Two.
- **The pieces won't fit**, however the PCs arrange them.
- **About a day out:** any PC wearing a piece feels a growing tug toward the other pieces.
- **An hour before docking:** the pieces fly together and smash out of their containers. They fuse into one glimmering jewel. It hovers, then floats to the ground.

> **🔒 GM-only (what the cult is doing):** As the Jewel forms, the cult's ritual is already under way. It is deep in a mountain on western Goldcrop. The demon [Varagix](gm_glossary.md#varagix-the-glimmering) taught his trusted followers two things. They can command gem-loving shale spitters, and they can raise the corpse of a **shale behemoth** beneath Goldcrop. He wants Poppy's city in rubble and a new nation built on its ashes. After that, he wants the rest of the Isles.

### The curse (p. 217)

Varagix corrupts the Jewel as it combines. He works through **Prince Kulupi as a conduit**. The wearer gains all four gems' abilities. The wearer also gains **all three curses** (**level 20**).

| Curse | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Curse of Ill Fortune** (misfortune) | Roll twice, take the worse, on **all d20 rolls**. |
| **Curse of Festering Wounds** (necromancy) | **Can't recover HP by resting**. Other healing (Treat Wounds, magic) restores only **half**. |
| **Curse of Pliability** (enchantment) | Attitude becomes **friendly** toward every creature that isn't actively hostile. Helpful attitudes stay helpful. Any save against an effect that would make the wearer **controlled** is a critical failure, with no roll. |

**Moving the curse around:**
- If the bearer sets the Jewel down for a moment, the curse stays on that bearer.
- If the bearer strays over **100 feet** from it, it **re-materializes** among the bearer's possessions.
- It passes only to someone who knowingly accepts or takes an object. They don't need to know it's cursed. It can hide in a nonmagical bag or pouch.
- It can't pass to a creature with Intelligence **−4 or less**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The curses come from Varagix through Kulupi. That's why the item block below lists only the Jewel's own powers.

**What Kulupi does about it:**
- If the PCs don't raise the idea, he suggests the curses are another of Poppy's tests.
- He won't carry the Jewel unless persuaded (**DC 24 Diplomacy**) or tricked (**DC 28 Deception**). If they manage it, he admits they were clever and takes it.
- He pushes hard for **Prismatic Colvi**. He says Colvi's theology and the sacred ground of the Temple of Many Colors might lift the curse.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi is the curse's conduit and a cult leader. The reveal comes in Ch 11. Play his suggestion as a caring friend's best advice. That's why it works.

### Jewel of the Indigo Isles *(Item 20)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** worn. **Bulk** negligible. (p. 218)

While invested, you gain all four shards' powers:
- Cast ***earthbind*** or ***ghostly weapon*** (metal or stone weapon only) as an innate occult spell, one per day.
- Breathe water as well as air.
- **Resistance 3 to bludgeoning.**
- Sheds **bright light in a 20-foot radius**.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact): rub it to turn the light on or off.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact), **once per day:** scrape a fingernail across it to make a glittering yellow-orange haze. You are **concealed** for 3 rounds. The glitter gives you away, so you can't Hide or Sneak. You and your gear stay dry underwater, but the haze doesn't let you breathe.

### The cult's plots (p. 218)

Kulupi went with the PCs. His chief co-conspirator, Colvi, would get the Jewel into his own hands in Rumplank.

> **🔒 GM-only (the conspirators don't trust each other):** Colvi suspected the curse. He can also suppress Varagix's curses. He told Kulupi neither fact, so the prince wouldn't look suspicious in front of the PCs. When Kulupi sees the curse, he guesses one of two things. Either Colvi planned for the temple visit, or Colvi didn't foresee it and Kulupi can upstage him. Kulupi's pride sends him to the temple either way. If the PCs skip the temple, Colvi expects the curse to make them desperate to get rid of the Jewel.

The cult runs **three other plots** during the festival:

| Plot | Who | What happens | Where in this doc |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The ritual** | Grand Oracle [Zellura](gm_glossary.md#zellura) (CE female g'mayun ritualist 9, p. 329) and **Raku Darino** (NE male orpok cultist 8), a [Quill & Scroll Academy](gm_glossary.md#quill--scroll-academy) professor | Confuses guards across the city. Cast from the Crawling Caves beneath the city. Meets an unexpected counter | [Scene 3](#scene-3--event-16-divine-visitors) |
| **Arson** | Hired saboteurs and arsonists | Fire attacks across the city | [Scenes 4–5](#scene-4--alleyway-ambush-area-s) |
| **Disappearances** | Cult members and their targets | Cultists leave on their own. The cult abducts inconvenient people, above all **Princess Atriella**. | [Scene 6](#scene-6--into-thin-air) |

> **🧩 Sets up:** Zellura's stat block is on p. 329 (Chapter 12). The cult escapes through the Crawling Caves, and that starts the Chapter 10 chase.

## How the chapter is built

One long day, in two halves:

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **Time for Celebration** (p. 218–220) | Land, visit the Temple of Colors, report to the king and queen | First. Temple and report in either order |
| **Festival Events** (p. 220–232) | Six contests and spectacles | Any number, in about the book's order |
| **Event 16: Divine Visitors** (p. 233) | Amon and Castilli arrive and break the ritual | As the PCs wrap up the festival |
| **Alleyway Ambush, area S** (p. 234–236) | Saboteur scouts, then burning oozes | After Event 16 |
| **Match to a Powderkeg, area T** (p. 237–241) | Arsonists at Treasure Beach and Monbak's house | After area S |
| **Into Thin Air** (p. 241–242) | The king's plea and the secret tunnel | After the fires |
| **The Crawling Caves** (p. 243) | The cult's trail and a guard of nobblers | Last |

**Recommended festival order** (p. 220). The parade is the centerpiece. The Rum Run goes last (*"once everyone is drunk, it's difficult to perform further contests"*):
1. Peerless Ruby Tall Tale Trial
2. Pirate Panache (runs for hours, alongside other events)
3. Show of Skill
4. Grand Parade
5. Poppy's Prize
6. Rum Run

The PCs can time things to enter every event they want. Reorder or cut events to fit your table.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** Running every contest fills a whole session. If your players want action, run two or three contests, then Event 16. The book awards no XP for contests. Cutting them costs nothing on the level curve.

> **💡 GM tip (the turn):** The second half is back-to-back fights with no rest. Let the change in mood land: snowball fight, then ambush.

### Combat dueling

Pirate Panache uses the combat dueling subsystem from the *Pathfinder Gamemastery Guide*. The book reprints it on p. 222.

- **Initiative every round.** Each round, each duelist picks **Deception, Intimidation, or Perception** for initiative.
- **Dueling reactions.** If a duelist is trained or better in the chosen skill, they get that skill's reaction for the round. Neither duelist knows the other's choice.
- **Acting twice in a row** (last, then first) bends "until the start of your next turn" effects. The second actor can dodge those effects that turn. Or, if they win initiative, they can choose to go second next round.
- Familiars, companions, and bystanders get no dueling reactions.

| Reaction | Skill | Trigger | Effect | Bonus against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bullying Press** (flourish) | Intimidation | You attempt a melee Strike, before rolling | On a hit, opponent is **frightened 1** | Perception roller: **frightened 2** |
| **Deceptive Sidestep** (misfortune) | Deception | An opponent's melee Strike hits you (not a critical hit) | Opponent rerolls and takes the second result | Intimidation roller also takes **−2 circumstance** to the reroll |
| **Sense Weakness** | Perception | You attempt a melee Strike, before rolling | Opponent is **flat-footed** against the attack | Deception roller stays **flat-footed until the start of their next turn** |

Rock-paper-scissors: Intimidation beats Perception, Deception beats Intimidation, Perception beats Deception.

**Example:** A PC has Intimidation +18 and Perception +14. They roll Intimidation for initiative and gain Bullying Press. If Master Olo rolled Perception that round, a hit with Bullying Press leaves him frightened 2.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Duels end at **first blood**, so Bullying Press rarely matters. The book says Intimidation can still be worth rolling for the initiative alone, if it's a PC's best modifier.

### Afflictions: alcohol and rowdy river rum

**Afflictions** (stages, onset, re-exposure): see [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#afflictions-and-poison). Each drink is a new exposure.

**Rowdy river rum** (Item 0, alchemical, consumable, drug, ingested, poison; p. 228) follows the *Gamemastery Guide* alcohol rules, with one change. **Failed saves after the first one don't raise the stage.** Only more rum does. A drinker can't recover from the rum's sickened condition while affected.

**Rowdy river rum:** Price 1 cp (or more). Usage held in 1 hand. Bulk L. **Activate** Interact.
- **Saving Throw** DC 12 Fortitude. **Onset** 10 minutes.
- **Stage 1:** +1 item bonus to saves against fear (10 minutes).
- **Stage 2:** flat-footed, +1 item bonus to saves against fear (10 minutes).
- **Stage 3:** clumsy 1, flat-footed, stupefied 2 (10 minutes).
- **Stage 4:** clumsy 2 and sickened 2 (10 minutes).
- **Stage 5:** clumsy 2, sickened 2, and stupefied 2 (10 minutes).
- **Stage 6:** unconscious (8 hours).
- **Stage 7:** death.

**Example:** Two drinks before the race mean two DC 12 Fortitude saves. A level-8 PC likely passes most of them.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (stage 7 is death):** The Rum Run disqualifies anyone who passes out. Judges get them medical attention at once. It also disqualifies anyone who has more than **20 drinks**. That rule came in after a contestant cheated with *delay affliction* and nearly died.

### Rules from earlier chapters

- **Aid:** see [Chapter 5](gm_chapter_05.md#aid). Used in the Show of Skill. Also used for cheating at Poppy's Prize (**Thievery to Aid themselves**).
- **Persistent damage:** see [Chapter 6](gm_chapter_06.md#persistent-damage-and-bleed). Korog's bombs deal 2 persistent fire, or **7 persistent fire** from a lava bomb.
- **Counteracting:** see [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting). The flames from the [explosive barrels](#t2-trashed-archive-moderate-8) have **DC 21 (2nd level)**. A successful 1st-level *quench* works, because 2 is no more than 1 + 1.

## Scene 1 — Homecoming and the Temple of Colors

### Arrival (p. 217–218)

Growing crowds cheer as the PCs sail into harbor. Rumplank's people are here for **Founding Day**, along with visitors from settlements the PCs visited. The royals delayed the festival for days, until the prince and the heroes returned with the Jewel.

**About the festival:**
- A one-day celebration of the city's founding and its founder, Poppy von Barnacle.
- Almost always includes a **tall tale contest**, a **dueling contest**, a **big parade** with floats and a marching band, and the **Rum Run**, a drunken obstacle course.
- **Mystery pies:** hand pies sold without saying what's inside.
- Nobody agrees on the date, so it moves with public opinion. It always falls in **early summer**.

Ashore, the crowd mobs the PCs for stories and drinks. Then an impatient Kulupi clears a way to [Barrel Square](gm_glossary.md#barrel-square) to open the festival. For the rest of the day, the PCs will likely visit the temple, report to the royals, and enjoy the festival. **Kulupi goes with them to the temple if they go there first.**

> **⚠️ Gotcha (names):** **Founding Day** and **Founder's Day** are the same festival. **Temple of Colors** is the [Temple of Many Colors](gm_glossary.md#temple-of-many-colors).

### Temple of Colors (p. 218–219)

- **Who:** Prismatic Colvi, in his temple. Possibly Prince Kulupi.
- **Goal (the cult's):** Colvi gets the Jewel.

Colvi congratulates the PCs. When they tell him about the curse, or when he spots it, he acts surprised and horrified.
- **DC 31 Perception** to Sense Motive: he is more impressed by the curse's power and cleverness than horrified.
- He says the sacred ground of the **inner sanctum** should suppress it. He tells them to enjoy the festival and come back tomorrow to solve Poppy's "fiendish puzzle."
- He urges them to leave the Jewel and go celebrate.

**If they hand it over:** Colvi takes the Jewel and its curse. As he enters the sanctum, he suppresses the curse with a silent prayer to Varagix. To the PCs, this looks like proof the temple works. He thanks them for recovering a storied piece of Rumplank history. He promises to keep it safe.

**Guarding it:**
- **Kulupi present** and a PC worried: Kulupi offers **royal guards**. Colvi makes a show of refusing, saying the temple can defend itself. Then he accepts.
- **Kulupi absent:** Colvi promises he and his most esteemed priests will guard it.

**If the PCs ask for it back:** the full curse returns once Colvi hands it over. It only seems suppressed while he holds it in the sanctum.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Colvi controls the curse completely through silent prayer to Varagix. His refusal of the guards is half real. Humility is anathema to his faith. Accepting help galls him, even help with a scheme.

> **🔒 GM-only (the guards):** Any guards Kulupi posted vanish with Colvi. The signs suggest all of them were kidnapped (p. 241).

### Paranoia and precautions (p. 219)

The plot assumes the cult has the Jewel by the end of the festival. The PCs might still hold it then. If so, the cult goes ahead anyway and trusts it can grab the Jewel later. Colvi leaves a few cultists at the temple to accept it. One of them has learned to suppress the curse. Other routes:

1. **The rush to save the royals** may make the PCs leave it behind.
2. **An ambush in the Crawling Caves.** The PCs might carry the Jewel toward the salt desert. If so, cultists swarm in from the west right after the [nobbler fight](#smuggled-out-of-town-low-8). **Severe 8:** **6 Shivering Behemoth scallywags** (p. 258, Creature 5) and **Kelgrid**, a kragrak cultist (he/him) with a minor knack for magic.
   - Kelgrid's ***scroll of dominate*** targets whoever holds the Jewel. That PC critically fails, with no roll (Curse of Pliability).
   - They aim to grab the Jewel and run, not to kill the PCs, and they fight that way. Kelgrid orders the dominated PC to hand the Jewel over. Then he orders that PC to fight the others to cover the retreat.
   - They know what Chapter 10's area W2 scallywags know. Kelgrid also knows the suppression prayer.
   - The PCs may end up with a Jewel that is uncursed for a while. This can happen three ways: they retake it after Kelgrid suppresses the curse, they talk him into suppressing it, or they learn the prayer. That Jewel has the shard powers only, while the curse absorbs the rest. The book says to assume the suppression lasts the rest of their journey.
3. **At the cult's base:** if they still hold it, Kulupi praises their skill and steals it. This is Royal Betrayal (p. 299), Chapter 11.

**Kelgrid:** Creature 8, variant Shivering Behemoth guard (p. 298)
- **Items:** as the Shivering Behemoth guard, plus a *scroll of dominate*
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +12, Athletics +17, Occultism +13, Stealth +12, Survival +14
- **Str** +6, **Dex** +1, **Con** +4, **Int** +2, **Wis** +3, **Cha** +2
- **Occult Spontaneous Spells** DC 22; **Cantrips (4th)** *ghost sound*, *know direction*

> **📏 Threat math (party level 8):** Six level-5 scallywags are 15 XP each, for 90. Kelgrid (level 8) adds 40. That makes 130, between Severe (120) and Extreme (160). The book says **Severe 8**.

### Royal Report (p. 219–220)

- **Who:** [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather) and [Queen Maxelle](gm_glossary.md#queen-maxelle-redfeather), in private at the palace.
- **Kulupi skips it.** He has festival matters, and he wasn't invited.

The royals are overjoyed. They want the whole story, with a different part from each PC. The king wants every Kulupi story: heroics to brag about, or antics to tease him with. That's why he asked for the report without his son.

**The royals' terms:**
- The PCs own everything they found, **except the Jewel**.
- The PCs are the festival's **main event**. If they don't want to take part, the royals insist. It's part of the deal for the *Chromatic Queen* and the other treasures.
- The transfer happens at an **official ceremony at the end of the festival**.

> **💡 GM tip:** This is the last warm scene with the royals before their children vanish. Let the king laugh at a Kulupi story, so Scene 6 hurts.

## Scene 2 — Founder's Day festival events

Anyone can enter or watch. Visitors can double the city's population. The PCs can enter as many events as you like (p. 220).

> **🧩 Sets up:** The PCs meet the [Indigo Stranger](gm_glossary.md#indigo-stranger), likely after a strong showing in a contest. This party-loving dragon is fascinated by how they rose from the crowd to become famous heroes. He reveals his true form in Chapter 12.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (drunk PCs):** During Event 16, **Amon sobers up** any PC who is too drunk for the coming fights, or passed out. The book says not to punish fun, in the spirit of Rumplank.

### Read aloud (opening the festival)

> *The annual Founder's Day Festival is about to begin! Everywhere around you, the streets are alive with colors, sights, sounds, and smells as people from every ancestry and from across the Indigo Isles and beyond pour into the streets of Rumplank, jostling each other as they check out the stalls of street food, merchandise, games, and other festivities. The first main event for the day is called the Peerless Ruby Tall Tale Trial and is taking place in an open area nearby. The space between here and the contest is anything but empty, with hundreds of excited festival-goers pressing into each other in thick crowds, cheering and competing with each other to be heard over the din. A stout orpok chef is currently winning the volume contest with a steady cry of "Mystery pies! Mystery pies! Come get your mystery pies! Sweet! Savory! Spicy! Salty! Mystery pies!" The aroma of delicious foods blends with the smells of sea breeze and sweaty sailors.*

**Mystery pies** a PC might bite into:
- bitter rubalo with other root vegetables and coconut slices, a classic Rumplank comfort food
- flaky, sweet summer moon-shrimp
- buttery, salty indigo oyster
- gamey [blood mouse](gm_glossary.md#blood-mouse) with savory herbs
- spicy-sweet pikipoki peppers and white pineapples

**Background special effects (p. 220):**
- **Rumplank reveler:** can use **Party Lore** for the Tall Tale Trial, Show of Skill, and Poppy's Prize. They use the **lowest listed DC**.
- **Indigo pirate:** prepares for the official gifting of the *Chromatic Queen* after the festival. The ship is already promised to the PCs.
- **Indigo trailblazer:** the Fellowship of Indigo Trailblazers honors their mapmaking, above all finding the sunken Razorback Isle. The Fellowship raises them to **Trailblazer Superlative** and asks for a lecture series.

### 2A. Peerless Ruby Tall Tale Trial (p. 220–221)

[Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby) is a vermilion dragon from the nearby jungle. Decades ago, during a visit to the festival, Peerless Ruby founded this contest. Boastful Rumplank loved it.

**How it works:**
- Tellers take turns. Each teller works **one element from the end of the previous story** into the start of their own.
- Each teller attempts **DC 20 Performance** for an entertaining story, or **DC 25 Deception** for fibs so big the crowd eats them up.
- **Success:** a colorful jar of rye-melon flower honey.
- **Grand prize, for the best critical success:** a rune aurochs antler horn painted like vermilion dragon scales. It's a **virtuoso handheld instrument worth 100 gp**.
- **Finding the best critical success:** add **5** to each Performance critical success, then compare. The best NPC scored **35**, after adding the 5.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Deception critical successes don't get the +5. They still compete for the horn.

**Ten tall tales to start with** (p. 221). Let the PCs cut in when a tale gives them a good lead-in.
- A coast kragrak sailor hooked a leviathan in the deep trenches. It breached for a second, big as an island.

### 2B. Pirate Panache (p. 221–224)

A dueling tournament to crown the top duelist. It runs for hours alongside other events.

**The sashes**, from lowest to highest: **green, yellow, red, black**. There are seven black sashes, from **dan 1** (lowest) to **dan 7** (highest). The PCs are famous, so they start at **yellow**.

**Challenge rules:**
- If a challenger wins, they **trade sashes** with the higher rank. If the challenger loses, both keep their sashes. The loser can't challenge that duelist again.
- Green may challenge yellow, yellow may challenge red, and red may challenge black **dan 1 or 2**.
- Black sashes may challenge up to **2 dan higher**.
- The **7th dan** can't challenge. They win if nobody takes the sash.

**The PCs' path to victory:**
1. Fend off a green-sash challenger *(optional)*.
2. Challenge a red sash for their red sash.
3. Fend off a yellow-sash challenger *(optional)*.
4. Climb the black sashes to Master Olo. Other PCs, and maybe black-sash NPCs, can challenge them along the way.

**Duel rules:** solo [combat dueling](#combat-dueling) to **first blood**, with **melee weapons only**. No spells, magic items, alchemy, or poisons. A nonmagical flier forfeits if they end a turn in the air.

> **💡 GM tip (skipping fights (book suggestion)):** Green and yellow sashes are no threat. Run one red-sash challenge, then the named NPCs and fellow PCs.

> **💡 GM tip:** You can swap any generic contestant for an NPC the PCs met or an Act 2 crewmate.

#### Contestant stats (p. 223–224)

### 2D. Grand Parade (p. 225–226)

The festival's centerpiece. Many revelers plan their day around it.

> *It's time for the festival's main event, the Grand Parade! An endless procession of costumed dancers, musicians, and a marching band proceed through the city, interspersed with colorfully decorated floats. Some folks have been waiting here for hours, taking all the spots with the best views, and everywhere parents lift children onto their shoulders to see above the crowd. Each float has a different theme and is built by, or on behalf of, a group within Rumplank, whether it's a guild, a union, a school, a neighborhood, or even the royal family. The royal family's float is clearly the most impressive, as they spared no expense on the decorations, and the citizens crane their necks to see Prince Kulupi, Princess Atriella, and their parents wave to the crowd. A sizable contingent of guards march by the royal family's float to keep excitable citizens from coming too close. Throughout the event, children and the young at heart attempt to gather different feathered floral arrangements thrown from each of the floats.*

> **🔒 GM-only (nothing happens here):** No danger has ever touched the Redfeathers at a Grand Parade, and none will this year. If the PCs suspect an attack and investigate, they find no foul play. Kulupi keeps cult activity away from the heavy parade security.

**Collecting floral arrangements** (optional, good for a PC who hasn't shone yet):
- **Three checks.** Each is **DC 15 Party Lore**, or **DC 20** Acrobatics (leap), Diplomacy (coax), Intimidation (bully), Stealth (sneak to a better spot), or Thievery (steal).
- **Critical success:** 2 successes. **Critical failure:** the PC must stop. Maybe they trip, or get caught bullying or robbing a child.
- All participants get at least a few, unless they critically fail the first check.
- **2+ successes:** the envy of every small child.
- **4+ successes:** the **complete set**. The PC ranks among the top collectors. This earns respect, but no money.

### 2E. Poppy's Prize (p. 226–227)

A free tournament of Rumplank's favorite gambling game. It's held at the **Rumpled Feather**, the most popular tavern. The tavern is a painted, converted boathouse owned by [Clamardinia Wakamarsis](gm_glossary.md#clamardinia-wakamarsis). Today's novelty drink is the multicolored **Chromatic Queen**. It starts sweet and kicks hard. Serious players order it without alcohol.

**The favorite:** **Seliles** (CN chochori card shark) is all jovial smiles, whatever the hand. In the final round, Seliles goes quiet to read and bluff.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (pronouns):** The book calls Seliles both "his" and "their" (p. 227). This doc uses they/them.

**Entering:** a 1 gp fee buys **100 chips** (1 cp each). **Ante 5 chips** a round. Out of chips means out. No rebuys.

**Playing it for real:** a pirate-twist poker variant for a standard deck. Full rules are on **p. 381**. Win **three games** to take the tournament. You can play as Seliles against two players, or have all four PCs play each other.

**Playing it with dice instead:** three **Gambling Lore** checks in a row: **DC 20**, **DC 25**, then **DC 30**. Any failure knocks a PC out.
- **Cheating:** a PC can use **Thievery to Aid themselves**. They're disqualified if the Thievery result is below the round's Gambling Lore DC. Spells like *nudge the odds* (*Secrets of Magic*) also work.
- **Final table:** exactly four players. If four or more PCs pass DC 25, the three highest join Seliles. Otherwise, all remaining PCs go, plus Seliles, plus random NPCs to fill the seats.
- **Winning:** the highest PC result past DC 30 wins. The others take runner-up prizes. Reroll ties. If no PC passes DC 30, or you win the final as Seliles, **Seliles wins**.

**Prizes:**
- **Everyone in the final round:** a *cantrip deck* (*Secrets of Magic*). Seliles admires them and asks for adventure stories.
- **Grand prize:** the year's chips, worth **20 gp**, in a ***bag of holding* type I**. It also includes a silver bracelet engraved with the tournament name and year, worth **5 gp**.

### 2F. Rum Run (p. 228–232)

The most popular event. It's a drunken obstacle course, meant as a show with no serious injuries. Children run easier obstacles alongside at the same time. Watching them beat drunk adults is half the fun. Master Olo and Ash enter once their contests are done. NPCs with the party can enter too.

**Rules:**
- **Four obstacles**, each harder than the last.
- Magic, alchemy, and abilities that bypass an obstacle (burrowing, flying) are **banned**. Judges send cheaters back to do it fair and square.
- No attacking contestants or damaging the course. **Disarming a hazard is allowed.** Staff reset it.
- **Each skill check takes one round of the race** (not a combat round). Drinking or putting on rum apparel between obstacles takes no time.
- Racers who pass out from alcohol poisoning are **disqualified**. They get medical help at once.

**Drinking:**
- **Rum drinkers:** two drinks of [rowdy river rum](#afflictions-alcohol-and-rowdy-river-rum) before the start and **two after each obstacle**.
- **Fluffmelon drinkers:** anyone avoiding alcohol, for any reason, drinks **fluffmelon**. It's a sweet coconut-cream and rye-melon drink that upsets the stomach in large amounts.
  - From the **second obstacle**, they wear a **rum suit**: an awkwardly weighted tunic and breeches, plus light-distorting goggles. It makes them **clumsy 1 and stupefied 2** (visual).
  - From the **third obstacle**, a **rum coat** raises clumsy to **2**.
  - Nobody shames this choice. Both options are hilarious.
- More than **20 drinks** of either disqualifies.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (who wears the suit):** A race update on p. 229 says *"anyone not drinking fluffmelon must don a rum suit."* The main rules say **not drinking alcohol**. Treat it as a typo. Fluffmelon drinkers wear the suit, as its purpose implies.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Atriella):** Don't run Princess Atriella in the Rum Run. By now she's being kidnapped.

> **💡 GM tip (NPC racers (book suggestion)):** The Race Update entries stand in for dozens of NPC racers. To roll for a few, use leveled-up party NPCs, or contestants with similar modifiers.

#### Obstacle 1: Walk the Plank

An ever-narrower balance beam. Roll **DC 15**, **DC 20**, then **DC 25 Acrobatics** to Balance. Each success crosses one segment.
- **First segment only:** a running **Long Jump** (**DC 15 Athletics**) works instead. Jumping again on the plank only makes it wobble.
- These are the real Balance and Long Jump actions, so feats and items that boost them apply.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Critical success | Cross two segments (a first-check crit leaves DC 25 for the last) |
| Success | Cross one segment |
| Failure | No progress |
| Critical failure | Splash into the water, lose all progress, spend the next round drinking and climbing back to the start |

**Race update:** the fastest racers (Master Olo, Ash, maybe some PCs) clear it in the best possible time, **2 rounds**.

#### Obstacle 2: Wrestle the Rigging

A mostly horizontal tangle of taut and slack ropes. Racers may move the ropes carefully. They may not cut or damage them.

**Step 1, choose a path:** a **secret** **DC 20 Survival** or **DC 25 Perception** check. It doesn't take a round.

| Result | Successes needed |
|---|---|
| Critical success | 2 |
| Success | 3 |
| Failure | 4 |
| Critical failure | 6 |

Keep the number secret. Tell the PC when they're through.

**Step 2, each round:** **DC 22 Acrobatics** to squeeze through, or **DC 20 Crafting** to adjust the ropes.
- **Critical success:** 2 successes. **Success:** 1. **Failure:** stuck.
- **Critical failure:** hopelessly tangled. The racer spends a round getting free, takes an extra drink, makes a new path check, and starts fresh. The crowd roars.

**Race update:** the leaders (still Olo and Ash) clear both obstacles in **5 rounds**. PCs can do it in as few as 3. Rum-suit wearers add the rum coat now.

#### Obstacle 3: Scale the Mast

Climb a huge mast while wind traps blow at you. Roll **DC 25 Athletics** to Climb. It takes **three successes** to reach the top.
- **Critical success:** 2 successes. **Success:** 1. **Failure:** no progress.
- **Critical failure:** a harmless but embarrassing fall into water and mud. Lose all progress. Spend the next round drinking and returning to the start.

**Disabling the wind trap:** a racer can spend a round on **DC 20 Thievery** to Disable the trap aimed at them. It helps only that racer. Staff reset it at once.
- **Critical success:** the trap now helps. The Climb DC is **10** until they reach the top or fall.
- **Success:** the Climb DC is **15** until they reach the top or fall.
- **Failure:** no effect.
- **Critical failure:** blown off, as if they critically failed a Climb. The crowd loves it.

**Race update:** only Master Olo clears three obstacles, in **8 rounds**. Several NPCs are a round behind. PCs could do it in as few as 5.

#### Obstacle 4: Claim the Sunken Treasure

Dive into still, muddy water full of fake treasures.

1. **Spot a treasure** (takes the turn): **DC 25 Perception**.
   - **Critical success:** spot one close enough to appraise before swimming.
   - **Success:** spot one. Swim next turn.
   - **Failure:** nothing yet.
   - **Critical failure:** fall in the mud. Spend an extra turn getting out and drinking, then search again.
2. **Swim to it:** **DC 10 Athletics**. It takes 2 rounds, or 1 on a critical success.
3. **Is it real?** You roll a **secret DC 15 flat check**. Success means it's real.
4. **Appraise** (optional, secret, no turn, once per treasure): **DC 20 Crafting**. **Success:** they know. **Failure:** unsure. **Critical failure:** wrong answer.
5. **Keep it or drop it.** Drop a suspected fake and search again next turn. Or swim back: **DC 10 Athletics**, 2 rounds, or 1 on a critical success.
6. **Back at the start,** *faerie fire* lights up a real treasure, and the racer finishes. A fake means another Perception check next turn.

Luck stops the same locals from winning every year. Top racers grumble, and the crowd loves upsets.

**Race update and results:**
- Master Olo hits a streak of fakes. The first NPC finishes in **17 rounds**. Make it a crewmate or festival NPC the PCs liked. A second NPC of your choice finishes in **19**. More trickle in after.
- The all-time Rumplank record is **10 rounds**. The best possible time is 8.
- **A PC under 17 rounds wins.** Several PCs can place, in finishing order. A top PC at **18** takes second. At **19**, they tie for second. At **20 or more**, the best they get is a multi-way tie for third.
- Most racers tap out in the end, too drunk or too sick from fluffmelon.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book doesn't cover a PC tying the first NPC at 17. Its tie rule for first place has a judge assign first and second prizes at random.

**Prizes:**
- **1st:** a miniature treasure chest with **100 gp**, average lock.
- **2nd:** a smaller chest with **50 gp**, simple lock.
- **Every finisher** keeps their treasure, worth **1d10+5 gp**.
- **Ties:** a judge assigns prizes at random. Tied racers share the honor of that place equally.

**Rum Run events** to describe (p. 229):
- A drunk adult wanders into the children's version of an obstacle, fails, and is disqualified.
- A racer falls from near the top of the climbing wall into the mud.
- A racer hangs upside down by one foot in the rigging until another racer shoves them clear. The judges debate whether that counts as attacking or helping. They side with the cheering crowd.
- A drunk racer runs the course backward, warbling an off-key victory song.

## Scene 3 — Event 16: Divine Visitors

- **When:** as the PCs wrap up the festival, ready to move on (p. 220).
- **Where:** a town square.
- **Who:** [Amon](gm_glossary.md#amon), god of revelry, and [Castilli](gm_glossary.md#castilli), god of restraint.

The gods appear as the **viewer's own ancestry**, so each person sees something different (p. 233). The read-aloud calls them "two men."

### Read aloud

> *There could be no better proof that today's festival has outdone itself than the two men who stand in the town square before you.*
>
> *One is a short, rotund man with rosy cheeks and vibrant clothing being held together with rope and twine. He carries an oversized tankard of ale in one hand while his other hand "conducts" a group of drunken revelers singing a merry and bawdy song. The other is a tall, narrow-framed man who lingers nearby with his arms crossed, watching over the scene. His attire also makes a stark contrast with that of his companion, with an elegant full-length tunic, golden epaulets and greaves, a stylish fedora, and an immaculate beard and mustache. The portly man's presence is warm and inviting, stirring memories of your most precious moments of celebration to flood your mind and bringing merry songs to your lips without a moment's thought. By contrast, the tall man's presence makes your spine straighten of its own accord and evokes an implacable awareness of rules and decorum. These powerful and seemingly contradictory sensations exist in perfect harmony.*
>
> *There can be no doubt as to the identities of these two men: they are Amon and Castilli, the sibling gods of revelry and restraint. The arrival of gods could bring great blessings in its wake, so long as they enjoy their time walking the world. Amon at least is in excellent spirits, but Castilli's emotions are inscrutable beyond mild disapproval.*

### What happens (p. 233–234)

1. **The tankard.** As the PCs approach, Amon downs his ale. He drops the tankard to wave them into the song. Backwash splatters Castilli's crisp white tunic. Castilli sighs and cleans it with a wave.
   - Amon: *"You had half a wheat field of starch in there already, what's wrong with a few more drops?"*
   - Castilli: *"Don't be ridiculous, brother. One does not starch silk."*
   - Amon: *"But I am ridiculous brother, so I'd better starch it up!"*
2. **The snow.** Amon tosses up what looks like a bag of flour. It swells into a **20-foot sphere**. *"Look out below!"* Castilli shoves revelers clear. It lands as **snow**, and a snowball fight breaks out among singers and children. The PCs can play. Even a city guard joins in.
3. **The whisper.** Amon tosses a snowball at Castilli, who catches it. **DC 20 Perception** to overhear:
   - Amon: *"Really though, your face usually looks like a raisin when people are having fun, but today it's a walnut. What's got you so wound up?"*
   - Castilli: *"It's a matter incongruent with the merriment you seek. Do not worry yourself."*
   - Amon: *"Always gotta be a tough nut to crack, don'tcha?"*
   - Castilli's frown is barely visible to the PCs.
4. **The guard.** **DC 25 Perception:** the guard's eyes glaze. She draws a dagger and lunges at the child beside her. Whether anyone notices or not, **Castilli acts first**. He snaps his fingers, and she drops the dagger. *"Enough!"* thunders through everyone nearby. Silver magic around him breaks into dozens of motes. One flies into the guard. The rest race down other streets.
   - Amon: *"Hey now, that wasn't me."*
   - Castilli: *"I know. It was a ritual, and rituals require patience and focus, both of which you lack."*
   - Amon: *"Always the party pooper."*
5. **The charge.** Both gods agree the heroes of Rumplank should handle this.

**What Castilli tells them:**
- He arrived about an hour ago. He noticed disharmonious energy forming a ritual to **confound the city guards**.
- He doesn't know the caster. All the ritual's energy is now spent.
- The ritual took extensive preparation, so it **won't happen again anytime soon**.
- Both gods want the culprit held to account. Castilli wants it because the culprit broke lawful order. Amon wants it because the culprit tried to ruin a great party.

**If the PCs ask for more help:**
- Amon: *"These are your affairs, not ours. Though I gotta say, Casti'd usually rather sneer than interfere. I haven't seen him get that worked up in centuries."*
- Castilli: *"I will not abide such an egregious breach of decorum in my presence."*
- Amon: *"And I thought 'Egregious Breach of Decorum' was my title."*
- Castilli: *"And yet, for the sake of the world, I am ever stuck by your side."*
- Amon claps him on the shoulder: *"Aww, how sweet. I love you too."* The corner of Castilli's mouth quirks up in the slightest smile.

**Recall Knowledge** (p. 234): a worshipper of either god knows two things. So does anyone who makes **DC 15 Religion**. Amon rarely joins a party in person. Castilli follows to restore order, as the straight man to the joker. **Critical success:** Castilli thinks mortals meddling in the gods' affairs is a far worse breach of decorum. He won't tolerate it.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (don't take sides):** Some actions earn a withering glare from Castilli. These include defending him from his brother, scolding Amon, or any other egregious breach of decorum in his presence. The glare makes the PC **frightened 2**. The condition can't be reduced until the PC apologizes for interfering in the business of gods. Warn players who critically succeeded on the check above.

**Development:** PCs who investigate find more strange guard behavior. One guard babbled nonsense. Another nearly punched himself before snapping out of a trance. Then trouble finds the PCs. Go to area S.

> **🔒 GM-only (the cult reacts):** The ritual's failure alerts cultists across the city, and they hurry. During area S and the fires, they make their last abductions, **including Princess Atriella**. Colvi and Kulupi leave Rumplank.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** This is the fun turning point from celebration to crisis. It also shows why the ritual failed: the cult didn't plan for a god of restraint at the party.

## Scene 4 — Alleyway Ambush (area S)

## Scene 5 — Match to a Powderkeg (area T)

The smoke leads to the fastest-spreading fire. It's in a square of mostly thatched houses off Treasure Beach.
- The square holds much of the city's **surplus food**. It's the chefs' favorite festival cooking spot. Some food stores are burning.
- Most locals are at the festival. Arsonists have threatened anyone left who fights the fires.
- Hiding locals beg the heroes to stop the arsonists, so the locals can fight the fires. They heard **screaming from the house of the historian Monbak** (T2–T3).
- **Maps:** p. 238 (outside) and p. 239 (Monbak's home). The PCs approach from the **southwest corner** along the dirt path.

### Map quick reference

| Area | Threat | What's there | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** Flaming Chaos | Moderate 8 | S'eva Sootfeathers, 2 saboteur scouts | Scouts on 20-ft rooftops. Ladders or DC 20 Athletics |
| **T2** Trashed Archive | Moderate 8 | Korog, Ovin Rambones, 4 explosive barrels | Monbak's collection, torn up for kindling. 15-ft ceilings, normal light |
| **T3** Monbak's Bedroom | — | Monbak, unconscious | Behind a curtain on T2's east side |

### T1. Flaming Chaos *(Moderate 8)*

> *You approach the location where the fire was spreading the most, a square of mostly thatched houses just off Treasure Beach. Around you are various surplus food stores, and chefs have gathered to prepare food for the festival at various food stalls. Some of the nearby food stores are currently burning, but none of the chefs or locals are rallying to put out the flames. The lack of response is unnatural, and rather eerie. Instead, citizens hide behind buildings, and as you approach, they begin to call out for help.*

**Creatures:** **S'eva Sootfeathers** (CE female g'mayun powdermaster) leads the arsonists. She gives the PCs the same ultimatum she gave the citizens: flee and let Rumplank burn, or stand in the way and die. **Two saboteur scouts** watch from the rooftops.
- Unless the PCs give in, the scouts shoot PCs who haven't noticed them. Then they rush into melee to protect S'eva.
- S'eva is a flamboyant pirate. She works for the riches the cult promised. The cult paid her well, half up front. Her job is to kill anyone who interferes with the arson, or with her allies' looting nearby (T2–T3). She wants the rest of her pay.
- **Morale:** nobody dies for a sack of coin. Scouts at **25 HP or less** try to flee. S'eva tries to flee into **T2**. She surrenders if cornered.

**Saboteur Scouts (2):** [stats in Scene 4](#s1-from-the-rooftops-moderate-8). Initiative: Perception +15 or Stealth +17.

**S'eva Sootfeathers:** Creature 8, unique CE Medium g'mayun humanoid, p. 237
- **Perception** +17; low-light vision, truecolor vision. **Languages** Auran, Common
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +17, Athletics +14, Crafting +14, Intimidation +17, Society +12, Stealth +15
- **Str** +2, **Dex** +5, **Con** +1, **Int** +0, **Wis** +3, **Cha** +3
- **Items:** *blazons of shared power* (*Guns & Gears*), *+1 striking dueling pistol (4)* (20 rounds; *Guns & Gears*), kukri, meteor shot (×2; *Guns & Gears*)
- **AC** 27; **Fort** +13, **Ref** +19, **Will** +15; **HP** 135; **Speed** 25 feet
- **Melee** kukri +18 (agile, finesse, trip), 2d6+8 slashing
- **Ranged** dueling pistol +18 (concealable, concussive, fatal d10, range 60 feet, reload 1), 2d6+8 piercing
- **Reloading Strike** (1 action). She Strikes with her kukri at a foe in reach, then Interacts to reload her pistol. She doesn't need a free hand to reload this way.
- **Shoot and Slash** (2 actions). She Strikes with her kukri, then with her pistol. The kukri might hit, and the pistol might target the same creature. If both happen, the pistol Strike doesn't trigger reactions that only ranged attacks trigger, such as Attack of Opportunity. This counts as two attacks for her multiple attack penalty. The penalty applies only after both attacks.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (S'eva's size):** Her block says **Medium**, but the ancestry rules (p. 235) make g'mayuns **Small**. Pick one beforehand. Her stats don't change.

> **📏 Threat math (party level 8):** Two level-6 scouts (20 XP each) plus S'eva (level 8, 40) make 80, **Moderate**.

**If captured:** S'eva doesn't know what the cult is or what it wants. Her recruiter called herself the **Grand Oracle**. The recruiter was a **g'mayun woman with white feathers, red eyes, and tattered robes adorned with bones**.

> **🔒 GM-only:** That's [Zellura](gm_glossary.md#zellura), stat block in Chapter 12 (p. 329).

**Development:** the fighting outside makes the T2 arsonists hurry. As this fight ends, the PCs hear a crash inside T2.

### T2. Trashed Archive *(Moderate 8)*

**Monbak** (LN male g'mayun historian) is a leading scholar of Rumplank history. He bought this old food warehouse to house his huge collection: artifacts, recorded tales, histories, legends, and notes. He sleeps in one small curtained room. Ceilings are 15 feet. High windows give normal light. The collection lies smashed. Torn-up books pack four explosive barrels as kindling.

> *As you enter the house from which you heard the crashing sound, you see an eclectic home filled with a mishmash of cultural artifacts, tomes and scrolls full of writing, and scattered notes. While shelves line the wall, all the treasures and notes are scattered across the room, with some of the irreplaceable and delicate pieces smashed into fragments and many of the books ripped apart to serve as kindling for four explosive barrels full of fireworks. A thick curtain on the east side of this large chamber is drawn, blocking the view into the room beyond. A hardriggan man and a kragrak woman, who appear to be the ringleaders behind the arson, prepare to fan the flames even further!*

**Creatures:** the main arsonists, **Korog** and **Ovin Rambones**, are preparing an explosive finale. Neither is a true cult member. Arson is Korog's trade, and the cult made it easier.

**Korog's story** (p. 239):
- She was first hired to put on a fireworks show for Rumplank.
- **DC 20 Society** to Recall Knowledge: she's done shows in several towns and villages lately. **Critical success:** the PC spots a pattern. Arsons that seemed unrelated happened in the month after she left each place.
- Her method: she performs, picks whoever annoys her most, and waits out suspicion. Then she burns their property. She prefers wrecking the trappings of civilization over killing. She doesn't mind casualties, though.
- Cult inventor **Falarni Malabria** used her own crafting know-how to suspect Korog of the arsons. Falarni offered free explosives on two conditions. Korog had to lie low, and she had to burn Falarni's targets when Falarni chose.
- While researching the targets, Korog took personal offense at how Monbak reveled in civilization. That's why his house gets special attention.

**Ovin's story:** he's a talented medic who has been angry since childhood. He awakened to primal magic young. He hated his community's expectations and being taken for granted. He was banished as a teen for turning a harmless headbutting duel lethal. He happily went to live off the land in the woods. There he treated Korog for burns from her own fire, and he extorted her for it. That amused her and won her respect. They share a practical outlook and a distaste for civilization.

**Tactics:**
- **Ovin** opens with *fireball*, before using Ram's Transformation. He aims to catch a barrel, ideally one next to a PC. In round 2 he casts *organsight* and charges. Or he charges at once, depending on the pace.
- **Korog** throws one **lava bomb** and one **dread ampoule** each round.

**Ovin Rambones, Hardriggan Medic:** Creature 8, unique NE Medium hardriggan humanoid, p. 240
- **Perception** +18. **Languages** Common, Jotun
- **Skills:** Athletics +18, Intimidation +16, Medicine +18, Nature +18, Survival +18
- **Str** +4, **Dex** +1, **Con** +3, **Int** +0, **Wis** +4, **Cha** +2
- **Items:** *+1 striking handwraps of mighty blows (4)*, javelins (×5), wooden shield (5) with *energy resistant (fire)* (5)
- **AC** 26; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +15, **Will** +18; **HP** 115
- **Shield Block** (reaction). **Speed** 25 feet
- **Melee** headbutt +17 (parry, shove), 2d4+10 bludgeoning
- **Ranged** javelin +16 (range 30 ft., thrown), 1d6+10 piercing
- **Primal Prepared Spells** DC 25, attack +17. **4th** *fireball*, *heal*, *organsight* (*Secrets of Magic*); **3rd** *aqueous orb*, *earthbind*, *heal*; **2nd** *faerie fire*, *obscuring mist*, *scorching ray* (*Secrets of Magic*); **Cantrips (4th)** *detect magic*, *electric arc*, *light*, *stabilize*, *tame* (*Secrets of Magic*)
- **Ram's Transformation** (1 action; primal, polymorph, transmutation). +2 status bonus to headbutt attack rolls and +2d4 headbutt damage, but −2 to spell attacks and spell DCs. Lasts 10 minutes or until Dismissed.
- **Sudden Charge** (2 actions). He Strides twice. If he ends within melee reach of an enemy, he can Strike it. He can use it while Flying.

**Korog, Saboteur Firebringer:** Creature 8, unique NE Medium kragrak humanoid, p. 240
- **Perception** +14; low-light vision. **Languages** Aquan, Auran, Common, Orpok, Terran
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +16, Crafting +19, Nature +16, Stealth +18, Survival +14, Thievery +16, Volcano Lore +17
- **Str** +2, **Dex** +4, **Con** +2, **Int** +4, **Wis** +2, **Cha** +0
- **Items:** alchemist goggles, alchemist's tools, *+1 striking dagger (4)*, explorer's clothes, moderate alchemist's fire (×9), moderate dread ampoule (×6; *Advanced Player's Guide*), thieves' tools
- **AC** 24; **Fort** +16, **Ref** +18, **Will** +13; **HP** 130; **Speed** 25 feet
- **Melee** dagger +17 (agile, finesse, thrown 10 ft., versatile S), 2d4+2 piercing
- **Ranged** moderate alchemist's fire +19 (bomb, range increment 20 feet, splash), 2d8 fire plus 2 persistent fire and 5 fire splash
- **Ranged** moderate dread ampoule +19 (bomb, emotion, fear, range increment 20 feet, splash), 2d6 mental plus 5 mental splash and frightened
- **Lava Bomb** (2 actions). She enhances a moderate alchemist's fire with volcanic reagents and Strikes with it. It deals 2d8 fire plus 1d8 bludgeoning, **7 persistent fire**, and 5 fire splash. The primary target must succeed at a **DC 26 Fortitude** save. On a failure, it takes a **−10-foot circumstance penalty to its Speeds** until the persistent fire ends.
- **Targeted Bomb.** With a splash bomb or Lava Bomb, she can deal splash damage only to her primary target.

**Explosive Barrels (4):** Hazard 4, complex magical trap, p. 241
- **Stealth** +14
- Explosives stuffed with Monbak's records. They're built to spread fire and burn Rumplank's history at the same time. The flames feed on alchemical accelerants, fire magic, and gunpowder.
- **Disable:** **DC 24 Thievery or Crafting** to neutralize a barrel before it detonates. **DC 21 Survival or Thievery** to douse the flames in one square. **Counteract DC 21 (2nd level)** for effects that can counteract magical flames, such as *quench* (*Advanced Player's Guide*). > **⚠️ Gotcha (Monbak is in the fire zone):** Monbak lies unconscious behind the curtain in **T3**. The fire reaches him if it spreads through T2. The building collapses once both rooms burn. Ovin's opening *fireball* targets a barrel. Once one barrel goes off, every round counts.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** The barrels are a [complex hazard](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards). After the first detonation, they roll their own initiative. Any fire damage sets one off, including a PC's spell.

> **📏 Threat math (party level 8):** Korog and Ovin (level 8, 40 XP each) make 80. By the formula, four complex level-4 hazards add 10 XP each, for 120. Go with the book's **Moderate 8** label.

**If captured:** both talk in exchange for leniency. Korog likes the cult's style but owes it nothing. Ovin knows little. With the right incentive, **Korog tells everything**:
- The famous inventor **Falarni Malabria** (p. 294) supplied free incendiaries as long as Korog started fires. Wrecking Monbak's house mattered most.
- Korog guesses Falarni wanted some unnamed historical fact erased. Korog figures that if it was here, the torn-up books already destroyed it.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Falarni Malabria's workshop and stat block are in **Chapter 11** (p. 294). Note how the PCs learned her name.

**Treasure:** if you don't use Monster Parts, replace Ovin's shield with a ***minor sturdy shield*** and a ***ring of energy resistance (fire)***.

### T3. Monbak's Bedroom

A narrow room with a simple bed.

**Monbak** lies unconscious and battered. Once woken, he's terrified and distraught. Strangers broke in. A *"terrible volcano woman"* wrecked his collection before his eyes. A *"nasty hardriggan man"* beat him senseless when he tried to stop her.

**Background special effect:** a PC with the **indigo historian** background recognizes him as a foremost historian of Rumplank and the Isles. Monbak is grateful and impressed by their research on Poppy's era. He offers to **sponsor them into the Royal Society of History**, the Isles' exclusive circle of top historians. He also offers to work with them after the crisis.

### What happens if the PCs lose?

Guards drive off the saboteurs in time to save any PCs who didn't bleed out. Monbak dies from the flames and smoke. It's a somber note, but the adventure continues (p. 241).

## Scene 6 — Into Thin Air

The PCs, other adventurers, and the gods spared Rumplank several disasters. When the cult returns, it will face a city able to defend itself. But the **most important part of the plan succeeded** while everyone was busy elsewhere. The cult abducted key figures. Some were cult members letting themselves be "abducted," and Prismatic Colvi was one of them. **The cult now has the Jewel** (p. 241).

**How the PCs find out:**
- **At the temple:** if they return to Colvi, he is gone. So are the Jewel and any guards the prince posted. The signs suggest all of them were kidnapped.
- **At Castle Rumplank:** if they go to report.
- **By messenger:** otherwise, a messenger soon arrives with an urgent summons to the king and queen.

### Read aloud (the messenger)

> *From a distance, rapid footsteps approach, after which a wheezing g'mayun messenger crouches before you. It's unclear whether she is inclining her head out of respect or is simply exhausted. The royal heraldry on her outfit suggests she might have run all the way from the palace in the Rosewater district, which is quite the distance. She implores, "A message for you, heroes. His Majesty apologizes for the need to call on you on such short notice, but an urgent and sensitive matter has arisen, which strikes close to home and to His Majesty's heart. If you could proceed to the palace at your most immediate convenience?" The messenger waits patiently for a response, withdraws a pen and parchment, and calls a nearby parrot to her shoulder. While she is in no shape to return to the palace without a rest, the parrot can deliver your response in her stead.*

### Read aloud (the king)

At the palace in [Rosewater](gm_glossary.md#rosewater), the king gives the news.

> *After clearing the room, the king speaks in a solemn voice, "My daughter Artriella and my son Kulupi have been abducted! After the parade, the pair went to take a break from the crowds and enjoy some tea together. When a servant went to fetch them for dinner, she reported that the room was empty—the guards were gone, the teacups were half-drunk, and the door to a secret passage out of the castle that I thought was known only to the royal family was open. Royal alchemists determined that the prince and princess's tea had both been laced with a paralytic poison. The guards that we sent to the secret passageway to investigate have not yet returned. > **⚠️ Gotcha (typos in the book):** The read-aloud says **"Artriella"**, and the book's page says **"King Okeno Redfeather"**. They should be **Atriella** and **Okeki**. Read the correct names aloud.

- A **royal bard heals visibly hurt PCs to full HP** before they go.

**Investigating the tea room:**
- It matches the king's account.
- **DC 25 Crafting** detects the poison in the tea.
- The **secret door is still open**.
- Nobody in the castle saw anyone unusual nearby.

> **🔒 GM-only (what happened):** **Prince Kulupi and an inner circle of loyal guards staged the whole kidnapping.** There's no other culprit. Cultists captured the guards the king sent down the tunnel. Play the king's grief straight. The players learn the truth in Chapter 11.

**Following the tunnel:**
- It runs far **southeast** to the **Canary Cliffs**, near the **Crawling Caves** entrance.
- Kulupi's group tried to hide their tracks. They couldn't hide the **wheeled serving cart**. It carried a **nobbler** with the **paralyzed princess in its stomach**.
- The trail joins the trails of **dozens of other cultists**. Some had wagons or food trucks.
- **DC 17 Survival** tracks the escape into the Crawling Caves.
- On a failure, a befriended NPC soon spots the trail and tells them.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** The PCs saved the food stores and a historian, and they still lost. The king's plea, "not as a king but as a father," makes the chase personal. It also sets up the biggest reveal: the "victim" is the villain.

## Scene 7 — The Crawling Caves

Rumplank considers the nearby **Crawling Caves** deadly. The royals have long offered 1,000 gp for a complete map (Rumplank gazetteer, p. 354). The cultists cleared the active dangers right before passing through, but they left guards.

**Features (p. 243):**
- **Pitch dark.** Tunnels are usually 10 feet high. The large cavern's ceiling is 25 feet.
- Abandoned escape gear lies around: wagons, food carts with hidden lower compartments, burlap sacks, and celebratory costumes. **Squares with gear are difficult terrain.**
- **Map:** p. 242. The PCs approach from the **southeast corner**.

### Smuggled Out of Town *(Low 8)*

> *The Crawling Caves have a bad reputation in Rumplank for being extremely dangerous to traverse, but so far, the journey has been much safer than the caves' reputation implies, almost as if someone else had passed this way first and cleared a path. Before long, the 10-foot high tunnels open up into a cavernous chamber with a 25-foot tall ceiling. Here, assorted pieces of gear lie scattered across the cavern floor, including wagons, food carts, burlap sacks, and celebratory costumes.*

**Creatures:** four of the burlap sacks are **nobblers**, the constructs the cult used for abductions. The cult left them behind to handle pursuers, rather than tend them on the long trip.

**Nobblers (4):** BB 67, Creature 5. Initiative: Stealth +11.
- **Monster Parts** 18/45/80 gp
- **Eligible refinements:** weapons (bludgeoning), armor (light), perception item, skill items (Acrobatics, Athletics, Deception, Stealth)
- **Eligible imbued properties:** bane, constitution, energy resistant (negative, positive), sensory (darkvision), strength, wild

## Concluding the chapter

The PCs have the trail, but they're likely tired from a day of feasting and fighting. Even if they want to push on, the book calls this a good place to **end the session** (p. 243).

To keep going, run **Royal Request**, Chapter 10's opening. It can go one of two ways:
- The PCs **go back to Rumplank to rest**, then go to the castle.
- They **push on through the caves**. The royals send a messenger with mounts, provisions, and a note. The PCs rest safely the first night, with no combat (Chapter 10, p. 244).

**No level-up here.** The party stays at **8** until meeting Peerless Ruby in Chapter 10.

> **💡 GM tip:** The book calls this one of the last breathers before the finale. Give the players a scene to rest, take stock, and react to their losses.
