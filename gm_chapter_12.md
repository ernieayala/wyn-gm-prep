## The chapter in one minute

The PCs fly home on [Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby). They tell the royals that their son leads the cult. The cult's undead behemoth and armies march on [Rumplank](gm_glossary.md#rumplank). The king gives the PCs command of the defense. The Indigo Stranger reveals he is the indigo dragon [X'na'zed](gm_glossary.md#xnazed).

The PCs get seven days, 600 Resource Points, and the special resources they found from Chapter 4 on. They fortify five districts against five tower-defense waves.

In the last wave they fight in person:
- an undead breach in [Barrel Square](gm_glossary.md#barrel-square)
- [Prismatic Colvi](gm_glossary.md#prismatic-colvi), unmasked
- [Zellura](gm_glossary.md#zellura) with a shale-armored undead [Poppy von Barnacle](gm_glossary.md#poppy-von-barnacle)
- [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi) and his illusory duplicates on the behemoth's head

Kulupi dies, the behemoth falls, and the city celebrates.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Built Rumplank's defenses** and survived five waves of the cult's armies ([Scene 2](#scene-2--seven-days-to-prepare), [Scene 3](#scene-3--rumplanks-reckoning-the-five-waves)).
2. **Unmasked and driven off or beaten Prismatic Colvi** ([Scene 4](#colvis-true-colors-moderate-10)).
3. **Defeated Zellura and the undead Poppy** ([Scene 5](#shattered-legends-moderate-10)).
4. **Killed Prince Kulupi**, which drops the behemoth and routs the cult ([Scene 6](#treacherys-end-severe-10)).

**Party level:** starts at **10**, ends at **11**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** After eleven chapters of raiding, the PCs now defend their own city. The generals are people they know. The resources are ones they gathered without knowing why. The finale puts three cult leaders and Rumplank's own founder in their way.

## How the chapter is built

The book gives a loose outline (p. 308):

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **The Heroes Return** | Royal audience, X'na'zed's reveal, the king's gift | First |
| **Siege of Rumplank: preparation** | Seven days: build defenses, assign generals, downtime | Second. The army attacks the morning of the **eighth day** |
| **Rumplank's Reckoning** | Five waves of armies, run with the siege subsystem | Third, in one long day |
| **Battle of Barrel Square** | Commander of the Dead, then Colvi's True Colors | During the final wave, whatever the siege outcome |
| **The Behemoth Rises** | 10-minute rest, then Shattered Legends (Zellura and undead Poppy) | Fourth |
| **The Behemoth** | Astride the Giant, then Treachery's End (Kulupi) | The campaign's last fight |
| **Aftermath, Celebration, Conclusion** | District fates, royal speech, Amon and Castilli | Epilogue |

The last five fights come one after another with almost no rest. The book says to allow a little healing and condition removal between battles. There's likely not enough time to Refocus or Treat Wounds. There are two built-in breaks (p. 308, 326, 330):
- a **10-minute rest with assisted Treat Wounds** before Shattered Legends
- a **full refresh from the Jewel's energy** after it

> **💡 GM tip (the subsystem is optional):** If the siege rules don't suit your group, the book says you can narrate the waves instead. Base them on the PCs' defenses. Have each PC describe a cool deed in one district (p. 308). The personal fights still happen.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** The book says the waves take "several hours" in-game. The time between waves affects no fight (p. 319). Waves with several armies run long. Track everything on a sheet the players can see.

## The siege subsystem

The book calls it a "tower defense subsystem" (p. 308). The PCs plan defenses that fight the armies. Then, each round, each PC takes one **heroic action** with a squadron. Give players **pp. 310–318** before they build (p. 309). The book recommends they read the Flow of War first (p. 311).

### The siege at a glance

| Piece | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Districts** | Five: Barnacle Bay, Barrel Square, Claw Quarter, Old Rumplank, Rosewater. Each starts with **100 HP** |
| **Resource Points (RP)** | **600** to spend on defenses. Gold and RP don't convert either way |
| **Defenses** | Towers damage, walls add **Guard**, camps give bonuses, others do special things |
| **Tiers** | Tier 2 upgrades a tier 1 defense with a **special resource** |
| **Special resources** | Found from Ch 4 on. Each upgrades one defense or converts to **10 RP** |
| **Armies** | Each has **Damage**, **HP**, sometimes **Guard**, and **traits** |
| **Waves** | Five, each played in rounds until its armies are beaten |
| **Heroic actions** | One per PC per round, in the PC's district |

> **💡 GM tip (the Indigo Stranger as rules voice):** The book suggests X'na'zed teach the subsystem in character. You can also teach it out of character (p. 307, 309).

### Hit Points and Guard

- **District HP** stands for the district's integrity and its citizens' lives. At **0 HP the district is ruined**, and its armies move to an adjacent district (p. 311, 313).
- **Guard** comes from defenses, mostly walls. Guard takes damage first. Once a defense's Guard is 0, damage hits district HP.
- **Track Guard per defense.** Some defenses have on-hit effects. A defense at 0 Guard can still be Repaired (p. 313).
- **Who chooses:** when there are several armies or Guard-bearing defenses to hit, the side **dealing** damage picks where it goes (p. 312). PCs pick their towers' targets. You pick which wall an army hits.
- **Bypassing Guard:** some traits skip it. Flying ignores all Guard.
- **Medic and physicker camps** raise a district's max HP above its starting 100 (p. 309).
- **Armies can have Guard too.** Towers must break it before they hit HP. Several armies change when it breaks.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (adjacency):** PC movement, fallen districts, and infiltrators all use **adjacent** districts. Only the **p. 310** map defines adjacency. The text gives one example: **Rosewater** borders the **Claw Quarter** and **Barrel Square** (p. 318). Mark adjacencies before play.

### Before each wave: scouting and repairs

Before each wave (p. 311):

1. **Scout.** Each PC moves to a district. They roll a **secret DC 25 Perception** or **DC 20 Warfare Lore** (or similar Lore) check ([Secret checks](gm_chapter_01.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge)).
   - **Critical success:** the PC learns whether this district is attacked this wave, plus the army count and traits.
   - **Success:** the PC learns whether it's attacked.
   - **Failure:** no signs either way.
   - **Critical failure:** the PC believes the opposite of the truth.
2. **Share** what they learn by semaphores and signals.
3. **Repair instead** (after Wave 1): a PC can skip scouting to [Repair](#heroic-actions) damaged Guard.
4. **Reposition:** each PC can move to an adjacent district.

### Wave rounds

Rounds last far longer than six seconds. There is no initiative (p. 311–312):

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Ambush** | Armies with the **ambush** trait damage their district |
| **2. Heroic action** | Each PC takes one [heroic action](#heroic-actions) in their district |
| **3. Defenses** | Each district's defenses damage its armies. The PCs assign the damage |
| **4. Assault** | Every surviving army without ambush damages its district |
| **Check** | Wave over? If not, back to step 1 |

**Fallen districts:** in step 1 or 4, an army that would damage a 0-HP district moves to an adjacent district instead. That move spends its turn. A PC in a fallen district spends their heroic action retreating to an adjacent district.

**Why defenders go first:** the book uses this order to show the defender's advantage. Armies destroyed before step 4 deal no damage (p. 312).

**PCs don't spend resources.** Heroic actions never cost spells, Focus Points, or consumables, however the player describes them. This keeps PCs fresh for the personal fights (p. 312). The book says spells cast into a wave usually have "a negligible effect." Encourage players to save them, but let a clever idea work (p. 319).

**Squadrons:** each PC leads soldiers. The soldiers act on the PC's orders in step 2 (p. 312).

### Ending a wave

A wave ends one of two ways (p. 312):

1. **All its armies are defeated.** PC victory.
2. **The cult cedes (your call).** You can end the wave after a round where the enemy dealt no district HP damage **and** the PCs Repaired more Guard than the enemy dealt. Use it when a wave is winding down, to stop PCs from farming Repairs against a few leftover armies. Don't use it early, after a few lucky critical Repairs.

### Worked example 1: the First Wave

The book's example (p. 312). Two armies attack a district. Each has **25 HP** and deals **10 damage**. The district has **two walls** (25 Guard each) and **five arrow towers** (5 damage each). No PC is present.

- **Round 1:** no ambush and no PC. The towers deal 5 × 5 = **25 damage** to one army and destroy it. The other army deals 10 to a wall. That wall drops to **15 Guard**. The other wall stays at 25. District HP is untouched.
- **Round 2:** the towers destroy the last army before it acts. The wave ends.

> **📏 What this shows:** walls soak damage and towers kill. Defenses act before ordinary armies. Five arrow towers (100 RP) kill one 25-HP army per round.

### Worked example 2: the Second Wave

The book's second example (p. 312–313). **Armies A and B:** 25 HP, 10 damage each. **Army C:** 30 HP, 10 damage, **ambush**. The district has **one wall** (25 Guard), **five arrow towers**, and one PC's squadron.

- **Round 1:** C hits the wall, dropping it to **15 Guard**. An assault deals at most 20 on a critical success, so it can't kill a second 25-HP army this round. The PC **Repairs** instead and succeeds: +10, back to **25**. The towers destroy A. B hits the wall, dropping it to **15 Guard**.
- **Round 2:** C hits the wall, dropping it to **5 Guard**. Thanks to the Repair, no damage has reached district HP. The PC Repairs again, back to **15 Guard**. The towers destroy B. No non-ambush army is left to assault.
- **Round 3:** C hits the wall, dropping it to **5 Guard**. The PC Repairs with a **critical success**: +20, back to **25**. The towers deal 25 to C, leaving it at **5 HP**.
- **Wave end:** that round, the enemy dealt 10 Guard damage and no district HP damage. The PC Repaired 20. If this is the last district still fighting, the GM rules that the cult cedes.

> **💡 GM tip:** Show the players this example. It teaches that Repair often beats an attack, because Repair keeps damage off district HP.

### Army traits

Traits change where and how armies attack (p. 313–314).

| Trait | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ambush** | Acts in step 1, before the PCs and defenses |
| **Amphibious** | **Aquatic** in water. May attack on land if its district has many wave or torrent towers |
| **Aquatic** | Can attack any district (all of Rumplank is on water). **Ignores the Guard of any defense without the aquatic trait** |
| **Burrow** | Ignores any defense without the **anti-burrow** trait |
| **Counter** | A defense or PC that attacks it **in melee** skips its next turn |
| **Flying** | **Ignores the Guard of all defenses.** Melee defenses can't damage it |
| **Infiltrator** | After attacking each round, it moves to the adjacent district whose defenses could hurt it least, after counting damage it ignores. Ties go to the district with lower Guard |
| **Ranged** | Takes no damage from spiked walls, and attacks spiked walls before other Guard |
| **Regeneration X** | Recovers X HP each round |
| **Resistant X** | Takes X less damage from **each** physical defense. Every defense is physical except **inferno towers and storm towers** |
| **Trample X** | X of its damage goes straight to district HP, ignoring Guard |
| **Undermine** | Deals **double damage against Guard**. Against a district with Guard, it attacks before other attackers (still after the PCs) |
| **Unreliable** | Each round, roll a **DC 8 flat check**. Success: it attacks the district. Failure: it attacks other armies there. If it's alone, it attacks the district with no check |

**The book's trait examples:**
- **Resistant 2:** one defense hits the army for 5 and another for 7. The army takes 3 + 5 = **8**, not 12.
- **Trample 5, damage 11, district Guard 6 or more:** 5 to district HP, then 6 to Guard.
- **Trample 5, damage 13, district Guard 7:** 5 goes to HP and 7 to Guard (now 0). The last 1 goes to HP, so **6 HP** are lost.
- **Undermine, damage 10, district Guard 12:** double the damage to 20. Spend 12 destroying the Guard. Halve the remaining 8, so **4** reaches district HP. The rule: if Guard is under twice the damage, double the damage and spend it on Guard. Then halve the rest onto HP.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (what "ignore" means):** Compare the anti-burrow text ("can apply their Guard"). Burrow ignores only a defense's **Guard**. Towers still damage burrowers. Quake and tectonic towers hit them hardest.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (which defenses are "physical"):** The book names only **inferno towers and storm towers** as non-physical. Rule on flame cannon and tempest towers before Resistant armies arrive, and tell the players.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (aquatic armies skip almost every wall):** The **boom chain** is the only aquatic defense. So it's the only Guard that aquatic armies respect. **Applying the rules** (not a book example): Wave 2's Glaucus slugs are aquatic **and** counter. Each wave or torrent tower (melee) that hits them skips its next turn.

### Building the defenses

Build rules (p. 313–315):

- **Budget:** **600 Resource Points** across the five districts. Gold and RP don't convert either way.
- **No limits** on defenses per district or on build time.
- **Tier 1** costs RP. **Tier 2** usually needs a tier 1 defense **plus** a special resource, which the upgrade uses up. Example: a **torrent tower** is a wave tower (20 RP) plus red sargassum.
- **Resource-only defenses**, like the **net launcher**, need a resource.
- **Converting:** any special resource can become **10 generic RP**.
- **The book's advice:** build most or all of the upgrades the resources allow. 10 RP is a poor return for a resource. The exception is a defense that leans on a skill no PC has. Build the rest around those upgrades. "Walls are a good idea!" (p. 318)

> **🔒 GM-only (who attacks where):** The targets are fixed. **Barnacle Bay: Wave 2 only.** Rosewater: Waves 3 and 4. Barrel Square, the Claw Quarter, and Old Rumplank: several waves. Players learn this only by scouting ([the wave rosters](#scene-3--rumplanks-reckoning-the-five-waves)).

### Heroic actions

After the defenses act, each PC takes one action in their own district. Or the PC **Moves** to an adjacent one (p. 317–318).

| Action | Check | Crit success | Success | Failure | Crit failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Move** | None | — | Move to an adjacent district | — | — |
| **Melee Assault** | Melee attack roll vs. **AC 30** | 20 damage to an army of choice | 10 damage | 5 damage | No effect |
| **Ranged Assault** | Ranged attack roll vs. **AC 30** | 20 damage | 10 damage | 5 damage | No effect |
| **Repair** | **DC 27 Crafting** | Up to 20 missing Guard to one defense | 10 Guard | 5 Guard | No effect |
| **Inspired Stratagem** | **DC 27 Diplomacy or Performance**, or **DC 25 Warfare Lore** | Two defenses in the district deal their damage again this turn | One defense deals its damage again | One defense deals half its damage again | No effect |
| **Cook Superior Meals** | **DC 25 Cooking Lore** or **DC 30 Crafting**. Needs a quartermaster in the district | Until wave end, PC checks here get **+3** (quartermaster) or **+4** (gourmet) | **+2** or **+3** | Unchanged: **+1** or **+2** | **+0** or **+1** |

Rules around the actions:
- **Inspired stratagem cap:** each defense repeats its damage from inspired stratagem only **once per round**. This holds however many PCs use it there.
- **Bonuses don't stack:** the meal's circumstance bonus replaces the quartermaster's.
- **Any number** of assaults or repairs can help one district.
- **Hero points:** once per round, a PC can spend one to **repeat** a heroic action. The book notes this often beats a reroll, since many failures still help.
- **Counter armies:** a PC who melee assaults one loses their next turn.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** PCs roll their own attack and skill modifiers. A quartermaster's bonus applies to all of them.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (a typo):** The book prints inspired stratagem's DC as "C 27." Read it as DC 27.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (action order):** The book says heroic actions come "after the defenses act" (p. 317). But its round order puts them in step 2, before defenses (p. 311). Both worked examples do the same (p. 312). Run them in [step 2](#wave-rounds).

## Scene 4 — Battle of Barrel Square

> *The Indigo Stranger flies in from above, "Excellent work! The enemy armies are routing, but something unexpected is happening in Barrel Square, on the street toward the Temple of Many Colors. ### Commander of the Dead *(Low to Severe 10)*

- **Where:** Barrel Square's blood-stained ruby-red streets (map **p. 324**). The PCs enter on the **eastern road**.
- **What's happening:** treachery let tireless undead slip past the tired defenders.

**Creatures:** a slaugh made by the cult leads shaleborn legions (p. 323). It's faithful so far, though slaughs tend to betray their makers. Barrel Square's HP sets the number of legions:

| Barrel Square HP | Shaleborn legions | XP budget (party level 10) | Book label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **61 or more** | 2 | Slaugh 20 + 2 × 20 = 60 | Low |
| **31–60** | 3 | 20 + 3 × 20 = 80 | Moderate |
| **30 or fewer** | 4 | 20 + 4 × 20 = 100 | Severe (top of "Low to Severe") |

> **📏 Threat math:** 100 XP sits between Moderate (80) and Severe (120). The book's label is "Low to Severe." A well-defended Barrel Square makes this fight easier.

**Stats:**
- **Slaugh:** BB 106, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +17. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- **Shaleborn Legions (2, 3, or 4):** p. 395, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +16. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.

**Tactics (p. 323):**
- **Slaugh:** pre-casts ***circle of protection***. It uses **Necromantic Blow** on PCs casting dangerous spells like *heal*. It uses **Reap** when it can hit two. It saves ***Harm*** for healing, except a one-action *harm* to finish an adjacent healer on the brink. It doesn't cast *animate dead*, because the PCs outclass its summons.
- **One legion** tries to **Engulf** PCs, spellcasters first. Engulfed PCs must hold their breath.
- **The others** use **Stone Barrage** unless the PCs are split up or mixed among the undead.
- **All legions** use **Rain of Armor** when available. Then they **Pummel** with every action left. The undead fight until destroyed.

**Treasure:** with fewer than 4 legions, the missing legions' Monster Parts still lie on the ground (p. 324).

He also stops when the PCs cast, approach, or start initiative some other way. He compliments the PCs and tries to recruit them, expecting to fail. **What he says depends on his relationship with the PCs.** The book's example is below. Read or paraphrase it if it fits:

> *Despite the chaos around him, Colvi speaks to you calmly, clearly, and without any sign of personal malice toward you. "While I knew that none of you was my equal, together you have far exceeded my expectations. Color me suitably impressed. When we first met while you were looking for the map fragments, I never expected you to come this far or gain so much strength. So how about this? Join me. Varagix isn't like the other demon lords who perform evil unnecessarily or just for fun. He is the lord of bounty and prosperity, and he lavishes great rewards on those who serve him. You will never flourish with these weaklings like you would serving Varagix."*

If the PCs argue against his faith, he rebuts them politely. If he finds no flaw in their argument, he sighs:

> *"I will have to decline. The sorts of commitments I have made are not so easily undone. Farewell heroes. Truly, you impressed me, but your journey ends here."*

> **🧩 Pays off Ch 1 (how the PCs treated Colvi):** The Chapter 1 notes on each PC's dealings with Colvi at the Temple of Many Colors shape his dialogue. They don't feed any mechanic (p. 325). His sample opens with the meeting during the map hunt.
>
> **💡 GM tip:** Tailor his pitch to each PC. Thank the statue-puzzle solver for their scholarship. To the PC who named the winged statue as [Pazuzu](gm_glossary.md#pazuzu): "You knew that statue at once. I wondered then how much you saw." Recall the Ch 9 visit when they handed him the Jewel. Each callback shows he was in front of them all campaign.

**Colvi's tactics (p. 325–326):**
- From range: *chromatic ray* and *spiritual guardian*. With a spare action next to a PC: *harm*.
- If PCs reach him in the air: *cloak of colors* to punish and protect, and *sudden shift* against multiple melee attacks. If many PCs are in range and few allies are in the way: *repelling pulse*.
- *Roaring applause* (costly): when a PC vexes him with reactions. He casts it in advance if Kulupi's reports warned him.
- **Retreat:** below 50 HP, he heals. Then he lets a phase tiger Grab him and Drag him Beyond the Veil, unresisted. If all the tigers are down and he has no upper hand, he retreats at any HP. If **caught**, he surrenders if he expects mercy. That's his best chance to slip away.

**Phase tiger tactics (p. 326):**
- All three use **Ethereal Pounce** on one **low-defense PC**, ideally the main healer or support. They keep trying **Drag Beyond the Veil**. Once one succeeds, all three return to the Ethereal Plane. There they kill the isolated PC away from healing. They repeat this after each kill.
- **If Colvi is defeated,** they keep killing whoever they hold on the Ethereal Plane. They never return to the Material Plane, even if the victim escapes.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (a dragged PC is in real danger):** Three level-7 tigers can kill a lone PC. Following them ethereally, or a spell that recalls the victim, saves a life. Otherwise, beat Colvi fast or make the tigers let go.

## Scene 5 — The Behemoth Rises

The armies are nearly beaten. To end the cult, the PCs must take down Kulupi on the behemoth. The behemoth is heading straight for them with the remaining leaders (p. 326).

**The rest:** first, **10 minutes**. Allied defenders help with Treat Wounds. Each PC recovers **2d8+30 HP** without spending actions. A PC can use their own Treat Wounds instead if it heals more (p. 326). This rest is the planned exception to the outline's warning. The outline says the PCs are "very unlikely to have long enough to Refocus or Treat Wounds" (p. 308).

Read or paraphrase (p. 327):

> *"The enemy's armies are shattered, but I fear this won't end until someone finds a way to stop the behemoth's rampage. Otherwise, it will simply level the town, devour the fallen, and spawn new armor of shaleborn," the Indigo Stranger frets, trying to come up with a plan to corner the fast-moving creature. Suddenly, there's a loud roar in the distance as the behemoth approaches. It's headed right for you! The Indigo Stranger snorts, "Well, that's convenient."*

### Shattered Legends *(Moderate 10)*

- **Where:** a market square with a stage on the south end.
- **Who:** Zellura, Shaleborn Poppy, and a shaleborn soldier.
- **Goal:** break the barriers around the prince and princess.

Before the fight, give the PCs a moment. The book calls it the perfect time for loose ends, preparation, or an epic speech to the NPCs. **Let a PC give it**, not the Indigo Stranger. The stage is right there (p. 327).

> **💡 GM tip:** Ask, "Who's giving the speech?" Then go around the table for a line each.

The book builds this encounter to demoralize Rumplank. Read or paraphrase (p. 327):

> *Before you can challenge the behemoth, three figures stand before you, blocking your path. In the back is the g'mayun woman with bone-white feathers and blood-red eyes who you saw at the ritual with Prince Kulupi. But more alarmingly are her two guards, a g'mayun man and a woman, each transformed into the shale-covered undead you fought before in Barrel Square. They both seem far more powerful than the shale warriors you faced before, and you realize something horrifying when you look at them more closely. The undead g'mayun woman is none other than Poppy von Barnacle!*

> **🔒 GM-only:** Zellura ("Grand Oracle," "Lady of Bone and Bounty") began the cult's revival. She sees herself as its true spiritual leader. She juggles Colvi's and Kulupi's egos, with Kulupi as the official head. Her plan for victory: Kulupi takes the undead army abroad, Colvi rules Rumplank, and she studies rituals for new disasters. To hurt Rumplank most, she **dug up Poppy's body and one of Poppy's disciples** and raised them as shaleborn (p. 327).

**Creatures:**
- **Zellura, Grand Oracle** (stat block below), Creature 9.
- **Shaleborn Poppy** (stat block below), Creature 9.
- **Shaleborn Soldier** (Poppy's disciple): p. 396, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +15. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.

> **📏 Threat math:** Zellura (30) + Poppy (30) + soldier (10) = 70 XP, between Low (60) and Moderate (80). Use the book's Moderate. Her pre-cast spells push it up.

**Zellura's pre-cast spells (p. 327–328):**
- **Start of day:** she casts *spell immunity* on herself **and on Prince Kulupi**. It guards against a PC favorite spell that Kulupi named. If there's no good choice, she casts *heal* on herself and keeps the 5th-level slot for battle.
- **Before the latest push:** *freedom of movement* and *see invisibility* on herself.
- **If the PCs don't approach stealthily:** she casts *armor of bones* before the battle. This switches on her **Curse of Death's Grasp**.

**Tactics:**
- **Zellura:** she uses 5th-level *command* to hinder. She uses *divine wrath* (if she knows the PCs are mostly good) or *agonizing despair* to hurt. She casts *Harm* mainly when it also heals her side: two actions for a badly hurt ally, or three to damage foes while lightly healing allies. If needed, her *greater staff of necromancy* gives a 4th-level *vampiric touch* for temporary HP.
- **Her whole side kills PCs who can cast *heal* first.**
- **Poppy:** **Evoke Legend** into **Poppy's Finisher**, with a Strike first if actions allow. *Stone toss* at easier targets out of reach. She takes every **Opportune Riposte** she can. **Rain of Armor** whenever available.
- **Shaleborn soldier:** flanks with Poppy. It uses **Grab** and **Bonecrush** to enfeeble Strength-based melee PCs. It uses Rain of Armor when it can.
- **Morale:** Zellura expects Varagix to raise her. She fights to the death while she can still win or kill PCs for Kulupi. If she's plainly losing, she **retreats onto the behemoth to fight beside Kulupi**. The undead cover her and fight until destroyed.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (Zellura in the final fight):** The book doesn't adjust Treachery's End for her. It also doesn't square her retreat with the Development below, which triggers on her defeat. One reading: fleeing counts as defeat. The barriers falter, and she joins Kulupi, adding threat to a Severe fight.

## Scene 6 — The Behemoth

The PCs climb the behemoth toward Kulupi on its head. Flying straight there means fighting Kulupi, the chargers, and the obsidian shale beast at once. If the players consider it, the Indigo Stranger suggests clearing the back first (p. 330).

Read or paraphrase (p. 330):

> *With the Indigo Stranger and your other allies defending from the rear, it's finally time to begin the final assault! You prepare to ascend the behemoth and challenge the prince, but that's easier said than done. The creature is constantly in motion at intense speeds, meaning the only easy access point is onto its tail. The creature is rampaging through Barrel Square, shattering buildings with titanic ease, when its tail swings in your direction, allowing a brief moment when its possible to leap on. The leap isn't far, but the timing is incredibly tight. Fortunately, with the Indigo Stranger's assistance, you're able to time it perfectly. But as you begin your ascent, working your way up the necrotic shale body of the behemoth, covered in sharp crystals, you hear a skittering sound. Why is it never easy?*

**The map (pp. 332–333):**
- Before they leap, the PCs see Kulupi and Atriella in their domes at the top (**K** and **A**).
- The PCs start at the **tail on the west** and climb east.
- Massive **crystal squares** are **greater difficult terrain**. They **block line of sight** for all but shale creatures and Prince Kulupi.

### Astride the Giant *(Moderate 10)*

The behemoth throws the PCs onto sharp crystals. Shale spawn burst from polyps and chrysalises in its body (p. 330–331).

**It moves 120 feet every round.** A creature off its body, such as a flyer, avoids the hazard. But it must Fly 120 feet each round to keep up. Tell the players this before anyone commits.

**Creatures:** halfway up, two shale chargers and an obsidian shale beast emerge. They're hostile to all intruders. No one can reason with them, even with Wild Empathy. They fight to the death and Burrow through the behemoth.
- **Shale Chargers (2):** BB 94, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +13, Stealth +13 (+17 in caves). Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.
- **Obsidian Shale Beast:** BB 95, Creature 10. Initiative: Perception +19. Monster Parts 125/275/500 gp.

**Tactics:**
- The chargers use **Tunneling Charge** and flank one PC for horn Strikes. Or they use **Spew Shale** on clumped PCs.
- The beast pelts the chargers' target with obsidian shards from range. It saves **Song of the Earth** for allies with serious clumsy, enfeebled, or stupefied conditions. The song removes those conditions and makes the allies quickened.

**Hazard:** every PC faces the treacherous hide.

**Treacherous Hide:** Hazard 8, complex environmental, p. 334
- **Stealth** +21 (expert) to predict the behemoth's movements. Noticing that it moves is DC 0.
- **Description:** the behemoth's movement throws those on its back onto the crystals.
- **Disable:** **DC 28 Nature** (expert) to convince the behemoth to stop shaking so much for the rest of the encounter (**two checks required**). Or **DC 24 Athletics or Crafting** (expert) to wrest free the hide in one 5-foot square and make a brace. The brace negates the hazard for that square, and for any creature in it, for the rest of the encounter.
- **AC** 27; **Fort** +19, **Ref** +15. **Hardness** 15, **HP** 66 per 5-foot square of hide (BT 33). **Immunities** critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Stir** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature walks onto the behemoth's back. **Effect:** the behemoth thrashes. Its body becomes difficult terrain, and the hazard rolls initiative.
- **Routine** (2 actions). The behemoth moves forward. Every creature on its back, except the shale spitter family and anyone under the protective dome, makes a **DC 26 Reflex** save.
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** flat-footed until the start of its next turn.
  - **Failure:** thrown onto the spiked carapace. Falls **prone** and takes **2d12 piercing**.
  - **Critical failure:** as failure, plus the spikes resonate for another **2d12 sonic**.

> **📏 Threat math:** two level-6 chargers (10 each) + the level-10 beast (40) + the level-8 complex hazard (20) = 80 XP, Moderate.

> **💡 GM tip:** Point out the Disable options early. A PC who braces their own square ignores the hazard all fight. That's a good job for a Crafting or Athletics specialist.

### Treachery's End *(Severe 10)*

At the head, Kulupi laughs and calls out. Read or paraphrase (p. 334):

> *You finally ascend to the undead behemoth's head, with Rumplank below, forming a shaking tableau that rushes past you at unsettling speed as the creature continues its implacable rampage. Its heart glows and pulses with all the glittering colors of the Jewel of the Indigo Isles. Prince Kulupi stares you down from behind some kind of strange biological crystalline control area, while to the side, Princess Atriella remains trapped in a barrier that now seems much weaker than before. His features are especially hard and cruel, and his wings are larger than before, likely capable of flight. He calls out to you, "So, it has come to this. I should have known that my minions would be insufficient to take care of persistent pests like you. They were too weak to lead, and our God has shown them his displeasure through your hands. I suppose I should thank you for disposing of them for me. Listen well, so-called heroes. I am the chosen of the god Varagix, and all who stand against me shall perish!"*

As he finishes, his shield shatters and he rolls initiative. **Track his HP**: the fight has three phases.

**Creatures:**
- **Demon-Blessed Prince Kulupi** (stat block below), Creature 12.
- **Illusory Princes (3)** (stat block below), Creature 6, plus more he creates.

> **📏 Threat math:** Kulupi (level 12, +2: 80) + three level-6 princes (10 each, 30) = 110 XP, near Severe (120). The book says Severe. He makes a new prince each round, so long fights get harder.

**Phase 1: Come and Find Me! (p. 335)**
- Kulupi **splits into four identical princes**. One is real. Three are illusions that speak his every word. They act on his initiative but **aren't minions**. Each gets three actions and acts on its own.
- **Opening turn:** Create Clone (a fourth duplicate), activate *boots of speed*, **All That Glitters**, Strike a PC. After that, he hides among the duplicates with **Illusory Transposition** and **Clone Swap**. He uses **Create Clone** every round. Duplicates seem to carry his items, so gear gives no clue.
- **He fights to the death** and uses any low tactic. Varagix demands he win or die here. Normally he'd flee and let clones take the fall.
- **No hostage:** he loves his sister and wants her to see his new world. He knows she'd be a poor hostage, so he leaves her in her barrier.
- If Kulupi or a clone flies off or is forced off, the behemoth circles back for them.

**Princess Atriella's barrier:** with Zellura gone, the PCs can break it (**AC 10, Hardness 30, 60 HP**). Atriella is an unarmed swashbuckling fighter and can't break it alone. **Once freed,** she doesn't fight. She runs to Kulupi's controls. She saw his commands, so she can slow the behemoth, though she can't stop it. Everyone on it gains a **+2 circumstance bonus to saves against the behemoth's actions in Phase 3**.

**Varagix's rebuke:** any PC who **broke the altar of Varagix** (p. 297) sees glittering spots and feels stabbing eye pain. That PC is **dazzled for the first round** (no save). Their **first save against All That Glitters** is one degree of success worse.

**Phase 2: Behold, My True Form! (p. 335)** At **160 HP or fewer**, Kulupi transforms. This isn't an action and can happen anytime, even when he can't act. It's **cosmetic only**: his body catches up to the powers Varagix granted at the start. Read or paraphrase:

> *A bright light envelops Prince Kulupi's features and those of all his clones, and he begins to change. His feathers turn platinum, and his wings begin to shimmer and grow even further, tearing away his shirt. The color of his eyes inverts, flipping into black eyes with white pupils. The undead behemoth's heart, containing the power of the Jewel of the Indigo Isles, begins to emit wild colors and energies, washing over all of the Kulupis. "Behold my true form!" all the demon-blessed princes cry out as one in an otherworldly chorus of despair.*

**Phase 3: The Behemoth Goes Berserk! (p. 335–336)** At **80 HP or fewer**, the Jewel's power in the heart goes out of control. Read or paraphrase:

> *The power of the Jewel of the Indigo Isles within the behemoth's heart surges further and further out of control. At this point, it's clear that even Prince Kulupi no longer controls its desperate movements, his demonic concentration broken by your many attacks. "You fools, what have you done?" Kulupi shouts, "At this rate, it will... No. This ends now. Die!"*

At the **end of each round**, roll **1d4** for the behemoth's action. If a result repeats, consider picking a new one until Phase 3 has lasted four rounds. Every result hits **Kulupi and his clones too**.

- **Melee** claws +17 (agile, finesse), 2d6+8 slashing
- **Ranged** feather darts +17 (range increment 30 ft), 2d4+8 piercing
- **Vocal Projection:** when Kulupi speaks, all illusory princes speak the same words at the same time.

> **💡 GM tip (telling them apart):** Sharp players spot the real prince by his numbers (+26 for 3d8+12 vs. the clones' +17). That's fine. Finding him is part of the fight. Illusory Transposition can still swap a clone into the attack.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (spell immunity):** Zellura's morning *spell immunity* protects Kulupi against the PCs' favorite spell (p. 327). Pick the spell before the fight.

**Treasure:** without Monster Parts, Kulupi's signet ring is a *messenger's ring* (p. 336).

**XP:** an extra **80 XP** for foiling the cult's plots (p. 336).

**When Kulupi falls:** the spark of Varagix in him dies. With no Zellura or Colvi to sustain it, Varagix's link to Rumplank falters. The behemoth crashes into a small crater, and the cult's last forces flee. Read or paraphrase (p. 336–337):

> *"I... no!... Impossible! Lord Varagix... I did... everything you asked... Why... why have you forsaken me? Sister... I..." Kulupi gasps out as he reaches out towards his sister, but before he can finish, his eyes grow dull, he crumbles to the ground, and he breathes his last. The moment Kulupi dies, the behemoth convulses once, twice, and then grows still. All the links tethering Varagix to this world appear to have been severed, and so has the animating force compelling the enormous creature.*
>
> *You suddenly hear a chorus of screams and cries as the remaining cultists wail in agony as their invincible juggernaut is defeated and their morale is shattered. In an instant, the remaining enemies flee all at once, but with nobody to lead them, the scene is disorganized chaos. Seeing the PCs triumphant, the defenders send up a cheer, quickly rally, and start picking off the retreating enemies with ease.*
>
> *The Battle of Rumplank is finally over! Cheers of victory emerge from every corner of Rumplank!*

### The behemoth's heart gem

It holds the Jewel's remaining power (p. 337).
- **Recognizing it:** a **DC 25 Perception** or **DC 20 Crafting** check notices that its coloring still matches the Jewel.
- **Value:** a **2,800 gp** Monster Part. It can refine any item toward any common *Battlezoo Bestiary* imbued property. It can also refine toward the new rare **prismatic** weapon property, which studying the gem unlocks.
- **Without Monster Parts:** a Crafting ingredient of the same value for any magic item the PCs know.
- **Sharing:** the PCs can split it to give each weapon prismatic.

**Prismatic** (rare imbued property, weapon, p. 338). **Parts:** the undead shale behemoth, a monster with a "prismatic" attack or spell, or something similar at GM discretion. The table shows the paths of Magic and Might. The path of Technique is listed below it.

| Level | Path of Magic | Path of Might |
|---|---|---|
| **2nd** | Choose arcane or occult when first imbued. *Dancing lights* cantrip, heightened to half item level rounded up. Each light is a random color | — |
| **4th** | *color spray* once per day | +1 fire damage |
| **6th** | *color spray* heightens to 2nd level | +1 acid damage (stacks with fire) |
| **8th** | *hypnotic pattern* once per day; *color spray* heightens to 3rd | +1 electricity damage. Critical hit: slightly petrified, **clumsy 1** until the start of your next turn |
| **10th** | +1 fire damage | — |
| **12th** | Loses *color spray*; gains *chromatic ray* (APG) and *chromatic wall*, each once per day | Fire, acid, and electricity damage ignore resistances |
| **14th** | +1 acid damage (stacks with fire) | Critical hit: also **stupefied 1** until the start of your next turn |
| **16th** | *chromatic image* (SoM) once per day, losing *hypnotic pattern*. *Chromatic ray* heightens to 6th | — |
| **18th** | *prismatic spray* once per day, losing one of the previous three spells (your choice). *Chromatic wall* heightens to 7th if kept | +1 poison damage that ignores resistances (stacks) |

**Path Technique** (p. 338):
- **4th:** Strikes deal 1 persistent fire damage.
- **6th:** plus 1 persistent acid (stacks).
- **8th:** +1 fire damage and 1 persistent electricity (stacks). Critical hit: **clumsy 1** until the start of your next turn.
- **12th:** fire, acid, and electricity damage, persistent included, ignore resistances.
- **14th:** plus 1 persistent poison that ignores resistances (stacks).
- **16th:** critical hit: also **stupefied 1** until the start of your next turn.
- **18th:** critical hit: **slowed 1** for 1 round unless the target succeeds at a Will save against the weapon's DC.
- **20th:** creatures taking this persistent damage are ringed by seven shifting colors. Each melee attack they make against you, hit or miss, deals them damage equal to it. That's usually 1 fire, 1 acid, 1 electricity, and 1 poison. This damage doesn't grant a flat check to end the persistent damage.

## Scene 7 — Aftermath and celebration

### The fate of each district

Each district's final HP sets its fate (p. 338–340). The Indigo Stranger relays the news. Decide which generals and adjutants in fallen districts survive. Base it on their personalities and your group's taste. If losing favorites wouldn't be fun, let them all escape.

**General rule (p. 338):**
- **61+ HP:** mercifully few casualties.
- **1–60 HP:** significant losses; most Rumplankers know someone hurt or killed.
- **0 HP:** few defenders survive; generals and adjutants likely die.

| District | 61+ HP | 1–60 HP | 0 HP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Barnacle Bay** | Ports and beaches mostly unscathed. Divers tell of treasure in sunken enemy ships. Twin Talons untouched | Splintered docks and sabotage delay the port and raise import prices. Locals make substitute "mystery pies." Some displaced residents move to Twin Talons, annoying its hermits and inventors | Docks ruined, harbor choked with wreckage. Only essentials ship, which breeds smuggling and piracy. Idle dockworkers make it worse. Many Twin Talons homes leveled |
| **Barrel Square** | Battered but undaunted. Within hours, dancers, musicians, and vendors start an improvised celebration. The **Gilded Pirate statue survives** many attempts to destroy it. Weeks later, gilded PC statues appear overnight, maker unknown | The district mostly stands, but the square is ruined and the **Gilded Pirate statue smashed**. Streets are stained with blood and rum. Rumplankers gather to celebrate and mourn | Nearly leveled: shops ruined, food stores damaged, even **Pinkwater Well** tainted. People scrape together a festival elsewhere, but the economic damage is severe |
| **Claw Quarter** | Mostly unharmed except for the behemoth's furrow. Repairs leave it sturdier than it's been in decades | Patchy: some streets leveled, others spared. Repairs are slow, and crime grows | In shambles. By royal decree, the poor are relocated with food and clothing. Criminals, monsters, and haunts take the rest |
| **Old Rumplank** | Its narrow streets held. Life returns to its slow, traditional pace | Many wooden buildings damaged, but the stone taruki endure. Hospitals and relief centers fill parks and beaches | Mostly demolished, residents scattered. The Claw Quarter expands into it. Surviving taruki become monuments to a fading way of life |
| **Rosewater** | Minimal damage. The towers stay, painted vivid colors, as lookouts and zip-line stations | Heavy damage, but the capital stands. Months of repairs | The castle stands amid rubble. Most castle guards died; the children and others sheltering inside survived |

**The crater (p. 339):** the behemoth's corrupted corpse is packed with gems and raw materials. Rumplankers strip it to rebuild. Later, a memorial to the fallen rises there, decorated with monster parts and PC statues.

### Celebration

The royals and a recovering Princess Atriella gather the defenders. Add each PC's deeds to the speech. Adjust it to match how the districts fared. Read or paraphrase (p. 340):

> *As the crowd cheers you on, you advance to the makeshift stage where the remaining Redfeathers have gathered. The queen calls out, "Today, Rumplank faced the greatest trial the city has ever seen. Poppy herself fought to defeat this evil cult, but it emerged from the earth like a sickness and spread, infecting even our beloved son. Ultimately, we never suspected so many of our loved ones had fallen under the cult's sway, nor that they commanded a beast so powerful as to shake the very ground below and the firmament above. We were naive, and we would have had no chance on our own... But we were not alone. These brave heroes managed to unravel the cult's plan, muster our defenses, and put an end to this evil once and for all. In stopping the cult once and for all, these heroes of Rumplank have outdone even the Gilded Pirate herself! Everyone, three cheers for the heroes of Rumplank!"*

Far more than three cheers follow, and a feast runs late. There are fewer props than at Founder's Day, but the passion beats any festival in decades. NPCs from the campaign return to thank the PCs as their relationships warrant. The Indigo Stranger gives his assessment of the districts. He blames the PCs for nothing. He's proud of them, however many districts fell (p. 340).

**[Amon](gm_glossary.md#amon) and Castilli return**, this time as women. Alacar's gods have no mortal gender. Each viewer still sees them as their own ancestry. Read or paraphrase (p. 340–341):

> *A pair of women approach you. You haven't seen them before, but something about them is quite familiar. One is a short, rotund woman with rosy cheeks and vibrant clothing in a mismatched riot of colors. She carries an oversized tankard of ale in one hand and waves her other hand fancifully about. The other woman is tall with a narrow frame. She lingers nearby with her arms crossed, wearing attire that makes a stark contrast with her companion, sporting a black and white silk suit complete with a black top hat, white gloves, and silver spectacles. Finally, you place them. These are Amon and Castilli. The gods don't have only one form and are not limited to a single gender, though the outfits certainly helped you recognize them.*
>
> *"Is it just me, or is something a little different this time?" Amon wonders, "I can't really put my finger on it though."*
>
> *Castilli sighs, "Sister, it's obvious."*
>
> *Amon puts a finger to her lips, "Oh, I know. The ale is worse this time."*
>
> *Castilli rolls her eyes, "Yes, that's it. That's exactly the biggest change here." She turns to you, "Sorry for her. As you've seen now on two occasions, she's always like this. What you've accomplished is impressive. You've proven yourselves true heroes to this city, and in one small way, to the world of Alacar itself. But there's always another threat out there, a greater evil..."*
>
> *"And more adventure!" Amon bursts in to interrupt, "You are all so very entertaining!"*
>
> *"Yes, yes, sister, and more adventure too. Remain vigilant. Something tells me those shadow creatures will be back." Castilli ponders.*
>
> *"Wait, there were shadow creatures? I saw a big stone bug, some metal crabs, and all sorts of things, but when did the shadow creatures attack the city? I haven't drunk enough yet to forget that," Amon disagrees.*
>
> *"Not here. Before. Can't you remember things that happened more than a day ago?" Castilli chides.*
>
> *"Where's the fun in that, dear sister? Live in the moment! Just live like these mortals are doing. Keep up the good work and keep me entertained!" Amon reaches over and gives a high five to everyone who seems to agree with her sentiment... and a big bear hug to each PC!*

> **🧩 Sets up:** Castilli's "shadow creatures" are the Shadow Plane invaders from Moonshadow Island (Chapters 6–7). They're a hook for whatever comes next.

**Background special effect (Rumplank reveler):** victory day becomes an annual festival honoring the PCs, held shortly after Founder's Day. The festival has events named after Rumplank reveler PCs (p. 341).

**Background special effect (bluebell chef):** that PC likely becomes the Academy of Tastes' next Dean de Cuisine (p. 308).

### Conclusion

- **If Rumplank survived:** of hundreds of heroic tales, the PCs' tale is the grandest. They become as famous on the Indigo Isles as Poppy. Princess Atriella gets to know them and may offer marriage to one she finds compatible (p. 341).
- **If Rumplank was destroyed** but the PCs beat the behemoth, the castle's survivors rebuild.
- **If the PCs didn't stop the cult:** the cult makes Rumplank its base and worships Varagix openly. It sets out to conquer the Isles and beyond. Surviving PCs must raise a force against it. Many in Alacar would oppose a new demon-worshipping land.

> **💡 GM tip (the epilogue):** The book says to weigh each PC's story arc in their ending, background arcs included (p. 308). Give each player a closing beat.
