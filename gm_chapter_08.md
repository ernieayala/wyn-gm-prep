## The chapter in one minute

The PCs hold three fragments of the [Jewel of the Indigo Isles](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles). Poppy's notes put the fourth in [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef), a huge field of razor coral. An older map puts [Razorback Isle](gm_glossary.md#razorback-isle) in the same spot. Both are right. The cult's step pyramid stood on the isle. The isle sank into the reef when the cult fell. Now only the top of the pyramid shows above the sea floor.

The PCs cross the reef and meet [chochori](gm_glossary.md#chochori) shark hunters. Then they dive into a maze of coral and toxic kelp. They swim to the pyramid's top, which sits in a bubble of air. Inside are five floors of cult guardians and constructs. **Teaknis** (water elementals) are tearing the temple apart to flood it. In the deepest vault, the dead high priest and her followers have fused into a **skeletal cult dragon**. It guards the **Tangerine Shard**. Taking the shard ends the magic that holds back the sea. The temple floods within minutes.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Found where Razorback Isle used to be** (Scene 1).
2. **Got past the coral maze** and into the sunken temple (Scenes 2–4).
3. **Claimed the Tangerine Shard** from the skeletal cult dragon and escaped the flooding temple (Scene 5).

**Party level:** starts at 7, ends at **8**. This is the end of Act 2.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** This is Act 2's dungeon finale. It is also the PCs' first walk through the cult's own home. The library, murals, and carvings show who the cult was and what it wanted. They also show that the "treasure" is the cult's relic. The chapter delivers the last fragment, so the Jewel can form in Ch 9.

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|

| **Sea Hunters** (p. 194–196) | Social scene, four chochori shark hunters | On arrival |
| **Kalacho** (p. 195–197) | Optional undersea trading town 6 miles away | Any time, if the PCs go |
| **The Coral Maze** (p. 196–201) | Five areas, Q1–Q5, 40 feet underwater | Before the temple |
| **The Sunken Temple** (p. 201–215) | Seventeen areas, R1–R17, five floors, air inside | Last. R17 holds the shard |
| **Their Triumphant Return** (p. 215) | Back to the ship, level 8 | Closes Act 2 |

The PCs enter at Q1. Q2–Q4 lie on the way to the temple top at Q5. Wallopula says the temple lies west of the wreck. Breaking the coral at Q3 opens a shortcut. Inside the temple, the PCs work their way down to the vaults and the shard on the bottom floor.

**Threat math at party level 7** (see [Ch 1's XP rules](gm_chapter_01.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp)). XP per creature: level 3 is 10, level 4 is 15, level 5 is 20, level 6 is 30, level 8 is 60, level 9 is 80, and level 10 is 120.
- **R7:** Acolyte Bituminous (level 8, 60) plus two incense holders (level 3, 10 each) = 80 = **Moderate**.
- **R11:** the hydrojinxer (level 8, 60) plus a teakni sneak (level 5, 20) = 80 = **Moderate**.
- **R17:** the skeletal cult dragon (level 10, 120) = **Severe**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (two labels that don't match the math):** **Q5** is one complex level-8 hazard worth 60 XP. That adds up to Low, but the book says **Moderate 7**. **R13** is four simple level-8 hazards at 12 XP each, 48 total. That is a bit over Trivial, and the book says **Trivial 7**. Use the book's labels.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** There are 13 rated encounters plus exploration. Aim for two sessions: the voyage and maze, then the temple. The flood clock pushes the pace in the temple for you.

From Chapters 2 and 3:
- **NPC attitudes and Make an Impression** ([Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#npc-attitudes-make-an-impression-and-request)): Longjaw starts **indifferent**. Making an Impression on him is **DC 20** Diplomacy (Scene 2). Each step up gets more help.
- **Counteracting** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting)): a 4th-level *dispel magic* can shut down one Q5 **elemental backlash** rune (**counteract DC 24**). A critical success ends the whole hazard. It can also shut down one R13 **vault ward** (**counteract DC 26**).

### Underwater: swimming and aquatic combat

The maze is 40 feet down (p. 197). The book points to the *Core Rulebook* for these rules. In short:

- **Swim** (1 action, Athletics): no check needed in calm water. A success moves most PCs 10 feet (5 feet, plus 5 per 20 feet of land Speed). A critical success moves 15 feet. If you end your turn in water without a successful Swim, you sink 10 feet. The book gives no Swim DC for the maze, so treat the water as calm.
- **Swim Speed:** move at that Speed with no checks.
- **Sinking on purpose:** a PC wearing or holding something heavy sinks to the bottom and can walk the sea floor as difficult terrain (p. 197).

**Fighting underwater:**
- **Flat-footed** without a swim Speed.
- **Resistance 5 to acid and fire**.
- **Melee** slashing or bludgeoning attacks through water: **−2 circumstance penalty**.
- **Ranged** bludgeoning or slashing attacks **always miss** if the attacker or target is underwater. Ranged piercing attacks have their **range increments halved**.
- **No fire spells** or fire-trait actions.

Example: the Q4 shale swimmer has a swim Speed. Most PCs are flat-footed against it, and arrows reach half as far.

### Holding your breath and suffocation

*Water breathing* removes this problem. That's why Scene 1 pushes the PCs to get it. Without it:

- Hold breath for **5 + Constitution modifier** rounds.
- End of each turn: lose **1 round of air**, or **2** if you attacked or cast a spell that turn.
- Lose 1 more for each critical hit you take, or each critical failure on a save against a damaging effect.
- **Speaking uses up all remaining air**, including verbal spell components and command activations.
- **Out of air:** you fall unconscious and start suffocating. At the end of each turn, roll a **DC 20 Fortitude** save. Failure deals 1d10 damage. Critical failure kills. Each later save adds 5 to the DC and 1d10 to the damage. Getting air ends suffocation.

Example: the Q5 **elemental lash** refills a breath-holder's air on an air-trait hit and empties it on a water-trait hit.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A caster holding their breath who casts a verbal spell runs out of air at once.

### Navigating Runaway Reef (chapter subsystem)

This has two exploration activities, done in order. Any PC can attempt either one. First comes **Navigate to Razorback Isle**, then **Sail through Runaway Reef** (p. 194). Most results cost **marine resources**. With none to spend, it costs **1 extra week** and **100 gp** per missing resource. Gold can't replace a resource the party has. The outcomes are in [Scene 1](#scene-1--to-the-reef).

> **⚠️ Gotcha (these are defense resources too):** Marine resources are also Ch 12 defense resources (p. 117, p. 315). Each one spent here means one fewer Rumplank upgrade. The book lets you allow a different resource instead. Tell players before they spend.

### The flooding temple (chapter subsystem)

The temple floods in **stages** (p. 207), starting at **Stage 0**. The stages describe the bottom floor (R12–R17). Higher floors flood later.

| Stage | Bottom floor (R12–R17) | Other floors |
|---|---|---|
| **0** (start) | A bit over 1 foot deep. Difficult terrain for Small and smaller | Dry |
| **1** | 2 feet. Difficult terrain for Medium and larger, greater difficult for Small and smaller | Dry |
| **2** | 4 feet. Greater difficult terrain for Medium and larger. Small and smaller must swim | R6–R11 now floods at 2 stages less |
| **3** | 6 feet. Air still breathable, but everyone must swim | |
| **4** | Completely flooded | R3–R5 now floods at 4 stages less |
| **5** | | R2 floods at 5 stages less |
| **6** | Interior walls burst from the pressure | R1 floods at 6 stages less |
| **7–9** | Levels keep flooding, the temple groans | |
| **10** | Whole temple floods and collapses | |

**What raises the stage:**
- **Teaknis:** +1 per hour they work without interruption after the PCs meet them. If the PCs defeat them in R11 **or** R12, but not both, it's +1 per **2 hours** (p. 210).
- **Taking the Tangerine Shard (R17):** the protection ends for good. +1 **every minute** (p. 213).

Example: the PCs pull the shard at Stage 0. Three minutes later, everyone on the bottom floor is swimming. At four minutes, the bottom floor is underwater. At ten minutes, the temple collapses.

> **💡 GM tip:** Show players the stages as a countdown. Describe the water at each step. A clear clock scares players more than a vague one.

### The incapacitation trait

Effects that can take a creature out of a fight in one go have this trait. If a creature is **higher level than the hazard, creature, or item** making the effect, it treats its save as one degree better. For spells, the creature's level must be more than twice the spell's level.

Example: the R13 **vault wards** are level 8. Their petrification has the incapacitation trait. Level-7 PCs get no benefit, so a critical failure means **petrified for 24 hours**.

### Monster abilities: Grab, Trample, sweep

The skeletal cult dragon uses all three.
- **Grab** (1 action): use it right after a hit with a Strike that lists Grab. The target is grabbed until the end of the monster's next turn. The body part holding the target can't Strike others. The target can Escape.
- **Trample** (3 actions): Stride up to double Speed through creatures of the listed size or smaller. Each creature it moves into takes the listed Strike's damage, with a **basic Reflex** save at the listed DC. Each creature takes this only once per use.
- **Sweep** (weapon trait): +1 circumstance bonus to attack if it already attacked a different target this turn with that weapon.
- **Reach:** it can Strike anything within that distance without moving adjacent.

## Scene 1 — To the Reef

- **Where:** at sea, Moonshadow Island to Runaway Reef, **3 days** (p. 193).
- **Who:** Prince Kulupi, Garnikor, Slate Hodda, Fippin and Mippin.
- **Goal:** find where Razorback Isle used to be.

### Two maps, one answer

Poppy's notes put the last fragment in Runaway Reef. The reef is sunken razor coral that every wise sailor avoids. **Gustavius Gollihew's older map** shows Razorback Isle in the same place (p. 192).
- Kulupi and the sailors can't decide. Is there an island in the reef? Or does "razorback" mean the coral ridges?
- **DC 15 Nature** or **DC 13 Sailing Lore** reaches the same answer. Both are possible, so go and look.

> **🔒 GM-only (the truth):** Both maps are right. The cult built its headquarters, a huge step pyramid, on Razorback Isle. It drew elemental magic from stone and sea. The cult fell at the end of the War of the Shivering Behemoth. Then the magic failed, and earth magic pulled the pyramid into the muck of the sea floor. Only its top still shows. The top doors were once guard posts. Now they are the only way in. The final fragment is on the lowest level, guarded by creatures the cult left behind (p. 192–193).

### Recall Knowledge: the chochoris

- **DC 18 Society** (or trained in **Chochori Lore**): chochoris are sea dwellers who live near Runaway Reef.
- **Critical success:** their town is **Kalacho**. It's known for shark hunters who are serious but not unfriendly.

### Underwater preparation

The temple means long stretches underwater. Water breathing, a swim Speed, or both solves that (p. 193).
- Several alchemical and magic items grant these for a short time. **Kalacho** is the best place to buy them.
- A cleric or druid can prepare *water breathing*.
- Crew such as Garnikor sell a few items to PCs who are out of options. They might give them free to PCs who impressed them.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** [Holding your breath](#holding-your-breath-and-suffocation) and [aquatic combat](#underwater-swimming-and-aquatic-combat) decide the maze fights. Review them first.

### On the way

- **Fippin and Mippin** have fished the reef for rare crabs (plenty of butter and garlic). They never went near the map's spot. They share their old captain's advice on how not to run aground.
- **Slate Hodda** keeps watch. She's eager to see the famous coral.
- **Sea resources:** the PCs can gather marine resources on this leg (p. 117).

### Reaching Razorback Isle

Kulupi and Garnikor help plot a course, but they know no more than the PCs. The map gets them close. The PCs guide the rest ([subsystem overview](#navigating-runaway-reef-chapter-subsystem)).

**Navigate to Razorback Isle** (concentrate, exploration). **DC 22 Nature**, or **DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** or **Sailing Lore**.
- **Bonus:** +1 circumstance if another PC trained in one of these skills assists. It's +2 instead if the PCs also **impressed Fippin and Mippin** on the way to Moonshadow.
- **Critical success:** a safe course to where the isle should be.
- **Success:** they find a course, but coral damages the ship. Expend at least **1 marine resource** (the GM may allow another resource).
- **Failure:** as success, but at least **2**.
- **Critical failure:** they have no sense of where the isle is. Expend at least **1** for repairs, then retry.

**Sail through Runaway Reef** (concentrate, exploration). **Requirement:** a successful Navigate to Razorback Isle. **DC 23 Perception** or **DC 20 Sailing Lore**.
- **Bonus:** +1 circumstance if another PC who is master in Perception or trained in Sailing Lore assists. It's +2 instead if the PCs also **impressed Slate Hodda** on the way to Moonshadow.
- **Critical success:** the ship reaches the spot fast. There's no land there.
- **Success:** the ship arrives damaged. Expend at least **1 marine resource** (or another, GM's choice).
- **Failure:** as success, but at least **2**.
- **Critical failure:** the directions lead into danger. Expend at least **1** for repairs, then retry.

**XP:** **30 XP** on reaching where Razorback Isle stood.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** "No land there" is the reveal. Let the empty sea sit for a moment before the hunters arrive.

## Scene 2 — Sea Hunters

- **Where:** on deck, at the spot in the reef (p. 194).
- **Who:** four chochori shark hunters: **Longjaw** (he/him), **Hammerhead**, **Graysquid**, and **Tigerstripe**.
- **Goal:** win them over for directions, warnings, and maybe potions.

The hunters know this reef is dangerous, but they know of no island. They board to warn the strangers. Read or paraphrase:

> *A crew member's surprised shout barely registers before four colorful figures haul themselves onto the deck of the ship. These chochoris look muscular and grim. They're armed with several tridents and spears each, although they don't have any weapons in hand. Most bear jagged scars, missing digits, or both.*
>
> *"These are dangerous waters you've entered. Even we avoid them. What manner of foolishness brings you right into Runaway Reef?"*

- They can't tell who's in charge. **Kulupi stays out of sight** because he fears a trap. So the PCs do the talking.
- **Polite PCs:** the hunters sit on a barrel or gunwale and chat.
- **Rude, dismissive, or secretive PCs:** they shrug and swim off.

**Who they are:** chochoris take hunting-names from their fiercest kill. The leader is **Longjaw** (LN terath chochori hunter 6, personal name **Intha**, p. 195). He killed the great white shark Longjaw, long a menace to chochori travelers. The four are old friends. They boast of hunts and tease each other over every scar.

### Chochori advice

Longjaw starts **indifferent**. The PC who has talked the most attempts **DC 20 Diplomacy** to [Make an Impression](gm_chapter_02.md#npc-attitudes-make-an-impression-and-request).
- **Gift:** if offered a gift or payment, Longjaw names one of the PCs' resources his people could use. Giving it raises his attitude **one step**, whatever the roll.

**What he shares by attitude:**
- **Indifferent or worse:** his hunters rarely come here because of the maze of coral and kelp about **40 feet** down. He points out its entrance (**Q1**) and leaves.
- **Friendly** (adds):
  - Dangerous creatures live inside. A roof of **toxic kelp** blocks entry from above.
  - Rumor of a strange structure in a **bubble of air** somewhere in the maze.
  - His hunters won't go near the maze. They will keep sharks and other ordinary predators off the ship while the PCs are here.
  - They live in **Kalacho**, about **6 miles** away. Some surface traders stay there in air-filled buildings. He suggests buying *potions of water breathing*, *scrolls of water breathing*, or *sea touch elixirs* there. He gives only distance and direction, so finding it takes some searching.
- **Helpful** (adds): he offers to fetch **one *potion of water breathing* per PC** from Kalacho by calling in favors. He hopes the PCs will thin out the maze's threats for his hunters.

**XP:** **30 XP** if the hunters end friendly or better.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Longjaw's requested resource is one the PCs could spend on Ch 12's defenses. Make the trade-off clear.

### Recall Knowledge: Kalacho

After the PCs meet the hunters, roll a **secret** check for each PC: **DC 20 Society**, or **DC 15 Chochori Lore** or **Indigo Isles Lore** (p. 195–196).
- **Critical success:** as success, plus more. Several chochori sacred sites hide in the reef. Their elders worship strange gods of the deep ocean and are respected in Kalacho. So divine and primal items are plentiful there. Visitors who revere the elders get a **10 percent discount** on such items.
- **Success:** Kalacho is a friendly undersea chochori community in the reef. It has several air-filled buildings for traders who breathe air.
- **Failure:** only "it's an underwater town."
- **Critical failure:** a confident lie. Kalacho's chochoris are sly mystics who curse outsiders. They sell fake items that work for a while, then fail at the worst moment.

## Scene 3 — Kalacho (optional)

**Kalacho** is Settlement 5, a neutral chochori trading town under the sea. It's about 6 miles from the reef site (p. 195). Use it for supplies, rest, Monster Parts crafting, or meeting locals. The map is on p. 196.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Government** | Council, one member per [chora](gm_glossary.md#chori-chochori-chora) |
| **Population** | 861 (98% chochori, 2% other) |
| **Languages** | Chochori, Common, Aquan |
| **Religions** | [The Balance](gm_glossary.md#the-balance), [The Eld](gm_glossary.md#the-eld) |
| **Threats** | Aquatic monsters, ocean storms |

**Shopping:**
- **Undersea trading post:** at any time, it has 1d4 permanent items of level 6–10, and twice as many consumables of level 6–10. The stock changes over about a month.
- **Wave magic:** divine and primal items up to level 7 are regularly for sale, mostly scrolls.
- **Common items** of 5th level or lower, including those Longjaw mentions.

**Notable people** (accomplishment names, with personal names in brackets):
- **Ordinance of Prosperity** [Hara]: N hypisk chochori councilor 7, council chair.
- **Longjaw** [Intha]: LN terath chochori hunter 6, veteran hunter and sea warden.
- **Airdweller's Annex** [Kaso]: CG aikrys chochori merchant 5, eccentric inventor of the Airdweller's Annex and Airdweller's Market.
- **Renovation of the Ascent** [Jikr]: CN jamambri chochori architect 4, builder, dreamer, marketer.

**Kalacho directory** (p. 197):
- **The Ascent:** a huge vertical shaft through the hiding sea caves and reefs. It's the only known way in or out. It's also a known weak point, so it's heavily guarded. It was recently renovated with a **diving bell** down to the Airdweller's Annex and sturdy automatic emergency gates.
- **Reefmarket:** grander than the town's size suggests, with inns and taverns in its annexes. Visiting merchants can swell the population by up to 50% during pilgrimages and holidays.
- **Airdweller's Annex:** the Reefmarket annex at the foot of the diving bell. It's the only comfortable home for surface folk. A permanent air bubble fills its dome, kept fresh by gardens of surface plants. It offers helpful merchants, a directory in many languages, and delivery to the PCs' ship for a small fee (airtight or waterlogged as needed).
- **Great Shrine:** a small shrine to all faiths. Rival donations of art and labor grew it into a striking collage of cultures and styles.
- **Hall of the Council:** an elegant coral hall for the seven councilors and three witnesses each. It has private side rooms.
- **Deepcaves:** older chochoris know these caves lead into a giant dungeon below Kalacho. They also reach the surface by many paths. The young no longer learn a safe path. Elders fear the whole town could be trapped if the Ascent fails.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Kulupi and the sailors want to finish the voyage. They disapprove if the PCs linger too long (p. 197).

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the Ascent isn't the only way out):** The book calls it "the only known way to enter or leave Kalacho." Then it says elders know safe Deepcave paths (p. 197). Read "only known" as "the one most residents use."

> **💡 GM tip:** The Deepcaves are a hook the book leaves unwritten, with no map or encounters. Use them only if you want to build something.

## Scene 4 — The Coral Maze

- **Where:** the sea floor, 40 feet down. Map on p. 198.
- **Goal:** reach the temple top at **Q5**.

The maze grew on the ridges of old Razorback Isle. Earth magic leaked from the cult's rituals and made the stone fertile. Coral and **stinging kelp** ran wild. Most life here is harmless, but a few big predators hunt the maze (p. 196).

### Coral maze features

- **Depth:** the sea floor is about **40 feet** down. Ridges of stone and coral rise **10 to 20 feet**. They form winding passages and dead ends.
- **Walls:** sharp and painful to grab or climb.
- **Movement:** mostly swimming. A PC with something heavy sinks and can walk the floor as **difficult terrain** ([underwater rules](#underwater-swimming-and-aquatic-combat)).
- **Light:** dimmer than at the surface, but visibility is otherwise normal. The kelp canopy blocks little light.
- **Swimming in from above:** the kelp forms a loose, toxic ceiling.
  - Entering from above anywhere but Q1 takes a **DC 23 Athletics** check to Force Open a path. It also takes a **DC 20 Fortitude** save against the toxin. Failed save: **clumsy 1** and **enfeebled 1** for 1 hour (poison).
  - An Athletics **critical success** improves the save by one degree. A **critical failure** worsens it by one.
  - The kelp closes behind each swimmer, so **every PC** must check.
  - **Swimming up** through it needs no Athletics check, only the save.
- The hunters can warn the PCs not to enter from above.

### Map quick reference

| Area | Threat | What's there | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** Maze Entry | — | The way in | DC 20 Nature or DC 15 Ocean Lore: fish shun the kelp and the south passage to Q2 |
| **Q2** Coral Grotto | Moderate 7 | **Wallopula** (myzoform) + giant glaucus slug | Talkable. Knows the ship and temple locations |
| **Q3** Sunken Ship | — | *Distant Star* wreck | **Phantom stardust** (defense resource). Breakable shortcut to Q5 |
| **Q4** Churned Sand | Low 7 | **Shale swimmer** | Greater difficult terrain on foot. Harmless dead turtle in dead end |
| **Q5** Trapped Air | Moderate 7 | **Elemental Backlash** (complex hazard) | Temple top. Touch triggers it |

### Q1 — Maze Entry

> *Walls of sharp rock adorned with colorful coral rise from the sea floor, creating twisting passages leading west. A forest of waving kelp rises from atop the coral-encrusted walls, twining together in a canopy about thirty feet overhead. Small fish, crustaceans, and other sea creatures dart in and among the coral and across the sea floor, though these animals seem to avoid the kelp fronds.*

**DC 20 Nature** or **DC 15 Ocean Lore** while studying the sea life: the animals avoid the kelp because of its toxin. They avoid the passage south to **Q2** because a predator lairs there.

### Q2 — Coral Grotto *(Moderate 7)*

> *This open area has a riot of colorful coral in rainbow hues. The widened channel turns west, then ends in an area where the coral has been scraped away into strange patterns.*

This is the home of **Wallopula** (they/them), a myzoform that eats memories and personalities. Their latest meals were the two *Distant Star* survivors (Q3): **Gortum** (female orpok bosun) and **Karkak** (male g'mayun deckhand). Wallopula swaps between those two forms to savor each one's memories. The scraped coral is Wallopula's art. It shows overlapping portraits of everyone Wallopula has eaten, a muddle of faces up close.

Wallopula's pet **giant glaucus slug** hunts small sea animals. Wallopula lives on recycled memories alone.

**Creatures:**
- **Wallopula:** myzoform, BB 65, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +16. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- **Giant glaucus slug:** BB 38, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.

**When the PCs arrive:**
- Wallopula carves as the orpok. Then they become the g'mayun without missing a stroke.
- They are too absorbed to notice the PCs unless the PCs are noisy. The slug perks up and follows Wallopula's lead, whether that means fighting or staying clear.

**Talking to Wallopula:** fighting isn't their first instinct. PCs who aren't hostile get a conversation.
- Shifting forms, they tell how the *Distant Star* ran aground. Gortum and Karkak survived in the air-filled hold. Wallopula glosses over the part where "they died there."
- **DC 28 Perception** to Sense Motive: Wallopula found and ate them.
- The *Distant Star* lies **northwest**. A strange building in an air bubble lies farther **west**. Wallopula never explored it, because it seemed suspicious and short on prey.
- A pleasant PC, or one who praises the carving, can try **DC 24 Diplomacy**. On a success, Wallopula says the building seems to have a **magical hazard**.
- Only the slug knows about the shale swimmer. A PC who can talk to the slug learns of a dangerous predator to the **west**, in **Q4**.

**If a fight starts:**
- Wallopula retreats below **80 HP**. They retreat below **30 HP** instead if they are captivated after draining a PC with **Leech Mind**.
- A living slug flees with them. Both prefer to flee to **Q1**. If it's blocked, they swim up through the kelp.

**Befriending Wallopula:** gives **full XP**, plus eel skin and slug mucus worth both creatures' full Monster Parts value.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** This is a predator the PCs can talk to. Friendship pays the same XP and treasure as a fight. It also adds a warning about Q5.

> **💡 GM tip:** The shift from orpok to g'mayun mid-sentence is the creepy part. Play it calm and friendly. Let Sense Motive land the horror.

### Q3 — Sunken Ship

> *Most of this wide area between the ridges of stone and coral is filled with a sunken ship that appears to have settled here recently. Large sections of the ship's hull have been torn away and are clearly the cause of the vessel's demise. One of its two masts is snapped, and the other sticks up into the waving kelp canopy. On the prow, near a figurehead of a g'mayun peering through a spyglass, is the name Distant Star.*

The reef tore the hull open a few weeks ago. The myzoform in Q2 ate the two survivors.

**The shortcut:** the coral behind the wreck was smashed in the sinking and has only just regrown. It's weaker than the coral elsewhere.
- **DC 22 Nature or Survival** to spot it while investigating the ship.
- It's tedious but easy to break: **60 HP, Hardness 5 per square**.
- It opens into the dead end **north of Q5**. This makes a shorter route between the temple and the outside of the maze.

**Defense special resource (phantom stardust (1 batch, p. 200)):** almost everything else aboard is ruined, but **phantom stardust** remains in the hold. It weighs **20 Bulk**, so a *bag of holding* is the likely way to carry it.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Phantom stardust is a Ch 12 defense special resource (p. 315). It upgrades **Decoys** to **Illusory Decoys**, a tier-2 defense. With it, attacking armies in the district deal 2 less damage instead of 1 (p. 317). **Write it down.**

### Q4 — Churned Sand *(Low 7)*

> *This wide area between the coral is filled with smooth sand.*

The cult bred many shale spitters (gem-crusted isopods). Most died when the island sank. The aquatic **shale swimmer** thrived and has hunted near the temple ever since. This is its favorite spot. Its repeated Ground Spray has ground the floor to sand.
- **Terrain:** **greater difficult terrain** for walkers (deep sand).

**Shale Swimmer:** p. 394, Creature 8. Initiative: Stealth +18. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- It hides in the south ridge, waiting for large prey. Its wavesense and tremorsense likely let it notice the PCs first.
- It surges out with **Ground Spray** (p. 394). This is a once-per-round Stride or Swim. If it ends on solid ground, above or below water, it kicks up a 15-foot cone of sand. Creatures in the cone must succeed at a **DC 26 Fortitude** save or be **dazzled** for 1 round (1 minute on a critical failure).
- It has never been beaten. It **doesn't flee**, and it chases foes who run.
- Its items include an **emerald heart gem worth 100 gp** (p. 394).

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** It has resistance 10 to piercing and slashing (p. 394). PCs without a swim Speed are also flat-footed ([aquatic combat](#underwater-swimming-and-aquatic-combat)). This Low fight can hit harder than its label.

#### The dead end

The south exit leads to a gruesome surprise.

> *A skeletal sea turtle gripped in strands of kelp floats in the water at this dead end, the seaweed growing in and around holes punctured in its shell. The creature's bony head turns from side to side as though peering about for danger, but its empty eye sockets are vacant and lifeless.*

It's an ordinary dead turtle. Kelp grew through its shell into the ceiling and holds it up in the water. A few small green crabs live inside and make the skull turn. Paranoid PCs may attack it. Let them.

### Q5 — Trapped Air *(Moderate 7)*

> *A strange building stands in this open area. It's approximately fifteen feet square, and it's surrounded by a dome of air. Two doors lead into the structure, and its base widens a bit at ground level, as though it were only the upper part of a larger structure under the sea floor.*

This is the temple's top. Both doors lead to **R1**.
- **Doors:** stone, and stuck. **DC 28 Athletics** to Force Open, or **1 minute** of work.
- **Air bubble:** leaked air, held in place by magic. Anyone can pass in freely. A few **dead fish** swam in and suffocated.
- **DC 20 Arcana or Nature:** the PC understands the boundary and knows the building is full of air. **Critical success:** the PC also learns the boundary might be hazardous.

**Hazard:** the cult's elemental energy leaks out as mixed air, water, and earth. It keeps the bubble going, and it lashes out. **Whenever a creature touches the structure, including either door,** the energy overloads.

**Elemental Backlash:** Hazard 8, unique complex magical trap (p. 201)
- **Stealth** +18 (expert)
- **Description:** four invisible runes, one on each side of the building, leak elemental energy.
- **Disable:** **DC 26 Arcana** (expert), **DC 24 Nature** (expert), or **DC 28 Thievery** (expert) drains one rune. A critical success also drains a rune on an adjacent side, if one is active. A successful ***dispel magic*** (4th level; **counteract DC 24**) disables one rune. A critical success on it dispels the whole hazard ([counteracting](gm_chapter_03.md#counteracting)).
- **Rune:** Hardness 15; HP 50 (BT 25); object immunities.
- **Awakened Energy** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature touches any part of the structure, including the doors. **Effect:** the hazard makes an elemental lash Strike against that creature. Then it rolls initiative.
- **Melee** elemental lash +20 (air, earth, or water, equal chance of each; reach 10 feet), **3d6+10 bludgeoning** plus elemental effect.
- **Elemental effect:**
  - **Air:** if the creature hit is holding its breath, it renews all its air, as if it took a fresh breath.
  - **Water:** the creature loses all its air if it's holding its breath.
  - **Earth:** the creature is pushed back 5 feet. If it can't be pushed, it takes an extra 1d6 bludgeoning instead.
- **Routine (4 actions):** the trap loses 1 action for each rune drained, dispelled, or destroyed. Each action uses a different active rune. That rune makes an elemental lash Strike against a random creature in reach. If no creature is in reach, it makes an **eruption** under 1 random creature within 60 feet (no line of sight needed). The eruption deals **6d6 bludgeoning**, **DC 26 basic Reflex**. It has an equal chance of the air, water, or earth trait. A creature that fails takes that element's effect. The hazard doesn't target the same creature twice in a turn unless it has no other options.
- **Reset:** all four runes come back 24 hours after the hazard has no targets for its routine.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (you can't open the door without touching it):** Forcing the doors triggers the trap. Some players get a warning from Wallopula or a critical Arcana/Nature success. Reward them if they disable runes first.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** This is a routine hazard like Ch 1's snake pit ([complex hazards](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards)). Each disabled rune costs it one action each round.

### If the PCs get into trouble underwater

The book suggests this (p. 201). PCs who are knocked out or out of air float up through a rare gap in the kelp. The crew fishes them out and nurses them back to health.

## Scene 5 — The Sunken Temple

> **🔒 GM-only (what happened here):** The War of the Shivering Behemoth ended here two centuries ago. Poppy's forces stormed the pyramid, stopped the rituals, and beat the defending cultists. The cult's elemental rituals were meant to stop a ranged attack from the sea. They failed against a ground force, and their wild magic sank the island and temple. The only recent visitors are **teaknis**, curious and destructive water elementals who came to explore and sabotage (p. 201).

Cult symbols cover the temple: towering shale spitters, gemstone designs, and mountains cracked open to release wicked energy. Nothing here is hidden, so the PCs can piece together much of the cult's history and aims. Everything is old, with chipped stone and many faded murals (p. 203).

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** This is the cult's own house. Each room reveals more about the cult at the end of Poppy's trail. The vault mosaics show what the cult meant for the Isles.

### Sunken temple features

- **Construction:** tightly fitted stone, including the stairs. Stone doors. **15-foot** ceilings.
- **Air:** old elemental protections trap stale, breathable air. Unintended magic holds it in as much as pressure does. Seepage pools at the bottom.
- **Flooding:** see [the flooding temple](#the-flooding-temple-chapter-subsystem). Removing the shard in R17 floods the temple fast.
- **Doors:** none are locked. The vault doors on the bottom level are trapped (R13).
- **Light:** magical lights glow on landings and in halls. Rooms are dark unless noted.
- **Map:** p. 202.

> **⚠️ Gotcha: four levels or five?** The features text says "four levels" (p. 203). The flood sidebar and R10 stone model use **five**: R1, R2, R3–R5, R6–R11, and R12–R17. Run five, because the flooding rules need them.

**Background Special Effect:** this temple is huge news for an **indigo historian** PC, because Poppy worked to erase it from history. Information and items they bring back make them a star with the **Royal Society of History** (p. 203).

### R1 — Temple Top

> *This small room has a staircase in the middle of the floor that descends in a narrow, tight spiral. Stone doors lead out to the north and south.*

Guards watched the isle and sea from here and carved what they saw. The carvings are amateurish views from atop a narrow-ridged island. They are most detailed near the doors. Both doors lead to Q5. The air is breathable. PCs may expect a trap after Q5, but **this room is safe**.

### R2 — Guard Room

> *A narrow spiral staircase pierces this room, extending up through the ceiling and down through the floor. Old weapon racks and sagging benches fill the room. The walls are adorned with images of strange crustaceans.*

Off-duty guards relaxed here. The walls are carved with the shale spitters the cult prized.
- The PCs can recognize **shale spitters, shale swarms, and shale chargers**.
- Some carvings show **much larger** kinds the PCs haven't faced yet.
- Bored guards gave many carvings googly eyes and fanged smiles. Beside one shale charger's mouth is a stick figure bent in half: *"Oops! I'm Barlo and I got caught sleeping on guard duty and I got fed to a shale charger."*

> **🧩 Sets up:** The giant carvings foreshadow the **shale behemoth** the cult raises in Ch 11 and sends at Rumplank in Ch 12.

### R3 — Barracks

> *Several metal bunk beds have been shoved into the northwest corner of this room. Torn and rotted bedding litters the floor.*

Nobody knows what wrecked the room. No tracks show anyone passing in ages. A search finds a fallen **wooden plaque**. It's a warning from cult leaders: *"Barlo's fate will be yours if your attention wavers! Watching from the pinnacle is an obligation, not a privilege."*

### R4 — Training Room *(Low 7)*

> *This long room has only a stone double door as an entrance. The walls are badly chipped and scorched, particularly near the narrower north end of the room.*

Guard recruits trained here against a crab-like construct. They passed by landing three damaging hits on it.

**Elite Mechanical Artillerist:** BB 9, 61, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +17. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- It crouches in the southeast corner. When a living creature enters, it says: *"Training program commencing. Three hits to deactivate."*
- Its programming is broken. On the third damaging hit, it says: *"Congratulations!... Training program commencing. Three hits to deactivate."* It fights **until destroyed**.
- It pursues foes but can't fit on the stairs, so it stops at the next landing. It can crawl sideways through the doors to **R3** and **R5** as difficult terrain.

> **💡 GM tip:** Let the players believe the three-hit rule. The reset is the joke.

### R5 — Library *(Moderate 7)*

> *This irregularly shaped room has shallow bookshelves against most of its walls. These are filled with books and scrolls that are in very poor shape. Near the west wall, a mound of fallen books are soaked into a soggy mess, and a small translucent creature lies on the floor near them. A freestanding bookshelf has fallen over in the middle of the room.*

These are records for the rituals on the floor below. They were never well kept, and most works are fragments.
- **The dead teakni:** a teakni sneak crept up here and shredded books out of boredom. The library's guardian killed it a couple of days ago.

**Living Library:** BB 57, Creature 9. Initiative: Stealth +20. Monster Parts 90/200/360 gp.
- It absorbed cult wizards' spellbooks and gained sentience and a little malevolent will. It formed after the sinking, so it knows little of the cult. It has seen almost no one but the teakni, so it thinks **all visitors are saboteurs**.
- **Blend In**: it poses as books on the fallen shelf, or hides anywhere in the room.
- It ignores anyone passing through. If it's discovered, or anyone **peruses the books**, it casts ***cloudkill*** over nearly the whole library. Then it uses other spells and **Scroll Vortex**, and demands that the "saboteurs" leave.
- It **doesn't pursue**. Afterward, it Blends In again.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Research means perusing, and perusing sets off the library. Most groups fight first and read after.

**Perusing the library:** poking around reveals the cult's name, the **Cult of the Shivering Behemoth**. It also reveals the cult's aim: to use magic to unleash a terrible evil across the islands, wiping out all life not loyal to it. To learn more, a PC spends **1d4 hours** on a **secret** check: **DC 27 Perception**, **DC 23 Arcana or Nature**, or **DC 18 Library Lore**.
- **Success:** one new fact below.
- **Critical success:** two facts.
- **Critical failure:** a false clue you make up.

1. An annotated map. The cult's base was a pyramid atop treacherous Razorback Isle, guarded by cult-made monsters and ringed by Runaway Reef. The cult thought it nearly impossible to take, mostly thanks to the watch from its top.
2. Spy reports: neighboring islands were uniting against the cult's "unstoppable plan." The cult answered by speeding up its plans, however dangerous.
3. The cult's **Moonshadow Island** fortress existed to mine special metals from old cliffside tunnels. Letters with a cult leader there call the mission nearly done. They weigh abandoning the fortress.
4. The pyramid was cult headquarters. A note to a supplier lists guard chambers at the top, ritual rooms in the middle, and vaults at the bottom.
5. A description written for an honored, unnamed visitor. The main entrance is on the lowest level, which was then ground level. A grand processional staircase leads up to the middle levels. The main entrance gives no access to the vaults.
6. Notes among unfinished magic item formulas mention an important **rainbow-colored gemstone in four pieces**. Together, the pieces are as powerful as the mightiest artifacts. The notes don't say whether the cult made it or found it as part of a larger plan.

**Treasure:** a crushed skeleton in tattered cult robes lies under the fallen shelf. It wears ***gloves of storing*** that hold a ***scroll of stoneskin***.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Fact 6 reveals that the treasure is a four-piece cult relic. Fact 3 links back to Moonshadow (Ch 6–7). Both pay off in Ch 9, when the Jewel forms and the cult strikes.

### R6 — Landing

> *Several staircases meet at this wide landing: one up and one down to the west, and stately stairs descending to the east. Several doors lead off of this landing as well. Small bits of twisted metal litter the hallway between the double doors to the northwest and the one to the southwest.*

This landing was busy in the cult's day. It's usually empty now.
- The copper and iron bits are a smashed **incense holder** (**DC 23 Crafting**). **Critical success:** it was an animated creature, destroyed in an old skirmish.
- The double doors to the northwest (R7) and southwest (R8) each have a small pet-door flap. The flaps fit the animated incense holders.
- **Tiny explorers:** each time the PCs arrive, roll for a **1 in 6** chance. On a hit, an incense holder from R7 or R8 is out spying at the stairs and doors. If it hears the PCs, it freezes and poses as an object. When it can, or once it's found, it scurries back to warn its acolyte.

**Treasure:** the smashed holder's tiger-shaped handle is a ***tiger menuki talisman***.

### R7 — Ritual Room *(Moderate 7)*

> *This room has several wall sconces at a variety of heights, each illuminated with a flame but bearing no torch or other fuel. Drawings of waves cover the room's walls.*

When a ritual begins, the carved waves roll and the sconces move with them. Ritual skill checks here get a **+1 circumstance bonus**.

The cult kept two **automatic acolytes** for spellcasting and faith. **Acolyte Bituminous** wears a pitch-black robe that leaves sticky residue. It worked earth-and-water magic. Since the fall, it has been the bitter rival of **Acolyte Igneous** (R8). Neither can destroy the other. The tracking stones of its two pet animated incense holders sit inside it. This lets it follow and direct them from afar.

**Creatures:**
- **Acolyte Bituminous:** automatic acolyte, BB 19, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +18. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
  - **Cult Acolyte:** it's infused with negative energy for its Divinity Beam. Each spell-storing fist holds a 3rd-level ***hydraulic push*** that shows up as a rush of pitch. An empty fist regains its spell after 1 week.
- **Animated Incense Holders (2):** toy spy, BB 112, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp each.
  - **Divine Soporific:** the Needle Poison reservoir refills with **lethargy poison** every day at sunset.

**How it acts:**
- It assumes intruders are Igneous's agents or tricks. It's doubly sure of this for PCs tied to fire or dressed in bright red.
- **Patient or passive** PCs can convince it they mean no harm. It's quick to hear treachery, even where none is meant. When it does, it shrieks betrayal and attacks.
- Once calm, it can explain the room and share the **R5** library facts.
- It **never retreats or surrenders**. It stays in the room but sends the incense holders after foes who flee.

**Befriending it:** gives metallic components worth the full Monster Parts value of it and both incense holders.

**Treasure:** with Monster Parts, its cloak is magical but useless. It stains anyone who wears it for an hour or so with tar. **Without Monster Parts,** it also works as a ***cloak of elvenkind***.

### R8 — Summoning Chamber *(Moderate 7)*

> *A ring of runes is carved into the floor of this square chamber. Spots around the ring are crusted with clumps of dark wax, as though black candles once burned there. A smell like coal dust and sulfur hangs in the air.*

The circle held summoned fiends and elementals while casters questioned them or bargained. It has been unused since before the fall.

**Acolyte Igneous** assisted with summonings and treats the room as its domain. Wicked elementals and fiends made it short-tempered and cruel. It ranks its long-idle work above any other construct's. Its robe looks like cooling lava, black with glowing red cracks. Its two incense holders are dented and singed from its abuse.

**Creatures:**
- **Acolyte Igneous:** automatic acolyte, BB 19, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +18. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
  - **Cult Acolyte:** infused with negative energy for its Divinity Beam. Each spell-storing fist holds a ***blistering invective*** (*Advanced Player's Guide*). An empty fist regains its spell after 1 week.
- **Animated Incense Holders (2):** toy spy, BB 112, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp each. Divine Soporific as in R7.

**How it acts:**
- It's far chattier than Bituminous. It's self-important and loves holding "court." It can explain the room and share the **R5** library facts.
- Sudden rages end talks. **At some point it attacks, provoked or not.**
- Once it fights, the incense holders join in. They try to stay out of its melee reach. All three fight **until destroyed** and **pursue** foes who flee.

**Treasure:** Igneous's cloak is clever engineering, not magic. It's a ***heated cloak*** (*Pathfinder Guns & Gears*).

> **💡 GM tip:** The acolytes' feud is a gift for roleplay. Each blames the other for the PCs. Igneous badmouths Bituminous before its temper snaps.

### R9 — Storeroom

> *An acrid smell lingers in this cramped storeroom.*

The room holds ritual components for this floor. Some are spoiled.

**Treasure:** a ***moderate mistform elixir***, a ***greater comprehension elixir***, and a dose of ***wyvern poison***.

### R10 — Warding Chamber

> *Several skeletons sprawl on the floor of this room. In the northeast corner is a low table with stones stacked on it in a pyramidal shape. Strange blast marks and stains mar the room's bare stone.*

This is the pyramid's ward control room. Poppy and her crew finally beat the cult's leaders here.
- **DC 23 Medicine or Society:** the bones come from a great battle. One side was humanoids in sailors' clothes. The other was humanoids in heavy gray robes set with gems.
- Withered shale spitter husks lie among them.

**The model:** a stone model of the pyramid, slick with mud. It has two entrances at the top and a larger one at the bottom.
- **Five levels.** Each one lifts off to show the one below. The second-lowest level is stuck to the lowest.
- **Layout:** it shows R1 through R11 and the stairs down to the R12 doors. It shows layout only, not contents.
- **Damage:** on the model, R11's east wall and floor are damaged. A PC can peer through into R16. Salt water from an unknown source pools in the model's lowest level.
- **The enchantment works both ways.** On the table, the model mirrors the pyramid's damage: holes, leaks, and worsening. Changing the model changes the pyramid. Reshaping it took powerful magic, such as cult rituals with spells like *shape stone*. It resists ordinary tampering. **Restoring** it is easier. **DC 23 Crafting** repairs some of the elementals' damage. It also makes the teaknis more irritable and likelier to attack in R11 and R12.
- **Removing the stuck level:** only chosen cult members could lift it to examine (or change) the vaults. A PC can fake that authority with the **Trick Magic Item** feat. The check is **DC 23 Arcana or Occultism**, or **DC 25 Deception** for a PC who is expert or better in Deception.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book gives no flood-stage effect for repairing the model. Repairs only change the teaknis' mood.

**Treasure:** a pirate corpse holds a ***rod of wonder***. A cultist corpse grips a ***gallow's tooth*** talisman.

### R11 — Reception Room *(Moderate 7)*

> *Toppled and broken statues litter the floor of this chamber; not a single one remains upright. Arches along the walls are filled with murals depicting jagged coastlines leading down to churning seas, as though the wall were merely a window looking out over a rocky island. The far eastern edge of the room has portions of its floor crumbled away. The badly cracked stone wall above it leaks muddy water.*

The cult entertained its rare visitors here. The murals are an artistic take on the R1 carvings. They mimic a view over the isle and sea. PCs who saw R1 notice the same intent.

**The hole** is split by a wall below. The **north** side drops into **R12**. The **south** side drops into **R16**.

**The teaknis' story:**
- A few weeks ago, the trapped air drew them here. They squirmed through the outside muck to the main doors (R12).
- Their leader, the **hydrojinxer**, disrupted the sealing magic enough to slip them in. They **can't get out**. They don't want to leave until the temple collapses and the water takes back the air.
- They chose this east wall as the weakest. Their first breach broke the floor instead. The **hieroglyph scorpion** in R16 keeps driving them out of the vault. So they're widening the other half of the hole and the connecting wall.
- One sneak died upstairs, killed by the living library (R5). Two **teakni saboteurs** are widening the main doors in R12. The **hydrojinxer** and another **teakni sneak** are here.

**Talking to them:** they expect a fight but aren't hostile right away.
- The hydrojinxer (she/her) openly explains their aims and what teaknis are. This is likely new to the PCs.
- She gives **no name**. She feels a name would hold her back.
- She **turns hostile at once** at any suggestion to stop. The sabotage obsesses them all.
- She shrugs off the dead teakni. Not everyone likes teakni mischief.
- **If the PCs offer to work with them,** she shares what they've seen. That's only R6, R11, and R12, plus the strange attacker in the lower vault. She doesn't say what it is or how many there are. However the PCs help, she finds them incompetent. She is hard to please and harder to work with.

**The flood clock starts here.** The bottom floor is already at **Stage 0**. It rises +1 per hour the teaknis work without interruption after the PCs meet them. If the PCs defeat them here **or** in R12, but not both, it's every **2 hours** ([flood stages](#the-flooding-temple-chapter-subsystem)).

**If a fight breaks out:**
- The sneak keeps melee attackers off the hydrojinxer and fights to the death.
- The hydrojinxer uses water spells only.
- She **surrenders below 30 HP**, but she goes back to sabotage elsewhere if she can.

**Teakni Sneak:** p. 401, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.

**Teakni Hydrojinxer:** Creature 8, unique CN Small elemental water. Female teakni arcane saboteur (p. 210; teakni rules p. 401). Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- **Perception** +16; darkvision. **Languages** Aquan, telepathy 60 feet
- **Skills** Acrobatics +16, Arcana +16, Deception +15, Nature +16, Stealth +18, Thievery +16

  - **4th** (3 slots): *crashing wave* (APG), *dispel magic*, *hydraulic torrent*
  - **3rd** (3 slots): *aqueous orb* (APG), *magic missile*, *wall of water*
  - **2nd** (3 slots): *mirror image*, *see invisibility*, *telekinetic maneuver*

### R12 — Grand Entry *(Low 7)*

> *This wide room was obviously once a splendid entryway to the pyramid. A massive iron double door stands closed, although rivulets of mud and seawater leak in around its frame to cover the floor in dirty, foul-smelling water. A wide staircase leads up to the west. Alcoves to the north and south bear shattered statues, their broken pieces poking up through the water. The south alcove also has a massive hole in the ceiling.*

The ceiling hole is teakni work from R11. It extends past the south wall into R16. No route leads from here into that vault. But a PC could climb to R11 and drop into R16.

**Teakni Saboteurs (2):** p. 401, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp each.
- They take turns widening the hole and forcing the main doors, to speed up the flood.
- They're likelier to be hostile than the R11 teaknis, but they accept offered help.
- Once they're fighting, they keep going until they or their foes are destroyed.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A saboteur's waterspout ignores up to 10 Hardness when adjacent to an object (p. 401). That's how they wear through stone. It's also why these two are the real flooding threat.

### R13 — Vault Landing *(Trivial 7)*

> *Four double doors lead away from this flooded landing. Each is made of sturdy metal set into a stone frame and secured with a large lock plate.*

No vault door is locked. Before Poppy's attack, **[Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red)** ritually unlocked every pyramid door at once to surprise the cult. Arahosko was the galtzagorri wizard at Poppy's side in the War of the Shivering Behemoth. Nobody relocked the vaults.

Only the **Deep Vault (R17)** door is decorated. It bears an engraved gemstone shaped like a brilliant **5-pointed star**: the Jewel of the Indigo Isles. The carving is plain stone, yet it matches the PCs' fragments. The last fragment is likely inside.

**Vault Wards (4):** Hazard 8, magical trap, one on each vault door (p. 211)
- **Stealth** DC 28 (trained)
- **Description:** an invisible rune on each door jolts anyone who touches the door with petrifying magic.
- **Disable:** **DC 26 Nature** (expert) or ***dispel magic*** (4th level; **counteract DC 26**) counteracts the rune's magic. **DC 26 Thievery** (expert) safely erases it.
- **AC** 27; **Fort** +21, **Ref** +13; **Hardness** 16; **HP** 64 (BT 32); immune to critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Painful Petrification** (reaction; force, incapacitation). **Trigger:** a creature touches the door, by hand or with a tool. **Effect:** **6d6 force** damage and a **DC 26 Fortitude** save.
  - **Critical success:** no damage.
  - **Success:** half damage, and **slowed 1** for 1 round.
  - **Failure:** full damage, and **slowed 1** for 24 hours. It can try a new Fortitude save at the end of each hour to end the effect.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage, and **petrified** for 24 hours. It can try a new Fortitude save at the end of each hour to end the effect.
- **Reset:** 24 hours.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Level 8 with the [incapacitation trait](#the-incapacitation-trait), so level-7 PCs get no help on the save. Trained Perception is enough to find the runes ([hazards](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards)). Have the PCs Search.

### R14 — Energy Vault *(Moderate 7)*

> *Shimmering walls of force extend among six pillars, forming a glowing cage at the room's center. The walls are adorned with mosaics of great and terrible monsters being caged and controlled.*

Permanent walls of force held dangerous creatures or artifacts here. At the time of Poppy's attack, the cage held a living variant **shale swarm** made of orichalcum and crystal. It starved long ago.

The dead swarm drew four **temporal manifestations**, students of time and force. They've drained the cage until each of its **six walls** has **10 HP and Hardness 15**. They also drained the time-bending properties from the corpses. That left the orichalcum brittle and worthless.

**Temporal Manifestations (4):** BB 110, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp each.
- They start inside the cage, curious, and beckon the PCs closer.
- Within a few rounds, their time sense shows the PCs will soon **destroy the temple**. They attack to protect their research.
- **Time Step** carries them in and out of the cage for advantage.
- After three fall, the last one **flees** for good.

**Treasure:** **without Monster Parts,** crystals in the dead swarm are worth **1,000 gp**.

> **💡 GM tip:** The manifestations are right: taking the shard dooms the temple. If a PC asks why they attacked, the truth makes a good chill.

### R15 — Empty Vault

> *This stone chamber has an ornate pedestal at the southern end carved to look like a mountain. As the room is flooded with murky water, this pedestal resembles an island rising from the sea. The top of the pedestal has a bowl-shaped indentation.*

The vault was meant for a volcanic artifact the cult never got before Poppy's attack. Anything set in the bowl makes **illusory lava** bubble over the pedestal. It's dramatic and harmless. Nothing else is here.

### R16 — East Vault *(Moderate 7)*

> *This enormous room has several square pillars holding up the ceiling. The walls and all the pillars bear intricate mosaics of looming mountains, churning seas, and raging shale spitters in numerous sizes and configurations. The chamber's floor is obscured by brackish, muddy water leaking in from a hole in the ceiling in an alcove to the east.*

The vault was meant for tribute once the cult ruled the Isles. It was always mostly empty.
- **Water:** a foot deep, unless the PCs widened the opening or helped the saboteurs do it. **Difficult terrain** for Small and smaller.
- **The mosaics,** up close: terrified tiny crowds menaced by towering shale spitters or crushed by tidal waves. Gray-robed cultists ride the monsters or stand triumphant on mountaintops.

**Weak Hieroglyph Scorpion:** BB 9, 41, Creature 9. Initiative: Deception 35 when flattened, or Stealth +21. Monster Parts 90/200/360 gp.
- It was placed here before the vault held anything. It hides among the images as a scorpion-tailed shale spitter. Damage to the mosaics over time has weakened it, but it stays alert.
- It keeps ambushing and stinging the teaknis. That's why they can't get in.
- When the PCs arrive, it lurks in the **east alcove**, expecting another raid from above. It slinks along the walls and ceiling to the best spot to strike.
- It **doesn't surrender or flee**, and it **doesn't pursue** foes who leave.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The chapter never explains "Deception 35 when flattened." Check BB 41 for it before the session.

### R17 — Deep Vault *(Severe 7)* — the Tangerine Shard is here

> *Four square pillars hold up the ceiling in this imposing stone room. An alcove at the east end contains a pillar with a glowing yellow-orange gemstone across it. Small, yellow-orange flecks surround the pillar like a cloud of glitter. The flecks that touch the pedestal vanish into it, but the cloud gets no smaller.*

The whole Jewel once sat here, filling the temple with magic. Poppy took it and broke it. She left the **Tangerine Shard** behind. She feared that removing the whole Jewel would drop the temple on her at once.

The shard is far weaker than the Jewel, but it still powers the temple. Its flecks soak into the pedestal and keep the sea out.
- **Once the shard leaves the pedestal,** the protection ends **permanently**. That's +1 flood stage **every minute** ([flood stages](#the-flooding-temple-chapter-subsystem)). Run.

**Creature:** Poppy had no time to bury the last cultists defending the Jewel. Their skeletons lay for centuries around their **high priest**. She died beside the Jewel from Poppy's stab to the heart. Over time, anguish and thirst for revenge raised her as a barely sapient undead. She drew the others' bones into a **skeletal exosuit shaped like a dragon**. Her humanoid body shows inside it, with Poppy's rapier wound still over her heart.
- It defends the gem with single-minded fury, **until destroyed**.
- It saves special vengeance for a PC who **looks or acts like Poppy**. If no PC does, it picks a **Poppy fanatic** PC descended from one of Poppy's disciples. *"Poppy... die!"* it rasps in many voices.
- **It smashes the temple as it moves.** It can go through pillars and walls, destroying them. Destroyed walls leave difficult terrain. If needed, it goes through floors and ceilings to chase the PCs anywhere, leaving a dragon-sized hole.
- Describe shaking, rumbling, and maybe a little leaking water. None of this has an effect yet. The temple collapses after the PCs take the prize.

**Skeletal Cult Dragon:** Creature 10, unique CE Huge amphibious skeleton undead. Variant skeletal monstrosity (BB 103). Stat block p. 214. Monster Parts 125/275/500 gp.
- **Perception** +16; darkvision
- **Skills** Athletics +23, Intimidation +18
- **Str** +7, **Dex** +3, **Con** +0, **Int** −3, **Wis** +0, **Cha** +0
- **AC** 29; **Fort** +18, **Ref** +17, **Will** +16; **HP** 160
- **Immunities** death effects, disease, paralyzed, poison, unconscious. **Resistances** cold 10, electricity 10, fire 10, piercing 10, slashing 10
- **Destructive Collapse** (divine, evocation): when it's destroyed, its bones collapse in a huge heap. Creatures adjacent take **10d6 slashing**, **DC 28 basic Reflex**.
- **Speed** 30 feet, swim 30 feet
- **Melee** jaws +23 (reach 20 feet), 2d8+13 piercing plus Grab
- **Melee** wing scythe +23 (agile, reach 15 feet, sweep), 2d6+13 slashing
- **Melee** foot +23 (reach 10 feet), 2d12+13 bludgeoning
- **Ranged** bone splinter +19 (range increment 60 feet), 2d6+13 piercing
- **Eat Marrow** (1 action), once per round. **Requirement:** it has a creature grabbed in its jaws. **Effect:** it chews the creature and drinks its marrow: **3d12+11 bludgeoning**, **DC 29 basic Fortitude**. It **regains HP equal to the damage dealt**.
- **Cultists' Cry** (2 actions; auditory, divine, enchantment, fear, mental), once per 10 minutes. It screams curses at Poppy von Barnacle. It proclaims that all the Indigo Isles will soon belong to the Cult of the Shivering Behemoth. Creatures within **500 feet** who hear it must succeed at a **DC 28 Will** save or be **frightened 2**, whether they understand it or not. On a critical failure, they're **frightened 4** and **fleeing** for 1 round or until no longer frightened, whichever comes first. Whatever the result, creatures are then immune for 24 hours.
- **Splinter Burst** (2 actions; divine, evocation): **7d10 slashing** in a **15-foot cone**, **DC 28 basic Reflex**. Can't use it again for 1d4 rounds.
- **Sweeping Slash** (2 actions): it Strides and makes two wing scythe Strikes, each at any point during the Stride. Its multiple attack penalty doesn't increase until after both.
- **Trample** (3 actions): Large or smaller, foot, **DC 29**.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** See [Grab, Trample, and sweep](#monster-abilities-grab-trample-sweep) and the [grabbed and fleeing conditions](gm_chapter_07.md#conditions-new-this-chapter). Jaws, Grab, and Eat Marrow fill its three-action turn. That's about 22 jaw damage, plus about 30 from Eat Marrow on a failed save. It regains the Eat Marrow damage as HP.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the grab loop):** Each Eat Marrow heals what it deals. If the party can't break the grab (Escape or forced movement), the fight drags. Point the grabbed player to Escape early.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (book typos in Cultists' Cry):** The immunity line names "the skeletal monstrosity's Screech." That's left over from the base creature. Read it as **Cultists' Cry**.

> **💡 GM tip:** Its resistances cover piercing, slashing, cold, electricity, and fire (10 each). Bludgeoning and other types get through. Hint at this after a few resisted hits.

> **⚠️ Gotcha: who is the high priest?** This chapter's high priest is female and unnamed. Poppy's rapier killed her in R17. The cult article names the high priest Poppy's team slew as **[Jakorvo](gm_glossary.md#jakorvo)**, a male g'mayun (p. 372). R10 also has Poppy beating the leaders in the warding chamber. The book doesn't reconcile these. Pick one: she is Jakorvo's successor, a second high priest, or Jakorvo himself. Then stay consistent.

**XP:** **80 XP** for recovering the Tangerine Shard.

**Treasure:** once the dragon is destroyed, the PCs can take the shard. The temple floods fast, so they should flee at once.

#### Tangerine Shard *(Item 7)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** held in 1 hand. **Bulk** negligible.
- It's a glittering orange-yellow crystal broken from a larger gem. It's darker red along one break and darker yellow along the other.
- It sheds tiny chips of light that give off no heat. This makes **bright light in a 20-foot radius** (dim for the next 20 feet), like a torch.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact): rub it to put out or relight the glow. This lasts until you use it again.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact), **once per day:** scrape a fingernail across it. A dense haze of glittering yellow-orange particles surrounds you, within a few inches. You're **concealed**. You can't use this to Hide or Sneak, because the glitter gives away your position. The haze moves with you. While it lasts, you and your gear stay **dry, even underwater**. It doesn't let you breathe water. It fades after **3 rounds**.

> **💡 GM tip:** The daily haze is the escape trick. Remind its holder that it keeps gear dry, not lungs.

#### If the PCs lose

The likeliest disaster: the PCs pull the shard, flooding starts, and they lose it. It **glows**, so it's easy to spot in rising water. Badly beaten PCs may need to rest and come back. If so, the book suggests the dragon **puts the shard back**, which slows the flood to a trickle (p. 215).

#### If the fight spreads

Retreating PCs may lead the dragon through the bottom floor. It crushes walls and may pull in rooms the PCs haven't cleared. The book suggests (p. 215):
- **Teakni saboteurs (R12):** they watch and cheer from safety, but help if the PCs are losing. The PCs wrecked more in minutes than the teaknis did in weeks.
- **Vault wards (R13):** they hit the dragon as readily as the PCs. Crafty PCs can use this.
- **Temporal manifestations (R14):** they flee at once if the dragon crashes in.
- **Hieroglyph scorpion (R16):** it stays put and leaves intruders to the dragon. If the dragon destroys the wall the scorpion is in, the scorpion is destroyed too.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The dragon alone is Severe. The book warns that extra foes likely make the fight far too hard. Keep the dragon central.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The PCs think they're finishing Poppy's treasure hunt. In truth, they're bringing the cult's relic back within the cult's reach. The dragon cries that the Isles "shall soon belong to the Cult of the Shivering Behemoth." That's closer to true than anyone aboard knows.

## Concluding the chapter

### Their Triumphant Return

With the shard in hand, the PCs return to the ship (p. 215).
- The crew cheers. The trip back to Rumplank will be a celebration.
- **Prince Kulupi** is pleased and promises the king's favor.
- The long trip to Goldcrop Island allows rest, downtime, and **advancing to 8th level**.
- **Sea resources:** they can make one last attempt to gather marine resources on the way home (p. 117). Ch 9 confirms this is the last chance (p. 216).

**Level up:** the PCs reach **level 8** at the book's milestone: **collecting the final fragment** (end of Act 2). Short on XP? The book suggests a quest-completion award or encounters on the way back.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi has his own reason to be pleased: the Jewel is nearly whole. In Ch 9, Varagix uses Kulupi as his conduit to curse the Jewel as the four fragments combine (p. 217). Play his delight as a proud commander's, nothing more.

> **🧩 Sets up:** About a day out from Rumplank, the four fragments start pulling toward each other. An hour before docking, they fly together into the whole Jewel. This opens **Ch 9** and Part Three, *Battle at Barnacle Bay*.
