## The chapter in one minute

The *[Chromatic Queen](gm_glossary.md#chromatic-queen)* sails from [Seaview](gm_glossary.md#seaview) to [Moonshadow Island](gm_glossary.md#moonshadow-island), a dark, forested criminals' refuge. The spooked crew tells ghost stories on the way and won't go ashore. The PCs row in alone, fight through blighted forest, and crack Poppy's tile-puzzle vault in Gullstone Cave. The fragment is gone: Umbrocious, a shadowy fey serving the Flesh Stitcher, took it and dropped his notes. The PCs follow the cult path inland past an abandoned criminal village to the cottage of Rotsnout, a grumpy orpok witch. Invader assassins attack there. The PCs rest, level up, and prepare for the fortress.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Learned the fragment is gone** and who took it (N8 notes).
2. **Reached Rotsnout's cottage**, 5 miles inland and 4 miles short of the fortress.
3. **Found up to two defense special resources:** **fortress metal brackets** (N4) and **pyre combustibles** (O5).

**Party level:** starts at 5, ends at **6**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** The treasure isn't here. The cave pays off (puzzle, Poppy's trap), but the theft turns a treasure hunt into a rescue with a named villain. The walk inland builds Chapter 7 dread: an empty village, a murdered pirate queen, a hermit who watched the villagers marched to the fortress.

### Drained

Twigjack and eeko husks drain with Draining Impact.

- **Drained X:** status penalty X on Constitution-based checks (such as Fortitude).
- **Lose level × X HP**; **maximum HP drops by the same**.
- Drops by 1 per full night's rest.

**Example:** a level-5 PC critically fails a twigjack's DC 20 Fortitude save: drained 2, −10 HP now, −10 maximum HP until rest, −2 Fortitude.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Drained stacks across fights. M3, M4, and N1 in one push can leave the party's maximum HP well below normal. Draining Impact immunity (24-hour) is per husk.

### Persistent damage and bleed

The N8 gauntlet of cutlasses deals **1d6 persistent bleed damage** on a failed save.

- **Persistent damage** hits at the end of each of the victim's turns, then a **DC 15 flat check** ends it on a success.
- **Administer First Aid** (2 actions, Medicine) on a bleeding ally: success grants the ally an extra flat check.
- Same persistent damage twice doesn't stack; keep the higher.

### Restrained and Escape

The O1 spiked cages trap **restrains** (Escape DC 25).

- **Restrained:** flat-footed, immobilized, no attack or manipulate actions except **Escape** or Force Open.
- **Escape:** one action, attack trait. Unarmed attack, Acrobatics, or Athletics vs. the DC. Counts for multiple attack penalty.

At O1, a successful Escape (DC 25) also destroys the cage.

### Auras and Frightful Presence

An **aura** radiates to a listed distance. Twigjack husks (30 feet, DC 17) and the eeko husk (30 feet, DC 22) have **Frightful Presence**.

- First entering the aura: **Will save**.
- **Critical success:** unaffected. **Success:** frightened 1. **Failure:** frightened 2. **Critical failure:** frightened 4.
- Then immune for 1 minute.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Three twigjacks, three auras, three saves. Frightened doesn't add; keep the worst.

### Curses

A **curse** is a lasting magical affliction. Shackled Marni's sword lays a *geas* ritual on anyone who tries to wield it, compelling them to dominate others and terrorize the seas as a pirate captain. See [O4](#o4--leaders-hut-severe-5).

### Negative healing

The eeko husk has **negative healing**: negative energy heals it, positive hurts it (*heal* damages it, *harm* heals it). Remaster names: **void** (negative), **vitality** (positive).

> **💡 GM tip:** **20 or more fire or positive damage** from one attack or effect in a round ends its swarm. A cleric's *heal* on the husk is a real option.

### Temporary Hit Points

Rotsnout's stew grants **temporary Hit Points**: damage hits them first, they can't be healed, they expire with the duration, and they don't stack (keep the higher).

### Improvised weapons

Attacking with a non-weapon takes a **−2 item penalty** to the attack roll. At M4, returning the honeycomb heart is a melee attack with the heart as an improvised weapon.

### Rules from earlier chapters

- **Stupefied** ([Chapter 5](gm_chapter_5.md#conditions-new-this-chapter)): Rotsnout's Boorish Quip, stupefied 1 for 1 round on a failure (2 on a critical failure).
- **Weakness** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#weakness-resistance-and-immunity)): twigjacks fire 5; eeko husk axe 5, cold iron 5, fire 5. Multiple matches apply only the highest.
- **Concealment** ([Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)): the eeko's bee swarm; Event 15 poison clouds conceal creatures on opposite sides.
- **Counteracting** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting)):
  - **O1:** cage magic is 4th level, counteract DC 25. 3rd-level *dispel magic* works on a success (4 is within one level of 3); 2nd-level needs a critical success.
  - **O4:** *dispel magic* can force Marni's sword to weapon form (counteract DC 27). No effect level given; a level-8 creature counteracts at level 4 by the general rule.
- **Tiny creatures and reach 0** ([Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#tiny-creatures-reach-and-mounts)): twigjacks are Tiny with reach 0 feet claws, so they must enter a target's space to claw it (p. 152).
- **Seek and Search** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#seek-search-and-secret-doors)): some tiles and the O5 resource need a Searching or Investigating PC: **DC 20 Perception** in N3 and O5, **DC 22 Perception** in N4. Ask each PC's exploration activity before they enter a room.

## Part 1 — The voyage to Moonshadow

### Recall Knowledge about Moonshadow Island

Each PC can try any or all three; **Indigo Isles Geography Lore** substitutes at a lower DC (p. 148–149). [Secret checks](gm_chapter_1.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge).

**Nature (DC 12, or Indigo Isles Geography Lore DC 10):** the plants and creatures.
- **Critical success:** The success result, plus plant creatures of several types live on the island, safe from civilization among the shadows beneath the trees.
- **Success:** Moonshadow is known for a dense forest of trees so tall they cast the ground in perpetual shadow. Creatures that lurk in darkness are at home there, which is one reason others consider it so dangerous.
- **Failure:** Moonshadow Island has many tall trees.
- **Critical failure:** The island is an untapped source of timber, ripe for exploitation.

**Religion (DC 20, or Lore DC 18):** the restless dead.
- **Critical success:** The success result, plus the island's undead arise from tragedy. So many people die there in messy, violent ways that angry spirits aren't uncommon. You have to consider how such a spirit arose and then work to put it to rest permanently.
- **Success:** Spectral undead haunt the island, mainly angry spirits like wraiths and specters.
- **Failure:** Nothing about undead on the island.
- **Critical failure:** Anyone who eats the wrong piece of food grown on the island is cursed to die a painful death at the next moonrise and become a slavering undead creature.

**Society (DC 15, or Lore DC 13):** the people.
- **Critical success:** The success result, plus small groups of cutthroat criminals or escaped convicts sometimes hide there and keep their presence secret. There's even a small village of them in the interior.
- **Success:** Almost nobody lives on the island, except for shipwreck survivors or marooned sailors. The trees are too dense and tough to log, and rocky cliffs ring the island, so ships avoid it.
- **Failure:** Nobody lives on Moonshadow Island.
- **Critical failure:** A hidden pirate city with a fleet poised to prey on shipping across the Indigo Isles is on the island. Someone must find their base and stop them.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The Religion critical success is O4's lesson: Marni's spirit lingers from how she died and what she held onto; moving her sword out of the village lets her rest.

### Sea resources this leg

Any PC can **Seek Marine Resources** once this leg (p. 117; full rules in [Chapter 5](gm_chapter_5.md#seek-marine-resources)), a downtime check over the whole voyage:
- **DC 20 Athletics, Crafting, Nature, or Survival**, or **DC 18 Sailing Lore**.
- **Critical success** 2 Kelp Points, **success** 1, **failure** 0, **critical failure** −1. Pool the party's points.
- **2–3 points:** twinekelp. **4–6:** netweed and twinekelp. **7+:** red sargassum, netweed, and twinekelp.
- Reset each leg. Raise thresholds for more than four PCs.

Record results: Chapter 12 defense special resources.

### Tale Telling

Nearing the island on a clear evening, the somber crew swaps spooky stories (p. 149–150). Each teller has a check a PC can make to impress them.

**Fippin and Mippin:** a ship ran aground on a moonless night (they weren't aboard; a crewmate knew someone who knew someone who was). A dozen ragged people with bloody weapons met the survivors on the beach, led by an immense hardriggan woman with a flaming cutlass and enormous silver bracelets. She laughed, demanded treasure, and finding none said *"Nothing but your lives, so we'll take those."* The sailors swam into the dark sea. Some were rescued; none returned.
- **DC 20 Society**, or **DC 17 Pirate Lore or Sailing Lore:** the "bracelets" were shackles. She's the infamous, long-vanished pirate captain **Shackled Marni**. The cooks are impressed.

**"Slate" Hodda** shows a stone tile about 4 inches square and 1 inch thick: *"This is a key from Moonshadow Island, and it's all I have of my cousin."* Her marooned cousin hauled smugglers' crates into a cave lined with hundreds of small tiles and a big iron door. A smuggler joked the wall tiles fit the lock, but nobody knew which. The smugglers tried to murder the cousin; he killed them, took their gear and the tile, rowed home, and died of his wounds.
- **DC 20 Diplomacy:** cheers her up. She gives the PCs the tile (a carved **cow**).

**Garnikor** (petting Leafling): once sentenced to a prison ship, she landed on Moonshadow for supplies. In the shadows a branch moved with no wind and nudged a badly hurt blood mouse to her, proboscis torn out. She took it in; the tree was gone. Something there tortured Leafling nearly to death.
- **DC 20 Nature:** arboreals are generally kindly plant creatures; that one likely knew Garnikor could help the mouse more than it could. She respects a PC who knows this.

**Leafling:** a PC who talks to animals (such as *speak with animals*) learns it was captured and tortured by cruel people in an island village, and escaped into a tall tree that brought it to Garnikor. Impressions only, at animal intelligence.

**XP:** **30 XP** if a successful check earns at least one teller's respect. A PC's silly, tension-breaking story earns the crew's visible gratitude.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Hodda's tile is a wrong one:** The cow fits the lock but won't open it. The book says "the door in area M2"; the door is in **N2** (M2 is the fork).

> **🧩 Sets up:** Every story pays off: Marni's sword at O4, Hodda's cave is Gullstone Cave, Leafling's "cruel people in a village" are the O1–O5 criminals, now serving the invaders at the fortress (Ch 7).

### Moonshadow Island

Jagged cliffs ring the island (p. 150). The only obvious landing is a small rocky beach in a sheltered south bay. Smugglers' tunnels and secret paths are hard to reach; Kulupi won't risk the ship hunting for them, and the maps put the fragment in a cave near the beach.

- **Trees:** hundreds of feet tall, packed close. Dim by day, pitch dark at night. Few clearings.
- **The wood:** fibrous, nearly iron-tough, won't burn. *Fireball* can't ignite it; a tree lit on purpose doesn't spread fire. Harvesting takes great effort.
- **Food:** wild root vegetables, herbs, berries, mostly withered.
- **Sounds:** noisy and sinister:
  - Animal cries sometimes almost carry words; listeners disagree on the message but agree it's threatening.
  - Insect buzzing sometimes drops to a subsonic, teeth-grinding register.
  - Treetop wind moans like someone far off in pain.
- **Who lives here:** fleeing criminals, escaped convicts, misanthropic hermits, sailors marooned by crews who hated them.

## Part 2 — Moonshadow Shore (M1 to M4)

Map on p. 151. Read aloud as the ship arrives:

> *The shore in this sheltered bay is narrow, giving way almost immediately to dense stands of stout trees that grow hundreds of feet high and cloak the forest in perpetual shadow. The island has several tall hills, and any smaller hills are likely concealed by the dense forest. The island looks dim, inhospitable, and utterly abandoned—except for a dilapidated shack on the beach, just above the tide line.*

**The crew won't come,** not even to row. The PCs take the ship's boat alone.

**Kulupi's bag:** before they go, he presents a tattered sack as a great treasure, which it is: a ***bag of holding* (type II)**. The top objective is Poppy's fragment. He also asks them to bring back **resources to help Rumplank** in the bag, hinting high-quality timber might help, but trusts their judgment.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi is a cult leader (revealed in Ch 11); these resources defend Rumplank against his own cult in Ch 12. Play him straight: dutiful, a little pompous, staying aboard.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the bag fills up:** The book says the N4 fortress brackets (**50 Bulk**) fill the bag completely. The O5 pyre combustibles come later. Fill it with timber or loot first and the brackets won't fit.

> **💡 GM tip — the timber hint:** Moonshadow timber isn't a defense special resource (p. 315) and is hard to cut. If the PCs fell trees, remind them how tough the wood is and let them weigh the time.

### M1 — Old Shack *(Moderate 5)*

Doorless, roof collapsed. A **shinglelurk** perches on top, posing as the roof.
- **Inside:** under sand and debris, a small anvil, a hammer, and **six pairs of broken manacles**: tools someone used to free manacled people, who later moved to the village (O1–O5).
- **The lure:** legs wave down into the shack with a low clicking. **DC 18 Perception** notices; only a **critical success** reveals insectile movement on the ceiling.
- **Attacks** when three creatures approach or it's discovered. If the PCs don't approach, hunger wins and it flies at them.

**Shinglelurk** — BB 97, Creature 7. Initiative: Stealth +18. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp.
- **Morale:** retreats below **20 HP**, dropping scales worth its Monster Parts value.
- **Treasure:** a **shark tooth charm** in a hut corner.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The first threat is a flying level-7 creature. Only Moderate, but it hits like level 7. A fresh party can take it.

### M2 — Fork

> *Not far from the beach, the trees loom over the narrow path and cloak the ground in deep shadow. Amid the undergrowth, the narrow path splits. One path leads deeper into the island, while the right-hand path leads in the direction indicated on Poppy's map.*

- **Right:** to the cave (M3, M4, N1).
- **Inland:** no tracks in several weeks. The abandoned village (**O1**) is about 2 miles on. See [Part 4](#part-4--heading-inland).

### M3 — Blighted Grove *(Low 5)*

> *The vegetation in this open clearing is plainly unhealthy. Black spots of tarry goop cling to the underbrush, and portions of the trees are badly scarred, as though from acid. The woodland infection is worst around a narrow path leading south.*

**The resin:** black, tarry, unburnable, faintly like scorched rubber.
- **DC 25 Arcana, Nature, or Occultism:** extraplanar. **Critical success:** from dangerous regions of the Shadow Plane.
- Drips show one detonation here, another south.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Sklaggans "blight bombed" here on the Flesh Stitcher's orders, targeting gathering spots of the island's natural defenders.

**Twigjack Husks (3)** — p. 152 (BB 44, *Pathfinder Bestiary 2* 270), Creature 3. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. Tiny scowling dolls of branches and brambles, thorny-skinned, husked and dripping resin. They attack at once, favor claws and Splinter Spray up close, fight to the death, and chase fleeing foes.
- **Perception** +9; darkvision. **AC** 18; **Fort** +9, **Ref** +11, **Will** +7; **HP** 45. **Immunities** emotion. **Weaknesses** fire 5 ([weakness](gm_chapter_3.md#weakness-resistance-and-immunity)).
- **Frightful Presence** (aura) 30 feet, DC 17 ([auras](#auras-and-frightful-presence)). **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** blighted claw +11 (agile, finesse, reach 0 feet), 1d10+4 slashing plus draining impact ([reach 0](gm_chapter_2.md#tiny-creatures-reach-and-mounts)).
- **Ranged** blighted splinter +11 (deadly 1d6, range increment 30 feet), 1d6+4 piercing plus draining impact.
- **Draining Impact:** **DC 20 Fortitude**. Failure drained 1, critical failure drained 2 ([drained](#drained)). Immune to that husk's draining impact for 24 hours either way.
- **Splinter Spray** (2 actions): 15-foot cone, **4d6 piercing**, **DC 20 basic Reflex**. Recharges in 1d4 rounds.
- **Bramble Jump:** from undergrowth, teleports to undergrowth within 60 feet without triggering reactions.

### M4 — Warped Beehives *(Low 5)*

> *This clearing is also covered in tarry droplets and is even more sickly than the one to the northwest. Three stumps each bear a beehive the size of a rain barrel. The beehives have all been splattered with the same black resin and large chunks of them have decayed away. A glimmer of bright gold emanates from within each destroyed hive. A heap of earth and stone bearing a low cave entrance is just visible in another clearing to the east.*

East is **N1**, Gullstone Cave, where Poppy hid the fragment called the **Bruised Crystal**.

**What happened:** an eeko like Briargrove's tended these hives. When the blight came, it tore out its honeycomb heart and broke it up to save them, which left it open to corruption; it became a husk of anger and emptiness. A healthy honeycomb piece still in each hive makes the gold glow.

**Eeko Husk** — p. 154 (BB 44, 27), Creature 6. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp. Attacks when the PCs near any hive. Its open chest is empty but for a few rotting undead bees. Never retreats or surrenders; won't follow foes out of the clearing.
- **Perception** +17; low-light vision. Communicates only by sign language.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +14, **Will** +15; **HP** 110, [negative healing](#negative-healing).
- **Immunities** emotion, swarm mind. **Weaknesses** axe vulnerability 5, cold iron 5, fire 5. **Resistances** bludgeoning 5, piercing 5.
- **Frightful Presence** 30 feet, DC 22. **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** blighted branch +16 (agile, finesse), 2d6+6 slashing plus draining impact (**DC 24 Fortitude**, same results as the twigjacks').
- **Primal Innate Spells** DC 25: 3rd *earthbind*, *fear*, *vomit swarm*; 2nd *darkness*, *entangle*, *tree shape*; cantrips *dancing lights*, *detect magic*, *stabilize*, *tanglefoot*; constant *speak with plants*. It casts without sound.
- **Unleash Hive** (2 actions, once per minute): a Huge undead bee swarm centered on and moving with it. Lasts 1 minute, or until the eeko takes **20+ fire or positive damage from one attack or effect in a round**. While active: all-around vision, [concealment](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected), fast healing 3, and Charnel Stings.
- **Charnel Stings** (1 action, swarm active): each enemy in the swarm takes **2d8 piercing**, **DC 25 basic Reflex**, plus draining impact. Failure also sickened 1 (sickened 2 on a critical failure).

**Saving the eeko:**
1. **Pull each hive's honeycomb:** Interact, **DC 18 Nature, Survival, or Thievery**. Undead bees sting anyone who tries: **2d4 piercing plus 2d4 negative**, **DC 20 basic Reflex**. They blend into the hive, so the first reach-in likely reveals them.
2. **Reassemble the heart:** once all three pieces are out, Interact, **DC 20 Medicine or Nature**: a fist-sized, heart-shaped honeycomb.
3. **Put it back:** a melee hit with the heart as an [improvised weapon](#improvised-weapons) **paralyzes** the husk. It roots in place, unable to act, while the heart cleans out the blight over several hours.

**Restored:** effusively grateful, it helps however it can without leaving its hives and gives **magical honey worth its full Monster Parts value**.

**XP:** **80 XP** for restoring it, instead of 60 XP for defeating it.

> **💡 GM tip:** Players who recall Ch 5's eeko know eekos have honeycomb hearts. Point out the empty chest and heart-shaped glow; a Nature check confirms if asked.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the book's slip:** The text places the friendly eeko "on Seaview Island." Seaview is Bluebell Island's town; the eeko was in Briargrove. It means the Ch 5 eeko.

## Part 3 — Gullstone Cave (N1 to N8)

Map on p. 156.

> **🔒 GM-only — the cave's history (p. 155):** Long before Poppy, a pirate captain hid treasure here behind a reinforced iron door, locked by three tiles he carved to match the thousands of others. Poppy's crew suggested the cave as a hiding place. She solved the puzzle, took his treasure (part of the haul that founded Rumplank), and sealed the fragment in the vault with more traps and guardians, and stored lesser plunder around the cave.
>
> The sklaggans bombed outside but ignored the cave. It nagged at the Flesh Stitcher's advisor, the skotogelia **Umbrocious**, whose research found a powerful, unique magic item inside. Trusting his incorporeality, he went in, got the gem, triggered Poppy's trap, and left notes behind. **The fragment isn't here anymore.**

### Cave features

- **Slope:** high north, low south. Seawater seeps into the southern rooms: clammy, dripping.
- **Light:** only the PCs'. **Ceilings:** 10 feet unless noted.
- **The tiles:** thousands on walls and ceilings, thickest in N2 and N4, each about **4 inches square and 1 inch thick** and carved with one natural thing (bird, shrub, river, fish). All unique, supernaturally durable. A tool pries one out in several minutes; dozens of empty sockets show visitors did. The **gull** tile by the entrance names the cave.
- **The three key tiles** are **man-made** things: the pirate's **tricorn hat**, **cutlass**, and **pirate ship**. Never wall-mounted. Treasure hunters scattered many loose decoys.

### N1 — Cave Entrance *(Moderate 5)*

### N2 — Puzzle Chamber

> *This low cave is wet with moisture. Two natural passages lead up, and one leads down. The floor of each has been chipped into rough stairs. At the far end of the cave, furthest from the entrance, stands a massive iron door with an enormous lock. Some shattered chips of stone lie on the floor in front of the door.*

**The lock:** no handle, only **three indentations stacked vertically**.
- **Pick the Lock:** six successful **DC 35 Thievery** checks.
- **Force Open:** **DC 45 Athletics**.
- **The door:** Hardness 50, 250 HP.

**Floor clue:** the chips are a broken **mouse** tile, **"WRONG ONE"** scratched on the back by an explorer who learned some tiles fit but fail.

**Opening the door:** pirate tiles **top to bottom: tricorn hat, cutlass, pirate ship**. Wrong tile or order: a low click, still shut. Right: several clicks, and the door swings wide.

**XP:** **30 XP** for solving the puzzle and opening the door.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** DC 35 and DC 45 are out of reach at level 5. They show force won't work; the puzzle is the way in.

> **💡 GM tip:** Show every loose tile and keep a list. The theme (three man-made pirate objects among hundreds of nature carvings) is the answer. If they're stuck on the order: a pirate stands hat on head, cutlass at the hip, ship under the feet.

### N3 — Flooded Cave *(Low 5)*

> *The passage descending into this room is low and slippery, although the steps carved into the floor provide a modicum of traction. The passage opens into a cave with a narrow beach to the north. Clumps of seaweed drift gently in the water.*

The sea squirts came in through narrow south-wall fissures to the sea.

**Sea Squirt Patches (3)** — BB 87, Hazard 4. **Stealth DC 24.** Locations on the map.

**Giant Sea Squirt** — BB 87, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- Rushes out from underwater when anyone **triggers or disables** a patch.
- Can't trigger patches; mindless, but pushes or lures others into them.
- Never surrenders or leaves the water; its reach covers almost the whole room.

**Tiles:** tree, bat, and **tricorn hat** in the **patch farthest from the entrance**. Triggering or disabling it reveals them; otherwise Searching takes **DC 20 Perception**.

### N4 — Bound Boxes *(Moderate 5)* — defense resource

> *Several cargo boxes are stacked in this room, each sagging and coated with moss. Coils of thick rope are looped around the boxes, as though keeping them together and in place along one of the room's walls. A corpse is sprawled on the floor in front of the boxes.*

Seven boxes of Poppy's spare plunder; perishables (including straw-packed fine wine) have spoiled.

**The corpse:** a heavily tattooed orpok convict treasure hunter, plainly strangled with rope as thick as the boxes' ropes.

**Knotsman** — BB 54, Creature 7. Initiative: Perception +15. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp. The rope around the boxes, uncoiled.
- Attacks anyone who **tries to move the ropes or open the boxes**, pulling into humanoid form.
- Fights until destroyed but **won't leave the room**. Left alone, it coils back around the boxes.

**Resource — fortress metal brackets:** in the heaviest box, excellent, still-sound brackets for anchoring lumber, meant for a dock or fortress Poppy never built.
- **Weight:** 50 Bulk; fills Kulupi's *bag of holding*.
- **Use:** Chapter 12 defense special resource (p. 315).

**Tiles:** bee, **cutlass**, berry bush under one crate. Moving crates reveals them; a PC Investigating the boxes can roll **DC 22 Perception** to notice one sits unevenly.

> **💡 GM tip:** The corpse is the warning. Reward players who notice the strangulation; they can fight on their terms or back out, since the knotsman won't follow.

### N5 — Twisting Tunnels

Tunnels **no more than a foot wide** link caves. Gullet and fleeing creatures use them; even Small PCs can't.
- **Tiles:** a **mountain** and a **parrot**, within arm's reach inside a north-side tunnel mouth.
- **Treasure:** beneath them, an **iron cube talisman** resembling slag.

### N6 — Upper Pit *(Severe 5)*

> *This cave slopes steeply down to the south, where it narrows like a funnel to a small chamber below. A few ridges in the stone provide the only stable footing. A few corpses are scattered around the chamber, all wearing ragged clothing.*

- **Terrain:** **difficult terrain**. A creature falling **prone** slides **5 feet** toward N7.
- **The bodies:** long-dead pirates and convicts covered in tiny rat and snake bites.

**Mogadbs (4)** — BB 63, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. Ship's burlap sacks stuffed with sea snakes, rats, and other vermin. Two lurk in the top northwest and northeast corners, two near the bottom.
- **Little Sacks:** **Medium**, not Large; the Slough Skin rat swarm is still Large.
- Ignore the difficult terrain and fight until destroyed or ruptured. A rat swarm from a ruptured mogadb **flees**.

**Gullet** — Creature 5, unique NE Medium construct, variant nobbler (BB 67), p. 159. Initiative: Deception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp. A nobbler made from a smuggler's magic bag, crawling on all fours with the mogadbs, which accept it.
- **Bag of Smarts:** dim (Int −2) but, unlike most nobblers, not mindless; **not immune to mental effects**.
- **Tells:** it picks its way carefully over the slope the mogadbs ignore. Sharp-eyed players can spot it.
- **Tactics:** backs toward the pit when the fight starts, then, once out of sight in N7, plays dead among the empty sacks there.
- **Tiles:** it ate the **pirate ship** and a pig. Like all nobblers, it expels everything when destroyed.

### N7 — Lower Pit

> *This is a low, muddy pit. Several scraps of old sacks and other garbage litter the floor around a small, murky pool.*

An old smugglers' and pirates' dump that sometimes surges with primal power; sacks closing over vermin became mogadbs, and more could form. The pool is tainted seawater.

**Gullet's ruse:**
- Posing as a discarded bag, it leaps on PCs poking around and tries to **Gobble** one, then runs.
- Having swallowed a PC, or **below 40 HP**, it flees north through tiny passages to N5. Swallowed creatures sit in an extradimensional space, so it still fits.
- It may lead a chase and retry the bag trick, too dim to know it only works once.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — a swallowed PC goes with it:** The party can't enter foot-wide tunnels. Track where Gullet surfaces and keep the chase moving. The book says the PCs should be able to corner it eventually; they need the ship tile.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — ability names:** N6 calls Gullet's blend-in ability "Go Limp"; N7 calls it "Lie Still." Check BB 67.

### N8 — Gemstone Chamber *(Moderate 5)*

Reachable only through the N2 door.

> *Old ropes and rusty cutlasses dangle from the ceiling all throughout this wedge-shaped room. A low pedestal stands at the south end of the room, amid a few coils of frayed rope on the dusty floor.*

Trapped like Briargrove's gemstone chamber, with cutlasses in plain sight and too many to take. **Six pressure plates** trigger it, as does trying to remove a cutlass or cut a rope.

**Gauntlet of Cutlasses** *(complex mechanical trap, Hazard 7)* — p. 160–161
- **Stealth** +15 (expert). Ropes and cutlasses are **DC 0** to notice. A complex hazard's Stealth is its initiative; its hidden parts (the pressure plates) are DC 10 higher to spot.
- **Disable:** **DC 25 Thievery (expert)** adjusts the swing so cutlasses cut each other's ropes. Each success disables one of three sections and cuts routine damage by 2d6; the third disables the trap.
- **AC** 25; **Fort** +18, **Ref** +12. **Section** Hardness 15, HP 60 (BT 30). Immune to critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Cutlass Swing** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature steps on a pressure plate, or the ropes are pulled or cut. **Effect:** falling weights **slam the door shut**, and the cutlasses swing wildly. The trap Strikes the triggering creature with a cutlass, then rolls initiative.
- **Melee** cutlass +20 (forceful), 3d6+6 slashing.
- **Routine** (1 action): cutlasses sweep every N8 square **except the 4 squares around the pedestal**. Each creature takes **6d6 slashing**, **DC 25 basic Reflex**; failure adds **1d6 persistent bleed** ([persistent damage](#persistent-damage-and-bleed)).
- **Reset:** after **3 minutes** the weights settle and it goes inactive; counterweights reset it an hour later.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The door slams and the trap runs 3 minutes (30 rounds). Survival is the safe pedestal squares or the Thievery disable. The book doesn't say if the door opens from inside; decide beforehand.

**The fallen note:** thinking the gem unguarded, Umbrocious turned corporeal, brushed a rope, got cut up, grabbed the gem, and fled incorporeal. His notes lie in the dust, sliced and spattered with shadowy fluid between ink and ichor.
- **DC 28 Nature or Occultism:** **skotogelia** blood (a shadowy fey).
- The notes hold a crude web or net sketch (the **Skinloom**) and this handout:

> *The Master says the Skinloom needs more power; a potent item south of the fortress near the island's southern shore might provide this boost. This item appears to be a gem of some kind. A self-styled hero once bore it, but it's now just lying around for the taking. If I retrieve it, the Master can put it into the heart of the Skinloom. More favors are certain to be mine!*

**Treasure:**
- **Without Monster Parts:** Umbrocious also dropped a **runestone with a shadow rune**.
- **An open crate** in a corner: **three gold chalices** (40 gp each), **ten gold plates** (10 gp each), and **silverware** worth 100 gp in all.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The crate follows the runestone with "Additionally," so it may also be non-Monster-Parts only. Pick a reading; stay consistent.

**XP:** **80 XP** for finding the clue about the stolen fragment.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The Skinloom is area P21 of the Flesh Stitcher's fortress (**Chapter 7**). "The Master" is the Flesh Stitcher; the gem is the Bruised Crystal.

### What happens if the characters lose?

Per the p. 161 sidebar, the PCs must leave knowing **the fragment isn't here and went inland**; Umbrocious's note shows it best. If they can't beat the cutlass trap but want to go inland, encourage it. That's back on track.

## Part 5 — The abandoned village (O1 to O5)

Map on p. 162.

> **🔒 GM-only — the village's history (p. 162–163):**
> - For centuries a village has stood at this spring (good water, good drainage): the cult's **base camp** while building the fortress, then an outpost where cult scouts escorted visitors and prisoners from shore to fortress.
> - Empty after the cult fell, it was taken by mutinous convicts wrecked here soon after, who first built the beach shack (M1).
> - They took in fugitives and murdered the rare stray innocent. Infighting held them to one or two dozen. The latest name, **"Lawbreaker's Rest,"** was never settled.
> - **The sklaggans**, inhuman Shadow Plane monsters, recently reached the fortress and spread out, blighting forest and raising strange resinous structures. Finding a village that shared their wickedness, they lured everyone to the fortress with promises of power and aberrant technology.
> - Leader **Shackled Marni**, a pirate, warned they couldn't be trusted. They killed her publicly and messily. The rest complied.
> - Empty for a few months.

### O1 — Village Gate *(Moderate 5)*

> *The path leading deeper into the island runs past a wooden palisade that's in poor repair. A large wooden gate stands in the wall, slightly ajar, and reveals a small village of ramshackle huts within. There's no sign of activity in the village. Next to this gate is a crude sign reading, "Lawbreaker's Rest," but the word "Rest" has been crossed out and replaced with "Roost," which has been crossed out and replaced with "Rest" again.*

**Other ways in:** **DC 15 Athletics** to Climb the palisade, or **DC 20 Athletics** to Force Open a hole.

**Spiked Cages Trap** *(complex magical mechanical trap, Hazard 7)* — p. 163. Sklaggan-enchanted lumber and metal piled by the gate, building materials the criminals never used.
- **Stealth:** the book prints "DC +17 (expert)." The pile itself is **DC 0** to notice.
- **Disable:**
  - **DC 25 Thievery (expert)** frees someone else, destroying the cage.
  - **DC 27 Thievery (trained)** or **Escape DC 25** frees yourself, destroying the cage ([restrained and Escape](#restrained-and-escape)).
  - **DC 27 Occultism**, or ***dispel magic*** (4th level; counteract DC 25), dispels the pile's shadow magic so no more cages form ([counteracting](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting)).
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +20, **Ref** +12. **Cage** Hardness 15, HP 60 (BT 30). Immune to critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Encage** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature comes within **10 feet** of the pile by the gate. **Effect:** the trap makes a cage Strike against it, then rolls initiative.
- **Ranged** cage +20 (range 50 feet). **Effect:** the target is **restrained** (Escape DC 25).
- **Routine** (2 actions):
  1. Shadowy cords pull a spiked wood-and-iron cage around an uncaged creature within 50 feet (a cage Strike).
  2. Every occupied cage constricts: **2d10+9 piercing**, **DC 27 basic Fortitude**. A critical success Escapes automatically and destroys the cage.
- **Reset:** deactivates at the end of any turn with nobody caged, after one last cage Strike (it always takes its turn). Undispelled, it resets in **10 minutes**.
- **Treasure:** a **traveler's any-tool** in the pile.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — two different triggers:** The description says passing through the gate; Encage says within 10 feet of the pile. Use 10 feet. PCs climbing or breaking in elsewhere may never trigger it.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the constrict damage:** 2d10+9 a round at DC 27 is heavy at level 5, and caging continues. Free allies fast; an uncaged PC with *dispel magic* can stop new cages.

### O2 — Empty Huts

Shoddy, neglected, abandoned in a hurry days or weeks ago. Nothing of interest or value.

### O3 — Bunkhouse *(Moderate 5)*

Snarling hardriggan faces, painted by a resident amateur, cover the outside.

> *This irregularly shaped room is haphazardly stacked with bunk beds and footlockers. A battered table stands at the north end of the room, covered in old food stains. The odor of bad booze fills the room.*

Home of the rowdiest, most bloodthirsty villagers; old bloodstains and loose teeth show regular fights.

**Two objects out of place:** a **skagappa** (BB 163) under the northernmost bed and another under the southernmost, both used up and resin-wrapped so sklaggans can recover and recharge them.

**Sklaggans (2)** — BB 104, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.
- **Arrival:** when the PCs find the first skagappa or 5 minutes after entry, whichever is first. Likely from the gate side, skirting the trapped pile; any direction works.
- **Tactics:** Toxic Shadows at once, then *blur*, then fists; they grab skagappas only if convenient. A wounded sklaggan casts *vampiric touch*. Below **20 HP**, a sklaggan flees for good.
- **Captured:** they know the basics of what happened and the way to the fortress, traded only for a credible offer of freedom. They won't talk until freed, then bolt without answering, unless a PC succeeds at **DC 20 Diplomacy** (Request), **DC 22 Deception** (Lie), or **DC 25 Intimidation** (Coerce).

**Treasure:** **DC 18 Crafting or Occultism** restores a skagappa; failure destroys it. The resin flakes off on success, so one restoration only.

### O4 — Leader's Hut *(Severe 5)*

> *This large hut is draped with pirate flags, open sea chests filled with a variety of fine clothes, and a frame supporting a hammock that would be more suitable on a ship than in a forest village. A rough map is tacked to one wall. Strange globs of black resin cover another wall, forming a humanoid silhouette like a blast shadow. Tiny chunks of bone, skin, hair, and viscera are embedded in the wall, as though someone exploded against it with incredible force. A gleaming cutlass is jammed point-first into the hut's wooden floor.*

The best hut, held by the strongest. Last was **Shackled Marni** (she/her), a hardriggan pirate captain marooned by her crew for excess bloodthirst, who ran the village by leadership and blade and held meetings here.

**The wall map:** a non-to-scale sketch of Moonshadow showing what's up the path.
- The beach, winding path, village (huts around a spring in a palisade), and fortress (wooden, big central gate).
- A resin glob near the fortress, where a sklaggan pointed the villagers.
- Between village and fortress: tufted reeds and a small house with a cauldron, Rotsnout's cottage.

**How Marni died:** she saw through the sklaggans' promises and spoke out. To silence her and show strength, they blasted her against (and into) her own wall. Her spirit stays bound to the blade she killed so many with.

**Creature:** when anyone pokes through her things, the sword rises, uses Summon Ghost Pirate, and attacks until its pirate form is destroyed.

**Shackled Marni's Sword** — Creature 8, unique NE Medium construct, soulbound. Variant living blade (BB 55), p. 164–165. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- **Perception** +18; darkvision. **Languages** telepathy 100 feet, *tongues*.
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +18, Athletics +17, Deception +17, Intimidation +17, Sailing Lore +14.
- **AC** 27; **Fort** +12, **Ref** +18, **Will** +16; **HP** 100 (as ghost pirate).
- **Immunities:** bleed, death effects, disease, doomed, drained, fatigued, healing, necromancy, nonlethal attacks, paralyzed, poison, sickened, unconscious.
- **Speed** 25 feet.
- **Melee** scimitar +19 (forceful, magical, sweep), 2d6+9 slashing plus 1d6 fire.
- **Occult Innate Spells** DC 27, attack +17: 3rd *heroism*, *water breathing*; 2nd *water walk*; 1st *true strike*; constant (5th) *tongues*.
- **Rituals** DC 27: *geas* (see the curse).
- **Weapon Form:** with the ghost pirate inactive, it's a ***+1 striking flaming might (6) scimitar (7)***. It can't attack or move but can curse a wielder, and on its turn can cast spells targeting only its cursed wielder.
- **Summon Ghost Pirate** (3 actions, once per hour). **Requirement:** no intelligent creature is wielding or carrying the sword. **Effect:** it manifests a phantom of Shackled Marni from nondescript Ethereal Plane soul-stuff, with only fragments of her personality, letting the sword move and act. **DC 27 Perception** identifies the ghost pirate for what it is. The sword can dismiss it as a free action.
- **Back to weapon form:** at 0 HP, or when counteracted by *dispel magic* (counteract DC 27), until the next Summon Ghost Pirate.
- **Curse of the Pirate's Blade** (curse, enchantment, mental, occult). Anyone trying to wield the sword rolls a **DC 27 Will save**. The sword casts *geas* on them, always successfully, compelling them to dominate all other creatures and terrorize the seas as captain of a pirate vessel.
  - **Critical success:** immune to the curse for 24 hours.
  - **Failure:** succumbs to the *geas*. The victim can't willingly drop or give away the sword. The *geas* lasts **1 week**, then the wielder gets a new Will save.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the save has gaps:** Only critical success and failure are listed. Suggested ruling for success: resists this time, targetable on the next wield. Critical failure is unaddressed.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — cutlass or scimitar:** The read-aloud and one backstory line say cutlass; another line, the stat block, and the treasure say scimitar. Use scimitar stats.

**Spotting the curse before someone grabs it:**
- **Expert in Arcana, or DC 20 Arcana:** its strange solidity when the spirit carries it signals a dire effect on a wielder.
- **Expert in Religion, or DC 20 Religion:** the ghost pirate comes from a curse in the scimitar binding it here.
- **Critical success on either, or master in either:** describe the Curse of the Pirate's Blade in more detail.
- **DC 22 Arcana, Occultism, or Religion:** safely taking the scimitar out of the village breaks its hold on her spirit and frees her.

**Laying her to rest:** sword outside the village and nobody cursed: **Shackled Marni's spirit departs forever**.

**Treasure:** curse lifted, a normal ***+1 striking flaming might (6) scimitar (7)***. Without Monster Parts, a normal ***+1 striking flaming scimitar***.

> **💡 GM tip — carrying it out:** Summon Ghost Pirate needs the sword unwielded and uncarried, and the curse hits only a creature trying to **wield** it. A PC who stows it undrawn (pack or Kulupi's bag) keeps the ghost away and dodges the curse. That's a reading of the stat block wording; the book has no such rule and means the sword to be dangerous, so offer the knowledge checks before anyone grabs it.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** Pays off Fippin and Mippin's story and the Religion Recall Knowledge, and shows sklaggan ruthlessness so the Ch 7 fortress feels earned.

### O5 — Storage Shed — defense resource

> *This large storage shed of rough logs and overlapping leaves contains a variety of casks, barrels, and boxes. Nearest the entrance is a large anvil set on a flat stone. A few tools, planks of lumber, and sawed-off pieces of wood are scattered around it.*

The messy catch-all workroom Marni never had cleaned. Stolen, mostly useless goods: old nails, scrap lumber, casks of oil-tainted water.

**Resource — pyre combustibles:** a Searching PC with **DC 20 Perception** finds a **crate of alchemical supplies**, worth little alone but a set of **pyre combustibles** for a Chapter 12 inferno tower (p. 315).

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Easy to miss. No Searching PC, no DC 20 Perception success, and the shed reads as junk.

## Part 6 — The Witch's Cottage

> **🔒 GM-only — Rotsnout (p. 166):** The orpok witch **Rotsnout** (he/him) was an outcast even among criminals: a curmudgeonly misanthrope with casual hygiene nobody wanted nearby. Run out of the village, he went up the path (the fortress was too creepy) and built a cottage with a swamp dock for moonlight fishing in a boat that badly needs repair. Living so far off, he missed the sklaggans' arrival until the villagers trooped past behind strange creatures. He hid; the sklaggans never found him.

**3 miles** past the village, **4 miles** short of the fortress. Map on p. 167.

### The Swamp Witch *(Trivial 5)*

Trees and shrubs screen it, but anyone on the path notices; from the trail it looks like a lumber heap or ruin. Up close:

> *This cottage at the edge of a misty swamp looks haphazard at best, built of wood scraps and held together with salvaged metal. A wooden dock, perhaps the most solid-looking construction of the entire affair, juts into the swamp. A busted rowboat is partially concealed beneath the dock.*

- **Terrain:** vines and mold everywhere; only the **swamp water** is difficult terrain.
- **Rotsnout's entrance:** unless the PCs are unusually quiet, he barges out to berate the "unruly and unwelcome trespassers": a middle-aged orpok in never-washed black leather.
- **Grug:** his familiar, an animate lump of detritus that rarely leaves the cottage.
- **Won't fight armed strangers.** Attacked, he hides inside casting defensive spells. Once the PCs stop fighting, or if they never start, he grudgingly talks. A coward, he **surrenders at 30 HP or fewer**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Rotsnout at 0 HP:** He's ornery, never hostile. Dropped anyway, he's unconscious and dying under the normal PC dying rules, and holds no grudge; aggression is normal on Moonshadow (p. 168).

**Rotsnout** — Creature 4, unique NE Medium humanoid orpok, male orpok recluse (p. 167)
- **Perception** +11; scent (imprecise) 30 feet. **Languages** Common, Orpok.
- **Skills:** Arcana +10, Crafting +10, Medicine +9, Nature +11, Survival +11, Swamp Lore +10.

It takes **2d8 mental**, **DC 20 basic Will**. On a failure it's also [stupefied](gm_chapter_5.md#conditions-new-this-chapter) 1 for 1 round (stupefied 2 on a critical failure). Either way, the target is immune for 1 hour.

### What Rotsnout knows

Abrasive but secretly glad to talk. He mocks the PCs' manners, dress, and smell (his are worse) and hopes they'll handle the invaders, without asking. Read or paraphrase (p. 168):

> *"You probably saw that village down the trail, closer to the shore. There were some real nasty folks living there: escaped criminals, mutineers, bloodthirsty killers, and all kinds. I'm not particularly choosy about who I associate with because I like my privacy and I give others the same, so when I say folks are nasty, you can believe it.*
>
> *"I lived there for a little while, but I couldn't stand the neighbors, so I moved out. This path goes all the way to some old wooden fortress that was built ages ago and abandoned some time back as well. That was too much space for the likes of me, so I instead built this old cottage right here, and it suits me just fine. No one from the village comes this far unless they're willing to pay for my services, and no one comes from the old fortress, because there isn't anybody there.*
>
> *"At least, there wasn't anybody there until recently. It wasn't long ago that I caught wind of some new group that'd come to the island. Not just new to the island, like they sailed from anywhere, but new to this world. They came from some other plane, one that's all horror and shadow and evil. Spindly, alien things. They started raiding out from that fortress in small groups—not to kill, but to ruin. They've blighted parts of the forest with this gross, tarry resin. It turns the plants and animals wicked. Fortunately, they've left my swamp and my cottage alone; they might not even know I'm out here.*
>
> *"But they sure found the village, they did. And they didn't blight it or attack it. They...converted, I suppose, the people there. Must have promised them a lot of work or treasure or... improvements by coming to the fortress, because everyone in the village just up and relocated, tromping up that path like they were in a parade led by the most sinister creatures I ever saw. At least, it sounded like everyone; if they left anyone behind in that village, I'm sure they didn't leave them in any state I care to set eyes on.*
>
> *"Since then, none of the villagers have been back, and the raids across the island have stopped. Whatever those monsters are doing, they're doing it right there at the fortress. You won't catch me going within a mile of that place, as I value my hairy hide too much. But someone ought to check it out, even if it's a fortress filled with reprobates and alien monsters. Good luck with that."*

- **DC 20 Occultism:** Shadow Plane invaders.
- Told about sklaggans, he's seen them, plus similar **bulkier, tentacle-faced monstrosities**.
- Nothing on the Jewel, little on Gullstone Cave; he avoids the kindly plant creatures he thinks live near it (out of date, as the PCs know).

**Gift:** if the PCs commit to the fortress, he decides they're plainly not smart enough to succeed without him and fetches a ***ring of the ram***, a ***salve of slipperiness***, and a ***moderate healing potion***.

**XP:** **30 XP** for hearing his tale.

> **🎯 Milestone:** Meeting Rotsnout is the story milestone (p. 11). The PCs reach **level 6** resting here.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The "bulkier, tentacle-faced monstrosities" foreshadow the **Chapter 7** fortress.

### Event 15 — Swamp Raiders *(Severe 5)*

**When:** mid-conversation, such as as it winds down or while Rotsnout fetches the gift. The PCs need only see the mist past the dock's end.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Blabbing villagers recently told the sklaggans about Rotsnout. They sent a veenlijk and two loyal swarm assassins to kill him before he's a problem, and anyone with him.

> *The swamp mist thickens and curls into strange shapes. A few bells tinkle from within the mist.*

If Rotsnout is there: *"Those are my warning bells, from out in the swamp. Something's coming, and I've already had enough trespassers for one day."*

**Hazard — poisonous mist** raised by the veenlijk:
- **Three 10-foot bursts** roll north toward shore, placed per the map.
- A breathing creature **starting its turn** in one takes **2d8 poison**, **DC 20 basic Fortitude**.
- Each cloud moves **1d4 squares north** at the end of each round.
- Greenish and obvious from afar. No concealment inside, but a creature on one side is [concealed](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected) from the other, which lets the attackers close in.

**Creatures:**
- **Veenlijk** — BB 117, Creature 7. Initiative: Stealth +18. Monster Parts 45/100/180 gp. Climbs up the dock's end.
- **Corrupted Swarm Assassins (2)** — BB 109, Creature 3. Initiative: Stealth +11. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. Attack from the dock's sides.
  - **Corrupted:** sklaggan resin lets them ignore natural difficult terrain (swamp, undergrowth) and makes them immune to poison damage.
- All fight to the death.

> **💡 GM tip:** The book calls this difficult and allows more direct aid from Rotsnout if the PCs struggle: *heal*, *barkskin*, and *faerie fire* are prepared. Use them before it turns deadly.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The swarm assassins are poison-immune, so the mist can't hurt them. That line skips the veenlijk; check BB 117 before treating it as safe in the clouds.

### After the attack

Rotsnout urges a rest in his cottage before the fortress (the ship is a long trek back) and offers healing.

**Swamp Slurp Surprise:** he dumps laundry from the cauldron and cooks unwiped (last filthy sock included): mushrooms, swamp grasses, herbs, moss, a few live frogs. Grug stirs with a flat paddle, sometimes falling in and climbing out. Chunky green sludge that smells good and tastes better. Refusers hear: *"Your loss, friend, all the more for me!"*
- **A full bowl:** **10 temporary Hit Points** on waking, for **8 hours** ([temporary HP](#temporary-hit-points)).
- **A taste only:** maybe **1 temporary Hit Point**, so they know what they missed.
