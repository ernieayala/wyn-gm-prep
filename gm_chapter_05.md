## The chapter in one minute

The PCs sail Poppy's ship, the [*Chromatic Queen*](gm_glossary.md#chromatic-queen), to [Seaview](gm_glossary.md#seaview) on [Bluebell Island](gm_glossary.md#bluebell-island). [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi) commands. The council sends the PCs to the Academy of Tastes. There they cross-check Poppy's journal against explorer Gustavus Gollihew's maps. They get past a haunted archive and a possessed official. They leave with maps to all three missing Jewel fragments.

Angry spirits plague Seaview, so the council asks for help with three troubles: a surveyor's haunted maps, a farm full of hags, and a haunted inn. The inn reveals the cause. **Councilor Pelborii**'s protection ritual draws spirits and enrages them. Next the council sends the PCs to Briargrove. Poppy hid the **Aquamarine Wedge** there, behind traps and a rope golem. A parade and feast follow, and the PCs sail on.

**By the end the PCs have:**
1. **Three fragment maps:** a cave on Bluebell Island, a cave on Moonshadow Island's south side, and Razorback Isle somewhere in Runaway Reef (Scene 2).
2. **Stopped the hauntings** by dealing with Pelborii (Scene 6).
3. **The Aquamarine Wedge**, the second Jewel fragment (Scene 7).
4. **Defense special resources** for the Chapter 12 siege ([Things to track](#things-to-track-for-later-chapters)).

**Party level:** starts at 4, ends at **5**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** It builds Act 2's home base: the ship, the crew, and island-hopping. It also hands over the Chapters 6–8 itinerary in one scene. Under the food-town comedy, it plants the cult. Pelborii has true visions of the shale behemoth rising, and her notes spell out the cult's plan. Meanwhile, the cult's leader accepts bluebell crates at the feast.

## Part Two setup: ship, crew, and travel

This covers all of Part Two (Chapters 5–8). Reuse it for every voyage.

### The ship deal

[King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather) wants the Chromatic Queen on a "victory tour" from Rumplank to Seaview and beyond (p. 112–113).

- After centuries in drydock, **restoration costs 2,000 gp**. Outfitting alone is 250 gp or more.
- **The deal:** the royals pay if the PCs accept the **royal route** and **royal crew** for this first voyage. Back in Rumplank, the PCs can take full ownership.
- **Royal crew means Kulupi commands.** He's capable but green. He leaves most work to the hardriggan bosun, **Garnikor**. Or he leaves it to a suitable PC (like an indigo pirate) who follows his general orders.
- **PCs refuse to share authority?** Kulupi comes as envoy and advisor instead. Nothing else changes.
- **No Chromatic Queen (or prefer another ship)?** Swap in any ship. The deal still applies, since almost nobody can pay those costs alone.

**Background special effect (indigo pirate):** a PC with a reputation can bring recruited crew. The royals suggest that PC serve as **first mate**. The first mate makes practical decisions under Kulupi. After the voyage, that PC becomes captain with the Redfeathers' blessing. Kulupi praises good sailing. He can't act humble, but he avoids provoking a mutiny. Sometimes the PCs pay their own way and a PC is captain, with Kulupi as envoy. Then he struggles far more to hold back.

### Why Seaview first

The king plans to cross-check Poppy's vague maps against the maps of culinary explorer **Gustavus Gollihew** (p. 113). Gustavus was a famous Seaview orpok. He founded the **Academy of Tastes**, the Isles' most famous university. Seaview is a close trading partner, so it was the first stop anyway.

Kulupi meets the council to:
1. Show off the Chromatic Queen.
2. Buy **bluebells** and other useful resources.
3. Get the PCs into the Academy for research.

> **🔒 GM-only (the artifact's true history (p. 113–114)):** Poppy and an army of heroes fought the cult in the War of the Shivering Behemoth. The cult meant to rule the Isles through **shale spitters**. These are stony isopods, from tiny shale swarms up to titanic shale behemoths. Poppy took the Jewel from cult headquarters on Razorback Isle. Razorback Isle sank beneath Runaway Reef, with one piece still in its deepest vault. She escaped with the other three pieces:
> - She hid one in a **forest cave on Bluebell Island**.
> - She hid one **behind a puzzle-locked door in a cave near the Moonshadow Island coast**.
> - She took the Obsidian Star to Rumplank with her treasure.

### The crew

The ship carries **thirty people, not counting the PCs** (p. 114). It's the PCs' temporary home. They store gear, rest, and do [downtime](#downtime) aboard, like Crafting, retraining, or healing. Named crew:

| Crew member | Who they are | Hooks and services |
|---|---|---|
| 🔒 **Prince Kulupi** (CE male g'mayun prince 10) | Distant, competent commander | Friendly on the surface, but close to no one, PCs included. The crew is honored to serve him. They also blame him for anything that goes wrong, even weather |
| **Garnikor** (LN female hardriggan bosun 7) | Gruff bosun. The cat-o'-nine-tails at her hip almost never comes out | Ex-teamster. She was sentenced to ship labor for hiding relatives' smuggled goods. Later she was promoted to bosun, and her sentence was commuted. Terse, fair mentor. No rudeness or slacking. A little charmed by the PCs. **Sells potions of water breathing** (useful in Chapter 8) |
| **Samblain** (NG male g'mayun doctor 6) | Handsome, flirtatious surgeon | Flirts with nearly anyone, but stops if they're uncomfortable. All focus with a patient. Scolds the cooks for fatty food. **Medicine +15**. Treats Disease and Wounds for anyone aboard. Rarely fights. In dire need, he might sell his few unusable **scrolls of *restoration*** |
| **Fippin and Mippin Humcakes** (NG male and nonbinary orpok cooks 4) | Twin cooks from Seaview's ships | Tasty meals from poor ingredients. Fippin (he/him) is chatty and slacks off to gossip. Mippin (they/them) is quiet and hard-working. They befriend orpok and cook PCs (like a bluebell chef) and may invite them to help. Fippin pesters PCs about the Academy. Neither twin attended it |
| **"Slate" Hodda** (LG female kragrak scout 5) | Lookout in the tops | Talkative, but won't say why she left the mountains of central Goldcrop. Her nickname comes from her ashy skin, so dark it reflects almost no light. Spots reefs and sights. Has **dragon turtle scales** from a past attack. She might sell them to swimmers |
| **Leafling** (female [blood mouse](gm_glossary.md#blood-mouse), BB 14) | Ship's mascot | Mauve, cheeky, and fat on scraps. Garnikor smuggled her aboard. She lost her proboscis, so she's hand-fed. She steals from Kulupi, sometimes right off his plate. He wants her thrown overboard, so the crew hides her and slips her treats |

The other **two dozen sailors** are yours to name. Add any crew an indigo pirate PC recruited.

> **💡 GM tip:** Leafling is the only one who dares pester the prince. The crew bonds over grumbling about him. A crew that likes the PCs and rolls its eyes at Kulupi sets up Act 3.

> **🧩 Sets up:** In the Chapter 9 tournament, Garnikor is a black sash (4th dan). In Chapter 12, she is Barnacle Bay's default general. Hodda, Garnikor, and Leafling tell stories on the way to Moonshadow (Chapter 6). The synopsis suggests crew members as replacement PCs after an Act 2 death.

### Travel between islands

Bluebell Island is **four days** away through safe waters (p. 114). The book covers days at sea with a few short scenes. If you track time:

| Leg | Time |
|---|---|
| Rumplank to Seaview | 4 days |
| Seaview to Moonshadow Isle | 3 days |
| Moonshadow Isle to Razorback Isle | 3+ days (see p. 193) |
| Razorback Isle to Rumplank | 9 days |

- Islands on the map are to scale, but the water between them is wider than drawn. Don't measure off the map.
- The crew is expert. **Storms and sea monsters pose no threat** unless you add them.
- The PCs only have to steer in one place: Runaway Reef (**Chapter 8**), which is uncharted.

### Seek Marine Resources

Garnikor suggests harvesting sea plants on the way (p. 116–117). It's optional. Kulupi doesn't order it.

**When:** once per leg: Goldcrop to Bluebell, Bluebell to Moonshadow, Moonshadow to Runaway Reef, and Runaway Reef back to Goldcrop. It fills the leg but still leaves time to rest.

**Seek Marine Resources** *(downtime, manipulate)*. Each PC who takes part makes **one** check per leg. The check is **DC 20 Athletics, Crafting, Nature, or Survival**, or **DC 18 Sailing Lore**. Add the Kelp Points together.
- **Critical success:** 2 Kelp Points.
- **Success:** 1 Kelp Point.
- **Failure:** nothing.
- **Critical failure:** lose 1 Kelp Point.

The crew gives advice and teases, but earns no points. A relevant spell or ability can give a circumstance bonus. If one is made for harvesting and storing kelp, you can grant 1 Kelp Point with no check.

| Kelp Points (4 PCs) | Resources that leg |
|---|---|
| 7+ | Red sargassum, netweed, and twinekelp |
| 4–6 | Netweed and twinekelp |
| 2–3 | Twinekelp |
| Fewer than 2 | Enough seaweed to restock the ship, but no resources |

- **Twinekelp** (common) makes rope. **Netweed** makes tough netting. **Red sargassum** (rarest) has air bladders. Treated the right way, it floats in the air.
- **Kelp Points reset to 0** after each leg.
- **More than four PCs:** raise each threshold to match.
- All three are Chapter 12 **defense special resources** (p. 315). They add to what the islands give.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Chapter 12 caps each one at **4 bundles**, one per leg. Write down every leg's haul.

### Ocean encounters

Optional color (p. 117–118). For the short Rumplank-to-Seaview leg, **roll 1d4**. After Seaview, roll **1d8 plus islands visited**. Count Seaview and Moonshadow (+2 at most).

| Roll | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Meteor Shower.** Brilliant on a clear night. The crew trades rumors and superstitions about it |
| 2 | **Tiny Island.** An empty rock barely bigger than the ship. It might hold a clue about the next island |
| 3 | **Playful Winds.** Fickle elemental winds. **DC 20 Nature** to harness them: 1 day off travel (2 on a critical success). Failure: the winds leave. Critical failure: 1 day slower |
| 4 | **Sailor Rescue.** A dehydrated shipwreck survivor on a plank. They might carry a clue about the next island |
| 5 | **Leafling Tricks.** Kulupi catches Leafling shredding his underclothes and wants her executed for "seditious and humiliating mutiny." The crew asks the PCs to defend her |
| 6 | **Crew Conflict.** Two sailors fight over something petty, like the last grog ration. Kulupi and Garnikor want discipline. Fast talkers can calm it before her lash uncoils |
| 7 | **Doldrums.** Unnatural calm stalls the ship 1d4 days. The crew turns melancholy or superstitious |
| 8+ | **Storm.** Every PC helps with a relevant skill, or makes a Fortitude save to keep lunch down. **DC 18**, or **DC 16** with Sailing Lore |

**Storm outcomes:**
- **Critical success:** lasting crew respect and a nickname like Galebuster, Sealegs, or Knotmaster.
- **Success:** the PC helps keep the ship afloat.
- **Failure:** sickened 1, which can't be reduced for 1 day.
- **Critical failure:** as failure, and the ship is damaged. Repairs before leaving the next port cost **1 resource of any type**. With no resource, repairs take two weeks and 100 gp, and the crew is demoralized. Kulupi coldly blames this PC.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A Storm critical failure can eat a defense special resource. The first leg's 1d4 can't roll a Storm. Later legs can.

## PF2 rules new in this chapter

Chapter 1 covers [degrees of success](gm_chapter_01.md#degrees-of-success), [XP and threat](gm_chapter_01.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp), [basic saves](gm_chapter_01.md#basic-saving-throws), [secret checks](gm_chapter_01.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge), [flat checks](gm_chapter_01.md#flat-checks), [conditions](gm_chapter_01.md#conditions-youll-see-this-chapter), [terrain](gm_chapter_01.md#movement-and-terrain), [hazards](gm_chapter_01.md#hazards), and [Monster Parts](gm_chapter_01.md#monster-parts-treasure-system).

**XP at party level 4:** creature level 2 = 20 XP, 3 = 30, 4 = 40, 5 = 60, 6 = 80. A simple hazard is worth a fifth of a creature of its level.

### Downtime

Downtime is play measured in days: Crafting, retraining a feat or skill, recovering from injuries. Every voyage leg is downtime, and the ship works for these activities (p. 114). **Seek Marine Resources** is this campaign's own downtime activity ([rules](#seek-marine-resources)).

### Aid

**Aid** helps another character's check. In combat, Aid is a reaction. The helper prepares it with an action on their turn. Out of combat, the GM sets the preparation. The helper rolls, usually against **DC 20**:
- **Critical success:** +2 circumstance bonus to the ally's check (+3 if the helper is a master, +4 if legendary).
- **Success:** +1 circumstance bonus.
- **Critical failure:** −1 circumstance penalty.

**This chapter:** in H1, one PC leads the map cross-check. The others can Aid, even though it isn't an encounter (p. 123).

### Sense Motive

A Perception check to spot lying or odd behavior. It's usually against the target's Deception DC, unless the book sets a DC. The GM normally rolls it in secret.

**This chapter:** DC 15 to see that the Sous Scholar is worried (Scene 2). DC 20 at the feast to see that the councilors find Kulupi rude (Scene 8).

### Nonlethal attacks

Two fights here are against innocent people, so the book gives a sidebar (p. 124):
- A **nonlethal attack** knocks a creature out instead of killing it.
- Weapons with the **nonlethal** trait, including fists, always deal nonlethal damage.
- A nonlethal attack with a weapon that **lacks** the trait takes a **−2 circumstance penalty** to the attack roll. So does a lethal attack with a nonlethal weapon.
- Nonlethal spells and effects, like *daze*, knock a creature out at 0 HP.

**This chapter:** the possessed Sous Scholar (H3) and Pelborii (K6). The K1 hearth hounds' fire damage is also nonlethal.

### The dying track

The Sous Scholar and Pelborii **fall unconscious and start dying like a PC** at 0 HP (p. 125, 141):
- At 0 HP: unconscious and **dying 1**, plus its **wounded** value. It's **dying 2** instead from a critical hit or its own critically failed save.
- At the start of each turn: a **recovery check**. This is a flat check against **DC 10 + dying value**. Critical success −2 dying, success −1, failure +1, critical failure +2.
- Damage while dying: +1 dying. It's +2 from a critical hit or its own critically failed save.
- Dies at **dying 4**. **Doomed** lowers this number (below).
- Healing to 1+ HP ends dying. It adds **wounded 1** (or +1 wounded).
- Nonlethal damage to 0 HP knocks out **without** dying.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The Sous Scholar ends possession **doomed 1**, so he dies at dying 3. Stabilize him fast with *stabilize* or Administer First Aid (Medicine).

### Possession

The **killing intent** (BB 53) rides the Sous Scholar in H3 (p. 125):
- While he's possessed, the killing intent **isn't a separate combatant**.
- Possession lasts **3 rounds** from the start of combat, or until he drops to 0 HP. Then the killing intent **lunges out**. It fights alone until destroyed.
- While possessed, he has **22 temporary HP** and is **quickened**. The extra action can only Stride or Strike.
- Afterward he loses both. He also deals **4 less damage** with melee Strikes and gains **+2 AC**. He is **doomed 1** and **immune** to the killing intent's possession for 24 hours.

### Hazardous terrain

Hazardous terrain damages anything moving through it. Briargrove's thorns are **greater difficult terrain and hazardous terrain** (p. 142):
- Each square costs 10 extra feet of movement.
- Each square deals **2d6 piercing**.
- Vision ends at 5 feet.

Stick to the paths.

### Conditions new this chapter

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Stupefied X** | −X status penalty to Int-, Wis-, and Cha-based checks and DCs. This includes Will saves, spell attacks, and spell DCs. Casting needs a **DC 5 + X flat check**, or the spell is lost. Comes from the ink trap (I1) and the Sous Scholar's critical hits (H3) |
| **Quickened** | One extra action at the start of each turn, limited by the effect. The possessed Sous Scholar can only Stride or Strike with it |
| **Doomed X** | Dies at dying 4 minus X. Drops by 1 after a full night's rest |
| **Stunned** | Can't act. "Stunned for 1 round" loses that round's actions. Comes from calming the hearth hounds (K1) |

Also here: concealed ([Ch 2](gm_chapter_02.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)).

### Request and Command an Animal

- **Request:** Diplomacy. Ask a creature to do something ([attitudes and Request](gm_chapter_02.md#npc-attitudes-make-an-impression-and-request)).
- **Command an Animal:** Nature. Give an animal an order.

Normally, Request needs a friendly or helpful creature, and Command an Animal targets Will DC. At K1, the book sets DCs to calm the hearth hounds instead: **DC 23**, or **DC 18** for a PC whose Recall Knowledge critically succeeded (p. 137).

### Ambushes and Stealth for initiative

Hidden PCs usually roll **Stealth for initiative**. If a PC beats an enemy's **Perception DC**, that enemy hasn't noticed the PC yet. Deception actions like Lie and Create a Diversion also target **Perception DC**. Actions like Demoralize and Make an Impression target **Will DC**.

**This chapter:** if the PCs set a trap in Event 14, the hags' **Perception DC and Will DC drop from 21 to 19** until they notice trouble (p. 135). That makes both an ambush and a ruse easier.

### Flanking

Draw a line between the centers of two enemies' spaces. If it passes through opposite sides or corners of a creature's space, that creature is **flanked**. A flanked creature is flat-footed (off-guard in the Remaster) to the flankers. Both flankers must be able to act. Both must wield melee weapons or be able to make unarmed attacks. The Event 14 hags **don't coordinate flanking** (p. 135). Your PCs can.

### Covered in earlier chapters

- **Elite creatures:** [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#elite-and-weak-creatures). The **elite dishrag dervish** (K3) is Creature 5 (BB 9 adjustments, BB 101 base).
- **Concealment:** [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_02.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected). Barn-fire smoke at J6 conceals everyone in the barn. In Event 14, the tall bluebells conceal Medium or smaller creatures.
- **Invested items:** [Chapter 4](gm_chapter_04.md#artifacts-and-invested-items). The **Aquamarine Wedge** must be invested to grant water breathing.

## Scene 1 — The voyage and arrival in Seaview

- **Where:** at sea, then Seaview's harbor and the **High Chambers**. That's the council hall on a bluff.
- **Who:** Kulupi, the crew, the [Seaview Council](#the-seaview-council).
- **Goal:** the council sends the PCs to the Academy of Tastes.

### Read aloud (opening, p. 114)

> *The sea is all around you, with Rumplank at your stern and Seaview, the largest settlement on Bluebell Island, at your aft. The crew are all pleased to be sailing on such a fine day in well-traveled waters with heroes on board and Prince Kulupi in command.*

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book's "at your aft" is wrong. Aft and stern both mean the back of the ship. Seaview is ahead, so read it as "bow."

During the four days, run the [crew](#the-crew), [Seek Marine Resources](#seek-marine-resources), and an [ocean encounter](#ocean-encounters) (1d4).

### Recall Knowledge (secret check)

**Secret DC 12 Society or Indigo Isles Lore** for each PC (p. 114). A **bluebell chef** gets the critical success without rolling.
- **Critical success:** success, plus: a **four-member council** runs Seaview. Each member oversees one part of the economy or society. They squabble but work hard for the town.
- **Success:** Bluebell Island is known for orpoks, excellent food, and the namesake bluebells, used for rope, dye, and weaving.
- **Failure:** Not much. Maybe it's named after flowers?
- **Critical failure:** A shadowy council of pleasant-seeming backstabbers rules Seaview. It quietly and permanently gets rid of anyone who displeases it.

Common knowledge: Bluebell Island is mostly [orpoks](gm_glossary.md#orpok). Seaview is one of the Isles' largest towns. Its restaurants are excellent, and ocean-going ships prefer to load food here.

### Arrival

The ship docks on a sunny morning. A self-important orpok harbormaster logs it in his thick registry. Cheering orpoks line the docks for the Chromatic Queen. They congratulate everyone, even the harbormaster, who holds his registry high. Kulupi heads for the High Chambers and wants the PCs with him (p. 118).

> *Seaview is a bustling port city with tall buildings of stone and brick, and there is a palpable excitement among the crowd. The characteristic smells of fine cooking fill the air: fresh bread, spiced meats, the heady smells of rich cheese, as well as many more. The orpoks of Seaview are known as much for their cooking as for their industriousness.*
>
> *Seaview's ruling council meets in the High Chambers on a bluff overlooking the city. The walk to the High Chambers isn't long, but it's long enough for the word of distinguished visitors to spread. Functionaries at the High Chambers are quick to usher you into a meeting with the Seaview Council and introduce them as Pumbus Narheel, Gunlu Pongo, Monnino Gollihew, and Pelborii Karnihar.*

Seaview is on good terms with Rumplank. The councilors already know the PCs and guess why they came. They confirm the PCs' deeds (the Chromatic Queen, the treasure map). This shows the players that their fame has spread. The prince or the PCs explain that they need to check Poppy's journal, or the Star's visions, against the Isles' best maps. Then the councilors exchange knowing nods:

> *The most corpulent council member, Councilor Monnino, removes his small hat and holds it to his chest in an ostentatious display of reverence, "Of course, we all know of Gustavus Gollihew, founder of Seaview. He was an unequaled explorer as well as an unequaled epicurean, collecting recipes from across the isles. No other orpok has traveled as far or done as much as that illustrious personage."*
>
> *The g'mayun member, Councilor Pelborii, fluffs her feathers and adds icily, "Three full sentences and you haven't yet mentioned that Gustavus Gollihew is your ancestor, Monnino? You're afflicted with uncharacteristic humility today."*
>
> *The most academic-looking orpok on the council, Councilor Gunlu, ignores her colleague's snide remark and interjects, "Gustavus founded the great university called the Academy of Tastes. Its graduates include the best cooks and brightest scholars in Seaview."*
>
> *"Including yourself, Gunlu," grumbles the g'mayun.*
>
> *The last council member, Councilor Pumbus, is clearly running low on patience. He clears his throat to gain attention and harrumphs, "But to the matter at hand, esteemed visitors. ## Scene 2 — The Academy of Tastes

- **Where:** the Academy of Tastes campus. Map on p. 120.
- **Who:** **Sous Scholar Limbin Gumnammar** (he/him), a ghostwriter, two gossiping students, campus security, and a hidden killing intent.
- **Goal:** cross-check the maps and leave with the locations of the three fragments.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It delivers the Act 2 itinerary. It also raises Seaview's spirit problem in three steps: an enraged ghost, murder rumors, and a possessed administrator. The PCs only learn that something is wrong. They can't solve it here.

Stone towers rise over the city. The clocktower rings at each of the **seven mealtimes** many orpoks observe, not on the hour. Gustavus founded the Academy 200 years ago, in the first wave of orpok settlement. He was a scholar, cartographer, and gourmand. He worked out the gastronomic mathematics behind great recipes (p. 121).

**Background special effects:**
- **Bluebell chef:** a PC with publicized recipes gets a lunch invitation from professors.
- **Indigo trailblazer:** thrilled to handle a legendary cartographer's maps. That's the PCs' main goal anyway.

**Academy features:**
- Stone and brick, polished dark wood paneling, and statues, portraits, and plaques of past professors.
- Gardens of edible flowers and herbs.
- Rooms are **15 feet high** and lantern-lit into the evening.
- Doors have push plates, not knobs.
- The campus is usually busy around the clock. Now it often looks empty, because the trouble keeps people out of public areas.

### A bad taste of trouble

Earlier today, a student murdered the beloved **Dean de Cuisine** (he/him). She escaped custody, and the small security staff is hunting her. The dean's second-in-command, the Sous Scholar, insists he has it under control. He fears panic, so he keeps classes running with no announcement. Staff are scared. Students only sense something is off (p. 122).

The Sous Scholar meets the PCs at the entry arch and asks their business.
- **DC 15 Perception to Sense Motive** ([Sense Motive](#sense-motive)): he's worried about something.
- **Critical success:** his eyes keep flicking around. His worry has nothing to do with the PCs.

He's relieved that they only want maps:

> *"Maps?" says the Sous Scholar, patting the sweat on his forehead with what looks like a tea towel. "Why, we have several! Gustavus Gollihew, our illustrious founder, had quite a collection of them, and they're now in the archive wing of the library. There's..." the Sous Scholar pauses for a moment, raising his hand to his quivering chin. "Well, technically, I suppose the archive wing is haunted. But the ghost there doesn't hurt anyone, he just gets peevish and tries to scare people away. If you're firm with him, he'll leave you to your work.*
>
> *"Of course, we have rules in the archive wing because of the old documents there. Absolutely no water, no fire, and," here, he swallows as though about to deliver some very bad news, "no eating."*

He gives directions and trots off to check on security.

> **🔒 GM-only (the killer (p. 122)):** A possessing spirit, a **killing intent**, made the student do it. It's one of many spirits Pelborii's ritual drew here. The ritual also turns harmless spirits dangerous.

### H1 — Archive Wing *(Moderate 4)*

> *Dark wooden shelves are built into every wall of this large room, even around the thick pillars that undoubtedly support the upper levels of this old university building. The shelves are jammed with all manner of books, scrolls, and folios. Three small tables, each with a chair too small for even a g'mayun to consider comfortable, stand among the shelves in the archive wing. Everything bears a fine layer of dust and a forlorn sense of abandonment.*

The wing was built for Gustavus's collection. It became a dump for every dull book, thesis, and bland recipe collection someone wanted forgotten. Hardly anyone visits.

**Ghostwriter:** BB 37, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.
- It was born from the neglected collection over a century ago. It used to be merely peevish. It edited the few magical texts and scared people off. A firm, loud voice sent it back into a book.
- **Pelborii's disruption** filled it with rage. Only its tie to the wing keeps it from rampaging across campus. The PCs are the first visitors since then.
- When the PCs search the shelves, it bursts from a dull magic almanac and attacks. It favors ***magic missile*** and ***telekinetic projectile***. In melee it uses its ghostly quill.
- It **fights until destroyed** but won't chase anyone outside the wing. Rejuvenation brings it back in a few days, long after the PCs leave.

> **📏 Threat math:** one level-6 creature against a level-4 party is 80 XP, Moderate.

### Reviewing the maps

Two steps (p. 123):
1. **Find the maps:** **1d4 hours**, then **DC 19 Perception** or a relevant Lore. Several PCs can search at once. Each success beyond the first cuts 1 hour.
2. **Cross-check with the journal:** another **1d4 hours**, then **DC 19 Nature, Society, or Survival**. It's **DC 17** with a relevant Lore like Indigo Isles Geography Lore. This step can't be split up. One PC leads, and the others can [Aid](#aid).

- **Failed checks:** keep going until success. The clock keeps running.
- **Messy research:** maybe the PCs used acid or fire on the ghostwriter, or broke archive rules. Then each step takes **1d4+1 hours**, because of scorched text and spills.
- **More than 8 hours in the wing:** add a **third campus security guard** to H3.
- **XP:** **30 XP** for pinpointing the locations in **8 hours or fewer**.

**What the maps show** (p. 123):
- **Bluebell Island:** a cave in dense woodland a few hours' walk from Seaview.
- **Moonshadow Island:** a cave fairly near a south-side beach. Maybe even Poppy avoided going deep into that ill-omened island.
- **Razorback Isle:** a small, rocky island in **Runaway Reef**. The reef is hazardous shallows that sailors avoid. The island is on no recent map.
- The maps are specific but don't show what's inside the caves.

**Treasure:** a ***scroll of glitterdust*** and a ***scroll of searing light***, forgotten on the shelves. Careful research turns them up, and *detect magic* finds them at once. A note shows they belonged to a long-dead owner, not the school.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The Moonshadow map leads to [Moonshadow Island](gm_glossary.md#moonshadow-island) (Chapters 6–7). The Razorback map leads to [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef) (Chapter 8).

### H3 — Academy Plaza *(Moderate 4)*

> *Several stately brick buildings surround a pleasant plaza here on the campus. A fountain bearing a statue of a studious-looking orpok holding a tray of cupcakes burbles pleasantly. The flowerbeds are meticulously arranged with pleasant-smelling herbs and colorful flowers. The Sous Scholar stomps into the quad, followed by orpoks wearing the uniforms of campus security. "They're the killers!" he calls, pointing an oversized eating utensil in your direction. "Quick, before they kill again!"*

**What happened:** security caught the student while the PCs researched. But the killing intent had already left her. It overheard talk of the PCs and judged them better hosts. It secretly possessed the Sous Scholar. He then told the confused guards that the student was innocent and the newcomers were the real killers.

**Creatures:**
- **Sous Scholar Limbin Gumnammar** (possessed), stat block below. He attacks with his runcible spoon at once.
- **Campus Security Guards (2)**, stat block below. They're confused, but they fight beside him. Add a third if the PCs spent more than 8 hours in H1.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (don't kill the Sous Scholar):** The book says he falls **unconscious and dying** at 0 HP ([dying track](#the-dying-track)). That way the PCs can learn he was possessed. Killing an innocent victim feels like the game cheated them (p. 125). Remind players about [nonlethal attacks](#nonlethal-attacks). The guards' saps are nonlethal.

## Scene 4 — Clean Lines Surveying

- **Where:** Allasha's office near the High Chambers. Map on p. 128.
- **Who:** **Allasha the Surveyor** (LG female orpok cartographer 5) and two **yomhibdis**.
- **Goal:** clear the yomhibdis and find the clue pointing at the Wavewatcher Inn.

Allasha surveys nearly every property around Seaview. People trust her so much that her judgment replaces official city maps. Two farmers came to her to settle a dispute. Her copy of their line wove back and forth, and everyone knew it was wrong. Other maps had changed too. Doubt about her has hurt morale across the island (p. 127–128).

> **🔒 GM-only:** Two **yomhibdis** lurk in her plat maps. They're ghostly creatures that love redrawing written works. They enjoy her distress and will defend the shop they now consider theirs. Pelborii's rituals drew them, though they don't know it.

### I1 — Office Entry

> *This tidy office has a reception area to the south and a meeting room to the north, beyond a velvet privacy curtain that's currently open. Another privacy curtain—although this one is mostly closed—leads to a workroom located further back into the shop's interior. Detailed and technical maps of all kinds adorn every one of the walls, each of them stamped with the same wax seal.*

Allasha wrings her hands here. She wears a tidy green and black tunic with armbands. A silver circlet holds back her large ears. She mutters that she can't take clients. Then she recognizes the PCs and turns sharp and professional:

> *"I really appreciate you coming by. Here's the key issue: Seaview relies upon my maps, and my maps have become somehow unreliable. I don't mean that my newest maps aren't any good—my skills are as sharp as ever. I mean, existing maps, older maps, have changed. I can't recall every line and corner I've ever drawn, of course, but I know my style. Most of the ones I've pulled out—not all of them, but most of them—are in a different style, like they've been drawn with a clumsier hand. Perhaps someone is replacing them? Or somehow changing the ink on the page? I'm at a loss to see what's happening, and I could use some help."*

If pressed, she realizes the workroom's **south shelves** are unchanged. The **plat map cabinets on the north side** are not.

### Hazard — Ink Trap *(Hazard 6, simple, magical)*

The yomhibdis trapped the workroom (I2) curtain. Allasha doesn't know. She politely gestures for the PCs to go first. If they make her go first, she **critically fails** the save. She stays unconscious as long as she's stupefied, leaving the PCs alone (p. 129).
- **Stealth DC 23 (expert).** Ink spots on the back of the curtain.
- **Disable:** **DC 23 Arcana, Occultism, or Thievery (trained)** to fold the curtain so the darts are harmless.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +11; **HP** 56 (BT 28); immune to critical hits, object immunities, and precision damage.
- **Ink Darts (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature touches or moves the curtain to I2. **Effect:** solid ink darts deal **7d4+16 piercing** to the triggering creature. It makes a **DC 24 Reflex** save:
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage and **stupefied 1** for 1 round.
  - **Failure:** full damage and **stupefied 2** for 1 minute.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage and **stupefied 2** for 1 hour.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** This is not a basic save. Even a success stupefies, which hurts casters right before I2 ([stupefied](#conditions-new-this-chapter)). Spotting the trap needs expert Perception, so most parties trigger it.

> **📏 Threat math:** a simple hazard two levels above the party is 16 XP (a fifth of 80).

### I2 — Workroom *(Moderate 4)*

> *This workroom is crowded with deep shelves and cabinets. A table with blank parchment, ink, and cartographer's tools is shoved against a rear door. Low shelves filled with maps and ledgers line the south wall, while taller cabinets line the north wall. One of the cabinet drawers rattles, then is still.*

- **The back door:** the yomhibdis pushed the table against it. That way nobody could escape once they started feeding.
- **The map on the table** shows the **Dardus Farm**. Its bluebell-field lines zigzag oddly. **DC 19 Nature, Occultism, or Religion:** the new lines carry a subtle magical repulsion. It would keep peaceful nature spirits and friendly wood [eldamon](gm_glossary.md#eldamon) off the farm. Allasha confirms she didn't draw them.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The lines are the yomhibdis' unconscious echo of Pelborii's effects. They don't know her or her plans (p. 129).

**Yomhibdis (2):** BB 122, Creature 4. Initiative: Stealth +11. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp each.
- They burst from the plat cabinets in a spray of jagged inky letters when anyone tries to open them.
- They attack everyone **except Allasha**. They're saving her to eat later.
- Below **40 HP**, a yomhibdi uses **Dredge Sorrow** on apparent spellcasters or creatives.
- They fight until destroyed.

PCs who missed the spirit check can retry it. **DC 23 Society or Perception**, or **DC 18 if Allasha helps**: most lines form a **big spiral through town around the Wavewatcher Inn**. The inn is the center of the changes.

**Resource:** for freeing her, Allasha gives a **geomantic stone**. It upgrades a quake tower to a **tectonic tower** in Chapter 12 (p. 131).

**XP:** **30 XP** for working out the Wavewatcher Inn's significance.

## Scene 5 — Dardus Farm

- **Where:** the Dardus family farm, 2 miles outside Seaview. Map on p. 130.
- **Who:** three **feld hags** and their tar hounds, the surviving Darduses.
- **Goal:** rescue the family and kill the hags.

> **⚠️ Content warning (p. 131):** The book describes the victims in grisly detail. This doc keeps those details in running text so you can summarize. If your group prefers, skip how the bodies look and say the farmers are dead.

The Darduses are among Bluebell Island's most established farm families. They're humble and hardworking, and their bluebells matter to the island's economy. They own 40 acres around the farmhouse and give extra wealth to charity. Wild country borders their land on two sides.

> **🔒 GM-only (p. 131–132):** Benevolent spirits of camaraderie and hard work always protected the farm. So did several friendly wood eldamon. Pelborii's meddling made them vanish. That let a coven of three **feld hags** in for the first time. The hags killed a few Darduses, captured others, and scattered the rest. Killing the hags won't bring the spirits back. But the family can rally and build better ordinary defenses.

### Arrival

Windows on the farmhouse have checkered curtains that are tied open on the ground floor, but all closed on the upper level. The farmhouse's wide front porch leads to a screen door that's streaked with blood. There's no sign of activity, and no sound other than the buzzing of flies.*

The hags split up when they arrived. One took the farmhouse, one the barn, and one the fields (chasing workers). The barn hag has since joined the field hag.

Family beds, a children's room with two big bunk beds, a shared bathroom. Nothing of interest.

### J4 — Master Bedroom *(Low 4)*

> *This master bedroom contains an enormous feather bed heaped with handmade quilts. A roaring fireplace stands on the wall opposite the bed, making the room quite hot.*

**What happened:** family heads **Emogen and Aldebbo Dardus** slept here. Emogen was ill, and Aldebbo nursed her. The hag liked the idea of being cared for. She killed both and threw their bodies in the fire. She put on Emogen's nightclothes and made the **three grandchildren** wait on "Goodly Granny." Sometimes she uses Change Shape to look like an elderly orpok, but it doesn't fool the children. She makes them sleep in the bathroom (J5) where she can watch them (p. 132).

**The ruse:** she lies in bed as an old orpok woman, out of sight from the door. For extra creepiness, have her call from around the corner:

> *"Is someone there?" croaks an elderly female voice. The foot of the heavy bed is just visible around the corner, and the voice comes from someone occupying it beneath its heaps of blankets. "Goodly Granny is sick in bed, children, and bothering her would be very naughty. Come in, children, but only one at a time so Goodly Granny can see you up close."*

- She wants visitors **one at a time**. She may ask a lone visitor to set aside weapons, armor, and "other sharp things" and climb in to keep her warm. She attacks anyone that reckless.
- **If the ruse is challenged,** she takes her true form and attacks. The Change Shape also gets her out of bed.

- The children watch from the bathroom doorway, unharmed.

**Feld Hag:** BB 30, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.

### Event 14 — Hags Return *(Severe 4)*

The two field hags return, usually **five minutes** after a PC spots them (p. 134–135).

- **PCs leave early?** The hags may spot them and sneak up through the bluebells.

**Setting an ambush:** with a few minutes' warning, the PCs can plan a ruse or ambush. The unsuspecting hags' **Perception DC and Will DC drop from 21 to 19** until they notice trouble ([ambush rules](#ambushes-and-stealth-for-initiative)). The Darduses suggest:
- **The garden behind the farmhouse:** molehills make it **greater difficult terrain**. The hags don't see that as a problem. Lure them in.
- **The bluebell fields:** tall enough to **conceal** Medium or smaller creatures, even though the hags stunted them.
- **Bait:** the hags want escapees back. The **adults** will be bait, as long as they won't end up alone with an angry hag. The **children** won't, and their parents keep them away. If the family gets no job, it barricades itself in the farmhouse bedrooms.
- **Small rooms and doorways** in the barn or farmhouse can split the hags.
- **Sniping:** farmhouse windows and the hayloft door give cover.

**Feld Hags (2):** BB 30, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp each.
- They learned that splitting up was a mistake, so they fight **together**. But they fight selfishly: they **don't coordinate flanking** ([flanking](#flanking)).
- Their *igneous sudor* fire globs have poor range, and they know it. Against snipers upstairs, they **rush into whichever building has the fewest enemies**. They want to get into melee.
- **Summon animal:** one hag may summon **tar hound whelps**. These use **wolf** statistics plus **resistance fire 3** and the tar hound's **Frozen Tar** ability.

> **📏 Threat math:** two level-5 creatures, 60 XP each, 120 total, Severe. Summoned whelps add no XP.

For now, they give a **basket of provisions** (with the J2 treasure if the PCs left it). The basket holds **thunder lilies**. They upgrade a storm tower to a **tempest tower** in Chapter 12 (p. 135).

**XP:** **30 XP** for freeing the Darduses from all three hags.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Mission complete: one bluebell crate. The "spirit folk" story and the hags' sudden arrival both point to someone driving off Seaview's good spirits.

## Scene 6 — The Wavewatcher Inn

- **Where:** one of Seaview's best restaurants, near the docks. Map on p. 136.
- **Who:** two hearth hounds, a dishrag dervish, a hiding cook, and **Councilor Pelborii**.
- **Goal:** stop Pelborii's ritual.

The inn is sturdy wood that has survived gales and parties. Diners rent its small rooms, because guests get better spots on the restaurant's waitlist.

> **🔒 GM-only (p. 136):** Pelborii paid for a full month in the private back room. It's the headquarters for her spiritual "defenses" around Seaview. The staff left her alone and never saw how far gone she was. Her ritual twisted the inn's two **hearth hounds**. They're shy spirits who have lived in the main fireplace since the forgotten first owner. Patrons think the "Wavewatcher's ghost dogs" are an urban legend. The hounds have now driven everyone out with sorrowful howls and fiery breath, though nobody died. Pelborii doesn't know. She wouldn't mind, because it keeps her private.

### K1 — Common Room *(Moderate 4)*

> *This wide, comfortable common room is paneled in light-colored wood with swirling patterns. A long bar and three round tables fill the room and make it pleasantly cozy, although several chairs are knocked over. Old meals on the tables suggest diners fled days ago. Stairs go up to the west. A curtained hallway leads to the back rooms.

**Hearth Hounds (2):** BB 40, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp each.
- They come out of the hearth to attack, instead of greeting with wags and licks.
- Their good nature holds them back. Their **fire damage is nonlethal** and fades without burns or scars ([nonlethal](#nonlethal-attacks)).

**Recall Knowledge (secret check):** on first sight, roll **secret DC 19 Occultism, Religion, or Spirit Lore** for each PC. A PC who met **Ruff, Poppy's hearth hound in Chapter 4**, can't do worse than failure (p. 137).
- **Critical success:** Hearth hounds are good-aligned protectors, so something is wrong. Reminding them of their helpful nature can make them stand down. This PC can Request peace with **DC 18 Diplomacy** or Command an Animal with **DC 18 Nature**.
- **Success:** as critical success, but DC **23**.
- **Failure:** Hearth hounds aren't normally malicious. Something is agitating them.
- **Critical failure:** Only fire kills a hearth hound, and its spiritual nature hides fire damage until it's snuffed out.

**Calming them** ([Request and Command an Animal](#request-and-command-an-animal)): any PC who succeeded at Recall Knowledge, or has met a hearth hound, knows the trick. Use **DC 23 Diplomacy** to Request peace or **DC 23 Nature** to Command an Animal (DC 18 for a PC who critically succeeded).

**XP:** **80 XP** for calming the hounds and avoiding or stopping the fight, as if won.

Faint sobbing comes through the door, though the dervish may come first.

**Mupto Burgin** (NG male orpok cook 1) is the hapless junior cook. He hid here from the hearth hounds. By the time he dared leave, the dervish held the corridor. He has eaten well from the stores for days, but he's too scared to come out. He thinks all Seaview might be haunted.

The dervish fouled all the pantry food with grease or grimy water. It smeared the food on the pantry's back wall.
- **DC 18 Perception:** the damage gets worse toward the east and is worst in the pantry. The pantry's back wall reads **"stopstopstopstop"** in smeared food. The dervish can't tell what's beyond that wall. It senses the source of its agitation there.

### K6 — Private Room *(Moderate 4)*

**The door** has a flimsy inside latch. Use **DC 19 Thievery** to Pick a Lock or **DC 19 Athletics** to Force Open. On a **failed** attempt, Pelborii hears and casts ***mage armor*** (+1 AC).

> *Every inch of the walls of this large chamber is covered with scrawled notes and inscrutable diagrams. Twine leads from some of the diagrams to other diagrams, making a confusing web along the room's walls. Two serving tables are all but invisible beneath untidy heaps of paper. - *"The behemoth shivers! Beware of the Shivering Behemoth!"*
- *"What was cast beneath the sea shall rise up once more."*
- *"The diamond is the key, but only once it is made whole."*

"Cast beneath the sea" is the cult temple sunk at Runaway Reef (Chapter 8). "Made whole" is the Jewel the PCs are assembling. Let players copy the phrases. Act 3 explains them.

**Recall Knowledge (map):** the **south wall** carvings form a map of Seaview. For anyone who looks, roll **secret DC 19 Society**, or **DC 14 Seaview Lore or Indigo Isles Geography Lore**. Add **+1 circumstance** if the party visited Clean Lines Surveying **or** the Dardus Farm. Add **+2** if both (p. 139).
- **Critical success:** Four places are marked: Clean Lines Surveying, the Dardus Farm, the Wavewatcher Inn, and the Academy of Tastes. Lines join them into an occult symbol. The PC can attempt the symbol check and **can't get worse than a success**.
- **Success:** as critical success, but the symbol check is **DC 19**, not 14, and can fail.
- **Failure:** the four places are visibly marked, but the PC sees no occult meaning. No symbol check.
- **Critical failure:** the markings mean Pelborii plans a ritual to rip the High Chambers off its bluff and throw it into the sea.

**Recall Knowledge (symbol):** only after succeeding above. Roll **secret Occultism or Religion**: **DC 14** after a critical success, **DC 19** after a success.
- **Critical success:** The rune is meant to ward the city against malevolent spirits. But it's drawn wrong, so it **attracts new spirits and agitates the ones already here**. The PC also gains a **+1 circumstance bonus to AC and saves against Pelborii's spells**.
- **Success:** as critical success, without the bonus.
- **Failure:** It's a rune of protection against spirits.
- **Critical failure:** The rune reveals evil occult foundations under Seaview. They slowly warp the mind of anyone who spends a long time here. Short-lived orpoks are likely safe. Other ancestries are at serious risk.

**Pelborii's reaction:** she's reinforcing her "protection runes" against the titanic earth spirit of her visions. She's surprised. She decides the council sent the PCs to drag her away. She insists her work protects the town, and her claims grow wilder the longer she talks.
- **Fight:** hostile moves, or blaming her for the spirits, convince her the PCs mean to stop her. She **attacks at once**. She uses her spells well and stays out of melee.
- **Talk her down:** if no fight breaks out, use **DC 22 Diplomacy** to Make an Impression or **DC 22 Deception** to Lie. Either one gets her listening. Then the PCs show that the south-wall symbol has errors and protects no one. She realizes she's done far more harm than good and **collapses in grief** (p. 140).

> **⚠️ Gotcha (don't kill her):** She's a sitting councilor with no ill intent. Killing her, even in self-defense, reflects badly on the PCs and Kulupi. Like the Sous Scholar, she falls **unconscious and dying** at 0 HP ([dying track](#the-dying-track)). Remind players about [nonlethal attacks](#nonlethal-attacks) and the −2 penalty (p. 139, 141).

> **💡 GM tip:** Talking her down works best after the symbol check, because the PC can name the error. Otherwise, let players argue from what they've seen: hounds, dervish, hags, yomhibdis, and the spiral on Allasha's maps.

**Councilor Pelborii Karnihar:** Creature 6, unique CN Small g'mayun humanoid, female g'mayun spiritualist (BB 35) (p. 140)

  - **3rd** (3 slots): *bind undead*, *hypnotic pattern*, *magic missile*
  - **2nd** (3 slots): *dispel magic*, *faerie fire*, *spiritual weapon*

**Solovei:** BB 107, Creature 6. Initiative: Stealth +15. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.
- It's out of sight at first. It's sulking in its nest after failing to beat its rival, the reaver beaver to the southwest.
- Any noise from the PCs perks it up, and it comes to eat them.
- **Below 30 HP** it scrambles up the trees and abandons the clearing.

**Treasure:** soloveis collect shiny things. The nest holds a **silver-studded lifting belt** and a **potion of leaping** in a glittering glass vial.

### L5 — Fern Grove *(Low 4)*

> *A slight ridge of stone rises from the forest floor here, surrounded by a scattering of smaller stones. The many ferns growing atop the stony ridge look brown and decayed.*

The solovei was eating bugs here when an iron fern cut it. Its panicked Withering Whistle sickened the ferns and rusted the iron ferns. The forest has mostly regrown. The rocks are still bare.

**Iron Ferns (3):** BB 48, Creature 2. Initiative: Stealth +7. Monster Parts 5/12/22 gp each.
- Rust hides them among the dead ferns. They quiver as warm-blooded creatures come near, then attack.
- Two sharpen their leaves and scramble off the ridge. The third releases spores (harmless to iron ferns), then lunges.
- They're not intelligent. They fight until killed.

**Secret passage:** a thin patch behind the ridge, **DC 19 Perception**, leads back to **L3**.

> **📏 Threat math:** three level-2 creatures, 20 XP each, 60 total, Low.

### L6 — Spongeberry Bush

> *The forest gives way to the side of a low hill here. Brush surrounds a clearing at the side of the hill, and the clearing contains a large bush bursting with red, juicy berries.*

- This is the region's only spongeberry bush. Its roots hold back the thorns and make it **impossible to dig up**. With a little effort, the PCs can pick **several dozen** ripe berries.
- **The cave entrance** is in the hill to the south, behind thorny vines. It's about 4 feet high and 1 foot off the ground.
  - **Expert Perception** or better notices it without a check. Others need **DC 15 Perception** while Searching.
  - Pushing through the vines deals **2d6 piercing**. A long weapon or stick clears them easily.

### L7 — Gemstone Cave *(Severe 4)*

> *The narrow tunnel opens into a natural cavern that's been worked into a roughly square shape. Loose coils of rope are piled on the floor and hang from the ceiling twelve feet above. A wooden skull dangles from a hanging rope at the entrance, its forehead carved with the words, "Thieves Keep Out!"*

The **Aquamarine Wedge** sits on a pedestal in an east alcove, out of sight of the entrance. Poppy trusted Briargrove and her protections to keep it safe. When a PC sees the pedestal:

> *A circular stone pillar rests on the ground, rising about three feet from the floor. The top of the pedestal is flat and about two feet wide. Old carvings depicting a gemstone broken into four pieces encircle the pedestal. Atop it sits a blue-green piece of crystal shaped like a star. It reflects light across the top of the pedestal in shimmering flickers, making it seem like the crystal is floating on a shallow bowl of water.*

### Hazard — Swinging Cutlass Traps (3) *(Hazard 5, simple, mechanical)*

There's one trap in each of three marked spaces. Each is a cutlass on a rope in a ceiling slot. A tripwire in the dirt floor releases it (p. 145).
- **Stealth DC 23 (trained)**. It's hard to spot among all the rope.
- **Disable:** **DC 23 Thievery (expert)** to cut the tripwire safely.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +9; **Hardness** 13, **HP** 52 (BT 26); immune to critical hits, object immunities, and precision damage.
- **Cutlass Drop (reaction).** **Trigger:** the tripwire is pulled or cut. **Effect:** a cutlass Strike against each creature in the tripwire's square and every adjacent square. No multiple attack penalty.
  - **Melee** cutlass +19 (forceful), 2d6+9 slashing.
- **Reset:** only by the rope golem's scraps (below).

**Rope Golem:** BB 77, Creature 6. Initiative: **Deception 22** (its Impersonate Rope result, not rolled). Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.
- A rope heap across from the alcove rises to fight **once the PCs have triggered or bypassed the traps**.
- **At 100, 75, and 50 HP**, rope scraps slither off it like snakes. This happens instantly and costs no action:
  - Any PC it has **Tangled Up** automatically Escapes as the scraps leave.
  - The scraps drag a cutlass up into the ceiling ropes, **resetting a trap in a square adjacent to a PC**. They take it from a disabled trap, or from an untriggered one if none are disabled.
  - The PCs know about the reset trap only if they beat its Stealth DC.
  - Cutlasses the PCs **tuck away or break** can't be reset.
- It fights until destroyed and **never leaves the cave**. If the PCs retreat, it resets every trap and stays in golem form.

> **📏 Threat math:** the golem (level 6) is 80 XP. Each level-5 simple hazard is a fifth of 60, so 12 XP, or 36 for three. Total 116, about Severe.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (resets surprise the players):** A reset trap appears next to a PC without warning. Track positions at each threshold. Roll the PCs' Perception against DC 23 before they move.

**Treasure:**
- **The Aquamarine Wedge** (below). Taking it is **completely safe**. PCs may still be paranoid after last time's collapsing mountain. If they stall, you can have the fight jostle it off the pillar so it drops harmlessly. The "water" is a trick of the light.
- A coil of rope near the pedestal is a **moderate rope of climbing** (*Advanced Player's Guide*).
- **If not using Monster Parts:** a small sea chest with **2,000 sp**.

**XP:** **80 XP** for recovering the Aquamarine Wedge.

### Aquamarine Wedge *(Item 7)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** affixed to armor or an item worn around the neck. **Bulk** negligible.
- Shimmering blue and sea-green crystal, once part of a larger whole. One broken edge is deep dark blue. The other is pale green.
- **Invested and affixed** to something worn at the neck (necklace, choker, or armor), it lets the wearer **breathe water as well as air** ([invested items](gm_chapter_04.md#artifacts-and-invested-items)).

> **🧩 Sets up:** Water breathing matters in **Chapter 8**'s search for the cult temple sunk beneath Runaway Reef. There, the book says Garnikor might sell potions of water breathing to the rest of the party.

### If the PCs lose

Poppy ordered the golem to **dump unconscious survivors elsewhere in Briargrove**. She assumed they'd learn their lesson. The PCs can rest and retry with better knowledge. If they fail again, her orders allow no third chance (p. 146).

## Scene 8 — Triumphant Return

Grupple, or another orpok from the hinterlands, saw the PCs leave Briargrove and spread the word. The council throws a **parade and a feast** to reward the PCs and lift the town's spirits (p. 146).
- Banner-waving crowds line the streets. Candy makers, friends of Pumbus, sell **lollipops glazed with the PCs' faces** faster than they can make them.
- Gunlu leads the short parade without stealing the PCs' glory. She all but begs reluctant PCs to join, because Seaview needs this.
- People the PCs helped wave along the route: **Allasha**, **Mupto Burgin**, some **Darduses**.

### The feast

> *An airy plaza at the end of the parade route bears several large tables groaning under the weight of food placed upon them. Orpok cooks are bringing yet more food to these tables, and Sous Scholar Limbin Gumnammar directs them where to place the victuals based on color, flavor, and—most importantly—temperature. Councilor Monnino oversees the workers, who are bringing chairs and place settings to wedge in among all the food, providing enough room for the surrounding crowd to all find places to eat but with place settings of honor at the head of the impressive spread.*

Hundreds attend: Kulupi, the crew, all the councilors, most Academy staff, and more. Pelborii comes if the PCs turned her in. She's haggard but recovering quickly (p. 147).

The Sous Scholar sorts the food into the Academy's three temperature categories. Each PC is expected to try all three main dishes:

| Dish | What it is | Check (pick one) |
|---|---|---|
| **Hamlin's Hots** | Steaming meatballs in bubbling garlic sauce | DC 18 Society or DC 20 Fortitude |
| **Caleb's Colds** | Creamy buttermilk ice cream with frozen berries | DC 18 Medicine or DC 20 Fortitude |
| **Larry's Lukewarms** | Tiny cold-cut sandwiches whose pickles have gone a bit sour | DC 18 Survival or DC 20 Fortitude |

- **Failure:** sickened value rises by 1 for the rest of the feast (2 on a critical failure).
- **Background special effect (Rumplank reveler):** **+2 circumstance bonus** to all three.
- The feast runs late. At the end, the crowd congratulates PCs who aren't sickened. Sickened PCs get sympathetic looks and a few chuckles.

### Loose ends and resources

**Spongeberries:** if Grupple didn't get them, the PCs can give them to the council before or during the feast. Monnino makes a show of presenting them to the Sous Scholar. The Academy's best dessert chefs can then bake a memorable pie for a later feast.

**Resources** (p. 147):
- **Bluebell flowers:** **one large crate per mission completed** (up to 3). The crates go to Kulupi. He publicly congratulates the PCs for earning resources for Rumplank and strengthening ties with Seaview.
- **Culinary supply kit:** from Pumbus, if the council got the spongeberries.
- **Briarthorn:** from Grupple, if he got the spongeberries. Upgrades a spike wall into a **bramble wall** in Chapter 12.
- **Both:** if the council got the berries **and** the PCs took care of Grupple, such as paying his family's 100 gp.

**Kulupi's speech** thanks Seaview for these resources "during such difficult times." But he makes clear he hopes for bigger harvests in future years. The councilors smile politely. **DC 20 Perception to Sense Motive:** they think the prince is being a bit rude.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi accepts the bluebells at the same feast as the councilor whose visions describe his cult's plan. Play a stiff royal, gracious in public but plainly wanting more.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book promises "a few loose ends" but describes only the spongeberries. Two Seaview threads remain open in the text if you want more: Monnino's missing inn owner, and the Darduses' dinner invitation.

**Background special effect (bluebell chef):** the Sous Scholar offers the PC an **honorary professorship**. He says the Academy would be honored if they'd consider becoming the new **Dean de Cuisine** after their adventure.

## Concluding the chapter

The ship sails on the **morning tide**. However late the feast runs, Kulupi expects the PCs and crew to be ready. Orpok dockworkers help load the bluebell crates and other resources (p. 147).

**Milestone:** recovering the Bluebell Island fragment. Leaving Seaview, the PCs **level up to 5** ([milestones](gm_story_synopsis.md#levels-and-milestones)).

> **💡 GM tip:** Short of 1,000 XP? The book suggests a story award for the island, or a few extra encounters between Chapters 5 and 6. An [ocean encounters](#ocean-encounters) roll on the way to Moonshadow is an easy one.
