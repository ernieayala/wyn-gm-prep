## The chapter in one minute

The royal family gives the PCs half of a magic treasure map. Rumors in Rumplank point to the Iron Eels, a pirate gang that had the other half. Their captain tells the PCs two things. The missing half is on a wreck. The keystone that joins the halves is in a swamp. The PCs get both and put the map together. Then it starts to crumble to ash. Cliffhanger.

**By the end the PCs have:**
1. **Both map halves:** the king's (Scene 1) and the Gasping Gipper's (Scene 5).
2. **The [sapphire scale](gm_glossary.md#sapphire-scale)**, the keystone, from a Writhing Swamp thyalodon (Scene 6).
3. **Assembled the map**, triggering its curse (Scene 7).

**Party level:** 1 at the start, **2** at the end.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** It teaches the players the city. It offers the first companions. It plants NPCs who come back later:
> - Colvi (secret villain).
> - U'oko (Chapter 4 test answers).
> - Kuaranda and the thyalodon (Chapter 2).
>
> It also sets up the campaign's big irony. Rumplank remembers Poppy as a fun-loving pirate. In truth she was a war hero hiding a demon cult's relic.

## How the chapter is built

Five book parts (p. 14–45):

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **Getting Started** | Royal audience: quest, Map Half #1 | First |
| **Running Around Rumplank** | Six optional social scenes, each with a clue and bonus reward | Any order, any number |
| **Event Encounters** | Three floating events | Between visits, as pacing needs |
| **Confronting the Iron Eels** | Small dungeon ending with Melar Kan | After enough clues |
| **Missing Pieces** | Two wilderness dungeons: Gasping Gipper, Writhing Swamp | Either order |

The PCs put the map together at whichever dungeon they finish last. That ends the chapter.

> **💡 GM tip (the redundant-clue pattern):** Every road leads to the Iron Eels: D'marti, the Gutted Snake regulars, Nevergo, Master Olo, the Event 1 goons, Gather Information, the Indigo Stranger. That's the **Three Clue Rule**. Plant three or more clues for each thing the players must figure out, because they will miss some. You don't need to run every location. Once they say "let's go find the Iron Eels," move on.

> **💡 GM tip (pacing):** The book sets no minimum number of visits. Two or three locations plus Event 1 usually turn up the lead. Run more if the table enjoys the city. Each visit earns XP.

## PF2 rules you'll use all chapter

Later sections link here instead of repeating these rules.

### Degrees of success

Every check has four outcomes:
- **Critical success:** beat the DC by 10 or more.
- **Success:** meet or beat the DC.
- **Failure:** miss the DC.
- **Critical failure:** miss the DC by 10 or more.

A natural 20 moves the result one step better. A natural 1 moves it one step worse. Many entries list all four outcomes, so read the whole entry before you rule.

### Encounter difficulty and XP

The book labels every fight with a threat level and the party level it's built for, like **Low 1** or **Severe 1**. XP math for four PCs:

| Threat | XP budget | XP each PC earns |
|---|---|---|
| Trivial | 40 or less | 40 or less |
| Low | 60 | 60 |
| Moderate | 80 | 80 |
| Severe | 120 | 120 |
| Extreme | 160 | 160 |

Creature cost by level relative to the party:

| Creature level vs. party | −2 | −1 | same | +1 | +2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XP | 20 | 30 | 40 | 60 | 80 |

Hazards:
- A **complex** hazard costs the same as a creature of its level.
- A **simple** hazard costs a fifth of that (8 XP at party level).

**Worked examples (party level 1):**
- **Event 1:** two goons (level 0, 30 XP each) = 60 = **Low**.
- **C1 Snake Pit:** leshy husk (30) + snake pit, complex level-2 hazard (60) = 90 = **Moderate**.
- **B7 Haunted Hold:** wispy wayfarer (level 2, 60) + three rotten planks (simple level-1, 8 each) = 84 = **Moderate**.

**PCs level up at 1,000 XP.** Then the counter resets. The book says the listed encounters give about the right XP for each level-up. Milestone leveling works too (p. 7). This chapter's milestone is putting the map together.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Two fights break the math on purpose. Use the book's label for both.
> - **Event 4** is one level-1 duelist, so it's Trivial on paper. The book rates it Moderate (80 XP) because of the duel conditions.
> - **C6** adds up to more than Severe. The book keeps it at Severe because the confused thyalodon may attack the husks.

> **📏 Party size:** Budgets assume four PCs. For each PC added or removed, adjust the budget by 20 XP for Moderate (10 Trivial, 15 Low, 30 Severe, 40 Extreme). Each PC still earns the four-PC amount for that threat level.

### Basic saving throws

"DC 15 basic Reflex" is shorthand for:
- **Critical success:** no damage.
- **Success:** half damage.
- **Failure:** full damage.
- **Critical failure:** double damage.

### Secret checks and Recall Knowledge

**Recall Knowledge** lets a PC remember a fact. It usually uses Lore or a knowledge skill like Society or Religion. It's a **secret check**: you roll it behind the screen. On a failure you say "you don't know." On a critical failure you tell them something false. The player can't tell the two apart. The book writes out the false answers for you, like Scene 1's "Redfeathers are demon worshippers."

### Flat checks

A plain d20 with no modifiers. **DC 11 = 50%.** Nevergo's flasks use one.

### Conditions you'll see this chapter

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Flat-footed** | −2 circumstance penalty to AC. The PF2 Remaster calls it **off-guard**. Same rule. |
| **Prone** | Flat-footed, and −2 circumstance penalty to attack rolls. Standing up takes an action. |
| **Clumsy X** | −X status penalty to Dexterity-based checks and DCs, including AC and Reflex. |
| **Dazzled** | Everything is concealed to you. You need a DC 5 flat check to target anything. |
| **Frightened X** | −X status penalty to all checks and DCs. Drops by 1 at the end of each of your turns. |
| **Sickened X** | −X status penalty to all checks and DCs. You can Retch (1 action) for a Fortitude save to reduce it. |
| **Unconscious / dying** | At 0 HP: unconscious and dying 1 (dying 2 on a critical hit). **Nonlethal** damage knocks a creature out at 0 HP without dying. |

### Movement and terrain

- **Difficult terrain:** +5 feet of movement per square.
- **Greater difficult terrain:** +10 feet per square.
- **Swim / Climb:** Athletics vs. the listed DC. Failure: no progress. Critical failure on a Climb: a fall.
- **Falling:** bludgeoning damage equal to half the distance. A 20-foot fall deals 10.
- **Grab an Edge:** a reaction when you fall past an edge. Roll Reflex vs. the listed DC to catch on.
- **Cover:** standard cover +2 AC, **greater cover** +4.
- **Overland travel:** when the slowest Speed is 20, the party covers about 2 miles per hour on clear ground, 1 on difficult ground (p. 37). See the [travel distance table](#travel-from-rumplank).

### Hazards

- **Simple hazard:** one triggered reaction, then it's done (the Gipper's rotten planks).
- **Complex hazard:** rolls initiative and acts each round on a **routine** (the snake pit).
- **Stealth DC 20 (expert):** only expert Perception notices it. A party with only trained Perception steps on it.
- **Disable DC 15 Crafting (trained):** the check to make it safe, and the lowest rank that can try.

### Monster Parts (treasure system)

The campaign uses the *Battlezoo Bestiary*'s **Monster Parts**, **hybrid** variant by default (p. 7). PCs harvest parts from defeated creatures. They spend parts to **refine** an item (raising its level and item bonuses) or to **imbue** it with magic properties.

- **"Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp"**: values for the light/hybrid/full variants.
- **"+1 dagger (2)"**: refined to item level 2, +1 to attack rolls.
- **"If your group isn't using Monster Parts"**: replacement treasure to even out gold. Use it only if you skip the system.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Event 1 is likely the first fight. If you use Monster Parts, remind players to harvest the goons.

### NPC companions

Some NPCs can join the party (p. 8). Book defaults:
- They tag along, but they **don't fight or join major events** unless the text says so.
- **Two at a time, maximum**, for four players. Extras wait somewhere safe, like Rumplank.
- Mistreated companions leave.

Chapter 1 offers three: [D'marti](gm_glossary.md#dmarti-tixima) (Scene 2A), the [scroll mold](gm_glossary.md#scroll-mold) (Scene 5), and [Ignatius Barkley](gm_glossary.md#ignatius-barkley) (Scene 6).

## Scene 1 — Castle Rumplank (the hook)

- **Where:** the royal hall, [Castle Rumplank](gm_glossary.md#castle-rumplank).
- **Who:** [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather), [Queen Maxelle](gm_glossary.md#queen-maxelle-redfeather), [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi), [Princess Atriella](gm_glossary.md#princess-atriella).
- **Goal:** the PCs accept the quest and receive **Map Half #1**.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It brings the party together and points it at the city. In the book, Prince Kulupi picked the PCs from many applicants.

> **💡 GM tip (the hook):** "The king asked" may not be enough reason. The book suggests asking each player, "Why did your character answer the call?" Then the players own the reason (p. 7). Let them introduce their characters before the read-aloud.

### Read aloud (opening)

> *You find yourselves standing atop a wide swath of velvet carpet that cuts through the center of Castle Rumplank's magnificent royal hall. Nearby, the sweet smell of [Barnacle Bay](gm_glossary.md#barnacle-bay) wafts through the open glass windows. Before you stands a family of four beautiful [g'mayuns](gm_glossary.md#gmayun) in spectacular regal garb, flanked all around by countless royal guards. King Okeki — the family's dignified but slightly distracted-looking patriarch — eyes each of you carefully, then clears his throat.*

The king's speech (p. 15), paraphrased:
- A magical map to **Poppy von Barnacle's hideout** is the talk of the town.
- Royal scholars found a piece of it has hung in this hall for generations.
- Find the other half, follow it to Poppy's sanctum, and claim her legacy for Rumplank.

### Likely questions

- **Why?** History and civic pride. A royal envoy will later collect relics the PCs can't carry.
- **How much treasure do we keep?** Queen Maxelle: *"Why, all of it, dears!"* Rumplank wants glory and a better reputation.
- **How does the other half help?** Princess Atriella: court wizards found powerful magic on the parchment. It will make sense only once the halves are joined and repaired, maybe with a keystone or other magic item.
- **Where to start?** Prince Kulupi suggests the [Temple of Many Colors](gm_glossary.md#temple-of-many-colors), [Quill & Scroll Academy](gm_glossary.md#quill--scroll-academy), or [Treasure Beach](gm_glossary.md#treasure-beach). After a pause, he adds the [Gutted Snake](gm_glossary.md#gutted-snake) in the Claw Quarter, full of rumors. Atriella recommends [Prismatic Colvi](gm_glossary.md#prismatic-colvi) at the temple.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi is a cult mastermind (revealed in Ch 11). He steers the PCs to the Gutted Snake, the best lead in town. His sister steers them to Colvi, a cultist. Play both as sincere. In Act 3, players looking back will spot it.

### Recall Knowledge (secret check)

After the audience, roll a **secret DC 15 Society or Rumplank Lore** check per PC ([how secret checks work](#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge)).

- **Critical success:** Success, plus: Poppy built Rumplank into a thriving city, then retired from public life. Her fate is unknown. Making up myths about her is a local pastime.
- **Success:** Rumplank is a lively port on the sunny side of Goldcrop Island. It's known for rowdy citizens and parties all year. The pirate Poppy von Barnacle founded it over 200 years ago.
- **Failure:** Nothing specific.
- **Critical failure:** A confident lie. Under all the parties, Rumplank is a deadly port of pirates, cultists, and ghosts. The Redfeathers are secret demon worshippers who serve a false god, Poppy.

> **🧩 Sets up:** This lie is funny because part of it is true. A demon cult works in Rumplank, and part of the royal family serves it.

### Hand over the map

The king hands it over with care: *"Be careful with that map; you hold the history of Rumplank in your hands!"*

- A **crude sketch of [Goldcrop Island](gm_glossary.md#goldcrop-island)**. Its edge is so frayed that only a close look shows the tear.
- **10 HP**. *Mending* repairs it.
- A permanent **6th-level *nondetection*** hid it in plain sight.

## Scene 2 — Running around Rumplank

Six locations, in any order. Each is a short social scene with a **clue**. Each also has a **bonus reward** for impressing the NPC or passing a test (p. 17).

**XP per location, per PC:** **10 XP** for talking, **30 XP** with the bonus reward.

**Event timing (book suggestions):**
- **[Event 1 — Iron Fists](#event-1--iron-fists-low-1)** after the first or second location.
- **[Event 2 — Glitterfest Parade](#event-2--glitterfest-parade-trivial-1)** right before or after the Academy.
- **[Event 3 — The Indigo Stranger](#event-3--the-indigo-stranger-no-combat)** only if they're stuck.

> **💡 GM tip:** A PC from Rumplank (say, with a Player's Guide background) may want to visit a spot that isn't listed. Move a location's key details there (p. 17). The gazetteer (p. 342) has a city map for players.

> **📏 Gather Information:** At any time, a PC can learn about the Iron Eels two ways:
> - Spend **2 hours** and roll **DC 15 Diplomacy** to Gather Information.
> - Recall Knowledge with **DC 15 Rumplank or Sailing Lore** or **DC 20 Society**.
>
> Results: [What the city knows about the Iron Eels](#what-the-city-knows-about-the-iron-eels).

### 2A. The Gutted Snake (worst bar in town)

Sticky floors, sour grog, bad tempers, best gossip. Barkeep: [Molo "Make-Sick" Dogodor](gm_glossary.md#molo-make-sick-dogodor).

**NPC:** [D'marti Tixima](gm_glossary.md#dmarti-tixima) (LN genderfluid g'mayun ranger 1, they/them). A cheerful, scatterbrained jungle explorer with a huge, overstuffed backpack.

**Goal:** the PCs learn the **[Iron Eels](gm_glossary.md#iron-eels)** likely stole the other half.

**What D'marti says** (once the PCs mention Poppy or the map):
- Poppy legends: she dueled the amber dragon Helexes. She steered the *Chromatic Queen* through a hurricane to win the Nevala Regatta. Her comrades were the [Fortunate Four](gm_glossary.md#fortunate-four).
- About a week ago, on a jungle expedition, they found the other half. It was in an **ancient cherry-wood scroll case** poking out of the ground. Where? No idea. They were lost, as usual.
- They showed it off here, far too loudly, and someone stole it. Whispered: *"Between us, I think it was some of Melar Kan's goons."*
- The map is likely cursed. If so, go to **[Jakapo Town](gm_glossary.md#jakapo-town)**. It's the home of Poppy's wizard friend [Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red).

**Leaning on the barkeep:** **DC 17 Intimidation**. Dogodor saw high-ranking Iron Eel **[Squotan Malardy](gm_glossary.md#squotan-malardy)** take the map from D'marti's bags. Nobody has seen Malardy since. Another patron tells the same story on a **DC 15 or 17** check in a skill you pick.

**Bonus reward (pack D'marti's backpack):** D'marti can't make it all fit.
- **DC 15 Athletics** to force it, or **DC 17 Survival** to repack it.
- Reward: pick **two**: disguise kit, repair kit, climbing kit, firearm cleaning kit, healer's tools, snare kit.

**D'marti as a companion:** if the PCs pack the bag and make a good impression, D'marti offers to join. They know Goldcrop Island's plants and animals well, and the PCs are outsiders.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The cherry scroll case shows up again on the Gasping Gipper (Scene 5). Scroll mold got into it, and it "walked away" from the Iron Eels. Let PCs who compare notes connect the two.

### 2B. Nevergo's Antidotes (back-alley apothecary)

**NPC:** [Captain Nevergo](gm_glossary.md#captain-nevergo). A grumpy Claw Quarter g'mayun whose pirate drawl is hard to understand. By day he runs a messy, half-empty shop. By night he sells poison out the back door.

**Goal:** a backup route to D'marti. Show him the map and he grumbles that D'marti showed him one just like it a few days ago. He says to try the Gutted Snake.

> **💡 GM tip:** If the PCs already met D'marti, he names other gangs who heard D'marti bragging that night: Uta's Hard Girls, the Red-Bellied Three, Yellow Noxious. *"coulda been them Iron Eels for all I know."*

**Bonus reward (sort his flasks):** he mixed up the wormwood and the belladonna.
- **DC 17 Crafting** to Identify Alchemy by taste. A **DC 11 flat check** ([flat checks](#flat-checks)) decides which flask the PC tastes. On a failed flat check it's belladonna: **DC 19 Fortitude save** vs. the poison.
- **Safer option:** a PC who watched him can recall which corner each flask came from with **DC 20 Perception**.
- **Reward:** the **belladonna** flask (CRB poison). *"Ye gotcher spittle on it, 'sides."*

### 2C. Pekipak's Wagon (the [Twin Talons](gm_glossary.md#twin-talons))

**NPC:** [Sagoya Pekipak](gm_glossary.md#sagoya-pekipak). A g'mayun rat catcher who wants to be an inventor. A tiny leather helmet lets him talk to rodents. He eats the smart ones.

**The Rodential Respirator ("R.R.")**: a vacuum hose on a leather sack. The sack holds an air elemental gem and a fan feather token. His left hand is in a splint from the last test. He offers a gold coin to anyone who covers the nozzle.
- **DC 15 Fortitude.** Success: the hand stays put, not pulled in. Failure: **1d6+2 nonlethal bludgeoning**. Critical failure: also **clumsy 1** until a full night's rest.
- He pays the coin no matter what.

**What he tells them:** blood mice swarm in the jungle. He's heard music can stir up vermin: *"bigger the swarm, bigger the ditty."*

> **🧩 Sets up:** This hints at Chapter 2's **Hissing Chasm** (Landmark 2, p. 50). There, the PCs need a specific magical instrument to clear praying-mantis swarms. Pekipak is half right: most songs do nothing.

**Bonus reward (improve the device):**
- **DC 15 Crafting** or **DC 13 Engineering Lore** to make it safe.
- Critical success: it needs only the gem now, so he gives the PCs the **fan feather token**.

### 2D. [Temple of Many Colors](gm_glossary.md#temple-of-many-colors)

**NPC:** [Prismatic Colvi](gm_glossary.md#prismatic-colvi), high priest. Gracious, respected, and vain.

**Goal:** the PCs learn the map needs a **keystone**. They also learn it will **self-destruct** once it's whole.

**What Colvi says** after studying the map for a few minutes:
- A specific magical key joins the halves. He doesn't know what it looks like. It's likely with the missing half.
- Once whole, the map carries **disintegrating magic**. Read it fast.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Colvi is a demon worshipper. He's a powerful member of the [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth), which Poppy nearly destroyed 200 years ago. He's a major Act 3 villain (p. 218 on). The PCs can't expose him yet, so don't drop hints. **Note how each PC treats him.** The book suggests tying those moments into his final fight. His Chapter 12 dialogue depends on them.

**Bonus reward (name three lava-rock statues):** acolytes haul three rough statues up from the catacombs. Which gods are they?
- Any PC **trained in Religion** can roll **DC 18 Religion** to Recall Knowledge, **once per statue**.
- **Winged** (chochori-sized, sharp-tipped wings): demon lord [Pazuzu](gm_glossary.md#pazuzu).
- **G'mayun on three legs** (one may be a cane): the [Sibling of the Past](gm_glossary.md#sibling-of-the-past).
- **Orpok-sized, big belly:** [Ebrugeses](gm_glossary.md#ebrugeses), god of good food.
- **Name two or more:** **four common bludgeoning weapons** of the PCs' choice plus **one +1 weapon (2)** (any common CRB weapon they choose). Colvi warns them that many undead roam the island.

> **🔒 GM-only (the one tell):** Colvi knows the winged statue is Pazuzu, but he pretends not to. If a PC names it, he has "the cursed thing" taken back downstairs. That's the only crack in his act. Play it as calm faith.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Bludgeoning works well against the Gasping Gipper's skeletons. They resist piercing and slashing.

### 2E. Master Olo's Beach Fighting Camp

**NPC:** [Master Olo](gm_glossary.md#master-olo). A squat old orpok martial-arts master in white monk robes (*lami*). He drills three bright blue [chochori](gm_glossary.md#chochori) students from sunrise to sunset, on any Rumplank beach he likes.

**Goal:** another pointer to the Iron Eels. A few days ago, an Iron Eel ran across the beach at Ironsalt Cove. He was clutching a map like the PCs' map. Olo landed *"a warning blow."* A gang patch marked the runner as an Iron Eel. Olo guesses he was running between two points in the Claw Quarter.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The runner was Squotan Malardy. He was taking the stolen half from the Gutted Snake to the hideout.

**Optional fight (two Flying Pig Disciples):** the PCs spar with two students. Saying no costs nothing.
- **Stats:** weak chochori wardens (BB 9, p. 386), Creature 1.
- Fists only (**nonlethal**), no tridents. The students are at a disadvantage on land.

**Bonus reward (beat both with no PC knocked unconscious):** Olo offers to teach **Flying Pig Stance** and **Flying Pig Rush** (p. 378). A PC can **retrain** a feat into Flying Pig Stance on the spot.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Two level-1 foes vs. a level-1 party = 80 XP, Moderate. That's a tough first fight, even nonlethal.

### 2F. Quill & Scroll Academy

**NPC:** [Headmistress U'oko](gm_glossary.md#headmistress-uoko). A busy old g'mayun scholar whose door is always open. She's stuck on a petrified-wood puzzle cube, a gift from Professor A'aran. Students and alumni get more of her time.

**Goal:** lore on Poppy's companions, the [Fortunate Four](gm_glossary.md#fortunate-four). She also confirms that the map has self-destructive magic, as Colvi said.

**What she says about each companion:**
- **[Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red)** ("Shellfire"): an evoker with no equal. She was the only crew member born on Goldcrop, in the galtzagorri mushroom village of Jakapo. She was Poppy's chief mapmaker, and U'oko is sure she made this map. Joke: the only way to beat her was to **dress her in blue**, her least favorite color.
- **[Jesea Swiftarrow](gm_glossary.md#jesea-swiftarrow)** ("the Rugged Ranger"): a hardriggan archer no one could beat. She had an **X-shaped scar on her jawline, below her left ear**. She never told how she got it.
- **[Pinomeri Elemarte](gm_glossary.md#pinomeri-elemarte)** ("the Drifting Cleric"): Poppy's faithful indigo dragon. He worshipped Ebrugeses and rarely turned down a drink. *"Rum, wine, ale. He loved them all in equal measure."*
- **[Undayo of Stonegulch](gm_glossary.md#undayo-of-stonegulch)** ("Old Rock"): a kragrak barbarian who respected only raw strength. To earn his trust, you had to **beat him one-on-one**.

> **🧩 Sets up:** These four facts answer Poppy's final test at **G25 in Chapter 4** (p. 105). Write them down, or hand the players a note.

**Bonus reward (solve the puzzle cube):**
- A PC **trained in Thievery** can roll **DC 15 Thievery** to Disable a Device. The cube opens, and U'oko happily eats the luluki nut inside.
- **Reward:** three 1st-level scrolls: ***detect poison***, ***jump***, and ***mending***.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Save the *mending* scroll. On the assembled map (Scene 7) it restores no HP. Instead it stops the decay for hours equal to the HP it would restore. That's about 5 hours for a typical cast (Chapter 2, p. 48).

> **Trigger:** Consider running **[Event 2 — Glitterfest Parade](#event-2--glitterfest-parade-trivial-1)** as they leave.

## Scene 3 — Street events

Run these between locations, in order, wherever the pacing needs them (p. 25).

### Event 1 — Iron Fists *(Low 1)*

**When:** after the PCs ask about the map at one or two locations. It works best in the Claw Quarter.

Melar Kan sends his two lowest-ranking g'mayun pirates, **Crackmaw** and **Litzi**, to learn what the PCs know. If the goons learn the PCs carry the other half, they fight for it.
- **Iron Eel Goons (2):** BB 34, Creature 0. Initiative: Intimidation +5.
- **Morale:** a goon at **5 HP or less** flees down an alley. When one drops or flees, the other runs.
- **Treasure:** Monster Parts for refining slashing or piercing weapons, 2.25/5/9 gp per goon ([what the numbers mean](#monster-parts-treasure-system)).

**If questioned:** they won't reveal the boss's plans without magic. They do admit they're after Poppy's treasure. Crackmaw taunts the PCs and dares them to meet the boss at the **hideout near [Ironsalt Cove](gm_glossary.md#ironsalt-cove)**.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It's likely the first fight. It's a safe Low encounter where players learn the three-action turn. It's also a backup lead to the Iron Eels.

> **💡 GM tip:** If the PCs take too long, the book suggests sending more goons to bully them until they head for the hideout.

### Event 2 — [Glitterfest](gm_glossary.md#glitterfest) Parade *(Trivial 1)*

**When:** right before or after the Academy. Parades stay near Barrel Square and Rosewater. If your table wants a fight, run it before Master Olo or Event 1.

Read aloud:

> *Bits of confetti and glitter waft on the breeze, sticking to your clothes and clouding your vision, before the merrymakers responsible for the mess appear from around a corner. Dozens of Rumplankers dance, sing, and laugh as they march down the street, oblivious to their surroundings. It's impossible not to get swept up in the revelers' good cheer — or their quickening pace.*

**Merry Marchers:** each PC either holds their ground or keeps up.
- **Hold ground:** DC 17 Fortitude or Athletics.
- **Keep up:** DC 17 Reflex or Performance.
- **DC 15 Party Lore** can replace either.
- **Failure:** 1d4 nonlethal bludgeoning, and glitter on their clothes.
- **Critical failure:** also glitter in the face. The PC is **dazzled** for 1 minute or until an Interact action cleans it off.

**Then the toogtoog spill.** The parade knocks over an old merchant's cart. Dozens of hungry [toogtoogs](gm_glossary.md#toogtoog) hop toward the glitter.
- **Toogtoog Spill:** p. 403, Creature 1. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp.
- They swarm PCs covered in glitter. If no PC has glitter, they swarm an innocent reveler.
- **Peaceful option:** if the PCs calm them with no kills (Wild Empathy, say), the merchant pays them the Monster Parts value.

> **📏 Rules refresher (Wild Empathy):** a druid and ranger feature. It lets you use Diplomacy to Make an Impression on animals.

### Event 3 — [The Indigo Stranger](gm_glossary.md#indigo-stranger) *(no combat)*

Run this **only if the PCs are stuck** finding the hideout.

> **🔒 GM-only:** This is **[X'na'zed](gm_glossary.md#xnazed)**, an [indigo dragon](gm_glossary.md#indigo-dragon) (he/him). Magic disguises him as a g'mayun woman. He loves Rumplank and thinks Poppy's treasure will help it. He doesn't know he's helping the cult.

Read or paraphrase:

> **💡 GM tip:** He can come back in any disguise when players are stuck. He always has **indigo eyes**. Each use makes the story feel more forced, so use him rarely.

## Scene 4 — [Iron Eels'](gm_glossary.md#iron-eels) Hideout

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It's the first real dungeon, and its rooms are linked. Fleeing goons raise alarms, and noise draws in the next room. It ends in a social choice (duel or brawl). Either way, the PCs learn what sends them into the wilderness.

### What the city knows about the Iron Eels

The check is **DC 15 Diplomacy** (Gather Information), or **DC 15 Rumplank or Sailing Lore** or **DC 20 Society** (Recall Knowledge) (p. 27).
- **Critical success:** Success, plus: their base is an **abandoned rum distillery** in the Claw Quarter. The gang is named for Melar Kan's weapon: a giant eel skeleton cast in iron, on an iron chain.
- **Success:** They're one of Rumplank's more successful pirate gangs, and one of the nastiest. Melar Kan is cruel and calculating. He encourages petty crime on shore. They're often at sea on their white sloop, the ***[Sharpened Bone](gm_glossary.md#sharpened-bone)***.
- **Failure:** Nothing worth noting.
- **Critical failure:** They're secret assassins for the beloved bartender Clamardinia Wakamarsis. They hide in the catacombs under Quill & Scroll Academy. Melar Kan is a devil in disguise.

### Questioning defeated Iron Eels

Each pirate the PCs question gives **one** of these:
- First Mate Squotan Malardy vanished in the Writhing Swamp not long ago with *"some really important dragon scale."* The gang also took a loss at the Gasping Gipper. Melar Kan is furious.
- Melar Kan likes bloodshed, but he loves gambling and deals more. He rarely plays fair and uses loopholes.
- Most of the crew wants to sail. Settle things soon, or the gang may be gone for months.
- With Malardy gone, the chef **Clancy** is Melar Kan's favorite.
- Melar Kan heard the Redfeathers gave the other half to adventurers. He sent goons to scout them.

### The building

An abandoned distillery off the shore of Ironsalt Cove, near Rum Row. It has a 10-foot moat and walls patched with wood from wrecked hulls. A pine mast with a crow's nest flies the eel-skull flag.

**Hideout features:**
- Crumbling stone walls outside. Wood-slat walls and floors inside.
- Ceilings barely 8 feet high.
- **All doors unlocked.**
- Dim light through windows and cracks.

### A5 — Clancy's magical gumbo

Clancy is Melar Kan's lifelong friend. He's as handy with a knife on a gangplank as in the kitchen. If the PCs catch him in the galley, he claims to be a simple cook. Then he throws the pot.
- **Stats:** LE male orpok olfamancer, p. 390, Creature 1.
- **Hurled gumbo:** 15-foot cone, **1d4 fire**, **DC 15 basic Reflex** ([basic saves](#basic-saving-throws)). Any creature that fails or critically fails is also exposed to the gumbo's magic.
- **Magical gumbo:** **DC 15 Fortitude** (1st-level transmutation). Drinking a lot on purpose is an automatic critical failure.
  - **Failure:** stone fists for **1 hour**. The creature is **clumsy 1**. Its fist loses the nonlethal trait and rolls **2 damage dice** if it rolled 1.
  - **Critical failure:** same, for **24 hours**.
- **Treasure:** the greenish lump on the cutting board. It's calcite (1 Bulk, near worthless) coated in **yaramite**. A collector pays **10 gp** for it.

> **💡 GM tip:** A monk or unarmed fighter may drink on purpose. They trade clumsy 1 for two fist dice for 24 hours. It's a fair trade, so allow it.

### A7 — Riupito and Squints

- **Riupito:** female g'mayun pirate, BB 34, Creature 0.
- **Squints:** blood mouse, BB 14, Creature 1. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp.
- Unless alarmed, Riupito is too busy teaching Squints tricks to notice much. Stealth vs. her **Perception DC 16** sneaks past.

### A9 — Malardy's old room and the Living Divination Deck

After Malardy left, the crew took turns in his bed and painted on the walls. "Duko" is scrawled everywhere. There are rude cartoons of the Redfeathers. There are also stunning murals of Melar Kan fighting a giant squid and a skeleton horde.
- **DC 20 Perception** or **DC 15 Art Lore**: the matching paint shows that Duko, who can't read, did the scrawl and the murals. Clancy drew the cartoons.
- **Living Divination Deck** (BB 56, Creature 1): in a fine leather case under the mattress. It pretends to be cards, then attacks a moment later. It's impatient like its old owner.
- **Optional:** it's moody, and as likely to talk as fight. If befriended, it gives a card worth its normal Monster Parts value.
- **XP:** **30 XP** story award either way (a lone Trivial encounter gives none).

### A11 → A14 — [Melar Kan](gm_glossary.md#melar-kan)

A well-read scoundrel. He spends evenings on philosophy and tactics, but keeps only the brutal lessons. His brandy decanter is always full, and his hidden dagger is always sharp.

**Melar Kan:** Creature 1, unique LE Small g'mayun humanoid (pirate captain), p. 32
- **Perception** +8; low-light vision, truecolor sight
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +5, Athletics +7, Deception +6, Intimidation +6, Thievery +7
- **AC** 16; **Fort** +10, **Ref** +7, **Will** +4; **HP** 20; **Speed** 25 ft
- **Melee** fist +7 (agile, nonlethal), 1d4+4 bludgeoning
- **Melee** *+1 dagger* +8 (agile, finesse, magical, versatile S), 1d4+4 piercing
- **Melee** spiked chain +7 (disarm, finesse, trip), 1d8+4 slashing
- **Ranged** *+1 dagger* +8 (magical, thrown 10 ft), 1d4+4 piercing
- **Kick 'Em While They're Down** (free action, once per round). **Requirement:** his last action was a successful Trip against an adjacent creature. **Effect:** he makes a fist Strike against the tripped creature. It counts toward his multiple attack penalty.

> **📏 Rules refresher (his combo):** He Trips (1 action) with the chain. On a success, the target is **prone** and flat-footed ([conditions](#conditions-youll-see-this-chapter)). Then Kick 'Em adds a free fist Strike at a −4 multiple attack penalty. (Trip has the attack trait, and the fist is agile.) He still has two actions left. Expect a trip-and-stomp.

**How he reacts:**
- **PCs storm in:** he fights and calls his crew. If the PCs cleared the lower floors, only the two A12 goons come.
- **Surrender:** once his minions are down **and** he's at **10 HP or less**, he lays down his weapons.
- **Careful approach, or he hears them coming:** he spreads his hands. *"Come now. Let's sort this out like sensible people, alright?"* He proposes a duel under "the pirate's code." If they refuse or stall, he tsks and attacks with his goons.

> **🎯 Why he offers a duel:** He wants the PCs alive. With their half, and their work finding the rest, Poppy's treasure is his. A party that beat his crew downstairs would win a fair fight. So a rigged duel is his best bet.

### Event 4 — Duel atop the Crow's Nest *(Moderate 1, 80 XP)*

**Terms**, explained at the base of the mast:
- One PC duels Melar Kan **"to the grim."** That means until one duelist surrenders and begs for mercy. *"If you should fall dead before you're able to surrender, well... you should have spoken up earlier."*
- **Hand-to-hand.** Weapons go in a chest at the base. The duel starts on a count of three at the top.
- **No one else may come up** to the nest.
- **PC wins:** he reveals where the missing half and the keystone are.
- **Melar Kan wins:** the PCs hand over their maps and gear and serve the Iron Eels for **two months**.

**The hidden knife:**
- A ***+1 dagger (2)*** (mother-of-pearl handle, serrated blade) is stuck point-first under the crow's nest.
- **DC 18 Perception** to notice it. **DC 23 Perception** from the rooftop.
- A climbing PC can palm it with **DC 18 Thievery**. If no PC takes it, **Melar Kan palms it** on his way up.
- Without it, he has only his fists.

**All's fair below the nest:** goons throw insults and rocks at his opponent.
- The nest gives **greater cover** (+4 AC) against attacks from below, so rocks almost never land.
- Each goon tries to **Demoralize** (Intimidation +5) the duelist.
- Melar Kan points out that this is legal: they stay below the nest.
- There are up to six goons, depending on earlier losses. Two (from the balcony) is most likely.

> **📏 Rules refresher (Demoralize):** Intimidation vs. Will DC. Success: frightened 1. Critical success: frightened 2 ([conditions](#conditions-youll-see-this-chapter)). The target is then immune to that goon's Demoralize for 10 minutes.

> **💡 GM tip (the loophole goes both ways):** The rules don't stop other PCs from attacking the goons below, or Melar Kan, from off the nest. Reward it. It's a pirate's code.

**Running the duel:** use normal combat or the *Pathfinder Gamemastery Guide* dueling subsystem. The book says he's much deadlier here than in a normal fight, even without cheating. That's why one level-1 foe counts as Moderate.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (don't kill Melar Kan by accident):** The book says at 0 HP he falls unconscious and starts dying as normal. That way he lives to talk, and maybe to come back as a villain. If the PCs kill him anyway, **his journal in A11** has everything below.

**Background special effect:** an **indigo pirate** PC who takes the challenge and wins fast earns a name among pirates. A few surviving goons offer to crew for that PC if they get a ship. The *Gamemastery Guide*'s Leadership rules can track this.

### A Deal's a Deal — what Melar Kan reveals

He keeps his word, duel or brawl. His journal can give the PCs this instead.

1. **The map half is on the [Gasping Gipper](gm_glossary.md#gasping-gipper)**, a wreck beached on Goldcrop's southern coast. The undead rumors were true. He lost recruits and the map there (*"it walked away, believe it or not"*). But he found what he came for: the sapphire scale.
2. **The [sapphire scale](gm_glossary.md#sapphire-scale) is the keystone.** It's somewhere around the [Writhing Swamp](gm_glossary.md#writhing-swamp). On the long walk home from the Gipper, through *"that nasty bog to the southeast,"* **Squotan Malardy** stole it. He has surely sold it. The only buyers out there are the galtzagorris of [Jakapo Town](gm_glossary.md#jakapo-town).
3. **The map is likely cursed.** Poppy always tested her students. The curse likely triggers when the map is put back together. He says *"you'd have to find the map's maker — or someone like her — to undo such a curse."*

**XP:** **30 XP** story award per PC, plus combat XP.

> **🔒 GM-only (what happened):**
> - Poppy shipped the sapphire scale on the Gipper 200 years ago. The ship wrecked, and the scale sat aboard for centuries.
> - Old shipping records led D'marti to guess it was still there.
> - Malardy overheard. He stole D'marti's map half for Melar Kan, and the Eels raided the wreck.
> - They got the scale. But scroll mold got into the case holding their half, and the case wandered off into the ship.
> - Then Malardy stole the scale and sold it to a passing galtzagorri herder. Melar Kan was left with nothing.

### If the PCs lose

They wake the next morning in **A8**, bandaged and battered. They mop floors to earn supper. Melar Kan has no use for them dead. Ways out:
- They overhear Melar Kan planning his return to the Gasping Gipper.
- They win over the unhappy crew and start a mutiny.
- **Last resort:** the Indigo Stranger or Prince Kulupi rescues them.

## Scene 5 — The [Gasping Gipper](gm_glossary.md#gasping-gipper)

- **Travel:** 6 hours from Rumplank.
- **Goal:** get **Map Half #2** from the **scroll mold** in the cargo deck.

A g'mayun pirate caravel. A freak storm wrecked it on the rocks about 200 years ago. Most of the crew swam ashore. The captain stayed aboard through the crash and now haunts the ship.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** A haunted-ship crawl with a twist: the map half is (sort of) alive, and it runs. It also introduces a theme: Poppy's magic leaves pieces of people behind.

### Ship features

- **Doors:** thin rotten wood (Hardness 5, HP 20, BT 10).
- **Lower deck (B4–B7):** 6-foot ceilings. Taller creatures duck but aren't slowed. Dim light comes through cracks in the hull.
- **Below the waterline:** **greater difficult terrain** ([terrain rules](#movement-and-terrain)). A PC with a swim Speed, or who is confident in water, can Swim instead (**DC 12 Athletics**, because of the tides). In B5, the 15-foot-deep **port side** is swim-only.

### Hazard — Rotten Planks *(Hazard 1, simple)*

Two in B2 and three in B7, on the map's dotted squares (p. 36).
- **Stealth DC 20 (expert)**: a PC needs **expert Perception** to spot one.
- **Disable:** **DC 15** Crafting or Thievery, **trained**, to brace the plank.
- **AC** 16; **Fort** +5, **Ref** +10; Hardness 3, HP 16 (BT 8); weakness fire 3.
- **Break Plank (reaction).** **Trigger:** a Small or larger creature steps on the square. **Effect:** **2d6+5 slashing**, **DC 17 basic Reflex**. Failure: flat-footed until the start of its next turn. Critical failure: **prone** instead. Either way, the square becomes difficult terrain.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Most level-1 parties will step on these. The book designed it that way. The damage is high for level 1, so watch casters with low HP.

### B1 — Rocky Shoreline

No encounter. The PCs can see skeletons shuffling on deck, and they get a sense of the layout.

### B2 — Upper Deck *(Trivial 1)*

**Skeletal Iron Eels (2):** Creature −1, NE Small mindless skeleton undead, p. 38. The wispy wayfarer killed these Iron Eels and raised them as its minions.
- **Perception** +4; darkvision. **Skills:** Acrobatics +5, Athletics +4.
- **AC** 15; **Fort** +3, **Ref** +8, **Will** +3; **HP** 5 (negative healing)
- **Immunities:** death effects, disease, mental, paralyzed, poison, unconscious. **Resistances:** cold, electricity, fire, piercing, and slashing 5.
- **Speed** 25 ft. **Melee** talon +6 (agile), 1d4+2 slashing plus **bone ticks**.
- **Bone Ticks** (disease), **DC 14 Fortitude**. Stage 1: carrier, no effect (1d4 hours). Stage 2: clumsy 1 (1 day). Stage 3: sickened 1 and clumsy 1 (1 day). Stage 4: sickened 1 and clumsy 2 (1 day). Sickened doesn't fade on its own until the disease is cured.

> **📏 Rules refresher (resistance):** "Resistance slashing 5" subtracts 5 from each slashing hit. Piercing is resisted too. So most level-1 slashing and piercing hits deal only a point or two. **Bludgeoning isn't resisted.** That's why Colvi's maces and the B4 warhammer matter.

- **The grate:** the rusty grate in the middle of the deck lifts off easily. It's a 6-foot drop to **B6**. The glint below is a springjaw doll's glass eye.
- **Captain's door (to B3):** a table is jammed against it. **DC 20 Athletics** to Force Open.

### B3 — Captain's Quarters

- **The clue:** the desk drawer holds the ship's **manifest**. An entry over 200 years old lists a package for Poppy von Barnacle. It's a **"fine wooden scroll case, cherry,"** bound for **[Moonshadow Island](gm_glossary.md#moonshadow-island)**. It was crated with small items in the **cargo deck (B5)**.
- **The hole:** part of the floor has collapsed into the hold, **B7**. The wispy wayfarer lies on the footlocker down there, pretending to be a ragged sheet.
  - Spotting it: a **Seek** action, **DC 22 Perception**, and low-light vision, darkvision, or a light source to see into the dim hold.
- **Treasure (without Monster Parts only):** lesser juggernaut mutagen, oil of weightlessness, and full plate in fine condition despite the damp.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (a continuity snag in the book):** The manifest says the cherry scroll case is cargo for Moonshadow. Elsewhere, the book says two other things. Poppy shipped the *sapphire scale* here in a sealed chest. And D'marti found a cherry case in the jungle. The book never makes these fit. The clue's job is to send PCs to B5, so run it as written.

> **🧩 Sets up:** This is the first mention of Moonshadow Island, where Poppy hid another jewel fragment. The PCs visit it in Chapters 6–7.

### B4 — Crew's Quarters

No encounter. A half-flooded cabin with soaked pallets.
- **Treasure under the pallets:** ***+1 warhammer (2)***, 2 lesser bottled lightning, 2 light hammers. All of these work well against the undead and constructs aboard.

### B5 — Cargo Deck *(Trivial 1)* — the scroll case is here

Flooded and covered in mold. Something burbles in the water.

**Scroll Mold:** BB 86, Creature 0. Initiative: Perception +6 or Stealth +8. Athletics +0 (+4 to Swim). Monster Parts 2.25/5/9 gp.
- **Finding it:** Seek + **DC 18 Perception** spots the scroll case bobbing in the SE corner.
- **Once spotted**, it casts *longstrider* and swims for the prow.

**How it behaves (p. 40):**
- It's a harmless prankster that gives the PCs a tour of the ship. It spends **all its actions fleeing**.
- It casts attack spells **only alongside other monsters** (B6 dolls, B7 wayfarer).
- **Cornered alone:** it surrenders in mime at the first damage. If spared, it hands over mold spores worth its normal Monster Parts value.
- **Getting the map:** take the case and Interact to unstopper it. The parchment is dry. If the PCs already have the scale, they can put the map together now ([Scene 7](#scene-7--assembling-the-map)).
- **XP:** **30 XP** story award per PC for cornering it. A lone Trivial creature is worth less, but the chase can drag PCs into other fights.

> **🔒 GM-only (who's in the mold):** The map's magic put a piece of the **map maker's** mind into the mold. The map maker is **[Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red)**. If befriended, the mold shows hazy **towering mushrooms** and makes a sound of **buzzing wings**. **DC 14 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** or **DC 17 Society** identifies Jakapo Town and its flying galtzagorris. That's where to cure the curse.

**Optional companion:** if the PCs treat it kindly and have fewer than two companions, it may "ask" to join. It's more trouble than it's worth. It could be a mascot.

### B6 — Main Deck *(Moderate 1)*

Half-wrecked and mostly flooded. Light comes in through the grate.

**Springjaw Dolls (2):** BB 108, Creature 1. Monster Parts 3.5/7/13 gp. A tinkerer in the crew built them in adorable sailor suits and left them on standby before the wreck. When the PCs enter, their eyes click open and their limbs twist as they rise to attack.
- Underwater squares: **greater difficult terrain** here too (or Swim, DC 12).

### B7 — Haunted Hold *(Moderate 1)*

Soaked crates, rotten barrels, and one sturdy footlocker by the prow. Dust floats in the light from the hole in the ceiling.

**Wispy Wayfarer:** BB 100, Creature 2. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 5/12/22 gp. This is the Gipper's captain. He stayed with his stolen treasure and came back as a ghost. The ghost is disguised as a sheet on the footlocker and waits until the PCs come close.
- **Climbing up** through the ceiling hole to B3: **DC 15 Athletics**.
- **Hazard:** **3 Rotten Planks** (above).
- **Treasure in the footlocker:** ***+1 rapier (2)***, 20 gp in coins and gems. Without Monster Parts, add 75 gp.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A level-2 creature plus three planks makes this the Gipper's hardest fight. If the PCs come in hurt from B6, let them rest ashore. Nothing here is on a clock.

## Scene 6 — The [Writhing Swamp](gm_glossary.md#writhing-swamp)

- **Travel:** 4 hours from Rumplank.
- **Goal:** get the **sapphire scale**, tied to a thyalodon's forehead at **C6**.

Miles of salty muck, an inch underwater even in the dry season. Snakes everywhere. Only Jakapo Town's galtzagorris come here, and they fly over it.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** The rooms tell a story. A survivor, an empty tree, and a new "god" reveal what happened to the Embersand leshies. It's also the chapter's hardest fight.

**Finding the ruins:** whether they follow directions from Jakapo Town or wander, the PCs reach **C1**. It's a break in the trees leading toward suspicious ruins. If players doubt it, their characters hear a **thyalodon bleating** in distress to the north.

### Stopping at Jakapo Town first?

Melar Kan's guess about Malardy may send the PCs to Jakapo Town first. That's fine.
- The herder [**Kuaranda**](gm_glossary.md#kuaranda) bought a pricey dragon scale from a traveling g'mayun. She didn't know it was magic or stolen. She made it a head ornament for her favorite thyalodon, which is now missing.
- Her detailed directions lead to **C1**.
- **Don't run Events 5–8 yet.** They belong to Chapter 2, when the PCs come back with the finished map. Elder S'matzia is away on an errand.

### The backstory (read before running)

> **🔒 GM-only:**
> - The **Embersand** clan of wildfire leshies lived here.
> - While Ignatius Barkley was off exploring, a **dryad husk named Pyksara** crawled out of the C4 gnarled tree. It corrupted the whole clan into [leshy husks](gm_glossary.md#leshy-husk).
> - Pyksara grew sick of their worship and went north to corrupt other swamps.
> - The husks took a wandering **venomous snakedead** as their new idol. They tied it to the C5 statue so it couldn't leave them too. They feed it sacrifices.
> - The biggest sacrifice is happening now: a thyalodon at C6.

### C1 — Snake Pit *(Moderate 1)*

High ground winds through trees and boulders toward the ruins. Thick plants keep the PCs on the path.

**Leshy Husk (1):** BB 44, Creature 0. Initiative: Perception +3. Monster Parts 2.25/5/9 gp. It's a lookout facing south, distracted by the prisoner. It notices the PCs only within **30 feet** of the pit.

**Snake Pit** *(complex environmental trap, Hazard 2)*. It's already sprung and easy to find.
- **Stealth** +8 (trained) to hear the snakes, or +0 once the branches are gone.
- A 20-foot pit under branches. It's full of dozens of frightened snakes. They aren't venomous, but their fangs are sharp.
- **Disable:** **DC 14 Survival or Thievery** clears the branches without getting bitten. The snakes stop only if killed, or if lifted out by some means. Lifting them out takes **DC 17 Nature** (trained) to keep them calm.
- **AC** 18; **Fort** +10, **Ref** +5.
- **Branches:** Hardness 3, HP 15 (BT 7).
- **Snakes:** HP 20; immune to precision and swarm mind; resistance physical 6 (except bludgeoning); weakness area damage 3, splash damage 3.
- **Pitfall (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature walks onto the branches. **Effect:** it falls in and takes falling damage (10 bludgeoning for 20 feet). It can Grab an Edge (DC 20). The trap then **rolls initiative**.
- **Routine (1 action):** each creature in the pit takes **1d10+4 piercing**, **DC 20 basic Reflex**. Once the snakes are killed or freed, the routine stops.
- **Reset:** the pit stays open until someone covers it with new branches.

> **📏 Rules refresher (complex hazards):** The pit acts on its own initiative each round ([hazard rules](#hazards)). So a fallen PC takes 1d10+4 every round until they climb out or the PCs deal with the snakes. Swarm weaknesses make area spells and splash weapons (alchemist's fire) the fast answer.

**In the pit: [Ignatius Barkley](gm_glossary.md#ignatius-barkley)** (LG male wildfire leshy fighter 1), stripped of his gear. His thorny branches block the snakes, and his burning body unsettles them. But he won't last forever. He'd rather talk once he's out.
- He's the last Embersand. He came home for a hero's welcome and found his kin turned into husks. *"I'll as soon smash every last one of my ghoulish old 'pals' before I let them turn me into a blighted monstrosity!"*
- **Companion:** once out, he offers to join if the PCs have fewer than two companions. He's noble and capable. He won't put up with evildoers, and he leaves at the first sign of corruption. His life goal: find and destroy whatever corrupted his people.
- **Story XP:** **30 XP** per PC for rescuing him.

> **💡 GM tip (going around C1):** The PCs might skip C1 or scout the edge of the swamp. If so, two C3 husks on patrol find them. If those husks escape, they warn the rest. After this patrol fight, remove those two husks from C3.

### C2 — Ruined Columns

No encounter. Barkley's backpack lies among the fallen columns. No check needed to find it. If rescued, he gives the PCs all of it: trail rations, a bedroll, a lesser antidote, a lesser alchemist's fire, and five tindertwigs.

### C3 — Lookout Ledge *(Moderate 1)*

A squat stone ruin, a little under 10 feet high. Stairs on the east side. A good view to the west.
- **Leshy Husks (2)** (BB 44, Creature 0) watch west.
- **Skeletal Iron Eels (2)** (p. 38, Creature −1, Initiative Perception +5) stand on each side of the stairs, facing east.
- If either pair spots the PCs, they let out an unholy scream, and all four attack.
- If the PCs already fought the patrol, only the two skeletons remain.

### C4 — Gnarled Tree

No encounter. A dead gum tree with face-like shadows in its bark. Black fungus has rotted its trunk hollow, and slime cakes it.
- **DC 15 Nature:** the fungus is what's left of a **dryad husk**.
- Anyone who looks inside sees deep **claw gouges**. Something crawled *out*.
- Barkley guesses that the thing corrupted his people. He's right: it was Pyksara.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Pyksara is still out there, north of the swamp. It's a target for Barkley's personal quest.

### C5 — Honored One *(Moderate 1)*

A shattered stone statue with thick rope around its base.
- **Wisps:** leshy husk (BB 44, Creature 0). Barkley calls it *"my main mate, Wisps."* Wisps kneels, praying in Sylvan to the snakedead. It ignores nearby fighting. It snaps out of it on seeing anyone within **20 feet**.
- **Venomous Snakedead:** BB 118, Creature 2. Monster Parts 5/12/22 gp. Tied to the statue on a **10-foot** leash.
- **Untying it:** when Wisps spots the PCs, it rushes to untie the snakedead. That takes **4 Interact actions**, about a round and a half.
- Once freed, it attacks anything except leshy husks, until it's destroyed.
- If an escaped C3 lookout warned Wisps, the snakedead **starts untied**.

> **💡 GM tip:** Those 4 actions are a race. The fight can get much easier if the PCs rush Wisps, or stay out of the snakedead's reach while the leash holds.

### C6 — Sacrificial Animal *(Severe 1)* — the sapphire scale is here

A broad stone platform 5 feet high, a blood-stained marble altar, and a rusty ceremonial dagger. Two husks smear foul herbal paste on a limp, mewing **thyalodon**. An ornament on its forehead glows faint blue through the muck. That's the sapphire scale.

**Creatures:**
- **Leshy Husks (2):** BB 44, Creature 0.
- **Thyalodon:** p. 402, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +8. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp.

**The confused thyalodon:**
- The scale's stray magic upsets and confuses it. Violence makes it worse. It can't tell who's a friend, so it **attacks PCs and husks at random**.
- **Removing the scale** calms it at once:
  - **DC 22 Athletics** to wrestle it off a hostile thyalodon.
  - **No check** if the thyalodon is unconscious first.
- While it wears the scale, attempts to calm it (Nature, or Diplomacy with Wild Empathy) take a **−2 circumstance penalty**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha (the hardest fight in the chapter):** The thyalodon is two levels above the party. It hits harder and more often than anything so far. The book's Severe rating assumes one of two things: the PCs kill the husks first, or they get unlucky and face it alone. Its random targeting is the PCs' best friend. Tell players it's lashing out at everything. That steers them toward the scale instead of the kill.

**The thyalodon is Kuaranda's missing pet,** the runt of her herd. She hoped the shiny ornament would get it treated better. Instead it made things worse. The scale's magic sent it wandering into the swamp, where the husks caught it.

> **🧩 Sets up:** If the PCs return it alive to Kuaranda in Jakapo Town, she's a friend for life (**Event 7 in Chapter 2**, p. 57). She can also become a companion there.

## Scene 7 — Assembling the Map

Once they have **both halves and the sapphire scale**, the PCs hold the halves together and wave the scale over the torn edges. The book's narration (p. 45), in short:

- The parchment glows golden and swells into a **blinding flash**.
- Two faded scraps become one **seamless, slightly cryptic treasure map**.
- **Six points of light** dot it like a tiny constellation. They mark a path from **Jakapo Town to the base of Shivering Mountain**.
- The **sapphire scale** now glows faint gold and pulses slowly.

**Then the curse hits:**
- After a moment of celebration, the corners fray.
- Before their eyes, the map is **turning to ash**.
- To reach Poppy's treasure, they must stop its magic fast.

**Cliffhanger.** End the session. The PCs **level up to 2**.

> **💡 GM tip:** Are the PCs short of 1,000 XP? The book suggests a story award for figuring out how to put the map together. You can also run extra social scenes before Chapter 2. The **Gilded Treasure Map handout** (p. 46–47) rewards impatient players.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The assembled map has 20 HP and loses 1 per hour. *Mending* pauses the decay for a few hours but can't end it. Only Jakapo Town's galtzagorris can lift the curse (Chapter 2). Their leader is elder **S'matzia**, Arahosko's descendant. The mold's mushroom images and D'marti's advice both point there.

### Sapphire Scale *(Item 3)*

Rare, divination, magical. **Price** 50 gp. **Usage** held in 1 hand. **Bulk** negligible.
- A palm-sized, see-through scale. People say it was cut from a dragon's hide.
- **Passive:** on a map, sea chart, or atlas, it reveals hidden notes. With a suitable map, you get a **+1 item bonus** to Survival checks to Sense Direction.
- **Activate** (1 action, Interact), **once per minute:** hold it to your eye. Your next Seek this turn gets a **+1 item bonus** to Perception to detect invisible creatures. On a critical success against an invisible creature's Stealth DC, the creature becomes **observed** to you. It can still Sneak to become undetected again.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Chapter 2's map rules depend on how the scale reacts at each landmark. Keep it in the PCs' hands.

**Next chapter:** the PCs race to **Jakapo Town** to save the crumbling map.
