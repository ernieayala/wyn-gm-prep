## The chapter in one minute

The PCs hold three pieces of the [Jewel of the Indigo Isles](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles). Poppy's notes put the fourth in [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef), a vast razor-coral field; an older map puts [Razorback Isle](gm_glossary.md#razorback-isle) there. Both are right: the cult's step pyramid stood on the isle, which sank into the reef when the cult fell. Only the pyramid's top shows above the sea floor.

The PCs cross the reef, meet [chochori](gm_glossary.md#chochori) shark hunters, and dive into a maze of coral and toxic kelp to the pyramid's top in its air bubble. Inside: five floors of cult guardians and constructs, being torn apart by **teaknis** (water elementals) bent on flooding it. In the deepest vault, the dead high priest and her followers have fused into a **skeletal cult dragon** guarding the **Tangerine Shard**. Taking it ends the magic holding back the sea; the temple floods within minutes.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Found where Razorback Isle used to be** (Scene 1).
2. **Got past the coral maze** and into the sunken temple (Scenes 2–4).
3. **Claimed the Tangerine Shard** from the skeletal cult dragon and escaped the flooding temple (Scene 5).

**Party level:** starts at 7, ends at **8**. This is the end of Act 2.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** Act 2's dungeon finale, and the PCs' first walk through the cult's own home. Library, murals, and carvings reveal who the cult was, what it wanted, and that the "treasure" is its relic. It delivers the last fragment so the Jewel can form in Ch 9.

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|

| **Sea Hunters** (p. 194–196) | Social scene, four chochori shark hunters | On arrival |
| **Kalacho** (p. 195–197) | Optional undersea trading town 6 miles away | Any time, if the PCs go |
| **The Coral Maze** (p. 196–201) | Five areas, Q1–Q5, 40 feet underwater | Before the temple |
| **The Sunken Temple** (p. 201–215) | Seventeen areas, R1–R17, five floors, air inside | Last. R17 holds the shard |
| **Their Triumphant Return** (p. 215) | Back to the ship, level 8 | Closes Act 2 |

Enter at Q1; Q2–Q4 lie en route to the temple top at Q5 (west of the wreck, per Wallopula). Breaking the Q3 coral opens a shortcut. Inside, the PCs work down to the bottom-floor vaults and shard.

**Threat math at party level 7** (see [Ch 1's XP rules](gm_chapter_1.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp)): a level-8 creature is 60 XP, level 9 is 80, level 10 is 120, level 6 is 30, level 5 is 20, level 4 is 15, and level 3 is 10.
- **R7:** Acolyte Bituminous (level 8, 60) plus two incense holders (level 3, 10 each) = 80 = **Moderate**.
- **R11:** the hydrojinxer (level 8, 60) plus a teakni sneak (level 5, 20) = 80 = **Moderate**.
- **R17:** the skeletal cult dragon (level 10, 120) = **Severe**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — two labels that don't match the math:** **Q5** (one complex level-8 hazard, 60 XP) sums to Low; the book says **Moderate 7**. **R13** (four simple level-8 hazards, 12 XP each, 48 total) is a bit over Trivial; the book says **Trivial 7**. Use the book's labels.

> **💡 GM tip — pacing:** 13 rated encounters plus exploration: two sessions is a reasonable target (voyage and maze, then temple). The flooding clock pressures the temple for you.

From Chapters 2 and 3:
- **NPC attitudes and Make an Impression** ([Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#npc-attitudes-make-an-impression-and-request)): Longjaw starts **indifferent**, **DC 20** Diplomacy (Scene 2). Each step up unlocks more help.
- **Counteracting** ([Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting)): a 4th-level *dispel magic* shuts down one Q5 **elemental backlash** rune (**counteract DC 24**; a critical success ends the whole hazard) or one R13 **vault ward** (**counteract DC 26**).

### Underwater: swimming and aquatic combat

The maze is 40 feet down (p. 197). The book defers to the *Core Rulebook*; in short:

- **Swim** (1 action, Athletics): no check in calm water. Success moves 10 feet for most PCs (5 feet plus 5 per 20 feet of land Speed); critical success, 15 feet. End your turn in water without a successful Swim and you sink 10 feet. The book gives no maze Swim DC, so treat it as calm.
- **Swim Speed:** move at that Speed, no checks.
- **Sinking on purpose:** a PC wearing or holding something heavy sinks to the bottom and can walk the sea floor as difficult terrain (p. 197).

**Fighting underwater:**
- **Flat-footed** without a swim Speed.
- **Resistance 5 to acid and fire**.
- **Melee** slashing or bludgeoning attacks through water: **−2 circumstance penalty**.
- **Ranged** bludgeoning or slashing attacks **always miss** if attacker or target is underwater. Ranged piercing attacks have **range increments halved**.
- **No fire spells** or fire-trait actions.

Example: against the Q4 shale swimmer (swim Speed), most PCs are flat-footed and arrows reach half as far.

### Holding your breath and suffocation

*Water breathing* removes this problem, which is why Scene 1 pushes the PCs to get it. Without it:

- Hold breath for **5 + Constitution modifier** rounds.
- End of each turn: lose **1 round of air**, or **2** if you attacked or cast a spell that turn.
- Lose 1 more per critical hit taken or critical failure on a save against a damaging effect.
- **Speaking uses up all remaining air**, including verbal spell components and command activations.
- **Out of air:** unconscious and suffocating. End of each turn, **DC 20 Fortitude**: failure deals 1d10 damage, critical failure kills. Each later save adds 5 to the DC and 1d10 to the damage. Air ends suffocation.

Example: the Q5 **elemental lash** refills a breath-holder's air on an air-trait hit and empties it on a water-trait hit.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A caster holding their breath who casts a verbal spell runs out of air on the spot.

### Navigating Runaway Reef (chapter subsystem)

Two exploration activities in order, any PC attempting either: **Navigate to Razorback Isle**, then **Sail through Runaway Reef** (p. 194). Most results cost **marine resources**. With none to spend: **1 extra week** and **100 gp** per missing resource. Gold can't replace a resource the party has. Outcomes in [Scene 1](#scene-1--to-the-reef).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — these are defense resources too:** Marine resources are Ch 12 defense resources (p. 117, p. 315); each one spent here is one fewer Rumplank upgrade. The book lets you allow a different resource. Tell players before they spend.

### The flooding temple (chapter subsystem)

The temple floods in **stages** (p. 207), starting at **Stage 0**. Stages describe the bottom floor (R12–R17); higher floors flood later.

| Stage | Bottom floor (R12–R17) | Other floors |
|---|---|---|
| **0** (start) | A bit over 1 foot deep. Difficult terrain for Small and smaller | Dry |
| **1** | 2 feet. Difficult terrain for Medium and larger, greater difficult for Small and smaller | Dry |
| **2** | 4 feet. Greater difficult terrain for Medium and larger. Small and smaller must swim | R6–R11 now floods at 2 stages less |
| **3** | 6 feet. Air still breathable, but everyone must swim | |
| **4** | Completely flooded | R3–R5 now floods at 4 stages less |
| **5** | | R2 floods at 5 stages less |
| **6** | Interior walls burst from the pressure | R1 floods at 6 stages less |
| **7–9** | Levels keep flooding, the temple groans | |
| **10** | Whole temple floods and collapses | |

**What raises the stage:**
- **Teaknis:** +1 per hour they work uninterrupted after the PCs meet them. Defeat them in R11 **or** R12, but not both, and it's +1 per **2 hours** (p. 210).
- **Taking the Tangerine Shard (R17):** the protection ends for good. +1 **every minute** (p. 213).

Example: shard pulled at Stage 0. Three minutes later everyone on the bottom floor is swimming; four minutes, underwater; ten minutes, collapse.

> **💡 GM tip:** Show players the stages as a countdown and describe the water at each step. A clear clock is scarier than a vague one.

### The incapacitation trait

Effects that can take a creature out of a fight in one go carry this trait. A creature **higher level than the hazard, creature, or item** making the effect treats its save as one degree better. For spells, the creature must be more than twice the spell's level.

Example: the R13 **vault wards** are level 8, and their petrification has the incapacitation trait. Level-7 PCs get no benefit, so a critical failure means **petrified for 24 hours**.

### Monster abilities: Grab, Trample, sweep

The skeletal cult dragon uses all three.
- **Grab** (1 action): right after a hit with a Strike that lists Grab, the target is grabbed until the end of the monster's next turn. The body part holding the target can't Strike others. The target can Escape.
- **Trample** (3 actions): Stride up to double Speed through creatures of the listed size or smaller. Each creature it enters takes the listed Strike's damage, **basic Reflex** at the listed DC, once per use.
- **Sweep** (weapon trait): +1 circumstance to attack if it already attacked a different target this turn with that weapon.
- **Reach:** it can Strike anything within that distance without moving adjacent.

### Conditions new this chapter

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Petrified** | You're turned to stone. You can't act or sense anything, and you're an object. |

Also here: enfeebled (kelp toxin), fleeing (Cultists' Cry), grabbed (dragon's jaws) ([Ch 7](gm_chapter_7.md#conditions-new-this-chapter)); slowed (vault wards) ([Ch 4](gm_chapter_4.md#slowed-and-fatigued)); concealed (Tangerine Shard's haze) ([Ch 2](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)).

## Scene 1 — To the Reef

- **Where:** at sea, Moonshadow Island to Runaway Reef, **3 days** (p. 193).
- **Who:** Prince Kulupi, Garnikor, Slate Hodda, Fippin and Mippin.
- **Goal:** find where Razorback Isle used to be.

### Two maps, one answer

Poppy's notes put the last fragment in Runaway Reef, submerged razor coral every wise sailor avoids. **Gustavius Gollihew's older map** shows Razorback Isle in the same place (p. 192).
- Kulupi and the sailors can't settle it: an island in the reef, or "razorback" meaning the coral ridges?
- **DC 15 Nature** or **DC 13 Sailing Lore**: same conclusion. Both are possible; go and look.

> **🔒 GM-only — the truth:** Both maps are right. The cult built its headquarters, a huge step pyramid, on Razorback Isle, drawing elemental magic from stone and sea. When the cult fell at the end of the War of the Shivering Behemoth, the magic failed and earth magic pulled the pyramid into the sea-floor muck. Only its top shows. The top doors, once guard posts, are now the only way in. The final fragment is on the lowest level, guarded by creatures the cult left (p. 192–193).

### Recall Knowledge: the chochoris

- **DC 18 Society** (or trained in **Chochori Lore**): sea-dwelling chochoris live near Runaway Reef.
- **Critical success:** the town is **Kalacho**, known for serious but not unfriendly shark hunters.

### Underwater preparation

The temple means long stretches underwater; water breathing, a swim Speed, or both fixes that (p. 193).
- Several alchemical and magic items grant these briefly; **Kalacho** sells them best.
- A cleric or druid can prepare *water breathing*.
- Crew such as Garnikor sell a few items to PCs out of options, maybe free to PCs who impressed them.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** [Holding your breath](#holding-your-breath-and-suffocation) and [aquatic combat](#underwater-swimming-and-aquatic-combat) decide the maze fights. Review them first.

### On the way

- **Fippin and Mippin** have fished the reef for rare crabs (plenty of butter and garlic), never near the map's spot, and share their old captain's advice on not running aground.
- **Slate Hodda** keeps watch, eager for the famous coral.
- **Sea resources:** the PCs can gather marine resources this leg (p. 117).

### Reaching Razorback Isle

Kulupi and Garnikor help plot a heading but know no more than the PCs. The map gets them close; the PCs guide the rest ([subsystem overview](#navigating-runaway-reef-chapter-subsystem)).

**Navigate to Razorback Isle** (concentrate, exploration). **DC 22 Nature**, or **DC 20 Indigo Isles Geography Lore** or **Sailing Lore**.
- **Bonus:** +1 circumstance if another PC trained in one of these assists; +2 instead if the PCs also **impressed Fippin and Mippin** en route to Moonshadow.
- **Critical success:** safe course to where the isle should be.
- **Success:** course found, coral damages the ship. Expend at least **1 marine resource** (GM may allow another resource).
- **Failure:** as success, at least **2**.
- **Critical failure:** no sense of the isle. Expend at least **1** for repairs; retry.

**Sail through Runaway Reef** (concentrate, exploration). **Requirement:** a successful Navigate to Razorback Isle. **DC 23 Perception** or **DC 20 Sailing Lore**.
- **Bonus:** +1 circumstance if another PC master in Perception or trained in Sailing Lore assists; +2 instead if the PCs also **impressed Slate Hodda** en route to Moonshadow.
- **Critical success:** the ship reaches the spot swiftly. There's no land there.
- **Success:** arrives damaged. Expend at least **1 marine resource** (or another, GM's choice).
- **Failure:** as success, at least **2**.
- **Critical failure:** hazardous directions. Expend at least **1** for repairs; retry.

**XP:** **30 XP** on reaching where Razorback Isle stood.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** "No land there" is the reveal. Let the empty sea sit a moment before the hunters arrive.

## Scene 2 — Sea Hunters

- **Where:** on deck, at the spot in the reef (p. 194).
- **Who:** four chochori shark hunters: **Longjaw** (he/him), **Hammerhead**, **Graysquid**, and **Tigerstripe**.
- **Goal:** win them over for directions, warnings, and maybe potions.

The hunters know this reef is dangerous, but of no island. They board to warn the strangers. Read or paraphrase:

> *A crew member's surprised shout barely registers before four colorful figures haul themselves onto the deck of the ship. These chochoris look muscular and grim. They're armed with several tridents and spears each, although they don't have any weapons in hand. Most bear jagged scars, missing digits, or both.*
>
> *"These are dangerous waters you've entered. Even we avoid them. What manner of foolishness brings you right into Runaway Reef?"*

- They can't tell who's in charge. **Kulupi stays out of sight**, fearing a trap, so the PCs talk.
- **Polite PCs:** the hunters sit on a barrel or gunwale and chat.
- **Rude, dismissive, or secretive PCs:** they shrug and swim off.

**Who they are:** chochoris take hunting-names from their fiercest kill. Leader **Longjaw** (LN terath chochori hunter 6; personal name **Intha**, p. 195) killed the great white Longjaw, long a menace to chochori travelers. Old friends, they boast of hunts and tease each other over every scar.

### Chochori advice

Longjaw starts **indifferent**. The PC who's talked most attempts **DC 20 Diplomacy** to [Make an Impression](gm_chapter_2.md#npc-attitudes-make-an-impression-and-request).
- **Gift:** offered a gift or payment, Longjaw names one of the PCs' resources his people could use. Giving it raises his attitude **one step**, whatever the roll.

**What he shares by attitude:**
- **Indifferent or worse:** his hunters rarely come here because of the coral-and-kelp maze about **40 feet** down. He points out its entrance (**Q1**) and leaves.
- **Friendly** (adds):
  - Dangerous creatures inside; a roof of **toxic kelp** blocks entry from above.
  - Rumor of a strange structure in a **bubble of air** somewhere in the maze.
  - His hunters won't go near the maze but will keep sharks and mundane predators off the ship while the PCs are here.
  - They live in **Kalacho**, about **6 miles** away, where some surface traders stay in air-filled buildings. He suggests buying *potions of water breathing*, *scrolls of water breathing*, or *sea touch elixirs* there. He gives only distance and direction; finding it takes some searching.
- **Helpful** (adds): he offers to fetch **one *potion of water breathing* per PC** from Kalacho, calling in favors, hoping the PCs thin the maze's threats for his hunters.

**XP:** **30 XP** if the hunters end friendly or better.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Longjaw's requested resource is one the PCs could spend on Ch 12's defenses. Make the trade-off clear.

### Recall Knowledge: Kalacho

After the PCs meet the hunters, roll a **secret DC 20 Society** or **DC 15 Chochori Lore** or **Indigo Isles Lore** check for each PC (p. 195–196).
- **Critical success:** as success, plus: several chochori sacred sites hide in the reef. Their elders worship strange deep-ocean gods and are respected in Kalacho, so divine and primal items are plentiful there. Visitors who revere the elders get a **10 percent discount** on such items.
- **Success:** Kalacho is a friendly undersea chochori community in the reef, with several air-filled buildings for air-breathing traders.
- **Failure:** only "it's an underwater town."
- **Critical failure:** a confident lie: Kalacho's chochoris are sly mystics who curse outsiders and sell sham items that work for a while, then fail at the worst moment.

## Scene 3 — Kalacho (optional)

**Kalacho**: Settlement 5, a neutral chochori trading town beneath the sea, about 6 miles from the reef site (p. 195). Use it for supplies, rest, Monster Parts crafting, or meeting locals. Map on p. 196.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Government** | Council, one member per [chora](gm_glossary.md#chori-chochori-chora) |
| **Population** | 861 (98% chochori, 2% other) |
| **Languages** | Chochori, Common, Aquan |
| **Religions** | [The Balance](gm_glossary.md#the-balance), [The Eld](gm_glossary.md#the-eld) |
| **Threats** | Aquatic monsters, ocean storms |

**Shopping:**
- **Undersea trading post:** at any time, 1d4 permanent items of level 6–10 and twice as many consumables of level 6–10. Stock rotates over about a month.
- **Wave magic:** divine and primal items up to level 7, scrolls above all, regularly for sale.
- **Common items** of 5th level or lower, including those Longjaw mentions.

**Notable people** (accomplishment names; personal names in brackets):
- **Ordinance of Prosperity** [Hara]: N hypisk chochori councilor 7, council chair.
- **Longjaw** [Intha]: LN terath chochori hunter 6, veteran hunter and sea warden.
- **Airdweller's Annex** [Kaso]: CG aikrys chochori merchant 5, eccentric inventor of the Airdweller's Annex and Airdweller's Market.
- **Renovation of the Ascent** [Jikr]: CN jamambri chochori architect 4, builder, dreamer, marketer.

**Kalacho directory** (p. 197):
- **The Ascent:** huge vertical shaft through the hiding sea caves and reefs; the only known way in or out, and a known weak point, so heavily guarded. Recently renovated with a **diving bell** down to the Airdweller's Annex and sturdy automatic emergency gates.
- **Reefmarket:** grander than the town's size suggests, inns and taverns in its annexes. Visiting merchants can swell the population up to 50% during pilgrimages and holidays.
- **Airdweller's Annex:** Reefmarket annex at the diving bell's foot, the only comfortable home for surface folk. A permanent air bubble fills its dome, freshened by surface-plant gardens. Helpful merchants, a multilingual directory, and small-fee delivery to the PCs' ship (airtight or waterlogged as needed).
- **Great Shrine:** a small all-faiths shrine grown, through rival donations of art and labor, into a striking collage of cultures and styles.
- **Hall of the Council:** elegant coral hall for the seven councilors and three witnesses each, with private side rooms.
- **Deepcaves:** older chochoris know they lead into a giant dungeon below Kalacho and to the surface by many paths. The young no longer learn a safe path; elders fear the whole town could be trapped if the Ascent fails.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Kulupi and the sailors want to finish the voyage and disapprove if the PCs linger too long (p. 197).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the Ascent isn't the only way out:** The book calls it "the only known way to enter or leave Kalacho," then gives elders safe Deepcave paths (p. 197). Read "only known" as "the one most residents use."

> **💡 GM tip:** The Deepcaves are an unwritten hook, no map or encounters. Use them only if you want to build something.

## Scene 4 — The Coral Maze

- **Where:** the sea floor, 40 feet down. Map on p. 198.
- **Goal:** reach the temple top at **Q5**.

The maze grew on old Razorback Isle's ridges. Earth magic leaking from the cult's rituals made the stone fertile, and coral and **stinging kelp** ran wild. Most life here is harmless, but a few big predators hunt the maze (p. 196).

### Coral maze features

- **Depth:** sea floor about **40 feet** down. Stone-and-coral ridges rise **10 to 20 feet**, forming winding passages and dead ends.
- **Walls:** sharp, painful to grab or climb.
- **Movement:** mostly swimming. A PC with something heavy sinks and can walk the floor as **difficult terrain** ([underwater rules](#underwater-swimming-and-aquatic-combat)).
- **Light:** dimmer than the surface, visibility otherwise normal. The kelp canopy blocks little light.
- **Swimming in from above:** the kelp forms a loose, toxic ceiling.
  - Entering anywhere but Q1 from above: **DC 23 Athletics** to Force Open a path, plus **DC 20 Fortitude** against the toxin. Failed save: **clumsy 1** and **enfeebled 1** for 1 hour (poison).
  - Athletics **critical success** improves the save one degree; **critical failure** worsens it one.
  - The kelp closes behind each swimmer, so **every PC** checks.
  - **Swimming up** through it needs no Athletics check, only the save.
- The hunters can warn the PCs off entering from above.

### Map quick reference

| Area | Threat | What's there | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** Maze Entry | — | The way in | DC 20 Nature or DC 15 Ocean Lore: fish shun the kelp and the south passage to Q2 |
| **Q2** Coral Grotto | Moderate 7 | **Wallopula** (myzoform) + giant glaucus slug | Talkable. Knows the ship and temple locations |
| **Q3** Sunken Ship | — | *Distant Star* wreck | **Phantom stardust** (defense resource). Breakable shortcut to Q5 |
| **Q4** Churned Sand | Low 7 | **Shale swimmer** | Greater difficult terrain on foot. Harmless dead turtle in dead end |
| **Q5** Trapped Air | Moderate 7 | **Elemental Backlash** (complex hazard) | Temple top. Touch triggers it |

### Q1 — Maze Entry

> *Walls of sharp rock adorned with colorful coral rise from the sea floor, creating twisting passages leading west. A forest of waving kelp rises from atop the coral-encrusted walls, twining together in a canopy about thirty feet overhead. Small fish, crustaceans, and other sea creatures dart in and among the coral and across the sea floor, though these animals seem to avoid the kelp fronds.*

**DC 20 Nature** or **DC 15 Ocean Lore** while studying the sea life: the animals avoid the kelp for its toxin, and the passage south to **Q2** because a predator lairs there.

### Q2 — Coral Grotto *(Moderate 7)*

> *This open area has a riot of colorful coral in rainbow hues. The widened channel turns west, then ends in an area where the coral has been scraped away into strange patterns.*

Domain of **Wallopula** (they/them), a myzoform, which eats memories and personalities. Latest meals: the two *Distant Star* survivors (Q3), **Gortum** (female orpok bosun) and **Karkak** (male g'mayun deckhand), whose forms Wallopula swaps between to savor each one's memories. The scraped coral is Wallopula's art: overlapping portraits of everyone eaten, a muddle of faces up close.

Wallopula's pet **giant glaucus slug** hunts small sea animals; Wallopula lives on recycled memories alone.

**Creatures:**
- **Wallopula** — myzoform, BB 65, Creature 8. Initiative: Perception +16. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- **Giant glaucus slug** — BB 38, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp.

**When the PCs arrive:**
- Wallopula carves as the orpok, then becomes the g'mayun without missing a stroke.
- Too absorbed to notice unless the PCs are noisy. The slug perks up and follows Wallopula's lead, fighting or staying clear.

**Talking to Wallopula:** fighting isn't their first instinct; non-hostile PCs get a conversation.
- Shifting forms, they tell how the *Distant Star* ran aground and Gortum and Karkak survived in the air-filled hold, glossing over that "they died there."
- **DC 28 Perception** to Sense Motive: Wallopula found and ate them.
- The *Distant Star* lies **northwest**; a strange building in an air bubble lies farther **west**, suspicious and short on prey, so never explored.
- A pleasant PC, or one praising the carving, can try **DC 24 Diplomacy**: the building seems to have a **magical hazard**.
- Only the slug knows the shale swimmer. A PC who can talk to it learns of a dangerous predator **west**, in **Q4**.

**If a fight starts:**
- Wallopula retreats below **80 HP**, or below **30 HP** if captivated by draining a PC with **Leech Mind**.
- A living slug flees with them. Both prefer **Q1**; if it's blocked, they swim up through the kelp.

**Befriending Wallopula:** **full XP**, plus eel skin and slug mucus worth both creatures' full Monster Parts value.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** A talkable predator. Friendship pays the same XP and treasure as a fight, plus a Q5 warning.

> **💡 GM tip:** The mid-sentence orpok-to-g'mayun shift is the creepy part. Play it calm and friendly; let Sense Motive land the horror.

- **DC 22 Nature or Survival** to spot it while investigating the ship.
- Tedious but easy to break: **60 HP, Hardness 5 per square**.
- Opens into the dead end **north of Q5**, a shorter route between temple and maze exterior.

**Defense special resource — phantom stardust (1 batch, p. 200):** nearly all else aboard is ruined, but **phantom stardust** remains in the hold. At **20 Bulk**, a *bag of holding* is the likely way to haul it.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Phantom stardust is a Ch 12 defense special resource (p. 315). It upgrades **Decoys** to **Illusory Decoys**, a tier-2 defense: attacking armies in the district deal 2 less damage instead of 1 (p. 317). **Write it down.**

### Q4 — Churned Sand *(Low 7)*

> *This wide area between the coral is filled with smooth sand.*

The cult bred many shale spitters (gem-crusted isopods); most died when the island sank. The aquatic **shale swimmer** thrived and has hunted near the temple since. This is its favorite spot, ground to sand by repeated Ground Spray.
- **Terrain:** **greater difficult terrain** for walkers (deep sand).

**Shale Swimmer** — p. 394, Creature 8. Initiative: Stealth +18. Monster Parts 64/140/250 gp.
- Hides in the south ridge waiting for large prey. Wavesense and tremorsense likely let it notice the PCs first.
- Surges out with **Ground Spray** (p. 394): a once-per-round Stride or Swim; ending on solid ground, above or below water, it kicks up a 15-foot cone of sand: **DC 26 Fortitude** or **dazzled** for 1 round (1 minute on a critical failure).

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Resistance 10 to piercing and slashing (p. 394), plus flat-footed PCs without a swim Speed ([aquatic combat](#underwater-swimming-and-aquatic-combat)). This Low fight can hit harder than its label.

#### The dead end

The south exit leads to a gruesome surprise.

An ordinary dead turtle, hung in the water by kelp grown through its shell into the ceiling. A few small green crabs living inside make the skull turn. Paranoid PCs may attack it. Let them.

### Q5 — Trapped Air *(Moderate 7)*

> *A strange building stands in this open area. It's approximately fifteen feet square, and it's surrounded by a dome of air. Two doors lead into the structure, and its base widens a bit at ground level, as though it were only the upper part of a larger structure under the sea floor.*

The temple's top. Both doors lead to **R1**.
- **Doors:** stone, stuck. **DC 28 Athletics** to Force Open, or **1 minute** of work.
- **Air bubble:** leaked air held by magic; anyone passes in freely. A few **dead fish** swam in and suffocated.
- **DC 20 Arcana or Nature:** grasps the boundary and that the building is full of air. **Critical success:** the boundary might be hazardous.

**Hazard:** the cult's elemental energy leaks out as mixed air, water, and earth, sustaining the bubble and lashing out. **Whenever a creature touches the structure, including either door,** it overloads.

**Elemental Backlash** — Hazard 8, unique complex magical trap (p. 201)
- **Stealth** +18 (expert)
- **Description:** four invisible runes, one on each side of the building, leak elemental energy.
- **Disable:** **DC 26 Arcana** (expert), **DC 24 Nature** (expert), or **DC 28 Thievery** (expert) drains one rune. A critical success also drains a rune on an adjacent side, if one is active. A successful ***dispel magic*** (4th level; **counteract DC 24**) disables one rune, and a critical success dispels the whole hazard ([counteracting](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting)).
- **Rune:** Hardness 15; HP 50 (BT 25); object immunities.
- **Awakened Energy** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature touches any part of the structure, including the doors. **Effect:** the hazard makes an elemental lash Strike against that creature, then rolls initiative.
- **Melee** elemental lash +20 (air, earth, or water, equal chance of each; reach 10 feet), **3d6+10 bludgeoning** plus elemental effect.
- **Elemental effect:**
  - **Air:** the creature hit renews all its air if it's holding its breath, as if it took a fresh breath.
  - **Water:** the creature loses all its air if it's holding its breath.
  - **Earth:** the creature is pushed back 5 feet. If it can't be pushed, it takes an extra 1d6 bludgeoning instead.
- **Routine (4 actions):** the trap loses 1 action for each rune drained, dispelled, or destroyed. With each action, a different active rune either makes an elemental lash Strike against a random creature in reach or, if none is in reach, makes an **eruption** under 1 random creature within 60 feet (no line of sight needed). The eruption deals **6d6 bludgeoning**, **DC 26 basic Reflex**, with an equal chance of the air, water, or earth trait. A creature that fails takes that element's effect. The hazard doesn't target the same creature twice in a turn unless it has no other options.
- **Reset:** all four runes come back 24 hours after it has no targets for its routine.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — you can't open the door without touching it:** Forcing the doors triggers the trap. Reward players who disable runes first after a warning from Wallopula or a critical Arcana/Nature success.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** A routine hazard like Ch 1's snake pit ([complex hazards](gm_chapter_1.md#hazards)); each disabled rune costs it an action a round.

### If the PCs get into trouble underwater

Book suggestion (p. 201): PCs knocked out or out of air float up through a rare kelp gap, and the crew fishes them out and nurses them back.

## Scene 5 — The Sunken Temple

> **🔒 GM-only — what happened here:** The War of the Shivering Behemoth ended here two centuries ago. Poppy's forces stormed the pyramid, stopped the rituals, and beat the defending cultists. The cult's elemental rituals, meant to stop a ranged attack from the sea, failed against a ground force, and their wild magic sank island and temple. The only recent visitors: curious, destructive water elementals, **teaknis**, out to explore and sabotage (p. 201).

Cult symbols cover the temple: towering shale spitters, gemstone motifs, mountains cracked open to release wicked energy. Unhidden here, they let the PCs piece together much of the cult's history and aims. All is old: chipped stone, many faded murals (p. 203).

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** The cult's own house. Each room reveals more about the cult at the end of Poppy's trail, and the vault mosaics show what it meant for the Isles.

- **Doors:** none locked; bottom-level vault doors are trapped (R13).
- **Light:** magical lights glow on landings and in halls. Rooms are dark unless noted.
- **Map:** p. 202.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — four levels or five?** The features text says "four levels" (p. 203); the flood sidebar and R10 stone model use **five**: R1, R2, R3–R5, R6–R11, and R12–R17. Run five, which the flooding rules need.

**Background Special Effect:** huge news for an **indigo historian** PC, since Poppy worked to erase this temple from history. Information and items they bring back make them a star with the **Royal Society of History** (p. 203).

**Perusing the library:** poking around reveals the cult's name, the **Cult of the Shivering Behemoth**, and its aim: magically unleash a terrible evil across the islands, wiping out all life not loyal to it. For more, a PC spends **1d4 hours** on a **secret** check: **DC 27 Perception**, **DC 23 Arcana or Nature**, or **DC 18 Library Lore**.

1. An annotated map: the cult's base was a pyramid, guarded by cult-made monsters, atop treacherous Razorback Isle, ringed by Runaway Reef, thought all but impregnable thanks above all to the watch from its top.

3. The cult's **Moonshadow Island** fortress existed to mine special metals from old cliffside tunnels. Letters with a cult leader there call the mission nearly done and weigh abandoning the fortress.
4. The pyramid was cult headquarters. A note to a supplier: guard chambers at top, ritual rooms in the middle, vaults at bottom.
5. A description for an honored, unnamed visitor: main entrance on the lowest level (then ground level), a grand processional staircase up to the middle levels, and no vault access from the main entrance.
6. Notes among unfinished magic item formulas mention an important **rainbow-colored gemstone in four pieces**, together as powerful as the mightiest artifacts. Whether the cult made it or found it as part of a larger plan goes unsaid.

**Treasure:** under the fallen shelf, a crushed skeleton in tattered cult robes wears ***gloves of storing*** holding a ***scroll of stoneskin***.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Fact 6 reveals the treasure is a four-piece cult relic; Fact 3 links back to Moonshadow (Ch 6–7). Both pay off when the Jewel forms and the cult strikes in Ch 9.

### R10 — Warding Chamber

> *Several skeletons sprawl on the floor of this room. In the northeast corner is a low table with stones stacked on it in a pyramidal shape. Strange blast marks and stains mar the room's bare stone.*

The pyramid's ward control room, where Poppy and her crew finally beat the cult's leaders.
- **DC 23 Medicine or Society:** the bones are from a great battle between humanoids in sailors' clothes and in heavy gem-set gray robes.
- Withered shale spitter husks lie among them.

**The model:** a mud-slick stone model of the pyramid, two entrances at top and a larger one at bottom.
- **Five levels**, each lifting off to show the one below; the second-lowest is stuck to the lowest.
- **Layout:** R1 through R11 and the stairs down to the R12 doors. Layout only, not contents.
- **Damage:** on the model, R11's east wall and floor are damaged, and a PC can peer through into R16. Salt water from an unknown source pools in the model's lowest level.
- **The enchantment works both ways.** On the table, the model mirrors the pyramid's damage (holes, leaks, worsening), and changing the model changes the pyramid. Reshaping took powerful magic (cult rituals with spells such as *shape stone*); it resists mundane tampering. **Restoring** is easier: **DC 23 Crafting** repairs some of the elementals' damage, and makes the teaknis more irritable and likelier to attack in R11 and R12.
- **Removing the stuck level:** only designated cult members could lift it to examine (or change) the vaults. A PC can imitate that authority with the **Trick Magic Item** feat and **DC 23 Arcana or Occultism**, or **DC 25 Deception** if expert or better in Deception.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book gives no flood-stage effect for repairing the model, only the teaknis' mood.

**Treasure:** a pirate corpse holds a ***rod of wonder***. A cultist corpse grips a ***gallow's tooth*** talisman.

### R11 — Reception Room *(Moderate 7)*

The cult's rare visitors were entertained here. The murals, an artistic take on the R1 scrawls, mimic a view over the isle and sea; PCs who saw R1 notice the same intent.

**The hole** is split by a wall below: **north** drops into **R12**, **south** into **R16**.

**The teaknis' story:**
- A few weeks ago, drawn by the trapped air, they squirmed through the outside muck to the main doors (R12).

- They chose this east wall as weakest, but their first breach broke the floor instead. The R16 **hieroglyph scorpion** keeps driving them from the vault, so they're widening the other half of the hole and the connecting wall.
- One sneak died upstairs to the living library (R5). Two **teakni saboteurs** are widening the R12 main doors. The **hydrojinxer** and another **teakni sneak** are here.

**Talking to them:** they expect a fight but aren't immediately hostile.
- The hydrojinxer (she/her) openly explains their aims and what teaknis are, likely new to the PCs.
- She gives **no name**; she feels a name would constrain her.
- She **turns hostile at once** at any suggestion to stop. The sabotage obsesses them all.
- She shrugs off the dead teakni: not everyone likes teakni mischief.
- **If the PCs offer to work with them,** she shares what they've seen (only R6, R11, and R12) and the strange lower-vault attacker, though not what or how many. However they help, she finds the PCs incompetent: hard to please, harder to work with.

**The flood clock starts here.** The bottom floor is already at **Stage 0**, +1 per hour the teaknis work uninterrupted after the PCs meet them. Defeat them here **or** in R12, but not both, and it's every **2 hours** ([flood stages](#the-flooding-temple-chapter-subsystem)).

**If a fight breaks out:**
- The sneak keeps melee attackers off the hydrojinxer and fights to the death.
- The hydrojinxer uses water spells only.
- She **surrenders below 30 HP** but resumes sabotage elsewhere if she can.

- **Water Tools:** she can pull water from her body to make any level-0 item of up to 1 Bulk with few moving parts, such as thieves' tools. It turns back to water when she lets go.
- **Wave Steps:** her movement doesn't trigger reactions.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** The moral wrinkle. Chaotic neutral teaknis wrecking an old cult temple sounds harmless, until it drowns the dungeon the PCs stand in. Fight, help, or race them.

> **💡 GM tip:** Wave steps let the sneak slip past a front line without provoking Attacks of Opportunity to reach whoever menaces the hydrojinxer.

### R12 — Grand Entry *(Low 7)*

> *This wide room was obviously once a splendid entryway to the pyramid. A massive iron double door stands closed, although rivulets of mud and seawater leak in around its frame to cover the floor in dirty, foul-smelling water. A wide staircase leads up to the west. Alcoves to the north and south bear shattered statues, their broken pieces poking up through the water. The south alcove also has a massive hole in the ceiling.*

The ceiling hole is teakni work from R11, extending past the south wall into R16. No route leads from here into that vault, but a PC could climb to R11 and drop into R16.

**Teakni Saboteurs (2)** — p. 401, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp each.
- They alternate widening the hole and forcing the main doors to speed the flood.
- Likelier hostile than the R11 teaknis, but they accept offered help.
- Once fighting, relentless until they or their foes are destroyed.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A saboteur's waterspout ignores up to 10 Hardness when adjacent to an object (p. 401): how they wear through stone, and why these two are the real flooding threat.

### R13 — Vault Landing *(Trivial 7)*

> *Four double doors lead away from this flooded landing. Each is made of sturdy metal set into a stone frame and secured with a large lock plate.*

No vault door is locked: before Poppy's attack, **[Arahosko the Red](gm_glossary.md#arahosko-the-red)**, the galtzagorri wizard at her side in the War of the Shivering Behemoth, ritually unlocked every pyramid door at once to surprise the cult. Nobody relocked the vaults.

Only the **Deep Vault (R17)** door is decorated: an engraved gemstone shaped like a brilliant **5-pointed star**, the Jewel of the Indigo Isles. Plain stone, yet it matches the PCs' fragments. The last piece is likely inside.

**Vault Wards (4)** — Hazard 8, magical trap, one on each vault door (p. 211)
- **Stealth** DC 28 (trained)
- **Description:** an invisible rune on each door jolts anyone who touches it with petrifying magic.
- **Disable:** **DC 26 Nature** (expert) or ***dispel magic*** (4th level; **counteract DC 26**) to counteract the rune's magic, or **DC 26 Thievery** (expert) to safely erase it.
- **AC** 27; **Fort** +21, **Ref** +13; **Hardness** 16; **HP** 64 (BT 32); immune to critical hits, object immunities, precision damage.
- **Painful Petrification** (reaction; force, incapacitation). **Trigger:** a creature touches the door, by hand or with a tool. **Effect:** **6d6 force** damage and a **DC 26 Fortitude** save.
  - **Critical success:** no damage.
  - **Success:** half damage, and **slowed 1** for 1 round.
  - **Failure:** full damage, and **slowed 1** for 24 hours. It can try a new Fortitude save at the end of each hour to end the effect.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage, and **petrified** for 24 hours. It can try a new Fortitude save at the end of each hour to end the effect.
- **Reset:** 24 hours.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Level 8 with the [incapacitation trait](#the-incapacitation-trait), so level-7 PCs get no help on the save. Trained Perception is enough to find the runes ([hazards](gm_chapter_1.md#hazards)), so have the PCs Search.

### R14 — Energy Vault *(Moderate 7)*

> *Shimmering walls of force extend among six pillars, forming a glowing cage at the room's center. The walls are adorned with mosaics of great and terrible monsters being caged and controlled.*

Permanent walls of force held dangerous creatures or artifacts. At Poppy's attack the cage held a living variant **shale swarm** of orichalcum and crystal, long since starved.

The dead swarm drew four **temporal manifestations**, students of time and force. They've drained the cage until each of its **six walls** has **10 HP and Hardness 15**, and sapped the corpses' time-bending properties, leaving the orichalcum brittle and worthless.

**Temporal Manifestations (4)** — BB 110, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp each.
- Start inside the cage, curious, beckoning the PCs closer.
- Within a few rounds their time sense shows the PCs will soon **destroy the temple**; they attack to protect their research.
- **Time Step** carries them in and out of the cage for advantage.
- After three fall, the last **flees** for good.

**Treasure:** **without Monster Parts,** crystals in the dead swarm are worth **1,000 gp**.

> **💡 GM tip:** The manifestations are right: taking the shard dooms the temple. If a PC asks why they attacked, the truth makes a good chill.

### R15 — Empty Vault

> *This stone chamber has an ornate pedestal at the southern end carved to look like a mountain. As the room is flooded with murky water, this pedestal resembles an island rising from the sea. The top of the pedestal has a bowl-shaped indentation.*

Meant for a volcanic artifact the cult never got before Poppy's attack. Anything set in the bowl makes **illusory lava** bubble over the pedestal, dramatic and harmless. Nothing else is here.

### R16 — East Vault *(Moderate 7)*

> *This enormous room has several square pillars holding up the ceiling. The walls and all the pillars bear intricate mosaics of looming mountains, churning seas, and raging shale spitters in numerous sizes and configurations. The chamber's floor is obscured by brackish, muddy water leaking in from a hole in the ceiling in an alcove to the east.*

Meant for tribute once the cult ruled the Isles; always mostly empty.
- **Water:** a foot deep unless the PCs widened the opening or helped the saboteurs do it. **Difficult terrain** for Small and smaller.
- **The mosaics,** up close: terrified tiny crowds menaced by towering shale spitters or crushed by tidal waves, and gray-robed cultists riding the monsters or triumphant on mountaintops.

**Weak Hieroglyph Scorpion** — BB 9, 41, Creature 9. Initiative: Deception 35 when flattened, or Stealth +21. Monster Parts 90/200/360 gp.
- Placed before the vault held anything, it hides among the images as a scorpion-tailed shale spitter. Mosaic damage over time has weakened it; it stays vigilant.
- It keeps ambushing and stinging the teaknis, which is why they can't get in.
- When the PCs arrive it lurks in the **east alcove**, expecting another raid from above, and slinks along walls and ceiling to the best strike spot.
- **Doesn't surrender or flee**; **doesn't pursue** foes who leave.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The chapter never explains "Deception 35 when flattened." Check BB 41 for it before the session.

### R17 — Deep Vault *(Severe 7)* — the Tangerine Shard is here

> *Four square pillars hold up the ceiling in this imposing stone room. An alcove at the east end contains a pillar with a glowing yellow-orange gemstone across it. Small, yellow-orange flecks surround the pillar like a cloud of glitter. The flecks that touch the pedestal vanish into it, but the cloud gets no smaller.*

The whole Jewel once sat here, filling the temple with magic. Poppy took and broke it but left the **Tangerine Shard**, fearing the whole Jewel's removal would drop the temple on her at once.

Far weaker than the Jewel, the shard still powers the temple; its flecks soak into the pedestal and keep the sea out.
- **Once the shard leaves the pedestal,** the protection ends **permanently**: +1 flood stage **every minute** ([flood stages](#the-flooding-temple-chapter-subsystem)). Run.

**Creature:** Poppy had no time to bury the last cultists defending the Jewel. Their skeletons lay for centuries around their **high priest**, dead beside the Jewel from Poppy's stab to the heart. Over time, anguish and thirst for revenge raised her as a barely sapient undead, and she drew the others' bones into a **skeletal exosuit shaped like a dragon**, her humanoid body visible inside, Poppy's rapier wound still over her heart.
- Defends the gem with single-minded fury, **until destroyed**.
- Saves special vengeance for a PC who **looks or acts like Poppy**, else a **Poppy fanatic** PC descended from one of Poppy's disciples. *"Poppy... die!"* it rasps in many voices.
- **It smashes the temple as it moves:** it can go through pillars and walls, destroying them (walls leave difficult terrain), and if needed through floors and ceilings to chase the PCs anywhere, leaving a dragon-sized hole.
- Describe shaking, rumbling, maybe a little leaking water, with no effect yet. The temple collapses after the PCs take the prize.

**Skeletal Cult Dragon** — Creature 10, unique CE Huge amphibious skeleton undead. Variant skeletal monstrosity (BB 103). Stat block p. 214. Monster Parts 125/275/500 gp.
- **Perception** +16; darkvision
- **Skills** Athletics +23, Intimidation +18
- **Str** +7, **Dex** +3, **Con** +0, **Int** −3, **Wis** +0, **Cha** +0
- **AC** 29; **Fort** +18, **Ref** +17, **Will** +16; **HP** 160
- **Immunities** death effects, disease, paralyzed, poison, unconscious. **Resistances** cold 10, electricity 10, fire 10, piercing 10, slashing 10
- **Destructive Collapse** (divine, evocation): when it's destroyed, its bones collapse in a huge heap. Creatures adjacent take **10d6 slashing**, **DC 28 basic Reflex**.
- **Speed** 30 feet, swim 30 feet
- **Melee** jaws +23 (reach 20 feet), 2d8+13 piercing plus Grab
- **Melee** wing scythe +23 (agile, reach 15 feet, sweep), 2d6+13 slashing
- **Melee** foot +23 (reach 10 feet), 2d12+13 bludgeoning
- **Ranged** bone splinter +19 (range increment 60 feet), 2d6+13 piercing
- **Eat Marrow** (1 action), once per round. **Requirement:** it has a creature grabbed in its jaws. **Effect:** it chews the creature and drinks its marrow: **3d12+11 bludgeoning**, **DC 29 basic Fortitude**. It **regains HP equal to the damage dealt**.
- **Cultists' Cry** (2 actions; auditory, divine, enchantment, fear, mental), once per 10 minutes. It screams curses at Poppy von Barnacle and proclaims that all the Indigo Isles will soon belong to the Cult of the Shivering Behemoth. Creatures within **500 feet** who hear it, understanding or not, must succeed at a **DC 28 Will** save or be **frightened 2**. On a critical failure, **frightened 4** and **fleeing** for 1 round or until no longer frightened, whichever comes first. Whatever the result, creatures are immune for 24 hours.
- **Splinter Burst** (2 actions; divine, evocation): **7d10 slashing** in a **15-foot cone**, **DC 28 basic Reflex**. Can't use it again for 1d4 rounds.
- **Sweeping Slash** (2 actions): it Strides and makes two wing scythe Strikes, each at any point during the Stride. Its multiple attack penalty doesn't increase until after both.
- **Trample** (3 actions): Large or smaller, foot, **DC 29**.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** See [Grab, Trample, and sweep](#monster-abilities-grab-trample-sweep) and the [grabbed and fleeing conditions](gm_chapter_7.md#conditions-new-this-chapter). Jaws, Grab, and Eat Marrow fill its three-action turn: about 22 jaw damage plus about 30 from Eat Marrow on a failed save, and it regains the Eat Marrow damage as HP.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the grab loop:** Each Eat Marrow heals what it deals. If the party can't break the grab (Escape or forced movement), the fight drags. Point the grabbed player to Escape early.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — book typos in Cultists' Cry:** The immunity line names "the skeletal monstrosity's Screech," a base-creature leftover. Read it as **Cultists' Cry**.

> **💡 GM tip:** Resistances cover piercing, slashing, cold, electricity, and fire (10 each); bludgeoning and other types get through. Hint at it after a few resisted hits.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — who is the high priest?** This chapter's high priest is female, unnamed, and killed in R17 by Poppy's rapier. The cult article names the high priest Poppy's team slew as **[Jakorvo](gm_glossary.md#jakorvo)**, a male g'mayun (p. 372), and R10 has Poppy beating the leaders in the warding chamber. The book doesn't reconcile these. Make her Jakorvo's successor, a second high priest, or Jakorvo himself, and stay consistent.

**XP:** **80 XP** for recovering the Tangerine Shard.

**Treasure:** with the dragon destroyed, the PCs can take the shard and, with the temple flooding fast, should flee at once.

#### Tangerine Shard *(Item 7)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** held in 1 hand. **Bulk** negligible.
- Glittering orange-yellow crystal broken from a larger gem, darker red along one break, darker yellow along the other.
- Sheds tiny chips of heatless light: **bright light in a 20-foot radius** (dim for the next 20 feet), like a torch.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact): rub it to put out or relight the glow, until you use this again.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact), **once per day:** scrape a fingernail across it, and a dense haze of glittering yellow-orange particles surrounds you within a few inches. You're **concealed**, but you can't use it to Hide or Sneak, because the glitter gives your position away. The haze moves with you. While it lasts, you and your gear stay **dry, even underwater**. It doesn't let you breathe water. It fades after **3 rounds**.

> **💡 GM tip:** The daily haze is the escape trick. Remind its holder it keeps gear dry, not lungs.

#### If the PCs lose

Likeliest disaster: they pull the shard, flooding starts, and they lose it. It **glows**, easy to spot in rising water. If badly beaten PCs must rest and return, the book suggests the dragon **puts the shard back**, slowing the flood to a trickle (p. 215).

#### If the fight spreads

Retreating PCs draw the dragon through the bottom floor, crushing walls and maybe pulling in uncleared rooms. Book suggestions (p. 215):
- **Teakni saboteurs (R12):** watch and cheer from safety, but help if the PCs are losing; the PCs wrecked more in minutes than the teaknis did in weeks.
- **Vault wards (R13):** hit the dragon as readily as the PCs, which crafty PCs can exploit.
- **Temporal manifestations (R14):** flee at once if the dragon crashes in.
- **Hieroglyph scorpion (R16):** stays put, leaving intruders to the dragon. If the dragon destroys the wall the scorpion is in, the scorpion is destroyed too.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The dragon alone is Severe; the book warns extra foes likely make it far too hard. Keep the dragon central.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The PCs think they're finishing Poppy's treasure hunt. They're delivering the cult's relic back into the cult's reach. The dragon's cry that the Isles "shall soon belong to the Cult of the Shivering Behemoth" is closer to true than anyone aboard knows.

## Concluding the chapter

### Their Triumphant Return

Shard in hand, the PCs return to the ship (p. 215).
- The crew cheers; the return to Rumplank will be a celebration.
- **Prince Kulupi** is pleased and promises the king's favor.
- The long trip to Goldcrop Island allows rest, downtime, and **advancing to 8th level**.
- **Sea resources:** they can make one last attempt to gather marine resources on the way home (p. 117), confirmed as the last chance in Ch 9 (p. 216).

**Level up:** **level 8**, at the book's milestone of **collecting the final fragment** (end of Act 2). Short on XP? The book suggests a quest-completion award or encounters on the way back.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi has his own reason to be pleased: the Jewel is nearly whole. In Ch 9, Varagix uses Kulupi as his conduit to curse the Jewel as the four pieces combine (p. 217). Play his delight as a proud commander's, nothing more.

> **🧩 Sets up:** About a day out from Rumplank, the four fragments start pulling toward each other. An hour before docking they fly together into the whole Jewel, opening **Ch 9** and Part Three, *Battle at Barnacle Bay*.
