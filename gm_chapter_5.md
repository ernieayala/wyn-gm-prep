## The chapter in one minute

The PCs sail Poppy's ship, the [*Chromatic Queen*](gm_glossary.md#chromatic-queen), to [Seaview](gm_glossary.md#seaview) on [Bluebell Island](gm_glossary.md#bluebell-island) under [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi). The council sends them to the Academy of Tastes to cross-check Poppy's journal against explorer Gustavus Gollihew's maps. After a haunted archive and a possessed official, they have maps to all three missing Jewel fragments. Angry spirits plague Seaview, so the council asks for help with a surveyor's haunted maps, a hag-infested farm, and a haunted inn. The inn reveals the cause: **Councilor Pelborii**, whose protection ritual draws and enrages spirits. The council then sends them to Briargrove, where Poppy hid the **Aquamarine Wedge** behind traps and a rope golem. A parade and feast follow, and they sail on.

**By the end the PCs have:**
1. **Three fragment maps:** a cave on Bluebell Island, a cave on Moonshadow Island's south side, and Razorback Isle somewhere in Runaway Reef (Scene 2).
2. **Stopped the hauntings** by dealing with Pelborii (Scene 6).
3. **The Aquamarine Wedge**, the second Jewel fragment (Scene 7).
4. **Defense special resources** for the Chapter 12 siege ([Things to track](#things-to-track-for-later-chapters)).

**Party level:** starts at 4, ends at **5**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** It builds Act 2's home base (the ship, the crew, island-hopping) and hands over the Chapters 6–8 itinerary in one scene. Under the food-town comedy it plants the cult: Pelborii has true visions of the shale behemoth rising and her notes spell out the cult's plan, while the cult's leader accepts bluebell crates at the feast.

## Part Two setup: ship, crew, and travel

This covers all of Part Two (Chapters 5–8). Reuse it for every voyage.

### The ship deal

[King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather) wants the Chromatic Queen on a "victory tour" from Rumplank to Seaview and beyond (p. 112–113).

- After centuries in drydock, **restoration costs 2,000 gp**; outfitting alone is 250 gp or more.
- **The deal:** the royals pay if the PCs accept the **royal route** and **royal crew** for this first voyage. Back in Rumplank, the PCs can take full possession.
- **Royal crew means Kulupi commands.** Capable but green, he leaves most work to hardriggan bosun **Garnikor**, or to a suitable PC (like an indigo pirate) who follows his general orders.
- **PCs refuse to share authority?** Kulupi comes as envoy and advisor instead. Nothing else changes.
- **No Chromatic Queen (or prefer another ship)?** Swap in any ship. The deal still applies; almost nobody can pay those costs alone.

**Background special effect (indigo pirate):** a PC with a reputation can bring recruited crew. The royals suggest the PC serve as **first mate**, making practical decisions under Kulupi; the PC then becomes captain after the voyage with the Redfeathers' blessing. Kulupi praises good sailing; he can't act humble but avoids provoking a mutiny. If the PCs paid their own way and a PC captains with Kulupi as envoy, he struggles far more to hold back.

### Why Seaview first

The king plans to cross-check Poppy's vague maps against those of culinary explorer **Gustavus Gollihew**, a famous Seaview orpok who founded the **Academy of Tastes**, the Isles' most renowned university (p. 113). Seaview, a close trading partner, was the first stop anyway.

Kulupi meets the council to:
1. Show off the Chromatic Queen.
2. Buy **bluebells** and other useful resources.
3. Get the PCs into the Academy for research.

> **🔒 GM-only — the artifact's true history (p. 113–114):** Poppy and an army of heroes fought the cult in the War of the Shivering Behemoth. The cult meant to rule the Isles through **shale spitters**, stony isopods from tiny shale swarms to titanic shale behemoths. Poppy took the Jewel from cult headquarters on Razorback Isle, which sank beneath Runaway Reef with one piece in its deepest vault. Of the three she escaped with, she hid one in a **forest cave on Bluebell Island**, one **behind a puzzle-locked door in a cave near the Moonshadow Island coast**, and took the Obsidian Star to Rumplank with her treasure.

### The crew

The ship carries **thirty people, not counting the PCs** (p. 114). It's the PCs' temporary home for storing gear, resting, and [downtime](#downtime) like Crafting, retraining, or healing. Named crew:

| Crew member | Who they are | Hooks and services |
|---|---|---|
| 🔒 **Prince Kulupi** (CE male g'mayun prince 10) | Distant, competent commander | Superficially friendly, close to no one, PCs included. The crew is honored to serve him and blames him for anything that goes wrong, even weather |
| **Garnikor** (LN female hardriggan bosun 7) | Gruff bosun; the cat-o'-nine-tails at her hip almost never comes out | Ex-teamster sentenced to ship labor for hiding relatives' smuggled goods; promoted to bosun, sentence commuted. Terse, fair mentor; no rudeness or slacking. A little enchanted by the PCs. **Sells potions of water breathing** (useful in Chapter 8) |
| **Samblain** (NG male g'mayun doctor 6) | Handsome, flirtatious surgeon | Flirts with nearly anyone, stops if they're uncomfortable; all focus with a patient. Scolds the cooks for fatty food. **Medicine +15**; Treats Disease and Wounds for anyone aboard. Rarely fights. Might sell his few unusable **scrolls of *restoration*** in dire need |
| **Fippin and Mippin Humcakes** (NG male and nonbinary orpok cooks 4) | Twin cooks from Seaview's ships | Tasty meals from poor ingredients. Fippin (he/him) is chatty and shirks to gossip; Mippin (they/them) is quiet and diligent. They befriend orpok and cook PCs (like a bluebell chef) and may invite them to help. Fippin pesters PCs about the Academy, which neither attended |
| **"Slate" Hodda** (LG female kragrak scout 5) | Lookout in the tops | Talkative, but won't say why she left central Goldcrop's mountains. Nicknamed for ashy skin so dark it reflects almost no light. Spots reefs and sights. Has **dragon turtle scales** from a past attack she might sell to swimmers |
| **Leafling** (female [blood mouse](gm_glossary.md#blood-mouse), BB 14) | Ship's mascot | Mauve, cheeky, fat on scraps; Garnikor smuggled her aboard. Lost her proboscis, so she's hand-fed. Steals from Kulupi, sometimes off his plate; he wants her thrown overboard, so the crew hides her and slips her treats |

The other **two dozen sailors** are yours to name, plus any crew an indigo pirate PC recruited.

> **💡 GM tip:** Leafling alone dares pester the prince, and the crew bonds over grumbling about him. A crew that likes the PCs and rolls its eyes at Kulupi sets up Act 3.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Garnikor is a black sash (4th dan) in the Chapter 9 tournament and Barnacle Bay's default general in Chapter 12. Hodda, Garnikor, and Leafling tell stories en route to Moonshadow (Chapter 6). The synopsis suggests crew as replacement PCs after an Act 2 death.

### Travel between islands

Bluebell Island is **four days** away through safe waters (p. 114). The book covers days at sea with a few vignettes. If you track time:

| Leg | Time |
|---|---|
| Rumplank to Seaview | 4 days |
| Seaview to Moonshadow Isle | 3 days |
| Moonshadow Isle to Razorback Isle | 3+ days (see p. 193) |
| Razorback Isle to Rumplank | 9 days |

- Islands on the map are to scale; the water between is wider than drawn. Don't measure off the map.
- The expert crew means **storms and sea monsters pose no threat** unless you add them.
- The only place the PCs must steer is Runaway Reef (**Chapter 8**), uncharted waters.

### Seek Marine Resources

Garnikor suggests harvesting sea plants en route (p. 116–117). Optional; Kulupi doesn't order it.

**When:** once per leg: Goldcrop to Bluebell, Bluebell to Moonshadow, Moonshadow to Runaway Reef, Runaway Reef back to Goldcrop. It fills the leg but leaves time to rest.

**Seek Marine Resources** *(downtime, manipulate)*. Each participating PC makes **one** check per leg: **DC 20 Athletics, Crafting, Nature, or Survival**, or **DC 18 Sailing Lore**. Pool the Kelp Points.
- **Critical success:** 2 Kelp Points.
- **Success:** 1 Kelp Point.
- **Failure:** nothing.
- **Critical failure:** lose 1 Kelp Point.

The crew advises and teases but earns no points. A relevant spell or ability can give a circumstance bonus; if one is tailor-made for harvesting and storing kelp, you can grant 1 Kelp Point with no check.

| Kelp Points (4 PCs) | Resources that leg |
|---|---|
| 7+ | Red sargassum, netweed, and twinekelp |
| 4–6 | Netweed and twinekelp |
| 2–3 | Twinekelp |
| Fewer than 2 | Enough seaweed to restock the ship, but no resources |

- **Twinekelp** (common) makes rope. **Netweed** makes tough netting. **Red sargassum** (rarest) has air bladders; treated properly, it floats in the air.
- **Kelp Points reset to 0** after each leg.
- **More than four PCs:** raise each threshold to match.
- All three are Chapter 12 **defense special resources** (p. 315), on top of what the islands give.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Chapter 12 caps each at **4 bundles**, one per leg. Record every leg's haul.

### Ocean encounters

Optional color (p. 117–118). For the short Rumplank-to-Seaview leg, **roll 1d4**. After Seaview, roll **1d8 plus islands visited**, counting Seaview and Moonshadow (maximum +2).

| Roll | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Meteor Shower.** Brilliant on a clear night. The crew trades rumors and superstitions about it |
| 2 | **Tiny Island.** Uninhabited rock barely bigger than the ship. Might hold a clue about the next island |
| 3 | **Playful Winds.** Fickle elemental winds. **DC 20 Nature** to harness: 1 day off travel (2 on a critical success). Failure: winds leave. Critical failure: 1 day slower |
| 4 | **Sailor Rescue.** Dehydrated shipwreck survivor on a plank. Might carry a clue about the next island |
| 5 | **Leafling Tricks.** Kulupi catches Leafling shredding his underclothes and wants her executed for "seditious and humiliating mutiny." The crew asks the PCs to defend her |
| 6 | **Crew Conflict.** Two sailors fight over something petty, like the last grog ration. Kulupi and Garnikor want discipline; fast talkers can defuse it before her lash uncoils |
| 7 | **Doldrums.** Unnatural calm stalls the ship 1d4 days. The crew turns melancholy or superstitious |
| 8+ | **Storm.** Every PC helps with a relevant skill, or a Fortitude save to keep lunch down. **DC 18**, or **DC 16** with Sailing Lore |

**Storm outcomes:**
- **Critical success:** lasting crew respect and a nickname like Galebuster, Sealegs, or Knotmaster.
- **Success:** the PC helps keep the ship afloat.
- **Failure:** sickened 1, which can't be reduced for 1 day.
- **Critical failure:** as failure, and the ship is damaged. Repairs before leaving the next port cost **1 resource of any type**; with none, two weeks and 100 gp, and the crew is demoralized. Kulupi coldly blames this PC.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** A Storm critical failure can eat a defense special resource. The first leg's 1d4 can't roll a Storm; later legs can.

## PF2 rules new in this chapter

Chapter 1 covers [degrees of success](gm_chapter_1.md#degrees-of-success), [XP and threat](gm_chapter_1.md#encounter-difficulty-and-xp), [basic saves](gm_chapter_1.md#basic-saving-throws), [secret checks](gm_chapter_1.md#secret-checks-and-recall-knowledge), [flat checks](gm_chapter_1.md#flat-checks), [conditions](gm_chapter_1.md#conditions-youll-see-this-chapter), [terrain](gm_chapter_1.md#movement-and-terrain), [hazards](gm_chapter_1.md#hazards), and [Monster Parts](gm_chapter_1.md#monster-parts-treasure-system).

**XP at party level 4:** creature level 2 = 20 XP, 3 = 30, 4 = 40, 5 = 60, 6 = 80. A simple hazard is a fifth of a creature of its level.

### Downtime

Play measured in days: Crafting, retraining a feat or skill, recovering from injuries. Every voyage leg is downtime, and the ship serves for these activities (p. 114). **Seek Marine Resources** is this campaign's own downtime activity ([rules](#seek-marine-resources)).

### Aid

**Aid** helps another character's check. In combat it's a reaction prepared with an action on your turn; out of combat the GM sets the preparation. The helper rolls, usually **DC 20**:
- **Critical success:** +2 circumstance bonus to the ally's check (+3 if the helper is a master, +4 if legendary).
- **Success:** +1 circumstance bonus.
- **Critical failure:** −1 circumstance penalty.

**This chapter:** in H1, one PC leads the map cross-check and the others can Aid, though it isn't an encounter (p. 123).

### Sense Motive

A Perception check to spot lying or odd behavior, usually against the target's Deception DC unless the book sets one. The GM normally rolls it secretly.

**This chapter:** DC 15 to see the Sous Scholar is worried (Scene 2); DC 20 at the feast to see the councilors find Kulupi rude (Scene 8).

### Nonlethal attacks

Two fights here are against innocents, so the book gives a sidebar (p. 124):
- A **nonlethal attack** knocks a creature out instead of killing it.
- Weapons with the **nonlethal** trait, including fists, always deal nonlethal damage.
- A nonlethal attack with a weapon that **lacks** the trait takes a **−2 circumstance penalty** to the attack roll, as does a lethal attack with a nonlethal weapon.
- Nonlethal spells and effects, like *daze*, knock a creature out at 0 HP.

**This chapter:** the possessed Sous Scholar (H3) and Pelborii (K6). The K1 hearth hounds' fire damage is nonlethal too.

### The dying track

The Sous Scholar and Pelborii **fall unconscious and start dying like a PC** at 0 HP (p. 125, 141):
- At 0 HP: unconscious, **dying 1** (**dying 2** from a critical hit or its own critically failed save), plus its **wounded** value.
- Each turn's start: **recovery check**, a flat check against **DC 10 + dying value**. Critical success −2 dying, success −1, failure +1, critical failure +2.
- Damage while dying: +1 dying (+2 from a critical hit or its own critically failed save).
- Dies at **dying 4**, lowered by **doomed** (below).
- Healing to 1+ HP ends dying and adds **wounded 1** (or +1 wounded).
- Nonlethal damage to 0 HP knocks out **without** dying.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The Sous Scholar ends possession **doomed 1**, so he dies at dying 3. Stabilize him fast with *stabilize* or Administer First Aid (Medicine).

### Possession

The **killing intent** (BB 53) rides the Sous Scholar in H3 (p. 125):
- While he's possessed, it **isn't a separate combatant**.
- Possession lasts **3 rounds** from the start of combat, or until he hits 0 HP. Then the killing intent **lunges out** and fights alone until destroyed.
- Possessed, he has **22 temporary HP** and is **quickened** (extra action: Stride or Strike only).
- Afterward he loses both, deals **4 less damage** with melee Strikes, gains **+2 AC**, is **doomed 1**, and is **immune** to the killing intent's possession for 24 hours.

### Hazardous terrain

Damages anything moving through it. Briargrove's thorns are **greater difficult terrain and hazardous terrain**: each square costs 10 extra feet of movement and deals **2d6 piercing**, and vision ends at 5 feet (p. 142). Stick to paths.

### Conditions new this chapter

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Stupefied X** | −X status penalty to Int-, Wis-, and Cha-based checks and DCs, including Will saves, spell attacks, and spell DCs. Casting needs a **DC 5 + X flat check** or the spell is lost. From the ink trap (I1) and the Sous Scholar's critical hits (H3) |
| **Quickened** | One extra action at the start of each turn, limited by the effect. The possessed Sous Scholar can only Stride or Strike with it |
| **Doomed X** | Dies at dying 4 minus X. Drops by 1 after a full night's rest |
| **Stunned** | Can't act. "Stunned for 1 round" loses that round's actions. From calming the hearth hounds (K1) |

Also here: concealed ([Ch 2](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)).

### Request and Command an Animal

- **Request:** Diplomacy, ask a creature to do something ([attitudes and Request](gm_chapter_2.md#npc-attitudes-make-an-impression-and-request)).
- **Command an Animal:** Nature, give an animal an order.

Normally Request needs a friendly or helpful creature and Command targets Will DC. At K1 the book sets DCs to calm the hearth hounds instead: **DC 23**, or **DC 18** for a PC whose Recall Knowledge critically succeeded (p. 137).

### Ambushes and Stealth for initiative

Hidden PCs usually roll **Stealth for initiative**. Any enemy whose **Perception DC** a PC beats hasn't noticed that PC yet. Deception actions like Lie and Create a Diversion also target **Perception DC**; actions like Demoralize and Make an Impression target **Will DC**.

**This chapter:** if the PCs set a trap in Event 14, the hags' **Perception DC and Will DC drop from 21 to 19** until they notice trouble (p. 135), easing both ambush and ruse.

### Flanking

A creature is **flanked**, and flat-footed (off-guard in the Remaster) to the flankers, when a line between two enemies' space centers passes through opposite sides or corners of its space. Both must be able to act and wield melee weapons or make unarmed attacks. The Event 14 hags **don't coordinate flanking** (p. 135); your PCs can.

### Covered in earlier chapters

- **Elite creatures:** [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#elite-and-weak-creatures). The **elite dishrag dervish** (K3) is Creature 5 (BB 9 adjustments, BB 101 base).
- **Concealment:** [Chapter 2](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected). Barn-fire smoke at J6 conceals everyone in the barn; Event 14's tall bluebells conceal Medium or smaller creatures.
- **Invested items:** [Chapter 4](gm_chapter_4.md#artifacts-and-invested-items). The **Aquamarine Wedge** must be invested to grant water breathing.

## Scene 1 — The voyage and arrival in Seaview

- **Where:** at sea, then Seaview's harbor and the **High Chambers**, the council hall on a bluff.
- **Who:** Kulupi, the crew, the [Seaview Council](#the-seaview-council).
- **Goal:** the council sends the PCs to the Academy of Tastes.

### Read aloud (opening, p. 114)

> *The sea is all around you, with Rumplank at your stern and Seaview, the largest settlement on Bluebell Island, at your aft. The crew are all pleased to be sailing on such a fine day in well-traveled waters with heroes on board and Prince Kulupi in command.*

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book's "at your aft" is wrong; aft and stern are both the back. Seaview is ahead, so read it as "bow."

During the four days, run the [crew](#the-crew), [Seek Marine Resources](#seek-marine-resources), and an [ocean encounter](#ocean-encounters) (1d4).

### Recall Knowledge (secret check)

**Secret DC 12 Society or Indigo Isles Lore** for each PC (p. 114). A **bluebell chef** gets the critical success without rolling.
- **Critical success:** success, plus: a **four-member council** runs Seaview, each member over one part of economy or society. They squabble but work hard for the town.
- **Success:** Bluebell Island is known for orpoks, excellent food, and the namesake bluebells, used for rope, dye, and weaving.
- **Failure:** Not much. Maybe it's named after flowers?
- **Critical failure:** A shadowy council of pleasant-seeming backstabbers rules Seaview and quietly, permanently disposes of anyone who displeases it.

Common knowledge: Bluebell Island is mostly [orpoks](gm_glossary.md#orpok), Seaview is one of the Isles' largest towns, its restaurants are excellent, and ocean-going ships prefer to load food here.

### Arrival

The ship docks on a sunny morning, and a self-important orpok harbormaster logs it in his thick registry. For the Chromatic Queen, cheering orpoks line the docks, congratulating everyone, even the harbormaster, who holds his registry high. Kulupi heads for the High Chambers and wants the PCs with him (p. 118).

> *Seaview is a bustling port city with tall buildings of stone and brick, and there is a palpable excitement among the crowd. The characteristic smells of fine cooking fill the air: fresh bread, spiced meats, the heady smells of rich cheese, as well as many more. The orpoks of Seaview are known as much for their cooking as for their industriousness.*
>
> *Seaview's ruling council meets in the High Chambers on a bluff overlooking the city. The walk to the High Chambers isn't long, but it's long enough for the word of distinguished visitors to spread. Functionaries at the High Chambers are quick to usher you into a meeting with the Seaview Council and introduce them as Pumbus Narheel, Gunlu Pongo, Monnino Gollihew, and Pelborii Karnihar.*

Seaview is on good terms with Rumplank, and the councilors already know the PCs and guess why they came. They confirm the PCs' deeds (the Chromatic Queen, the treasure map), which shows the players their fame has spread. Once the prince or PCs explain they need to check Poppy's journal, or the Star's visions, against the Isles' best maps, the councilors exchange knowing nods:

> *The most corpulent council member, Councilor Monnino, removes his small hat and holds it to his chest in an ostentatious display of reverence, "Of course, we all know of Gustavus Gollihew, founder of Seaview. He was an unequaled explorer as well as an unequaled epicurean, collecting recipes from across the isles. No other orpok has traveled as far or done as much as that illustrious personage."*
>
> *The g'mayun member, Councilor Pelborii, fluffs her feathers and adds icily, "Three full sentences and you haven't yet mentioned that Gustavus Gollihew is your ancestor, Monnino? You're afflicted with uncharacteristic humility today."*
>
> *The most academic-looking orpok on the council, Councilor Gunlu, ignores her colleague's snide remark and interjects, "Gustavus founded the great university called the Academy of Tastes. Its graduates include the best cooks and brightest scholars in Seaview."*
>
> *"Including yourself, Gunlu," grumbles the g'mayun.*
>
> *The last council member, Councilor Pumbus, is clearly running low on patience. He clears his throat to gain attention and harrumphs, "But to the matter at hand, esteemed visitors. Although the Academy of Tastes has hundreds, perhaps even thousands, of Gustavus's recipes, expanded by generations of scholars, it also holds Gustavus's enormous collection of maps he drew while exploring the islands. You should consult its cartographic archives. Tell the university staff that we've sanctioned your visit, and they should accommodate you."*

Kulupi stays to negotiate "ruler to rulers." He asks the PCs to research at the Academy and return here, if only to thank the council, sighing that he'll likely still be here (p. 119, 121).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — two spellings:** The book uses both "Gustavus" and "Gustavius" Gollihew. Same person; pick one.

### The Seaview Council

| Councilor | Stat label | Portfolio | At the table |

## Scene 2 — The Academy of Tastes

- **Where:** the Academy of Tastes campus. Map on p. 120.
- **Who:** **Sous Scholar Limbin Gumnammar** (he/him), a ghostwriter, two gossiping students, campus security, and a hidden killing intent.
- **Goal:** cross-check the maps and leave with the locations of the three fragments.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** It delivers the Act 2 itinerary and escalates Seaview's spirit problem in three steps (enraged ghost, murder rumors, possessed administrator). The PCs only learn something is wrong; they can't solve it here.

Stone towers rise over the city; the clocktower rings at each of the **seven mealtimes** many orpoks observe, not on the hour. The Academy was founded 200 years ago in the first wave of orpok settlement by Gustavus, a scholar, cartographer, and gourmand who worked out the gastronomic mathematics behind great recipes (p. 121).

**Background special effects:**
- **Bluebell chef:** a PC with publicized recipes gets a luncheon invitation from professors.
- **Indigo trailblazer:** thrilled to handle a legendary cartographer's maps, the PCs' main goal anyway.

**Academy features:** stone and brick, polished dark wood paneling, statues, portraits, and plaques of past professors, gardens of edible flowers and herbs. Rooms are **15 feet high**, lantern-lit into the evening. Doors have push plates, not knobs. Usually busy around the clock, the campus now often looks empty because the trouble keeps people out of public areas.

### A bad taste of trouble

Earlier today a student murdered the beloved **Dean de Cuisine** (he/him), escaped custody, and is being hunted by the small security staff. The dean's second, the Sous Scholar, insists he has it under control; fearing panic, he keeps classes running with no announcement. Staff are scared; students only sense something's off (p. 122).

The Sous Scholar meets the PCs at the entry arch and asks their business.
- **DC 15 Perception to Sense Motive** ([Sense Motive](#sense-motive)): he's worried about something.
- **Critical success:** his eyes keep flicking around; his worry has nothing to do with the PCs.

Relieved that they only want maps:

> *"Maps?" says the Sous Scholar, patting the sweat on his forehead with what looks like a tea towel. "Why, we have several! Gustavus Gollihew, our illustrious founder, had quite a collection of them, and they're now in the archive wing of the library. There's..." the Sous Scholar pauses for a moment, raising his hand to his quivering chin. "Well, technically, I suppose the archive wing is haunted. But the ghost there doesn't hurt anyone, he just gets peevish and tries to scare people away. If you're firm with him, he'll leave you to your work.*
>
> *"Of course, we have rules in the archive wing because of the old documents there. Absolutely no water, no fire, and," here, he swallows as though about to deliver some very bad news, "no eating."*

He gives directions and trots off to check on security.

> **🔒 GM-only — the killer (p. 122):** A possessing spirit, a **killing intent**, made the student do it. It's one of many spirits Pelborii's ritual drew here; the ritual also turns harmless spirits dangerous.

### H1 — Archive Wing *(Moderate 4)*

> *Dark wooden shelves are built into every wall of this large room, even around the thick pillars that undoubtedly support the upper levels of this old university building. Built for Gustavus's collection, the wing became a dump for every dull book, thesis, and bland recipe collection someone wanted forgotten. Hardly anyone visits.

**Ghostwriter** — BB 37, Creature 6. Initiative: Perception +14. Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.
- Born from the neglected collection over a century ago. It used to be merely peevish, editing the few magical texts and scaring people off; a firm, loud voice sent it back into a book.
- **Pelborii's disruption** filled it with rage. Only its tie to the wing keeps it from rampaging across campus. The PCs are the first visitors since.
- When the PCs search the shelves, it bursts from a dull magic almanac and attacks. It favors ***magic missile*** and ***telekinetic projectile***, and its ghostly quill in melee.
- It **fights until destroyed** but won't pursue outside the wing. Rejuvenation returns it in a few days, long after the PCs leave.

> **📏 Threat math:** one level-6 creature against a level-4 party is 80 XP, Moderate.

### Reviewing the maps

Two steps (p. 123):
1. **Find the maps:** **1d4 hours**, then **DC 19 Perception** or a relevant Lore. Several PCs can search at once; each success beyond the first cuts 1 hour.
2. **Cross-check with the journal:** another **1d4 hours**, then **DC 19 Nature, Society, or Survival**, or **DC 17** with a relevant Lore like Indigo Isles Geography Lore. It can't be split up: one PC leads; others can [Aid](#aid).

- **Failed checks:** keep going until success; the clock runs.
- **Messy research:** if the PCs used acid or fire on the ghostwriter or broke archive rules, each step takes **1d4+1 hours** from scorched text and spills.
- **More than 8 hours in the wing:** add a **third campus security guard** to H3.
- **XP:** **30 XP** for pinpointing the locations in **8 hours or fewer**.

**What the maps show** (p. 123):
- **Bluebell Island:** a cave in dense woodland a few hours' walk from Seaview.
- **Moonshadow Island:** a cave fairly near a south-side beach. Maybe even Poppy avoided going deep into that ill-omened island.
- **Razorback Isle:** a small, rocky island in **Runaway Reef**, hazardous shallows sailors avoid. On no recent map.
- The maps are specific but don't show what's inside the caves.

**Treasure:** a ***scroll of glitterdust*** and a ***scroll of searing light***, forgotten on the shelves; diligent research turns them up, and *detect magic* finds them at once. A note shows a long-dead owner, not the school.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The Moonshadow map leads to [Moonshadow Island](gm_glossary.md#moonshadow-island) (Chapters 6–7); the Razorback map to [Runaway Reef](gm_glossary.md#runaway-reef) (Chapter 8).

## Scene 4 — Clean Lines Surveying

- **Where:** Allasha's office near the High Chambers. Map on p. 128.
- **Who:** **Allasha the Surveyor** (LG female orpok cartographer 5) and two **yomhibdis**.
- **Goal:** clear the yomhibdis and find the clue pointing at the Wavewatcher Inn.

Allasha surveys nearly every property around Seaview, so trusted that her judgment replaces official city maps. When two farmers came to settle a dispute, her copy of their line wove back and forth in a way everyone knew was wrong, and other maps had changed too. Doubt about her has hit morale island-wide (p. 127–128).

> **🔒 GM-only:** Two **yomhibdis**, ghostly creatures that love redrawing written works, lurk in her plat maps. They enjoy her distress and will defend the shop they now consider theirs. Pelborii's rituals drew them, unknown to them.

### I1 — Office Entry

> *This tidy office has a reception area to the south and a meeting room to the north, beyond a velvet privacy curtain that's currently open. Another privacy curtain—although this one is mostly closed—leads to a workroom located further back into the shop's interior. Detailed and technical maps of all kinds adorn every one of the walls, each of them stamped with the same wax seal.*

Allasha wrings her hands here, in a tidy green and black tunic with armbands and a silver circlet holding back her large ears. She mutters that she can't take clients, then recognizes the PCs and turns sharp and professional:

> *"I really appreciate you coming by. Here's the key issue: Seaview relies upon my maps, and my maps have become somehow unreliable. I don't mean that my newest maps aren't any good—my skills are as sharp as ever. I mean, existing maps, older maps, have changed. I can't recall every line and corner I've ever drawn, of course, but I know my style. Most of the ones I've pulled out—not all of them, but most of them—are in a different style, like they've been drawn with a clumsier hand. Perhaps someone is replacing them? Or somehow changing the ink on the page? I'm at a loss to see what's happening, and I could use some help."*

If pressed, she realizes the workroom's **south shelves** are unchanged; the **plat map cabinets on the north side** are not.

### Hazard — Ink Trap *(Hazard 6, simple, magical)*

The yomhibdis trapped the workroom (I2) curtain, unknown to Allasha, who politely gestures for the PCs to go first. If they make her go first, she **critically fails** the save and stays unconscious as long as she's stupefied, leaving the PCs alone (p. 129).
- **Stealth DC 23 (expert).** Ink spots on the curtain's back.
- **Disable:** **DC 23 Arcana, Occultism, or Thievery (trained)** to fold the curtain so the darts are harmless.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +11; **HP** 56 (BT 28); immune to critical hits, object immunities, and precision damage.
- **Ink Darts (reaction).** **Trigger:** a creature touches or moves the curtain to I2. **Effect:** solid ink darts deal **7d4+16 piercing** to the triggering creature, which makes a **DC 24 Reflex** save:
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage and **stupefied 1** for 1 round.
  - **Failure:** full damage and **stupefied 2** for 1 minute.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage and **stupefied 2** for 1 hour.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Not a basic save: even a success stupefies, hurting casters right before I2 ([stupefied](#conditions-new-this-chapter)). Spotting needs expert Perception, so most parties trigger it.

> **📏 Threat math:** a simple hazard two levels above the party is 16 XP (a fifth of 80).

### I2 — Workroom *(Moderate 4)*

> *This workroom is crowded with deep shelves and cabinets. A table with blank parchment, ink, and cartographer's tools is shoved against a rear door. Low shelves filled with maps and ledgers line the south wall, while taller cabinets line the north wall. One of the cabinet drawers rattles, then is still.*

- **The back door** is blocked by the table, which the yomhibdis pushed there so nobody could escape once they started feeding.
- **The map on the table** shows the **Dardus Farm**, its bluebell-field lines zigzagging oddly. **DC 19 Nature, Occultism, or Religion:** the new lines carry a subtle magical repulsion that would keep peaceful nature spirits and friendly wood [eldamon](gm_glossary.md#eldamon) off the farm. Allasha confirms she didn't draw them.

> **🔒 GM-only:** The lines are the yomhibdis' unconscious echo of Pelborii's effects; they don't know her or her plans (p. 129).

**Yomhibdis (2)** — BB 122, Creature 4. Initiative: Stealth +11. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp each.

- They attack everyone **except Allasha**, saved for later eating.

## Scene 5 — Dardus Farm

- **Where:** the Dardus family farm, 2 miles outside Seaview. Map on p. 130.
- **Who:** three **feld hags** and their tar hounds, the surviving Darduses.
- **Goal:** rescue the family and kill the hags.

> **⚠️ Content warning (p. 131):** The book describes the victims in grisly detail, kept in running text so you can summarize. If your group prefers, skip the bodies' state and say the farmers are dead.

The Darduses are among Bluebell Island's most established farm families, humble and hardworking, and their bluebells matter to the island's economy. They own 40 acres around the farmhouse, give surplus wealth to charity, and border wild country on two sides.

> **🔒 GM-only (p. 131–132):** Benevolent spirits of camaraderie and hard work, plus several friendly wood eldamon, always protected the farm. Pelborii's meddling made them vanish, letting a coven of three **feld hags** in for the first time. They killed a few Darduses, captured others, and scattered the rest. ### J4 — Master Bedroom *(Low 4)*

- **If the ruse is challenged,** she takes true form and attacks; the Change Shape also gets her out of bed.
- Shrieking about "attacking a sick old granny," she uses birch whip and claws and **neither retreats nor surrenders**.
- The children watch from the bathroom doorway, unharmed.

**Feld Hag** — BB 30, Creature 5. Initiative: Perception +11. Monster Parts 18/45/80 gp.

The barn hag's two **tar hounds** herded the farmers to the back. When the bravest fought back, the hag killed her and mashed her in the butter churn to cow the rest, tied the others in the hayloft under the hounds' guard, and joined the hunt in the fields.

- **Fire hazard:** each **fire** effect a PC uses here triggers a **secret DC 11 flat check**. On a success the hay and dust catch; a round later smoke gives everyone in the barn **concealment** for **1d4 minutes** ([concealment](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)). - **Tar globs:** **DC 20 Perception** spots hot tar dribbled into the hay from the hounds' leaps.
- **Too much noise:** the hounds drop from the loft with **Tar Hound's Leap**, unharmed and dripping tar. Once fighting starts, the captives shout, some to be freed, some begging the PCs to save the children.

**Hearth Hounds (2)** — BB 40, Creature 4. Initiative: Perception +13. Monster Parts 12/27/50 gp each.

- **Failure:** Hearth hounds aren't normally malicious; something is agitating them.

**XP:** **80 XP** for calming the hounds and avoiding or stopping the fight, as if won.

### K6 — Private Room *(Moderate 4)*

**The door** has a flimsy inside latch: **DC 19 Thievery** to Pick a Lock or **DC 19 Athletics** to Force Open. On a **failed** attempt, Pelborii hears and casts ***mage armor*** (+1 AC).

- *"The diamond is the key, but only once it is made whole."*

"Cast beneath the sea" is the cult temple sunk at Runaway Reef (Chapter 8). "Made whole" is the Jewel the PCs are assembling. Let players copy the phrases; Act 3 explains them.

**Recall Knowledge (map):** the **south wall** carvings form a map of Seaview. For anyone who looks, roll **secret DC 19 Society** or **DC 14 Seaview Lore or Indigo Isles Geography Lore**. **+1 circumstance** if the party visited Clean Lines Surveying **or** the Dardus Farm, **+2** if both (p. 139).
- **Critical success:** Four places are marked: Clean Lines Surveying, the Dardus Farm, the Wavewatcher Inn, and the Academy of Tastes, joined by lines into an occult symbol. The PC can attempt the symbol check and **can't get worse than a success**.
- **Success:** as critical success, but the symbol check is **DC 19**, not 14, and can fail.
- **Failure:** the four places are visibly marked, but no occult meaning; no symbol check.
- **Critical failure:** the markings mean Pelborii plans a ritual to rip the High Chambers off its bluff and throw it into the sea.

**Recall Knowledge (symbol):** only after succeeding above. **Secret Occultism or Religion**, **DC 14** after a critical success, **DC 19** after a success.
- **Critical success:** The rune is meant to ward the city against malevolent spirits but is drawn wrong, so it **attracts new spirits and agitates the ones already here**. The PC also gains **+1 circumstance bonus to AC and saves against Pelborii's spells**.
- **Success:** as critical success, without the bonus.
- **Failure:** It's a rune of protection against spirits.
- **Critical failure:** The rune reveals evil occult foundations under Seaview that slowly warp the mind of anyone who spends a long time here. Short-lived orpoks are likely safe; other ancestries are at serious risk.

**Pelborii's reaction:** she's reinforcing her "protection runes" against the titanic earth spirit of her visions. Surprised, she decides the council sent the PCs to drag her away and insists her work protects the town, her claims growing wilder the longer she talks.
- **Fight:** hostile moves, or blaming her for the spirits, convince her they mean to stop her; she **attacks at once**, using her spells well and staying out of melee.
- **Talk her down:** if no fight breaks out, **DC 22 Diplomacy** to Make an Impression or **DC 22 Deception** to Lie gets her listening. When the PCs show that the south-wall symbol has errors and protects no one, she realizes she's done far more harm than good and **collapses in grief** (p. 140).

> **⚠️ Gotcha — don't kill her:** She's a sitting councilor with no ill intent; killing her, even in self-defense, reflects badly on the PCs and Kulupi. Like the Sous Scholar, she falls **unconscious and dying** at 0 HP ([dying track](#the-dying-track)). Remind players about [nonlethal attacks](#nonlethal-attacks) and the −2 penalty (p. 139, 141).

> **💡 GM tip:** Talking her down works best after the symbol check, since the PC can name the error. Otherwise let players argue from what they've seen: hounds, dervish, hags, yomhibdis, the spiral on Allasha's maps.

### L7 — Gemstone Cave *(Severe 4)*

> *The narrow tunnel opens into a natural cavern that's been worked into a roughly square shape. Loose coils of rope are piled on the floor and hang from the ceiling twelve feet above. A wooden skull dangles from a hanging rope at the entrance, its forehead carved with the words, "Thieves Keep Out!"*

The **Aquamarine Wedge** sits on a pedestal in an east alcove, out of sight of the entrance. Poppy trusted Briargrove and her protections to keep it safe. When a PC sees the pedestal:

> *A circular stone pillar rests on the ground, rising about three feet from the floor. The top of the pedestal is flat and about two feet wide. Old carvings depicting a gemstone broken into four pieces encircle the pedestal. Atop it sits a blue-green piece of crystal shaped like a star. It reflects light across the top of the pedestal in shimmering flickers, making it seem like the crystal is floating on a shallow bowl of water.*

### Hazard — Swinging Cutlass Traps (3) *(Hazard 5, simple, mechanical)*

One in each of three marked spaces: a cutlass on a rope in a ceiling slot, released by a tripwire in the dirt floor (p. 145).
- **Stealth DC 23 (trained)**; hard to spot among all the rope.
- **Disable:** **DC 23 Thievery (expert)** to cut the tripwire safely.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +17, **Ref** +9; **Hardness** 13, **HP** 52 (BT 26); immune to critical hits, object immunities, and precision damage.
- **Cutlass Drop (reaction).** **Trigger:** the tripwire is pulled or cut. **Effect:** a cutlass Strike against each creature in the tripwire's square and every adjacent square, no multiple attack penalty.
  - **Melee** cutlass +19 (forceful), 2d6+9 slashing.
- **Reset:** only by the rope golem's scraps (below).

**Rope Golem** — BB 77, Creature 6. Initiative: **Deception 22** (its Impersonate Rope result, not rolled). Monster Parts 30/65/125 gp.
- A rope heap across from the alcove rises to fight **once the PCs have triggered or bypassed the traps**.
- **At 100, 75, and 50 HP**, rope scraps slither off it like snakes, instantly and at no action cost:
  - Any PC it has **Tangled Up** automatically Escapes as the scraps leave.
  - The scraps drag a cutlass from a disabled trap (or an untriggered one, if none are disabled) up into the ceiling ropes, **resetting a trap in a square adjacent to a PC**.
  - The PCs know of the reset trap only if they beat its Stealth DC.
  - Cutlasses the PCs **tuck away or break** can't be reset.
- It fights until destroyed and **never leaves the cave**. If the PCs retreat, it resets every trap and stays in golem form.

> **📏 Threat math:** golem (level 6) 80 XP; each level-5 simple hazard a fifth of 60, 12 XP, 36 for three. Total 116, about Severe.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — resets surprise the players:** A reset trap appears next to a PC without warning. Track positions at each threshold and roll their Perception against DC 23 before they move.

**Treasure:**
- **The Aquamarine Wedge** (below). Taking it is **completely safe**, though PCs may be paranoid after last time's collapsing mountain. If they stall, you can have the fight jostle it off the pillar to drop harmlessly. The "water" is a trick of the light.
- A coil of rope near the pedestal is a **moderate rope of climbing** (*Advanced Player's Guide*).
- **If not using Monster Parts:** a small sea chest with **2,000 sp**.

**XP:** **80 XP** for recovering the Aquamarine Wedge.

### Aquamarine Wedge *(Item 7)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** affixed to armor or an item worn around the neck. **Bulk** negligible.
- Shimmering blue and sea-green crystal, once part of a larger whole; one broken edge deep dark blue, the other pale green.
- **Invested and affixed** to something worn at the neck (necklace, choker, or armor), it lets the wearer **breathe water as well as air** ([invested items](gm_chapter_4.md#artifacts-and-invested-items)).

> **🧩 Sets up:** Water breathing matters in **Chapter 8**'s search for the cult temple sunk beneath Runaway Reef, where the book says Garnikor might sell potions of water breathing to the rest of the party.

### If the PCs lose

Poppy ordered the golem to **dump unconscious survivors elsewhere in Briargrove**, lesson presumably learned. The PCs can rest and retry with better knowledge. Fail again, and her orders allow no third chance (p. 146).

## Scene 8 — Triumphant Return

Grupple, or another hinterlands orpok, saw the PCs leave Briargrove and spread the word. To reward them and lift the town's spirits, the council throws a **parade and a feast** (p. 146).
- Banner-waving crowds line the streets. Confectioners, friends of Pumbus, sell **lollipops glazed with the PCs' faces** faster than they can make them.
- Gunlu leads the short parade without stealing their glory, and all but begs reluctant PCs to join: Seaview needs this.
- People the PCs helped wave along the route: **Allasha**, **Mupto Burgin**, some **Darduses**.

### The feast

> *An airy plaza at the end of the parade route bears several large tables groaning under the weight of food placed upon them. Orpok cooks are bringing yet more food to these tables, and Sous Scholar Limbin Gumnammar directs them where to place the victuals based on color, flavor, and—most importantly—temperature. Councilor Monnino oversees the workers, who are bringing chairs and place settings to wedge in among all the food, providing enough room for the surrounding crowd to all find places to eat but with place settings of honor at the head of the impressive spread.*

Hundreds attend: Kulupi, the crew, all the councilors, most Academy staff, and more. Pelborii comes if the PCs turned her in, haggard but recovering quickly (p. 147).

The Sous Scholar sorts the food into the Academy's three temperature categories, and each PC is expected to try all three main dishes:

| Dish | What it is | Check (pick one) |
|---|---|---|
| **Hamlin's Hots** | Steaming meatballs in bubbling garlic sauce | DC 18 Society or DC 20 Fortitude |
| **Caleb's Colds** | Creamy buttermilk ice cream with frozen berries | DC 18 Medicine or DC 20 Fortitude |
| **Larry's Lukewarms** | Tiny cold-cut sandwiches whose pickles have gone a bit sour | DC 18 Survival or DC 20 Fortitude |

- **Failure:** sickened value rises by 1 for the rest of the feast (2 on a critical failure).
- **Background special effect (Rumplank reveler):** **+2 circumstance bonus** to all three.
- The feast runs late. Unsickened PCs are congratulated at the end; sickened ones get sympathetic looks and a few chuckles.

### Loose ends and resources

**Spongeberries:** if Grupple didn't get them, the PCs can give them to the council before or during the feast. Monnino makes a show of presenting them to the Sous Scholar so the Academy's best dessert chefs can bake a memorable pie for a later feast.

**Resources** (p. 147):
- **Bluebell flowers:** **one large crate per mission completed** (up to 3), given to Kulupi, who publicly congratulates the PCs for earning resources for Rumplank and strengthening ties with Seaview.
- **Culinary supply kit:** from Pumbus, if the council got the spongeberries.
- **Briarthorn:** from Grupple, if he got the spongeberries. Upgrades a spike wall into a **bramble wall** in Chapter 12.
- **Both:** if the council got the berries **and** the PCs took care of Grupple, such as paying his family's 100 gp.

**Kulupi's speech** thanks Seaview for these resources "during such difficult times" but makes clear he hopes for bigger harvests in future years. The councilors smile politely. **DC 20 Perception to Sense Motive:** they think the prince is being a bit rude.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi accepts the bluebells at the same feast as the councilor whose visions describe his cult's plan. Play a stiff royal, gracious in public but plainly wanting more.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book promises "a few loose ends" but describes only the spongeberries. Two Seaview threads remain open in the text if you want more: Monnino's missing inn owner and the Darduses' dinner invitation.

**Background special effect (bluebell chef):** the Sous Scholar offers the PC an **honorary professorship** and says the Academy would be honored if they'd consider becoming the new **Dean de Cuisine** after their adventure.

## Concluding the chapter

The ship sails on the **morning tide**; however late the feast runs, Kulupi expects the PCs and crew ready. Orpok dockworkers help load the bluebell crates and other resources (p. 147).

**Milestone:** recovering the Bluebell Island fragment. Leaving Seaview, the PCs **level up to 5** ([milestones](gm_story_synopsis.md#levels-and-milestones)).

> **💡 GM tip:** Short of 1,000 XP? The book suggests a story award for the island or a few extra encounters between Chapters 5 and 6; an [ocean encounters](#ocean-encounters) roll en route to Moonshadow is an easy one.

**Next chapter:** a three-day voyage to [Moonshadow Island](gm_glossary.md#moonshadow-island). Run [Seek Marine Resources](#seek-marine-resources) for that leg.
