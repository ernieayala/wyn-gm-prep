## The chapter in one minute

The [Jewel of the Indigo Isles](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles) fuses, cursed, as the PCs sail home to Rumplank's delayed [Founder's Day](gm_glossary.md#founders-day) festival. [Prismatic Colvi](gm_glossary.md#prismatic-colvi) offers to suppress the curse. The gods [Amon](gm_glossary.md#amon) and [Castilli](gm_glossary.md#castilli) appear, and Castilli breaks a cult ritual on the guard. While the PCs fight saboteurs and arsonists, [Princess Atriella](gm_glossary.md#princess-atriella), [Prince Kulupi](gm_glossary.md#prince-kulupi), Colvi, and the Jewel vanish, and the trail leads into the Crawling Caves.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **Brought the Jewel home**, likely handing it to Colvi (Scene 1).
2. **Competed in the festival** for prizes and fame (Scene 2).
3. **Met Amon and Castilli** and learned of the ritual (Scene 3).
4. **Beaten the saboteurs and arsonists**, maybe saving the historian Monbak (Scenes 4–5).
5. **Taken the king's plea** and **found the cult's trail** (Scenes 6–7).

**Party level:** starts at **8**, ends at **8**. The next level comes in Chapter 10, on meeting [Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby).

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** One day of glory, then the loss of the Jewel, the heirs, and the sense of winning. The festival shows the city they'll defend in Chapter 12; the cult wins because the PCs acted like heroes.

## Part Three setup

Part Three, *Battle at Barnacle Bay*, has no introduction; its first pages (p. 216–219) open Chapter 9 and set up Chapters 9–12. Read this first.

### The Jewel comes together (p. 216)

- **Aboard ship:** "good" grog flows. Kulupi gives each PC a bottle of fine rum and sends word ahead on a magical seabird made with his signet ring.
- **No more marine resources:** the last check was at the end of Part Two.
- **The pieces won't fit** however the PCs arrange them.
- **About a day out:** any PC wearing a piece feels a growing tug toward the others.
- **An hour before docking:** the pieces fly together, smashing out of containers, fuse into one glimmering jewel, hover, and float to the ground.

> **🔒 GM-only — what the cult is doing:** As the Jewel forms, the cult's ritual is already under way deep in a mountain on western Goldcrop. The demon [Varagix](gm_glossary.md#varagix-the-glimmering) taught his trusted followers to command gem-loving shale spitters and raise the corpse of a **shale behemoth** beneath Goldcrop. He wants Poppy's city in rubble, a new nation on its ashes, then the rest of the Isles.

### The curse (p. 217)

Through **Prince Kulupi as a conduit**, Varagix corrupts the Jewel as it combines. Its wearer gains all four gems' abilities and **all three curses** (**level 20**).

| Curse | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Curse of Ill Fortune** (misfortune) | Roll twice, take the worse, on **all d20 rolls**. |
| **Curse of Festering Wounds** (necromancy) | **Can't recover HP by resting**; other healing (Treat Wounds, magic) restores only **half**. |
| **Curse of Pliability** (enchantment) | Attitude becomes **friendly** toward every creature not actively hostile (helpful attitudes stay). Any save against an effect that would make them **controlled** is a critical failure, no roll. |

**Moving the curse around:**
- Setting it down briefly leaves the curse on its latest bearer.
- Stray over **100 feet** and it **re-materializes** among the bearer's possessions.
- It passes only to someone who knowingly accepts or takes an object, though they needn't know it's cursed, and it can hide in a nonmagical bag or pouch.
- It can't pass to a creature with Intelligence **−4 or less**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The curses come from Varagix through Kulupi, so the item block below lists only the Jewel's own powers.

**What Kulupi does about it:**
- He raises the idea that the curses are another of Poppy's tests if the PCs don't.
- He won't carry it unless persuaded (**DC 24 Diplomacy**) or tricked (**DC 28 Deception**), then concedes to their cleverness and takes it.
- He pushes hard for **Prismatic Colvi**, whose theology and the Temple of Many Colors' sacred ground might lift the curse.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Kulupi is the curse's conduit and a cult leader (revealed in Ch 11). Play his suggestion as a caring friend's best advice. That's why it works.

### Jewel of the Indigo Isles *(Item 20)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** worn. **Bulk** negligible. (p. 218)

While invested, you gain all four shards' powers:
- Cast ***earthbind*** or ***ghostly weapon*** (metal or stone weapon only) as an innate occult spell, one per day.
- Breathe water as well as air.
- **Resistance 3 to bludgeoning.**
- Sheds **bright light in a 20-foot radius**.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact): rub it to turn the light on or off.
- **Activate** (1 action, command, Interact), **once per day:** scrape a fingernail across it for a glittering yellow-orange haze: **concealed** for 3 rounds, but the glitter betrays you, so no Hide or Sneak. You and your gear stay dry underwater, with no breathing granted.

### The cult's plots (p. 218)

Kulupi went with the PCs; his chief co-conspirator, Colvi, would get the Jewel into his own hands in Rumplank.

> **🔒 GM-only — the conspirators don't trust each other:** Colvi suspected the curse and can suppress Varagix's curses, but told Kulupi neither so the prince wouldn't look suspicious in front of the PCs. When Kulupi sees the curse, he guesses Colvi either planned for the temple visit or didn't foresee it, letting Kulupi upstage him; his pride sends him to the temple either way. If the PCs skip it, Colvi expects the curse to make them desperate to be rid of it.

The cult runs **three other plots** during the festival:

| Plot | Who | What happens | Where in this doc |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The ritual** | Grand Oracle [Zellura](gm_glossary.md#zellura) (CE female g'mayun ritualist 9, p. 329) and **Raku Darino** (NE male orpok cultist 8), a [Quill & Scroll Academy](gm_glossary.md#quill--scroll-academy) professor | Confuses guards citywide, cast from the Crawling Caves beneath the city. Meets an unexpected counter | [Scene 3](#scene-3--event-16-divine-visitors) |
| **Arson** | Hired saboteurs and arsonists | Fire attacks across the city | [Scenes 4–5](#scene-4--alleyway-ambush-area-s) |
| **Disappearances** | Cult members and their targets | Cultists leave on their own; inconvenient people are abducted, most prominently **Princess Atriella**. | [Scene 6](#scene-6--into-thin-air) |

> **🧩 Sets up:** Zellura's stat block is on p. 329 (Chapter 12). The cult's escape through the Crawling Caves starts the Chapter 10 chase.

One long day, in two halves:

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|

| **Festival Events** (p. 220–232) | Six contests and spectacles | Any number, about the book's order |
| **Event 16: Divine Visitors** (p. 233) | Amon and Castilli arrive and break the ritual | As the PCs wrap up the festival |
| **Alleyway Ambush, area S** (p. 234–236) | Saboteur scouts, then burning oozes | After Event 16 |
| **Match to a Powderkeg, area T** (p. 237–241) | Arsonists at Treasure Beach and Monbak's house | After area S |

| **The Crawling Caves** (p. 243) | The cult's trail and a guard of nobblers | Last |

**Recommended festival order** (p. 220), parade as centerpiece, Rum Run last (*"once everyone is drunk, it's difficult to perform further contests"*):
1. Peerless Ruby Tall Tale Trial
2. Pirate Panache (runs for hours, alongside other events)
3. Show of Skill
4. Grand Parade
5. Poppy's Prize
6. Rum Run

The PCs can time things to enter every event they want. Reorder or cut to fit your table.

> **💡 GM tip — pacing:** All contests fill a session. For action-hungry players, run two or three, then Event 16. The book awards no XP for contests, so cutting them costs nothing on the level curve.

> **💡 GM tip — the turn:** The second half is back-to-back fights with no rest. Let the shift land: snowball fight, then ambush.

### Combat dueling

Pirate Panache uses the *Pathfinder Gamemastery Guide* combat dueling subsystem, reprinted on p. 222.

- **Initiative every round**, with **Deception, Intimidation, or Perception**, chosen each round.
- **Dueling reactions.** Trained or better in the chosen skill grants its reaction for the round. Neither duelist knows the other's choice.
- **Acting twice in a row** (last, then first) warps "until the start of your next turn" effects. The second actor can dodge them that turn, or choose to go second next round if they win initiative.
- Familiars, companions, and bystanders get no dueling reactions.

| Reaction | Skill | Trigger | Effect | Bonus against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bullying Press** (flourish) | Intimidation | You attempt a melee Strike, before rolling | On a hit, opponent is **frightened 1** | Perception roller: **frightened 2** |
| **Deceptive Sidestep** (misfortune) | Deception | An opponent's melee Strike hits you (not a critical hit) | Opponent rerolls and takes the second result | Intimidation roller also takes **−2 circumstance** to the reroll |
| **Sense Weakness** | Perception | You attempt a melee Strike, before rolling | Opponent is **flat-footed** against the attack | Deception roller stays **flat-footed until the start of their next turn** |

Rock-paper-scissors: Intimidation beats Perception, Deception beats Intimidation, Perception beats Deception.

**Example:** A PC with Intimidation +18 and Perception +14 rolls Intimidation for initiative, gaining Bullying Press. If Master Olo rolled Perception that round, a hit with it leaves him frightened 2.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Duels end at **first blood**, so Bullying Press rarely matters. The book says Intimidation can still be worth rolling if it's a PC's best modifier, for the initiative alone.

### Afflictions: alcohol and rowdy river rum

**Afflictions** (stages, onset, re-exposure): see [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#afflictions-and-poison). Each drink is a new exposure.

**Rowdy river rum** (Item 0, alchemical, consumable, drug, ingested, poison; p. 228) follows *Gamemastery Guide* alcohol rules, except **failed saves after the initial one don't raise the stage**; only more rum does. Its sickened condition can't be recovered from while affected.

**Rowdy river rum** — Price 1 cp (or more). Usage held in 1 hand. Bulk L. **Activate** Interact.
- **Saving Throw** DC 12 Fortitude. **Onset** 10 minutes.
- **Stage 1:** +1 item bonus to saves against fear (10 minutes).
- **Stage 2:** flat-footed, +1 item bonus to saves against fear (10 minutes).
- **Stage 3:** clumsy 1, flat-footed, stupefied 2 (10 minutes).
- **Stage 4:** clumsy 2 and sickened 2 (10 minutes).
- **Stage 5:** clumsy 2, sickened 2, and stupefied 2 (10 minutes).
- **Stage 6:** unconscious (8 hours).
- **Stage 7:** death.

**Example:** Two pre-race drinks mean two DC 12 Fortitude saves; a level-8 PC likely passes most.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — stage 7 is death:** The Rum Run disqualifies anyone who passes out (judges get them medical attention at once) or exceeds **20 drinks**, a rule made after a contestant cheated with *delay affliction* and nearly died.

### Rules from earlier chapters

- **Aid:** see [Chapter 5](gm_chapter_5.md#aid). Used in the Show of Skill and for cheating at Poppy's Prize (**Thievery to Aid themselves**).
- **Persistent damage:** see [Chapter 6](gm_chapter_6.md#persistent-damage-and-bleed). Korog's bombs deal 2, or **7 persistent fire** from a lava bomb.
- **Counteracting:** see [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#counteracting). The [explosive barrels](#t2-trashed-archive-moderate-8)' flames have **DC 21 (2nd level)**, so a successful 1st-level *quench* works (2 is no more than 1 + 1).

|---|---|

Also here: stupefied, from rum stages 3 and 5 and the rum suit ([Ch 5](gm_chapter_5.md#conditions-new-this-chapter)); concealed, from the Jewel's glitter ([Ch 2](gm_chapter_2.md#concealed-hidden-and-undetected)); darkness, in the Crawling Caves ([Ch 7](gm_chapter_7.md#darkness-and-vision)).

## Scene 1 — Homecoming and the Temple of Colors

### Arrival (p. 217–218)

Growing crowds cheer the PCs into harbor. Rumplank and visitors from settlements the PCs visited are here for **Founding Day**, which the royals delayed days until the prince and the heroes returned with the Jewel.

**About the festival:**
- A one-day extravaganza honoring the city's founding and founder, Poppy von Barnacle.
- Almost always includes a **tall tale contest**, a **dueling contest**, a **big parade** with floats and a marching band, and the **Rum Run**, a drunken obstacle course.
- **Mystery pies:** hand pies sold without saying what's inside.
- The date moves with public opinion, since nobody agrees on it, but always falls in **early summer**.

Ashore, the crowd mobs the PCs for stories and drinks until an impatient Kulupi clears a way to [Barrel Square](gm_glossary.md#barrel-square) to open the festival. The rest of the day they'll likely visit the temple, report to the royals, and enjoy the festival. **Kulupi goes with them to the temple if they go there first.**

> **⚠️ Gotcha — names:** **Founding Day** and **Founder's Day** are the same festival; **Temple of Colors** is the [Temple of Many Colors](gm_glossary.md#temple-of-many-colors).

### Temple of Colors (p. 218–219)

- **Who:** Prismatic Colvi, in his temple. Possibly Prince Kulupi.
- **Goal (the cult's):** Colvi gets the Jewel.

Colvi congratulates the PCs and, told of the curse or spotting it, acts surprised and horrified.
- **DC 31 Perception** to Sense Motive: he's more impressed by the curse's power and ingenuity than horrified.
- The **inner sanctum**'s sacred ground should suppress it, he says; enjoy the festival and return tomorrow to solve Poppy's "fiendish puzzle."
- He urges them to leave the Jewel and go celebrate.

**If they hand it over:** Colvi takes Jewel and curse, suppressing it with a silent prayer to Varagix as he enters the sanctum, seeming proof the temple works. He thanks them for recovering a storied piece of Rumplank history and promises to keep it safe.

**Guarding it:**
- **Kulupi present** and a PC worried: Kulupi offers **royal guards**. Colvi makes a show of refusing (the temple can defend itself), then accepts.
- **Kulupi absent:** Colvi promises he and his most esteemed priests will guard it.

**If the PCs ask for it back:** the full curse returns once Colvi hands it over; it only seems suppressed while he holds it in the sanctum.

> **🔒 GM-only:** Colvi controls the curse completely by silent prayer to Varagix. His refusal of the guards is half real: humility is anathema to his faith, so accepting help, even in a scheme, galls him.

> **🔒 GM-only — the guards:** Any guards Kulupi posted vanish with Colvi, with signs all were kidnapped (p. 241).

### Paranoia and precautions (p. 219)

The plot assumes the cult has the Jewel by festival's end. If the PCs still hold it at festival's end, the cult proceeds and trusts it can grab it later; Colvi leaves a few cultists at the temple to accept it, one taught to suppress the curse. Other routes:

1. **The rush to save the royals** may make the PCs leave it behind.
2. **An ambush in the Crawling Caves.** If the PCs carry it toward the salt desert, cultists swarm in from the west right after the [nobbler fight](#smuggled-out-of-town-low-8). **Severe 8:** **6 Shivering Behemoth scallywags** (p. 258, Creature 5) and **Kelgrid**, a kragrak cultist (he/him) with a minor knack for magic.
   - Kelgrid's ***scroll of dominate*** targets the Jewel's holder, who critically fails, no roll (Curse of Pliability).
   - They aim to grab the Jewel and run, not kill the PCs, and fight that way. Kelgrid orders the dominated PC to hand it over, then fight the others to cover the retreat.
   - They know what Chapter 10's area W2 scallywags know; Kelgrid also knows the suppression prayer.
   - Retaking the Jewel after Kelgrid suppresses the curse, talking him into it, or learning the prayer may leave the PCs a temporarily uncursed Jewel: shard powers only, while the curse absorbs the rest. The book says to assume the suppression lasts the rest of their journey.
3. **At the cult's base,** if they still hold it, Kulupi praises their skill and steals it: Royal Betrayal (p. 299), Chapter 11.

**Kelgrid** — Creature 8, variant Shivering Behemoth guard (p. 298)
- **Items:** as the Shivering Behemoth guard, plus a *scroll of dominate*
- **Skills:** Acrobatics +12, Athletics +17, Occultism +13, Stealth +12, Survival +14
- **Str** +6, **Dex** +1, **Con** +4, **Int** +2, **Wis** +3, **Cha** +2
- **Occult Spontaneous Spells** DC 22; **Cantrips (4th)** *ghost sound*, *know direction*

> **📏 Threat math (party level 8):** six level-5 scallywags (15 XP each, 90) plus Kelgrid (level 8, 40) is 130, between Severe (120) and Extreme (160). The book says **Severe 8**.

### Royal Report (p. 219–220)

- **Who:** [King Okeki](gm_glossary.md#king-okeki-redfeather) and [Queen Maxelle](gm_glossary.md#queen-maxelle-redfeather), privately at the palace.
- **Kulupi skips it** for festival matters, uninvited.

The overjoyed royals want the whole story, a different part from each PC. The king wants every Kulupi story, heroics to brag about or antics to tease him with, which is why he asked for the report without his son.

**The royals' terms:**
- The PCs own everything they found, **except the Jewel**.
- The PCs are the festival's **main event**; if they don't want to take part, the royals insist, as part of the deal for the *Chromatic Queen* and other treasures.
- The transfer happens at an **official ceremony at the end of the festival**.

> **💡 GM tip:** The last warm scene with the royals before their children vanish. Let the king laugh at a Kulupi story so Scene 6 hurts.

## Scene 2 — Founder's Day festival events

Anyone can enter or watch; visitors can double the city's population. The PCs can enter as many events as you like (p. 220).

> **🧩 Sets up:** The PCs meet the [Indigo Stranger](gm_glossary.md#indigo-stranger), likely after a strong contest showing. The party-loving dragon is fascinated by their rise from crowd to famous heroes, and reveals his true form in Chapter 12.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — drunk PCs:** During Event 16, **Amon sobers up** any PC too drunk, or passed out, for the coming fights. The book says not to punish fun in the spirit of Rumplank.

### Read aloud (opening the festival)

> *The annual Founder's Day Festival is about to begin! Everywhere around you, the streets are alive with colors, sights, sounds, and smells as people from every ancestry and from across the Indigo Isles and beyond pour into the streets of Rumplank, jostling each other as they check out the stalls of street food, merchandise, games, and other festivities. The first main event for the day is called the Peerless Ruby Tall Tale Trial and is taking place in an open area nearby. The space between here and the contest is anything but empty, with hundreds of excited festival-goers pressing into each other in thick crowds, cheering and competing with each other to be heard over the din. A stout orpok chef is currently winning the volume contest with a steady cry of "Mystery pies! Mystery pies! Come get your mystery pies! Sweet! Savory! Spicy! Salty! Mystery pies!" The aroma of delicious foods blends with the smells of sea breeze and sweaty sailors.*

**Mystery pies** a PC might bite into:
- bitter rubalo with other root vegetables and coconut slices, a classic Rumplank comfort food
- flaky, sweet summer moon-shrimp
- buttery, salty indigo oyster
- gamey [blood mouse](gm_glossary.md#blood-mouse) with savory herbs
- spicy-sweet pikipoki peppers and white pineapples

**Background special effects (p. 220):**
- **Rumplank reveler:** can use **Party Lore** for the Tall Tale Trial, Show of Skill, and Poppy's Prize, at the **lowest listed DC**.
- **Indigo pirate:** prepares for the official post-festival gifting of the *Chromatic Queen*, already promised to the PCs.
- **Indigo trailblazer:** the Fellowship of Indigo Trailblazers honors their mapmaking, above all finding the sunken Razorback Isle, raising them to **Trailblazer Superlative** and requesting a lecture series.

### 2A. Peerless Ruby Tall Tale Trial (p. 220–221)

[Peerless Ruby](gm_glossary.md#peerless-ruby), a vermilion dragon from the nearby jungle, founded this contest decades ago while visiting the festival, and boastful Rumplank loved it.

**How it works:**
- Tellers take turns, each working **one element from the end of the previous story** into the start of their own.
- Each attempts **DC 20 Performance** for an entertaining story, or **DC 25 Deception** for fibs so big the crowd eats them up.
- **Success:** a colorful jar of rye-melon flower honey.
- **Grand prize, best critical success:** a rune aurochs antler horn painted like vermilion dragon scales, a **virtuoso handheld instrument worth 100 gp**.
- **Finding the best critical success:** add **5** to each Performance critical success, then compare. The best NPC scored **35**, after adding 5.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** Deception critical successes get no +5 but still compete for the horn.

**Ten tall tales to start with** (p. 221). Let the PCs cut in on a good lead-in.
- A coast kragrak sailor hooked a leviathan in the deep trenches; it breached for a second, big as an island.
- A g'mayun explorer, sole guest at a misty valley inn, woke on a surreal island of metaphorical trials and traps, beat them, and woke in his room. The innkeeper thanked him with a story of a living island chain that makes heroes. Outside, the inn had vanished.
- A galtzagorri jokester's farce about a busy scarab-shaped life [eldamon](gm_glossary.md#eldamon) jumping between villagers, jolting them with vim and sowing misunderstandings.
- A chochori diver found sunken treasure among weathered stones carved by a lost civilization.
- A g'mayun pirate met the ghost ship of Poppy's rival, which vanished near Rumplank, scared off, the crew believed, by Poppy's legacy.
- An orpok pastry chef's wedding cake absorbed a nitpicking groom's negative energy and came alive as a cruel monstrosity; the groom stayed timid for the rest of the planning.
- A hardriggan mercenary guarded merchants from bandits and pirates who never attacked while she was there. She suspects an inside job targeting specific shipments, maybe magic item or ritual ingredients.
- A g'mayun archivist's epic from after the [Iris Refrain](gm_glossary.md#iris-refrain), of heroes fighting g'mayuns still allied with demons.
- An [aeternal](gm_glossary.md#aeternal-dragon) [amber dragon](gm_glossary.md#amber-dragon) claims she saw Princess Atriella in street clothes on the docks eight months ago, taking down a pirate gang alone.
- A wildfire [leshy](gm_glossary.md#leshy) met a huge fire tornado that left the ground around it unsinged.

### 2B. Pirate Panache (p. 221–224)

A dueling tournament for top duelist, running for hours alongside other events.

**The sashes**, lowest to highest: **green, yellow, red, black**, with seven black sashes from **dan 1** (lowest) to **dan 7** (highest). Famous, the PCs start at **yellow**.

**Challenge rules:**
- A winning challenger **trades sashes** with the higher rank; if the challenger loses, both keep their sashes and the loser can't rechallenge that duelist.
- Green may challenge yellow, yellow may challenge red, and red may challenge black **dan 1 or 2**.
- Black sashes may challenge up to **2 dan higher**.
- The **7th dan** can't challenge, and wins if nobody takes the sash.

**The PCs' path to victory:**
1. Fend off a green-sash challenger *(optional)*.
2. Challenge a red sash for their red sash.
3. Fend off a yellow-sash challenger *(optional)*.
4. Climb the black sashes to Master Olo. Other PCs, and maybe black-sash NPCs, can challenge them along the way.

**Duel rules:** solo [combat dueling](#combat-dueling) to **first blood**, **melee weapons only**: no spells, magic items, alchemy, or poisons. A nonmagical flier forfeits by ending a turn airborne.

> **💡 GM tip — skipping fights (book suggestion):** Green and yellow sashes are no threat. Run one red-sash challenge, then the named NPCs and fellow PCs.

> **💡 GM tip:** You can swap any generic contestant for an NPC the PCs met or an Act 2 crewmate.

### 2D. Grand Parade (p. 225–226)

The festival's centerpiece; many revelers plan their day around it.

> *It's time for the festival's main event, the Grand Parade! An endless procession of costumed dancers, musicians, and a marching band proceed through the city, interspersed with colorfully decorated floats. Some folks have been waiting here for hours, taking all the spots with the best views, and everywhere parents lift children onto their shoulders to see above the crowd. **Collecting floral arrangements** (optional, good for a PC who hasn't shone yet):

### 2E. Poppy's Prize (p. 226–227)

A free tournament of Rumplank's favorite gambling game at the **Rumpled Feather**, the most popular tavern, a painted converted boathouse owned by [Clamardinia Wakamarsis](gm_glossary.md#clamardinia-wakamarsis). Today's novelty drink, the multicolored **Chromatic Queen**, starts sweet and kicks hard; serious players take it nonalcoholic.

**The favorite:** **Seliles** (CN chochori card shark), all jovial smiles whatever the hand, until going quiet in the final round to read and bluff.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — pronouns:** The book calls Seliles both "his" and "their" (p. 227). This doc uses they/them.

**Entering:** 1 gp fee for **100 chips** (1 cp each). **Ante 5 chips** a round. Out of chips is out; no rebuys.

**Playing it for real:** a pirate-twist poker variant for a standard deck, full rules on **p. 381**. Win **three games** to take the tournament. Play as Seliles against two players, or have all four PCs play each other.

**Playing it with dice instead:** three consecutive **Gambling Lore** checks, **DC 20**, **DC 25**, then **DC 30**; any failure knocks a PC out.
- **Cheating:** **Thievery to Aid themselves**, disqualified if the Thievery result is below the round's Gambling Lore DC. Spells like *nudge the odds* (*Secrets of Magic*) also work.
- **Final table:** exactly four players. If four or more PCs pass DC 25, the three highest join Seliles; otherwise all remaining PCs go, plus Seliles and random NPCs to fill the seats.
- **Winning:** the highest PC result past DC 30 wins, others taking runner-up prizes; reroll ties. If no PC passes DC 30, or you win the final as Seliles, **Seliles wins**.

**Prizes:**
- **Everyone in the final round:** a *cantrip deck* (*Secrets of Magic*), and Seliles's admiring request for adventure stories.
- **Grand prize:** the year's chips, worth **20 gp**, in a ***bag of holding* type I**, plus a silver bracelet engraved with tournament name and year, worth **5 gp**.

### 2F. Rum Run (p. 228–232)

The most popular event: a drunken obstacle course, meant as a show with no serious injuries. Children run easier parallel obstacles at the same time, and watching them beat drunk adults is half the fun. Master Olo and Ash enter, their contests done, as can NPCs with the party.

**Rules:**
- **Four obstacles**, each harder than the last.
- Magic, alchemy, and abilities that bypass an obstacle (burrowing, flying) are **banned**; judges send cheaters back to do it fair and square.
- No attacking contestants or damaging the course. **Disarming a hazard is allowed**; staff reset it.
- **Each skill check takes one round of the race** (not a combat round). Drinking or donning rum apparel between obstacles takes none.
- Racers who pass out from alcohol poisoning are **disqualified** and get medical help at once.

**Drinking:**
- **Rum drinkers:** two drinks of [rowdy river rum](#afflictions-alcohol-and-rowdy-river-rum) before the start and **two after each obstacle**.
- **Fluffmelon drinkers:** anyone avoiding alcohol, for any reason, drinks **fluffmelon**, a sweet coconut-cream and rye-melon drink that upsets the stomach in quantity. From the **second obstacle** they wear a **rum suit** (awkwardly weighted tunic and breeches, light-distorting goggles): **clumsy 1 and stupefied 2** (visual). From the **third obstacle** a **rum coat** raises clumsy to **2**. Nobody shames this choice; both are hilarious.
- More than **20 drinks** of either disqualifies.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — who wears the suit:** A p. 229 race update says *"anyone not drinking fluffmelon must don a rum suit."* The main rules say **not drinking alcohol**. Treat it as a typo: fluffmelon drinkers wear the suit, as its purpose implies.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Atriella:** Don't run Princess Atriella in the Rum Run. By now she's being kidnapped.

> **💡 GM tip — NPC racers (book suggestion):** The Race Update entries stand in for dozens of NPC racers. To roll a few, use leveled-up party NPCs or contestants with similar modifiers.

#### Obstacle 1: Walk the Plank

An ever-narrower balance beam: **DC 15**, **DC 20**, then **DC 25 Acrobatics** to Balance, one segment per success.
- **First segment only:** a running **Long Jump** (**DC 15 Athletics**) works instead. Jumping again on the plank only wobbles it.
- These are the real Balance and Long Jump actions, so boosting feats and items apply.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Critical success | Cross two segments (a first-check crit leaves DC 25 for the last) |
| Success | Cross one segment |
| Failure | No progress |
| Critical failure | Splash into the water, lose all progress, spend the next round drinking and climbing back to the start |

**Race update:** the fastest (Master Olo, Ash, maybe some PCs) clear it in the best possible **2 rounds**.

#### Obstacle 2: Wrestle the Rigging

A mostly horizontal tangle of taut and slack ropes; moving them carefully is allowed, cutting or damaging them isn't.

**Step 1, choose a path:** a **secret** **DC 20 Survival** or **DC 25 Perception** check, taking no round.

| Result | Successes needed |
|---|---|
| Critical success | 2 |
| Success | 3 |
| Failure | 4 |
| Critical failure | 6 |

Keep the number secret; tell the PC when they're through.

**Step 2, each round:** **DC 22 Acrobatics** to squeeze through, or **DC 20 Crafting** to adjust the ropes.
- **Critical success:** 2 successes. **Success:** 1. **Failure:** stuck.
- **Critical failure:** hopelessly tangled: a round to get free, an extra drink, a new path check, and a fresh start, to the crowd's uproar.

**Race update:** the leaders (still Olo and Ash) clear both obstacles in **5 rounds**; PCs, in as few as 3. Rum-suit wearers add the rum coat now.

#### Obstacle 3: Scale the Mast

Climb a huge mast against wind traps: **DC 25 Athletics** to Climb, **three successes** to the top.
- **Critical success:** 2 successes. **Success:** 1. **Failure:** no progress.
- **Critical failure:** a harmless, mortifying fall into water and mud; lose all progress and spend the next round drinking and returning to the start.

**Disabling the wind trap:** a racer can spend a round on **DC 20 Thievery** to Disable the trap aimed at them. It helps only that racer, and staff reset it at once.
- **Critical success:** the trap now helps: Climb DC **10** until they reach the top or fall.
- **Success:** Climb DC **15** until they reach the top or fall.
- **Failure:** no effect.
- **Critical failure:** blown off, as a critically failed Climb, to the crowd's delight.

**Race update:** only Master Olo clears three obstacles in **8 rounds**, several NPCs a round behind. PCs could do it in as few as 5.

#### Obstacle 4: Claim the Sunken Treasure

Dive in still, muddy water full of fake treasures.

1. **Spot a treasure** (takes the turn): **DC 25 Perception**.
   - **Critical success:** spot one, close enough to appraise before swimming.
   - **Success:** spot one; swim next turn.
   - **Failure:** nothing yet.
   - **Critical failure:** fall in the mud; an extra turn getting out and drinking before searching again.
2. **Swim to it:** **DC 10 Athletics**, 2 rounds, or 1 on a critical success.
3. **Is it real?** You roll a **secret DC 15 flat check**; success means real.
4. **Appraise** (optional, secret, no turn, once per treasure): **DC 20 Crafting**. **Success:** they know. **Failure:** unsure. **Critical failure:** wrong answer.
5. **Keep it or drop it.** Drop a suspected fake and search next turn, or swim back: **DC 10 Athletics**, 2 rounds, or 1 on a critical success.
6. **Back at the start,** *faerie fire* lights a real treasure, finishing the race. A fake means another Perception check next turn.

Luck stops the same locals winning yearly; top racers grumble, the crowd loves upsets.

**Race update and results:**
- Master Olo hits a streak of fakes. The first NPC (a crewmate or festival NPC the PCs liked) finishes in **17 rounds**, a second of your choice in **19**, more trickling in after.
- The all-time Rumplank record is **10 rounds**; the theoretical best is 8.
- **A PC under 17 rounds wins**; several place in finishing order. A top PC at **18** takes second; at **19**, ties for second; at **20 or more**, at best a multi-way tie for third.
- Most racers eventually tap out, too drunk or fluffmelon-sick.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The book doesn't cover a PC tying the first NPC at 17. Its first-place tie rule has a judge randomly assign first and second prizes.

**Prizes:**
- **1st:** a miniature treasure chest with **100 gp**, average lock.
- **2nd:** a smaller chest with **50 gp**, simple lock.
- **Every finisher** keeps their treasure, worth **1d10+5 gp**.
- **Ties:** prizes assigned randomly by a judge; the place's honor shared equally.

**Rum Run events** to describe (p. 229):
- A drunk adult wanders into the children's version of an obstacle, fails, and is disqualified.
- A racer falls from near the top of the climbing wall into the mud.
- A racer hangs upside down by one foot in the rigging until another shoves them clear. The judges debate attacking versus helping, then side with the cheering crowd.
- A drunk racer runs the course backward, warbling an off-key victory song.

## Scene 3 — Event 16: Divine Visitors

- **When:** as the PCs wrap up the festival, ready to move on (p. 220).
- **Where:** a town square.
- **Who:** [Amon](gm_glossary.md#amon), god of revelry, and [Castilli](gm_glossary.md#castilli), god of restraint.

The gods appear as the **viewer's own ancestry**, so each person sees something different (p. 233). The read-aloud calls them "two men."

### Read aloud

> *There could be no better proof that today's festival has outdone itself than the two men who stand in the town square before you.*
>
> *One is a short, rotund man with rosy cheeks and vibrant clothing being held together with rope and twine. He carries an oversized tankard of ale in one hand while his other hand "conducts" a group of drunken revelers singing a merry and bawdy song. The other is a tall, narrow-framed man who lingers nearby with his arms crossed, watching over the scene. His attire also makes a stark contrast with that of his companion, with an elegant full-length tunic, golden epaulets and greaves, a stylish fedora, and an immaculate beard and mustache. The portly man's presence is warm and inviting, stirring memories of your most precious moments of celebration to flood your mind and bringing merry songs to your lips without a moment's thought. By contrast, the tall man's presence makes your spine straighten of its own accord and evokes an implacable awareness of rules and decorum. These powerful and seemingly contradictory sensations exist in perfect harmony.*
>
> *There can be no doubt as to the identities of these two men: they are Amon and Castilli, the sibling gods of revelry and restraint. The arrival of gods could bring great blessings in its wake, so long as they enjoy their time walking the world. Amon at least is in excellent spirits, but Castilli's emotions are inscrutable beyond mild disapproval.*

### What happens (p. 233–234)

1. **The tankard.** As the PCs approach, Amon downs his ale and drops the tankard to wave them into the song. Backwash splatters Castilli's crisp white tunic; he sighs and cleans it with a wave.
   - Amon: *"You had half a wheat field of starch in there already, what's wrong with a few more drops?"*
   - Castilli: *"Don't be ridiculous, brother. One does not starch silk."*
   - Amon: *"But I am ridiculous brother, so I'd better starch it up!"*
2. **The snow.** Amon tosses up what looks like a bag of flour; it swells into a **20-foot sphere**. *"Look out below!"* Castilli shoves revelers clear. It lands as **snow**, and a snowball fight breaks out among singers and children. The PCs can play, and even a city guard joins.
3. **The whisper.** Amon tosses a snowball at Castilli, who catches it. **DC 20 Perception** to overhear:
   - Amon: *"Really though, your face usually looks like a raisin when people are having fun, but today it's a walnut. What's got you so wound up?"*
   - Castilli: *"It's a matter incongruent with the merriment you seek. Do not worry yourself."*
   - Amon: *"Always gotta be a tough nut to crack, don'tcha?"*
   - Castilli's frown is barely visible to the PCs.
4. **The guard.** **DC 25 Perception:** the guard's eyes glaze as she draws a dagger and lunges at the child beside her. Noticed or not, **Castilli acts first**: a finger snap, and she drops it. *"Enough!"* thunders through everyone nearby. Silver magic around him breaks into dozens of motes; one flies into the guard, the rest race down other streets.
   - Amon: *"Hey now, that wasn't me."*
   - Castilli: *"I know. It was a ritual, and rituals require patience and focus, both of which you lack."*
   - Amon: *"Always the party pooper."*
5. **The charge.** Both gods agree the heroes of Rumplank should handle this.

**What Castilli tells them:**
- Arriving about an hour ago, he noticed disharmonious energy forming a ritual to **confound the city guards**.
- He doesn't know the caster; all the ritual's energy is now spent.
- It took extensive preparation, so it **won't happen again anytime soon**.
- Both want the culprit held accountable: Castilli because they broke lawful order, Amon because they tried to ruin a great party.

**If the PCs ask for more help:**
- Amon: *"These are your affairs, not ours. Though I gotta say, Casti'd usually rather sneer than interfere. I haven't seen him get that worked up in centuries."*
- Castilli: *"I will not abide such an egregious breach of decorum in my presence."*
- Amon: *"And I thought 'Egregious Breach of Decorum' was my title."*
- Castilli: *"And yet, for the sake of the world, I am ever stuck by your side."*
- Amon claps him on the shoulder: *"Aww, how sweet. I love you too."* The corner of Castilli's mouth quirks up in the slightest smile.

**Recall Knowledge** (p. 234): a worshipper of either god, or **DC 15 Religion**, knows Amon rarely graces a party in person, and Castilli follows to restore order, straight man to the joker. **Critical success:** Castilli considers mortals meddling in the gods' affairs a far worse breach of decorum, and won't tolerate it.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — don't take sides:** Defending Castilli from his brother, scolding Amon, or any other egregious breach of decorum in his presence earns a withering glare from Castilli: **frightened 2**, irreducible until the PC apologizes for interfering in the business of gods. Warn players who critically succeeded above.

**Development:** Investigating PCs find more strange guard behavior (one babbled nonsense, another nearly punched himself before snapping out of a trance) before trouble finds them. Go to area S.

> **🔒 GM-only — the cult reacts:** The ritual's failure alerts cultists citywide, and they hurry. During area S and the fires they make their last abductions, **including Princess Atriella**, and Colvi and Kulupi leave Rumplank.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** The fun hinge from celebration to crisis, and the reason the ritual failed: the cult didn't plan for a god of restraint at the party.

### S1. From the Rooftops *(Moderate 8)*

> *Like much of Rumplank, the streets here are crisscrossed by narrow alleyways, which are convenient for pedestrians at any time, but are almost necessary during the Festival. As you head into an alleyway, there are quiet sounds from the rooftops above... it's an ambush!*

**Creatures:** cult saboteurs are scattered citywide. These scouts, tipped off by the PCs' investigating or news of the gods, stalk them along the rooftops.

**Tactics:**

- Most locals are at the festival; arsonists have threatened anyone left who fights the fires.

### T2. Trashed Archive *(Moderate 8)*

**Monbak** (LN male g'mayun historian), a leading scholar of Rumplank history, bought this old food warehouse to house his huge collection of artifacts, recorded tales, histories, legends, and notes, sleeping in only one small curtained room. Ceilings are 15 feet; high windows give normal light. The collection lies smashed, and torn-up books pack four explosive barrels as kindling.

> *As you enter the house from which you heard the crashing sound, you see an eclectic home filled with a mishmash of cultural artifacts, tomes and scrolls full of writing, and scattered notes. While shelves line the wall, all the treasures and notes are scattered across the room, with some of the irreplaceable and delicate pieces smashed into fragments and many of the books ripped apart to serve as kindling for four explosive barrels full of fireworks. A thick curtain on the east side of this large chamber is drawn, blocking the view into the room beyond. A hardriggan man and a kragrak woman, who appear to be the ringleaders behind the arson, prepare to fan the flames even further!*

**Creatures:** the main arsonists, **Korog** and **Ovin Rambones**, prepare an explosive finale. Neither is a true cult member; arson is Korog's trade, and the cult made it easier.

**Korog's story** (p. 239):
- Hired first to put on a fireworks show for Rumplank.
- **DC 20 Society** to Recall Knowledge: she's done shows in several towns and villages lately. **Critical success:** a pattern of unrelated-seeming arsons in the month after she left each.
- Her method: perform, pick whoever annoys her most, wait out suspicion, burn their property. She prefers wrecking civilization's trappings to killing but doesn't mind casualties.
- Cult inventor **Falarni Malabria** suspected the arsons through her own crafting know-how and offered free explosives if Korog lay low and burned Falarni's targets when Falarni chose.
- Researching the targets, Korog took personal offense at how Monbak reveled in civilization, hence the special attention.

**Ovin's story:** a talented medic, angry since childhood. Awakened to primal magic young, he hated his community's expectations and being taken for granted. Banished as a teen for turning a harmless headbutting duel lethal, he happily went to live off the land in the woods. He treated Korog there for burns from her own fire and extorted her for it, which amused her and won her respect. They share a practical outlook and a distaste for civilization.

**Tactics:**
- **Ovin** opens with *fireball*, before Ram's Transformation, aiming to catch a barrel, ideally beside a PC. Round 2 he casts *organsight* and charges, or charges at once, depending on the pace.
- **Korog** throws one **lava bomb** and one **dread ampoule** each round.

## Scene 6 — Into Thin Air

The PCs, other adventurers, and the gods spared Rumplank several disasters, so the cult will return to a city able to defend itself. But the **most important part of the plan succeeded** while everyone was busy elsewhere: key figures were abducted, some of them cult members letting themselves be "abducted," Prismatic Colvi among them. **The cult now has the Jewel** (p. 241).

**How the PCs find out:**
- **At the temple:** if they return to Colvi, he, the Jewel, and any guards the prince posted are gone, with signs all were kidnapped.
- **At Castle Rumplank:** if they go to report.
- **By messenger:** otherwise, a messenger soon summons them urgently to the king and queen.

### Read aloud (the messenger)

> *From a distance, rapid footsteps approach, after which a wheezing g'mayun messenger crouches before you. It's unclear whether she is inclining her head out of respect or is simply exhausted. The royal heraldry on her outfit suggests she might have run all the way from the palace in the Rosewater district, which is quite the distance. She implores, "A message for you, heroes. His Majesty apologizes for the need to call on you on such short notice, but an urgent and sensitive matter has arisen, which strikes close to home and to His Majesty's heart. If you could proceed to the palace at your most immediate convenience?" The messenger waits patiently for a response, withdraws a pen and parchment, and calls a nearby parrot to her shoulder. While she is in no shape to return to the palace without a rest, the parrot can deliver your response in her stead.*

### Read aloud (the king)

At the palace in [Rosewater](gm_glossary.md#rosewater), the king gives the news.

> *After clearing the room, the king speaks in a solemn voice, "My daughter Artriella and my son Kulupi have been abducted! After the parade, the pair went to take a break from the crowds and enjoy some tea together. When a servant went to fetch them for dinner, she reported that the room was empty—the guards were gone, the teacups were half-drunk, and the door to a secret passage out of the castle that I thought was known only to the royal family was open. Royal alchemists determined that the prince and princess's tea had both been laced with a paralytic poison. The guards that we sent to the secret passageway to investigate have not yet returned. - A **royal bard heals visibly hurt PCs to full HP** before they go.

**Investigating the tea room:**
- It matches the king's account.
- **DC 25 Crafting** detects the poison in the tea.
- The **secret door is still open**.
- Nobody in the castle saw anyone unusual nearby.

> **🔒 GM-only — what happened:** **Prince Kulupi and an inner circle of loyal guards staged the whole kidnapping**; there's no other culprit. Cultists captured the guards the king sent down the tunnel. Play the king's grief straight; the players learn the truth in Chapter 11.

> **🎯 Why this scene exists:** The PCs saved the food stores and a historian and still lost. The king's plea, "not as a king but as a father," makes the chase personal and sets up the biggest reveal: the "victim" is the villain.

## Concluding the chapter

The PCs have the trail but are likely tired from a day of feasting and fighting. Even if they want to push on, the book calls this a good place to **end the session** (p. 243).

To keep going, run **Royal Request**, Chapter 10's opening, one of two ways:
- The PCs **go back to Rumplank to rest**, then go to the castle.
- They **push on through the caves**; the royals send a messenger with mounts, provisions, and a note, and the PCs rest safely the first night with no combat (Chapter 10, p. 244).

**No level-up here.** The party stays at **8** until meeting Peerless Ruby in Chapter 10.

> **💡 GM tip:** The book calls this one of the last breathers before the finale. Give the players a scene to rest, take stock, and react to their losses.
