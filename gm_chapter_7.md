## The chapter in one minute

Inland from Rotsnout's cottage stands a fortress the [Cult of the Shivering Behemoth](gm_glossary.md#cult-of-the-shivering-behemoth) built two centuries ago, now held by Shadow Plane invaders called **sklaggans**. Their three-faced leader, the **Flesh Stitcher**, is building the **Skinloom**, a loom of living flesh that can one day weave gateways to the Shadow Plane, around the Gullstone Cave [Jewel](gm_glossary.md#jewel-of-the-indigo-isles) fragment.

Criminals from Moonshadow's abandoned village hold the outer fortress in hollowed-out monster skins, expecting a reward while being fattened for the Skinloom. Behind a black resin dome, an old mithral mine is now an alien lair. There the PCs fight the Flesh Stitcher and pry the fragment from a trapped plate of flesh.

**By the end of the chapter the PCs have:**
1. **The Bruised Crystal**, the third Jewel fragment (P21, p. 191).
2. **Defeated the Flesh Stitcher**, scattering its shadow creatures and breaking its human lackeys' will.
3. **Up to 2 bundles of purifying tree bark** from the Pristine Clearing (p. 171), a defense special resource, if they harvest it.

**Party level:** starts at 6, ends at **7**.

> **🎯 Why this chapter exists:** The Act 2 dungeon crawl: two layers, many ways in. It pays off the Ch 6 investigation, delivers the third fragment, and shows the cult's reach (the P4 throne: "The Shivering Behemoth Rises, Under Our Control").

## How the chapter is built

The book's parts (p. 170–191):

| Part | What it is | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **In the Forest** | The 4-mile walk, past the Pristine Clearing. | First. The clearing can wait for the trip back |
| **Tainted Fortress: outer fortress (P1–P13)** | A wooden fort around a courtyard, held by lackeys in skinsuits. | Any order. Several entry routes |
| **The inner fortress (P14–P21)** | The old mine behind the resin dome, held by sklaggans and beasts. | Opened from P3 or P13 |
| **Skinloom and the Bruised Crystal** | Boss fight, then a complex trap to free the fragment. | Last |

**Ways into the inner fortress:** the P3 resin dome or the dark P13 rear hall. Either needs a sklaggan tool (a **skagappa**) or a handful of bloody viscera. The only viscera the PCs can easily find is the bowl in **P10**.

> **💡 GM tip — the "how do we assault a fortress" stall:** Book sidebar (p. 173). PCs who scout unseen by the swarm assassins see haphazard patrols. Tell them, or allow an easy Warfare Lore or similar check, that the defenders are disorganized and a direct approach can work. Heist fans can get coordinated defenders or the *Gamemastery Guide* **infiltration subsystem**.

> **💡 GM tip — pacing:** The book notes denizens often fight among themselves and nobody aids anyone (p. 176), so most rooms fight alone and careful PCs can rest between them. Alarms break this: the P1 swarm assassins, the P10 butcher, a P8 collapse, and Umbrocious warning his master (P12).

> **⚠️ Content warning (book sidebar, p. 173):** Some sklaggan bio-technology is grotesque. Options: a generic shadowy substance for the dome-opening viscera, and a Skinloom blending life force with shadow instead of twisting flesh. Decide before the session.

- **Haunts:** [Chapter 3](gm_chapter_3.md#haunts). The **arsonist spirit (P13)** yields to **Medicine** or **Religion** and stops resetting once the three P20 sklaggans die.

### Hardness on creatures (skinsuits)

Every humanoid in a **skinsuit** has **Hardness 5**: subtract 5 from each instance of damage.
- **Example:** a 12-damage hit on a skinsuited flenser costs it 7 HP.
- **Rupture:** below **half its maximum HP**, or **the moment it takes damage from a critical hit**. Hardness ends; AC, saves, attack modifiers, and Strike damage each drop by 2.
- **Weapons:** pincers while intact, then its own weapon (kukri, kama).
- **Level bump:** the suit adds 1 to the wearer's level, already in the stat blocks (p. 175).

> **💡 GM tip:** Break the suits, the book's best tactic. Crits rupture them, so fighters, flanking rogues, and flat-footing tricks shine.

### Regeneration

The **skornatha** (P15, P17) has **regeneration 5 (deactivated by electricity or fire)** (p. 399).
- +5 HP at the start of each of its turns. While active, its dying value stops at 3, so damage can't kill it.
- **Electricity or fire** switches it off until the end of its next turn. Land some the round you drop it.

### Conditions new this chapter

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Enfeebled X** | Status penalty X to Strength-based rolls and DCs: Strength-based melee attack and damage rolls, and Athletics. |
| **Blinded** | Can't see. Normal terrain is difficult terrain; Perception checks needing sight always critically fail. |
| **Fleeing** | Spend actions moving away from the source. No Delay or Ready. |
| **Grabbed** | Flat-footed and immobilized. A manipulate action needs a DC 5 flat check or it's lost. Escape ends it. |

Also in this chapter: drained ([Ch 6](gm_chapter_6.md#drained)), slowed ([Ch 4](gm_chapter_4.md#slowed-and-fatigued)), stupefied and stunned ([Ch 5](gm_chapter_5.md#conditions-new-this-chapter)), restrained ([Ch 6](gm_chapter_6.md#restrained-and-escape)).

### Balance and uneven ground

Three edges of the P8 pit are **uneven ground**: moving onto them takes **DC 22 Acrobatics** to **Balance**. Book ruling: a failure means falling into the pit.

### Escape, Disarm, and Shove

- **Escape:** [Chapter 6](gm_chapter_6.md#restrained-and-escape). Also frees a creature from Weave Together (Escape DC 25).
- **Disarm:** Athletics vs. the target's **Reflex DC**. Success: until the start of the target's next turn, a **−2 circumstance penalty** to attacks with that item and a **+2 circumstance bonus** to further Disarms against it. Critical success: the item drops. Snag Weapon (P21) extends the success.
- **Shove:** Athletics vs. the target's **Fortitude DC**: 5 feet, or 10 feet on a critical success. Used by the P8 flensers (into the pit) and the P5 bullies (Brutal Bully Push: prone unless the bully Strides after).

### Darkness and vision

The inner fortress is **completely dark** (p. 183). PCs without darkvision need a light source; low-light vision doesn't help. Every sklaggan creature has darkvision or greater darkvision, and a PC carrying light is easy to spot.

## In the Forest and the Pristine Clearing

**Where:** the 4-mile path from Rotsnout's cottage (p. 170–171). Tarry resin thickens on the trees as the PCs go; the sklaggans blight-bomb any possible rival.
- **Off the path:** the book discourages it. Nearly all life here is killed, twisted, or gone. There's nowhere to go but the fortress.

> **🔒 GM-only — what's going on:** The Flesh Stitcher needs living meat for the Skinloom, so it duped the villagers instead of killing them, setting them against one another with promises of power. The most vicious got "gifts" like skinsuits; the disloyal or meek were secretly flayed. The rest think they're favored. All are bound for the Skinloom (p. 171).

### Pristine Clearing *(defense special resource)*

Close beside the path, a resin-ringed stand of trees is untouched, with a purifying magic unique to the island.
- **Spotting the value:** **DC 22 Nature or Survival**, or **DC 17 Forest Lore**: the bark concentrates the effect.
- **Limit:** **one character**, **once**. **30 Bulk**, which fits in Prince Kulupi's bag of holding.

**Harvest Purifying Tree Bark** *(exploration, manipulate, secret)* — a few hours. **Secret DC 22 Arcana, Crafting, or Nature**, or **DC 20 Forest Lore**.
- **Critical success:** **after 1 week**, an **additional** resource: **2 bundles** total.
- **Success:** **1** purifying tree bark resource.
- **Failure:** after 1 week it becomes a **basic wood resource**: no special uses, but tradeable for Resource Points.
- **Critical failure:** after 1 week, worthless bark.

> **🧩 Sets up:** A Ch 12 **defense special resource**, up to **2 bundles of bark** here. Bark plus an arrow tower builds a **purifying tower**: **15 damage against undead and husks** instead of 5. Any special resource also trades for **10 generic Resource Points** (p. 315). **Record the bundles and any basic wood.**

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The roll is secret and resolves a week later; note it privately. PCs who pass the clearing on the way in can harvest on the way back (p. 191).

## The fortress: features and map

> **🔒 GM-only — history:** The cult built the fortress to guard cliffside mithral mines and stage its control of the island; a tower foundation dig found more mithral. The mines ran dry, and the cult left by the end of the War of the Shivering Behemoth. The sklaggans prize the twisting, defensible mine, expanded it, and sealed it with resin that reminds them of home. The fort they leave to their minions (p. 171).

### Outer fortress features (p. 171–173)

- **Walls and doors:** two-century-old flame-resistant island wood, **almost impossible to burn**. Most doors bolt from inside, but **none are bolted** when the PCs arrive.
- **Light:** hooked lanterns, mostly out in the pre-dawn hours while the inhabitants rest.
- **Ceilings:** 15 feet, except the open-air courtyard and access areas (**P1, P2, P3**).
- **Walls:** 20 feet outside, a low battlement around the flat roof. The ladders went for firewood. **DC 20 Athletics** to climb.
- **The roof:** unvisited, firm except over **P8**, where anyone crossing the middle breaks through: **Grab an Edge (DC 20 Reflex)** or fall 15 feet farther into the pit (**18 bludgeoning** total), drawing nearby inhabitants. Other noisy roof travel may draw the room below.

### What happens if the PCs lose (outer fortress)

Book sidebar (p. 176): victors keep beaten PCs alive to bargain with the sklaggans, who will likely flay them in the end, but not for a couple of days. Plenty of time to escape.

### Secrets of the Sklaggans *(Recall Knowledge)*

Once the PCs know the sklaggans run things, they can **Recall Knowledge with Occultism** on them, and on skadboks and skornathas (the skinsuit sources) before meeting live ones (p. 177).
- Success also reveals the sklaggan **hierarchy** and that they're **duping the convicts**.
- No humanoid here is trained in Occultism, so none knows its fate or will likely believe the PCs.

## The outer fortress (P1–P13)

### P1 — Forest Approach *(Low 6)*

> *An old wooden fortress, more than a hundred feet on each side, is built against a low, rocky hill. The walls rise up 20 feet from the ground to form a jagged battlement from sharpened tree trunks that make up the outer walls. A wide wooden gate stands shut in the middle of the wall. Several arrow slits all along the south face of the fortress allow anyone beyond to see out, but the other side is cloaked in deep shadow. Brush grows right up to the fortress's walls, indicating that it hasn't been well-tended for some time, but the path to the gate seems recently used and spattered with globs of black resin.*

Nobody clears the brush. The occupants don't expect an assault.

**Corrupted Swarm Assassins (4)** — BB 109, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +10. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp.
- **Corrupted:** resin-imbued. Ignores difficult terrain from natural features like swamp and undergrowth; **immune to poison damage**.
- **Placement:** 1d4 each: **1** west, **2** east, **3–4** front. Max two per spot; reroll a third.
- **Behavior:** random wandering. They attack skulkers and call the others. Underbrush withers as they pass, a tell.
- **Walking in:** most humanoids look alike to them, so PCs approaching openly are let in. Talking to one takes **DC 18 Deception**, or the swarms attack.
- **If bypassed:** fighting they hear inside brings them **after 2d6 rounds**.
- **Morale:** a swarm **below 25 HP**, or hit by a **critical hit from a splash weapon**, flees into the forest.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — swarm count:** The text posts **four**; the stat header reads "Corrupted Swarm Assassins (1–2)" (p. 174). Use four: Low 6 needs them (4 × 15 = 60 XP).

> **💡 GM tip:** Walking in skips this fight, but the swarms can join P3 mid-fight. Roll the 2d6 when the first inside fight starts.

### P2 — Front Gate

> *The front gate to the fortress consists of two wide doors. Both contain repeating images that look like hills marching off into the distance, but close inspection reveals that these "hills" are titanic crustaceans, like pillbugs.*

- A barring beam leans inside, never used. The gate is shut only against animals; people push it open.
- **If barred:** **DC 30 Athletics** to force from outside.

### P3 — Courtyard *(Moderate 6)*

> *The courtyard at the center of the fortress is surrounded by several buildings built into the fort's walls. The grass is heavily trampled and spattered with spots of tarry resin. The rock face at the back side of the fortress has a huge hemisphere of tarry resin bulging from it, like a black balloon. The hemisphere is almost entirely opaque, although there's a faint reflection on its surface of a passage leading back into the rock.*

#### The resin hemisphere (the dome)

Sklaggan bio-technology, far tougher than the tree resin.
- **Hardness 30, 60 HP per 5-foot square.** It **regenerates 1d4 HP every minute** when damaged, even with a section destroyed, unless the whole dome is destroyed.
- It **blocks all light, sound, and incorporeal creatures**, Umbrocious included. It runs several feet into the rock, so no burrowing or *earth glide* around it.
- **Opening it:** touch it with a sklaggan tool such as a **skagappa**, or **a handful of bloody viscera** (it can't tell them apart). It irises open within 6 feet of the touch.
- **Closing:** **after 3 rounds**. Anything in the opening takes **5d8 slashing, DC 22 basic Reflex** ([basic saves](gm_chapter_1.md#basic-saving-throws)) and can jump to either side. Non-magical items are sheared, magic items pushed aside. **A creature reduced to 0 HP by this damage is slain outright, sliced in half.**
- Humanoids are forbidden past it unless summoned or escorted, and keep viscera for that in the **P10** bowl.

> **⚠️ Gotcha:** The 0-HP rule bypasses dying. Count the 3 rounds aloud.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** All resin doors in the inner fortress work the same way (p. 183).

#### Creatures

Four lackeys drill in skornatha-skin **skinsuits**, snapping the pincers, enjoying the power.
- They know every villager, so they likely **spot the PCs as intruders** and **attack at once**: acolyte casting from the back, flensers in melee.
- **None surrenders or flees.** They chase but **don't call for help**; they want the credit.
- Midnight to a few hours after dawn, they rest in **P7**, suits on.

**Evaluating the suits:** **secret DC 18 Perception** per PC who sees them, reusable for later wearers.
- **Critical success:** a humanoid inside the hollowed-out skin of an alien monster. Enough damage, or a critical hit, ruptures the suit's protection.
- **Success:** a humanoid in some kind of bulky organic armor.
- **Failure:** an aberration that seems wounded and behaving strangely.
- **Critical failure:** can't tell them from ordinary skornathas.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Monster Parts:** Wearers' listed parts are **skinsuit shards**. The book never has PCs butcher humanoids.

**G'mayun Acolyte** — Creature 6, female CE Medium g'mayun humanoid, shadow priest (BB 35), p. 175
- **Perception** +14; low-light vision, truecolor sight (sees air, force, negative, and positive effects as easily as fire)
- **Languages** Auran, Common, Orpok
- **Skills** Acrobatics +12, Arcana +12, Intimidation +13, Religion +14, Thievery +12
- **AC** 24 (22 when her skinsuit ruptures); **Fort** +10, **Ref** +14, **Will** +16; **HP** 75; **Hardness** 5
- **Skinsuit** ([how it works](#hardness-on-creatures-skinsuits)). **Speed** 25 ft
- **Melee** pincer +17 (deadly d10), 2d8+7 piercing *(suit intact only)*
- **Melee** kukri +17 (agile, finesse, trip), 1d6+7 slashing *(suit ruptured only)*
- **Divine Spontaneous Spells** DC 24, attack +16. **3rd** (2 slots) *chilling darkness*, *vampiric touch*. **2nd** (3 slots) *darkness*, *death knell*, *faerie fire*. **1st** (3 slots) *bane*, *bless*, *fear*. **Cantrips (3rd)** *chill touch*, *daze*, *forbidding ward*, *guidance*, *sigil*
- **Monster Parts** 30/65/125 gp

**Orpok Flensers (3)** — Creature 3, CE Medium orpok humanoid, p. 176
- **Perception** +9, scent (imprecise) 30 feet. **Languages** Common, Orpok
- **Skills** Athletics +10, Intimidation +7, Medicine +9, Survival +9
- **AC** 19 (17 when their skinsuit ruptures); **Fort** +11, **Ref** +8, **Will** +9; **HP** 44; **Hardness** 5
- **Skinsuit** as the acolyte. **Speed** 25 ft
- **Melee** pincer +12 (deadly d10), 1d8+7 piercing *(suit intact only)*
- **Melee** kama +12 (agile, trip), 1d6+5 slashing *(suit ruptured only)*
- **Peel Skin** (2 actions): a melee Strike. On a hit, it deals an extra **1d8 persistent bleed** ([persistent damage](gm_chapter_6.md#persistent-damage-and-bleed)).
- **Monster Parts** 7/18/30 gp

> **📏 Rules refresher — threat math:** The acolyte (level 6, 40 XP) plus three flensers (level 3, 15 each) = 85 XP, **Moderate** at level 6.

### P4 — Great Hall

> *This large hall looks like it was once an audience chamber or receiving room. The walls are carved with images of mountains and towering crustaceans looming over ships and villages. A wooden throne at the east end of the room is carved with similar iconography, but it's been toppled and lies broken on the floor.*

The cult's audience hall. The throne's back reads **"The Shivering Behemoth Rises, Under Our Control"** in Common and Terran, framing whoever sat there.
- **Treasure:** in the toppled throne's base, found on examination: a **sturdy magic shield made of monster parts (refined shield 7, infused sturdy 7)**. Without Monster Parts, a **lesser sturdy shield**.

> **🧩 Sets up:** The cult wanted control of the behemoth. The reborn cult chases it again in Act 3 ([synopsis](gm_story_synopsis.md#the-cult)).

### P5 — Training Room *(Low 6)*

> *This room contains several weapon racks, an archery target, and a training dummy. The training materials here look ready to fall apart from too much use and too little repair. A cell stands in the northwest corner of this room.*

Two hardriggans drink smuggled tainted alcohol (hidden behind a weapon rack), brawl, and sneer at the other humanoids.
- **Distracted:** **−2** to Perception to notice intruders and to **initiative**.
- Intruders are victims to beat up and maybe lock in with the leshy, who stopped being fun days ago.

**Hardriggan Bullies (2)** — Creature 5, NE Medium hardriggan humanoid, p. 177
- **Perception** +12. **Languages** Common, Jotun
- **Skills** Acrobatics +11, Athletics +13 (+15 to High Jump or Long Jump), Intimidation +11, Survival +10
- **AC** 22 (20 when their skinsuit ruptures); **Fort** +13, **Ref** +11, **Will** +12; **HP** 70; **Hardness** 5
- **Skinsuit** as the acolyte. **Speed** 30 ft
- **Melee** fist +15, 2d8+8 bludgeoning
- **Melee** horn +15 (shove), 2d6+8 bludgeoning
- **Brutal Bully Push:** a creature pushed by the bully's Shove is knocked **prone** at the end of the forced movement, if the bully doesn't Stride after it.
- **Monster Parts** 18/45/80 gp

> **📏 Rules refresher:** Horn (shove trait) plus Brutal Bully Push means Shove, then prone. See [Shove](#escape-disarm-and-shove).

### P6 — Cell

The only door is **latched from the outside**, with a metal grill to look through.

> *This squalid cell contains only a mat of old, scorched hay and a stained bucket.*

Built for the cult's prisoners and dissidents, though it preferred prompt public executions. The sklaggans render enemies and lackeys alike into Skinloom material and don't use it.

**Goaflom** (CN male wildfire leshy 2; BB 120), held here nearly a week.

### P8 — Pit *(Low 6)*

> *The floor of this chamber drops away into a pit almost 20 feet deep. The narrow edges around the pit look unstable. The wooden walls are carved with images of armored isopods with glittering crystals in their carapaces.*

The easternmost room, without the arrow slits of the guard room west of it. The cult dug for mithral under the floor before fortifying it, weakening the structure.

**The pit:**
- The **northern edge is safe**. The other three are **uneven ground**: **DC 22 Acrobatics** to Balance ([how Balance works](#balance-and-uneven-ground)). Failure: fall in with crumbling stone.
- **Falling in from the edge:** **10 bludgeoning**, landing **prone**. From the roof: **18 bludgeoning**.
- **Climbing out:** DC 22.

**Collapsing the room:**
- The first failed Balance and fall exposes this corner's **foundation posts**. **DC 20 Crafting** or **DC 15 Engineering Lore** spots that pulling them could topple the room.
- **Setting it up:** another **DC 20 Crafting** or **DC 15 Engineering Lore** check, or **DC 25 Athletics** to pull the foundation away.
  - **Success:** the character sets a delay of **0 to 10 rounds** before the collapse.
  - **Critical success:** they can set the collapse for any time **within the next hour**.
- **The collapse:** everyone in the room, pit included, takes **4d12 bludgeoning, DC 22 basic Fortitude**.
  - **Failure:** the creature is **buried**. It's **restrained** and can't free itself.
  - **Critical failure:** buried, plus **1d6 persistent bludgeoning** while trapped.
  - **Digging out:** **DC 20 Athletics**, taking **4 minutes** (2 on a critical success). Double the time if the creature is in the pit. Halve it with shovels or proper tools.
- **Side effects:** a new hole in the wall, and **every humanoid in the outer fortress** comes running.

> **💡 GM tip:** Set a 10-round collapse, slip to P10 for the viscera, and open the dome while everyone gawks.

**Orpok Flensers (3)** — p. 176, Creature 3. Initiative: Perception +9. Monster Parts 7/18/30 gp. Stats as in [P3](#p3--courtyard-moderate-6).

## The inner fortress (P14–P21)

The old mines are now a gallery of alien horrors: play them more sinister and organic (p. 183).

|---|---|
| **Ceilings** | 18 feet, under several feet of powdery black foam crust (half-solid smoke, a sklaggan by-product). Functionally **15 feet** unless torn out |

### P18 — Roiling Shadows

A resin dome seals the only entrance.

> *Eerie shadows roil along the walls of this empty stone room.*

#### Recalibrate Shadow Energies

Anyone **trained or better in Arcana or Occultism** who studies the table sees it calibrates shadow energy.

**Recalibrate Shadow Energies** *(exploration, manipulate)* — **Requirements:** trained in Arcana or Occultism. It takes **5 minutes**. Roll **DC 22 Arcana or Occultism**.
- **Critical success:** reduce the **Disable DCs of all hazards in P21 by 2**, or **by 3** if the P18 shadow energy has been released.
- **Success:** reduce them **by 1**, or **by 2** if the P18 energy has been released.
- **Failure:** soul-chilling feedback deals **3d6 cold and 3d6 negative**, **DC 20 basic Fortitude**.
- **Critical failure:** as failure, but the save result is one step worse than rolled, and **non-sklaggans can't try again**.

**Limits:** the PC learns the degree of success, not the effect. Reductions **don't stack**; **up to three** attempts, then the controls seize for good.

> **💡 GM tip:** "Hazards in P21" means the five sinew traps and the Soulweaver Trap: a critical success with P18 released cuts every boss-room Disable DC by 3. Tell the PCs only "it worked well."

### P21 — Skinloom *(Severe 6)*

> *A complicated network of skin, tendons, and exposed muscle fills the eastern end of this room, resembling a grotesque loom. The colors around the machine are dull and muted, as though washed of pigment, except for a reddish-purple gemstone growing out of a flat plate of flesh in the loom's center. The gemstone is jagged on two sides, as though broken, and has the color of an angry bruise or swollen lesion.*

The Flesh Stitcher has built the **Skinloom** since arriving with its small army of aberrations. It draws on Moonshadow Island's occult nature, the Jewel fragment, and tapped shadow energy to weave an opening to the Shadow Plane. Its maker keeps adding fleshy flourishes for art's sake and never calls it finished.

**Hazards:** hard-to-see tendon loops coil around the room; careless steps tug one and a sinew lash whips out. See the map. **The Flesh Stitcher never triggers them**, too careful of its masterpiece.

**Whipping Sinew Traps (5)** — Hazard 6, magical mechanical trap, p. 188
- **Stealth** DC 23 (trained)
- **Description:** a hair-thin loop of tendon. When disturbed, a long lash of sinew running through the room animates and whips through the air.
- **Disable:** **DC 23 Thievery** (expert) to cut the loop safely, or **DC 21 Occultism** (trained) to block the animating magic.
- **AC** 22; **Fort** +15, **Ref** +9. **Hardness** 12, **HP** 50 (BT 25). **Immunities** critical hits, object immunities, precision damage
- **Tendon Touch** (reaction). **Trigger:** the loop is pulled or severed, or a creature moves into its space. **Effect:** a sinew Strike against the triggering creature, as long as it's within 10 feet of the trap, and against a random adjacent creature, if any. On a critical hit, the target is **enfeebled 1 for 1 minute**. No multiple attack penalty.
- **Melee** sinew +19 (deadly 1d12), 3d8+3 slashing
- **Reset:** after 1 hour, if the Skinloom hasn't been destroyed, a new loop squirms into the square.

#### The Flesh Stitcher

**Three sklaggans fused together, facing outward**: a prima donna of grafted flesh. Chitin and bone aren't art; it tolerates the reddish-purple crystal only for power.

- **Parley:** it resents intruders "wasting" skin on their own organs. **Effusive praise of the Skinloom**, or the **message from P11**, can buy **a round or two of pause**. It **won't let them leave with their skins**, can't be soothed for long, and eventually attacks.
- **Tactics:** fists, but it prefers disabling spells and **Weave Together** to pin pairs.
- It **never leaves this room**, even to chase.

**Flesh Stitcher** — Creature 8, unique NE Huge aberration shadow, variant sklaggan (BB 104), p. 189
- **Perception** +17; greater darkvision
- **Languages** Aklo, Sklaggan, Shadowtongue (can't speak any language); telepathy 100 feet
- **Skills** Athletics +15, Deception +16, Intimidation +16, Medicine +17, Occultism +20, Thievery +18
- **Str** +3, **Dex** +4, **Con** +4, **Int** +6, **Wis** +3, **Cha** +4
- **AC** 27, all-around vision; **Fort** +18, **Ref** +16, **Will** +13; +1 status to all saves vs. mental
- **HP** 135
- **Extra Reaction:** it gets an additional reaction each turn, usable only for Snag Weapon.
- **Attack of Opportunity** (reaction)
- **Snag Weapon** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature attacks it with a melee weapon and misses. **Effect:** it wraps strands of alien flesh around the weapon and tries to **Disarm** the attacker, using **Thievery** instead of Athletics. On a successful Disarm, the circumstance bonus and penalty **last until the creature or an adjacent creature spends an Interact action to peel off the flesh** ([Disarm](#escape-disarm-and-shove)).
- **Speed** 30 ft
- **Melee** fist +20 (agile, finesse, reach 10 feet), 2d10+7 bludgeoning
- **Occult Innate Spells** DC 26, attack +18. **7th** *plane shift* (self only; to Shadow Plane only). **4th** *confusion*, *nightmare*, *phantasmal killer*, *vampiric touch*. **3rd** *dream message*. **2nd** *blur* (self only), *calm emotions*, *status*. **1st** *mindlink* (×3). **Cantrips (4th)** *daze*, *shield*, *telekinetic projectile*
- **Toxic Shadows** (2 actions; mental, shadow). It Strides up to its Speed. Each creature within 5 feet of its path takes **7d6 mental** and attempts a **DC 25 Fortitude** save. It can't use Toxic Shadows again for 1d4 rounds.
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage.
  - **Failure:** full damage and **sickened 1**.
  - **Critical failure:** double damage and **sickened 2**.
- **Weave Together** (1 action, manipulate; once per round). It targets two **adjacent** creatures within 30 feet and stitches them together with fleshy strings of semisolid shadow. Both attempt a **DC 25 Reflex** save. If **both fail**, or if one critically fails and the other doesn't critically succeed, they're **flat-footed and stuck together (Escape DC 25)**. While stuck, neither can move away from being adjacent to the other. A creature that Escapes is no longer stuck or flat-footed. The other stays flat-footed until it Escapes too.
- **Monster Parts** 64/140/250 gp

> **📏 Rules refresher — threat math:** The Flesh Stitcher (level 8, party +2, 80 XP) plus five simple level-6 traps (8 XP each) = 120, **Severe**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — it fights around its own traps:** PCs trigger loops; it doesn't. Toxic Shadows is a Stride, so it can sweep the PCs and end behind loops, with 10-foot fist reach. Mark the trap squares first.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — Snag Weapon twice a round:** The extra reaction lets it snag two misses a round. The −2 lasts until someone spends an action peeling the flesh off.

> **💡 GM tip — Weave Together:** Stuck pairs are easy targets for Toxic Shadows and spells. Spread out.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — pronouns:** The book uses "it" except twice ("his," p. 183, p. 186). This doc uses "it."

#### What happens if the PCs lose (Flesh Stitcher)

Book sidebar (p. 189): it wants beaten PCs' skin soon, but recalibrating the Skinloom for that much flesh may take time, so minions hold them elsewhere. While it's busy, the PCs can escape, turn on their captors, and attack again.

### Recovering the gemstone *(Moderate 6)*

A **1-foot-square** plate of hardened flesh, stitched at **each of its four corners**, sits in the Skinloom's center. The **Bruised Crystal**, the next Jewel piece, is fused to it like a crystal bruise and gives the loom much of its eerie power.

- The gemstone **can't be removed from the flesh plate while within the inner fortress**, by any means. Attempts make the plate wail through its pores and bleed.
- The **flesh plate can be carefully removed** from the Skinloom, but **touching it triggers the trap**.
- **Once carried past P14**, the plate turns brittle and flakes away, freeing the crystal.

**Soulweaver Trap** — Hazard 8, unique complex magical trap, p. 190–191
- **Stealth** +18 (expert)
- **Description:** touching the gemstone woven into the Skinloom fills the room with draining pulses. Removing the flesh plate from the Skinloom disables the trap.
- **Disable**, one corner at a time:
  - **DC 28 Athletics** (trained) or **DC 26 Thievery** (expert) **pulls a corner free** and **removes one Feedback action** from the routine.
  - **DC 28 Occultism** (trained) or *dispel magic* (4th level; counteract DC 24) **loosens a corner** and **removes the soul-draining pulse** from the routine.
  - Freeing or loosening **all four corners**, in any combination, disables the trap.
- **Corners:** Hardness 10, HP 64 (BT 32). **Immunities** object immunities. **Resistances** bludgeoning 10, piercing 10. **Weaknesses** slashing 5.
- **Unleash Feedback** (reaction). **Trigger:** a creature touches the Bruised Crystal or the flesh plate. **Effect:** the trap uses **Feedback** on the triggering creature, then rolls initiative.
- **Feedback** (1 action; necromancy, occult). The target takes **4d10 mental** and attempts a **DC 26 Will** save.
  - **Critical success:** unaffected.
  - **Success:** half damage and **slowed 1 for 1 round**.
  - **Failure:** full damage and **slowed 1 for 1 minute**.
  - **Critical failure:** full damage and **slowed 2 for 1 minute**.
- **Routine** (4 actions):
  - **Three Feedback actions.** Each one targets the **closest non-slowed creature to the Skinloom** in P21. A creature that critically succeeds and isn't slowed won't be targeted a second time in that routine.
  - **Last action, the soul-draining pulse.** Every creature in P21 attempts a **DC 26 Fortitude** save. **Slowed creatures use a result one step worse than they rolled.**
    - **Critical success:** unaffected.
    - **Success:** **2d8 persistent negative**.
    - **Failure:** **4d8 persistent negative** and **drained 1**.
    - **Critical failure:** **4d8 persistent negative** and **drained 2**.
    - The drained condition stacks across failures, up to **drained 4**.
- **Reset:** 1 minute after no creatures remain in the room, it weaves all four corners back into the Skinloom.

> **📏 Rules refresher:** A [complex hazard](gm_chapter_1.md#hazards): own initiative, and each freed corner trims its routine. One complex level-8 hazard is 80 XP, **Moderate**.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — the Feedback actions chase the front line:** Feedback hits the closest non-slowed creature and slows even on a success, so it rolls down the line of corner-workers; the pulse then punishes everyone slowed. Rotate PCs, strongest Will save first.

> **💡 GM tip — the order of corners:** Occultism or *dispel magic* first stops the drain; Athletics or Thievery first cuts Feedback. Show the players the choice.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — book wording:** The corner line says "64 (BT 32) to destroy a rune," likely copied from another trap. Treat it as corner HP.

> **⚠️ Gotcha — negative damage:** The Remaster renames negative energy to **void**.

**XP Award:** **80 XP** for recovering the Bruised Crystal.

### Bruised Crystal *(Item 7)*

Unique, artifact, invested, magical, transmutation. **Usage** held in 1 hand. **Bulk** negligible. (p. 191, also p. 378)
- A reddish-purple wedge, broken at the base from a larger whole, near-black at one edge and red at the other.
- **While you hold it in your hand**, your flesh turns spongy: **resistance 3 to bludgeoning damage**, appearance unchanged.

## Concluding the chapter

Once the Flesh Stitcher falls (p. 191):
- **The remaining shadow creatures** shun the PCs and **flee the fortress**.
- **The remaining humanoids** lose heart and **surrender**, preferring prison to this place. The PCs choose:
  - let them go home to the village on Moonshadow,
  - take them aboard as prisoners,
  - or hire them as crew.
- **The bark:** harvest on the way back if skipped. See [the clearing](#pristine-clearing-defense-special-resource).
- **The crew** greets them with admiration and suspicion, and most of the crew's bets on their return from the ill-omened island get paid.

**Level up:** leaving Moonshadow Island, the PCs rest and reach **7th level** (milestone: **Defeating the Flesh Stitcher**, p. 11).

> **💡 GM tip:** Short of 1,000 XP? The book suggests an XP award or a few more encounters before Chapter 8.

> **🧩 Sets up:** Chapter 8: Razorback Isle and the cult's sunken temple, for the last fragment.
